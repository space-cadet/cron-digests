## 1. Holographic RG flows and wormholes from sinusoidal scalars
- **Authors:** Pompey Leung
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.06465
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06465
- **Categories:** gr-qc, hep-th
- **Abstract:** We study semiclassical geometries induced by turning on identical sinusoidal scalar sources on two asymptotic anti-de Sitter (AdS) boundaries. Varying the source strength, we find that large and small wormhole solutions appear beyond a certain threshold. Above this threshold, the large wormhole is always the dominant saddle in the two-boundary gravitational path integral; there is no regime where wormholes are subdominant. Under the ensemble interpretation of the gravitational path integral, this implies that once wormhole solutions exist, the putative ensemble has exponentially large fluctuations relative to the mean. This is unlike previously studied examples of wormholes sourced by inhomogeneous matter which have a region in parameter space where wormholes are subdominant, and therefore the ensemble is sharply concentrated around the mean. Interpreting the bulk geometries as holographic renormalization group (RG) flows, we also find that the sinusoidal modulation of scalar boundary sources results in effective holographic $\beta$-functions which are multivalued, indicating exotic RG flows. In particular, disconnected geometries correspond to flows that return to the starting fixed point in the infrared, while wormholes describe flows to a gapped phase in the infrared.
- **Summary:** This work explores Euclidean wormhole geometries in holography by placing identical sinusoidal scalar sources on two AdS boundaries. Using the gravitational path integral, the author finds that wormhole saddles become *always* dominant above a critical source strength — unlike earlier models where wormholes were subdominant in some parameter regime. The analysis also maps these geometries to holographic RG flows with multivalued $\beta$-functions, where disconnected topologies represent return-to-origin flows and wormholes correspond to gapped infrared phases. The result is significant because it identifies a clean holographic setup where ensemble averaging is inevitably dominated by wormhole contributions, with direct implications for the consistency of ensemble interpretations in quantum gravity.
- **Relevance:** 🔥 High — directly addresses holographic wormholes, ensemble averaging in quantum gravity, and exotic RG flows. Central to ongoing debates about the role of wormholes in the black hole information paradox and holographic ensemble duality.
- **Tags:** Holography, Black Hole Physics, Quantum Gravity

## 2. Rational regular black holes in non-polynomial gravity
- **Authors:** Aimeric Colléaux
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.17158
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17158
- **Categories:** gr-qc, hep-th
- **Abstract:** We consider the $d\geq 4$ non-polynomial pure gravity theories defined so that their 2D reduction to spherical symmetry minimally modifies that of General Relativity at small distances. They satisfy Birkhoff theorem and admit exact black hole solutions for an arbitrary number of couplings. These solutions have a logarithmic leading correction to the entropy and a quantum-like correction to the Newtonian potential, both arising from the regularised critical order $d=2p$ theory, which is related in even dimensions to the Euler characteristic of the 2D orbit space. These black holes can be made regular, without the need for infinite towers of corrections, using invariants of any order $p>d/2$. In even dimensions, their smoothness is linear in the number of corrections and does not require fine-tuning. We construct a class of theories admitting as unique (massless) vacuum an extremal black hole deformation of (A)dS$_4$ or M$_4$ with vanishing entropy, so that Nernst's third law of thermodynamics is satisfied without discontinuity in the entropy. We show that a Maxwell field does not disrupt the regularity of the metric, and that simple non-minimal couplings in the action enable to regularise the electric field. Considering infinite towers of corrections, we construct quasi-regular black holes with one horizon as well as regular (A)dS-core ones with a near-extremal inner horizon for any mass. When charged, these two types of black holes prevent or tame the mass inflation provided the mass and charge satisfy an inequality of the form $M > \alpha \ell + \beta Q^2 / \ell$, where $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are numerical factors depending on the specific model, while $\ell$ is the length scale controlling the high-energy corrections. The quasi-regular black holes are sufficient to resolve the Coulomb singularity without the need for non-minimal couplings.
- **Summary:** The author constructs exact regular black hole solutions in a class of non-polynomial higher-derivative gravity theories in $d \geq 4$ dimensions. A key innovation is that regularity can be achieved with *finite* sets of curvature invariants (no infinite tower needed) and without fine-tuning in even dimensions. The solutions satisfy the Birkhoff theorem, exhibit logarithmic entropy corrections, and include a zero-entropy extremal vacuum that respects Nernst's third law continuously. Charged extensions are shown to tame mass inflation, offering a concrete framework for singularity resolution within modified gravity that remains tractable and physically well-motivated.
- **Relevance:** 🔥 High — singularity resolution and regular black holes are core topics in quantum gravity. The finite-correction approach is more predictive than asymptotic safety or string-inspired infinite towers, making this particularly interesting for phenomenology.
- **Tags:** Black Hole Physics, Quantum Gravity, Field Theory & Mathematical Physics

## 3. Ostrogradsky's Theorem is Incompatible with Background Independence in Quantum Gravity
- **Authors:** Ken-ji Hamada
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.17364
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17364
- **Categories:** physics.hist-ph, gr-qc, hep-th, hep-ph
- **Abstract:** Ostrogradsky's theorem shows that higher-derivative dynamical systems inevitably yields a Hamiltonian featuring a ghost mode that is unbounded from below. However, we argue that this theorem is inapplicable to gravitational systems, as the Hamiltonian constraint, which encodes background independence by dictating that the total Hamiltonian vanishes, holds strictly. The ghost mode obeying this constraint constitutes an indispensable component in the construction of space and time. Nevertheless, it should be underscored that the use of the weak-field (graviton) approximation, being a background-dependent scheme presupposing the existence of absolute time, invokes this theorem; consequently, it is allowed only in the domain below the Planck scale, where second derivative terms dominate.
- **Summary:** Ostrogradsky's theorem famously forbids stable higher-derivative theories by predicting ghost instabilities, but this paper argues the theorem does not apply to gravity because the Hamiltonian constraint ($\mathcal{H} = 0$) enforces background independence. In this view, the would-be "ghost" is tamed by the constraint and actually participates in constructing spacetime itself. The author further points out that perturbative graviton calculations *do* invoke Ostrogradsky's theorem because they break background independence by fixing a background — which is why such approximations are only valid below the Planck scale. This reframes the ghost problem as an artifact of background-dependent approximation schemes rather than a fundamental obstruction to higher-derivative quantum gravity.
- **Relevance:** 🔥 High — challenges a foundational no-go theorem in quantum gravity. Directly relevant to approaches using higher-derivative gravity, asymptotic safety, and any program where background independence is taken as fundamental.
- **Tags:** Quantum Gravity, Foundations & Interpretations, Field Theory & Mathematical Physics

## 4. Vacuum Polarization in Quantum-Corrected and Effective Black Hole Geometries: a High Performance Approach
- **Authors:** Antonino Flachi
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.17670
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17670
- **Categories:** gr-qc, hep-th
- **Abstract:** We compute the renormalized scalar vacuum polarization $\langle \phi^2 \rangle$ of a massive, non-minimally coupled quantum field in the Hartle--Hawking state exterior to a family of static, spherically symmetric quantum-corrected, effective and regular black holes: the Kazakov--Solodukhin quantum-deformed black hole, the two effective loop-quantum-gravity geometries of Zhang--Lewandowski--Ma--Yang, the renormalization-group improved Schwarzschild black hole of Bonanno--Reuter, and the Bardeen regular black hole. We adapt the extended point-splitting mode-sum formalism, implemented as a high-performance code, and present the exterior profile of the vacuum polarization over a grid of the quantum-deformation parameter, the field mass $\mu=mM$ and the curvature coupling $\xi$. The five geometries display qualitatively distinct horizon responses. We verify this both in magnitude and in the $\xi$-dependence. In the near-extremal regime of the two geometries with inner horizons we find a sign change of the horizon polarization at light field mass -- absent for Reissner--Nordström at matched temperature and removed by conformal coupling -- identifying a genuinely quantum-geometric, de-Sitter-core-driven regime. Every result reduces to the Schwarzschild value in the classical limit, validating the calculation.
- **Summary:** This paper presents a systematic numerical study of vacuum polarization $\langle \phi^2 \rangle$ outside five distinct quantum-corrected black hole geometries, including two effective models from loop quantum gravity. Using a high-performance implementation of point-splitting regularization, the author maps how quantum deformations alter the vacuum polarization across parameter space. A notable finding is a sign reversal in the near-extremal regime of LQG-inspired geometries with de Sitter cores — an effect absent in classical Reissner–Nordström and eliminated by conformal coupling, suggesting it is a genuine quantum-geometric signature. The work provides a benchmarkable computational framework and concrete observational discriminators between different quantum gravity-inspired black hole models.
- **Relevance:** 🔥 High — directly compares loop quantum gravity effective geometries against other quantum-corrected black hole models using a concrete quantum field theory observable. The sign-change signature could help distinguish LQG phenomenology from other approaches.

## 5. Holographic Entanglement and Emergent Gravity
- **Authors:** Mohammad Ali-Akbari
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.15862
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15862
- **Categories:** hep-th
- **Abstract:** We investigate the emergence of bulk geometry from boundary entanglement entropy in the context of holographic duality. Considering a thermal $1+1$-dimensional conformal field theory dual to the BTZ black hole, we compute four distinct holographic entanglement measures: the standard spacelike HEE, its complementary spacelike counterpart HEE$^s$, the timelike HTEE and its complementary timelike counterpart HEE$^t$. These measures probe both the exterior and interior regions of the black hole horizon. Remarkably, by taking appropriate derivatives of these entanglement entropies with respect to the boundary intervals and the turning point of the extremal surface, we reconstruct the full BTZ metric, including the radial component $g_{zz}$, without imposing the Einstein equations. The reconstruction yields the correct metric components in all regions of the geometry. We further show that in the zero-temperature limit, our results consistently reduce to the pure AdS$_3$ metric and the familiar vacuum CFT$_2$ entanglement entropies. Our findings demonstrate that entanglement entropy data alone contains sufficient information to uniquely determine the dual gravitational background, offering a concrete realization of holographic emergence where gravity arises from the quantum information structure of the boundary theory.
- **Summary:** The author demonstrates that the full BTZ black hole metric can be reconstructed purely from boundary entanglement entropy data, without assuming the Einstein equations. By computing four variants of holographic entanglement entropy (spacelike, timelike, and their complements) and taking appropriate derivatives with respect to boundary intervals and extremal surface turning points, all metric components — including the radial direction — are recovered. This provides an explicit proof-of-concept for the ER=EPR and It-from-Qubit philosophies: entanglement structure on the boundary is sufficient to reconstruct the bulk gravitational geometry, including the black hole interior.
- **Relevance:** 🔥 High — a concrete reconstruction of a black hole spacetime from entanglement data alone. Directly connects to Deepak's interests in holography, black hole interiors, and the emergence of spacetime from quantum information.

## 6. The linear dilaton in cosmology and particle physics
- **Authors:** Eugenio Megias, Mariano Quiros
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.16772
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16772
- **Categories:** astro-ph.CO, gr-qc, hep-th, hep-ph
- **Abstract:** A warped extra dimension in a five-dimensional (5D) anti de Sitter (AdS) background was introduced in 1999 by Lisa Randall and Raman Sundrum to solve the gauge hierarchy problem in particle physics. As a bonus, a holographic interpretation in terms of four dimensional (4D) conformal field theories (CFT) was found. Interestingly enough, another 5D background, the linear dilaton (LD), was found to have a holographic interpretation in terms of Little String Theory. In this review we will show how a set of 5D backgrounds, parametrized in terms of a real parameter $\nu$, generalizes both theories and gives rise, in particular, to AdS for $\nu=0$ and to LD for $\nu=1$. Furthermore, working in the 5D theory, we will consider applications of the LD background to: i) Particle Physics, so that the 5D Planck scale can be lowered to sub-Planckian values, ii) Brane World Cosmology (BWC), based on the appearance of an extra vacuum characterized by a 5D black hole. In all cases we find a gapped continuum for bulk propagating fields, which makes connection with unparticles. In the case of BWC we also point out on the existence of a pressureless holographic fluid which could play the role of dark matter (DM), with feeble (gravitational) interactions to the Standard Model (SM), decoupled from the thermal SM bath, and generated by a freeze-in mechanism after inflation. We also point out the additional possibility of identifying DM with a long-lived feebly interacting massive graviton, as an isolated resonance generated by radiative corrections to the continuum graviton propagator self-energy.
- **Summary:** This review unifies Randall-Sundrum (AdS) and linear dilaton (Little String Theory) backgrounds into a one-parameter family of 5D geometries, then explores particle physics and cosmological applications. In the linear dilaton regime, the 5D Planck scale can be lowered to sub-Planckian values, and a novel brane-world cosmology vacuum emerges containing a 5D black hole. The authors identify a pressureless holographic fluid as a dark matter candidate produced by freeze-in, and also propose a feebly interacting massive graviton resonance as an alternative dark matter candidate from radiative corrections to the bulk graviton propagator.
- **Relevance:** Medium — connects string-theoretic holography to cosmological phenomenology and dark matter model building. The holographic fluid and massive graviton dark matter proposals are novel, though the framework is more phenomenological than directly connected to LQG.

## 7. Removability criterion for radiative corrections to the Starobinsky attractor in weakly nonlocal gravity
- **Authors:** Ekapob Kulchoakrungsun, Phongpichit Channuie, Daris Samart
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.17215
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17215
- **Categories:** gr-qc, hep-th
- **Abstract:** Radiative corrections at the percent level can shift the predictions of $\alpha$-attractor and Starobinsky-like models into the region of the $(n_s,r)$ plane preferred by ACT DR6. We show that the decisive property is not the size of a correction but whether it is removable. In the slow-roll $N$-formalism the observables are functionals of $\eps(N)$, and equality of $\eps(N)$ on an interval reconstructs the potential up to an amplitude rescaling and a shift of the canonical field. A larger class, selected by $(V/V')\Delta'={\rm const}$, preserves the leading predictions and provides a criterion for any proposed deformation. In weakly nonlocal completions of $R+R^{2}$ the improvement scale $\mu=H(\phi)$ makes the renormalization-group time one half of the logarithm of the tree potential, so the local one-loop flow passes the test and evolves the model onto the E-model family $V\propto(1-e^{-\sqrt{2/3}\,\phi/\mpl})^{p}$ with $p=2+K$. Since the divergent local beta functions of these super-renormalizable completions are one-loop exact, no higher-loop local running modifies this picture. The induced shifts are $\ord(\ln N/N^{2})$ in $n_s$, obtained in closed form for the removable part and numerically for the rest. The number of $e$-folds, which the criterion does not protect, contributes at the $22\%$ level with an opposite sign. Matter-induced corrections fail the same test at leading order, which is why they shift the predictions. Among the deformations proposed since ACT DR6, an $R^{n}$ term in $f(R)$ gives $(V/V')\Delta'=\ord(N^{n-1})$, so only $n=2$ is removable, and no deformation that increases $n_s$ satisfies the criterion. For Standard Model matter, the removable channel is fixed by the Higgs nonminimal coupling alone and is $\ord(10^{-11})$, because the measured amplitude sets the coefficient of $R^{2}$ to $5.1\times10^{8}$.
- **Summary:** The authors identify a sharp criterion — "removability" — that determines whether radiative corrections to inflationary potentials preserve or destroy the Starobinsky/attractor predictions. In weakly nonlocal $R + R^2$ gravity, the improvement scale $\mu = H(\phi)$ ensures one-loop corrections are removable, so the model remains observationally viable with only $\mathcal{O}(\ln N / N^2)$ shifts in $n_s$. By contrast, matter-induced corrections and generic $f(R)$ deformations fail the removability test, explaining why they can push models into tension with CMB data. The result provides a principled filter for distinguishing benign quantum corrections from dangerous ones in quantum gravity-inspired inflation.
- **Relevance:** Medium — connects quantum gravity corrections (nonlocal gravity) to inflationary observables. The removability criterion is a useful theoretical tool, though the focus is on cosmological phenomenology rather than quantum gravity fundamentals.
- **Tags:** Cosmology & Early Universe, Field Theory & Mathematical Physics, Quantum Gravity

## 8. Who Writes the Gravitational Second Law of Thermodynamics?
- **Authors:** V. R. Shajiee, M. M. Sheikh-Jabbari, V. Taghiloo
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.16997
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16997
- **Categories:** gr-qc, hep-th
- **Abstract:** We define gravitational entropy as the manifestly integrable surface charge associated with local transverse Lorentz boosts, entirely bypassing the conventional reliance on spacetime diffeomorphisms. Any notion of entropy must satisfy the second law. To answer the question posed in the title, an analogy with Newtonian classical mechanics is instructive: Newton's second law of motion is written by inertial observers who are defined by the first law of mechanics. We show that the second law of gravitational thermodynamics is written by free-fall observers with path parameterization in which the non-affinity of the geodesics is equal to the expansion of their velocity vector field. We then provide a proof of the local second law by studying variations in entropy as viewed by this class of covariantly-defined causal free-fall observers. We show that the entropy variation is strictly non-decreasing, provided the matter sector satisfies the integrated strong energy condition along the observer path.
- **Summary:** The authors construct gravitational entropy as an integrable surface charge for local transverse Lorentz boosts, avoiding the usual diffeomorphism-based approaches. They then prove a local second law by identifying a privileged class of free-fall observers — those whose geodesic non-affinity equals the expansion of their velocity field — and showing that entropy is non-decreasing along their trajectories when the integrated strong energy condition holds. The analogy to Newton's laws is conceptual: just as inertial observers "write" the second law of mechanics, these specially parameterized free-fall observers "write" the second law of gravitational thermodynamics.
- **Relevance:** Medium — advances the covariant formulation of black hole thermodynamics and entropy. The surface charge approach is elegant and background-covariant, though the strong energy condition assumption limits applicability to semiclassical regimes.

## 9. Gravity and Matter from Soldered Chiral Superconnections

- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17900
- **Categories:** math-ph, gr-qc, hep-th
- **Abstract:** We present a reformulation of four-dimensional chiral gravity in terms of a chiral gauge connection and a Clifford-valued coframe. The exterior algebra generated by $e^a \gamma_a$, together with the gauge curvature $F$, provides a natural and systematic construction of gauge-covariant differential forms. We show that suitable combinations of the chiral actions reproduce Einstein--Cartan--Holst gravity with a cosmological term. The original superconnection invariants yield only algebraic fermionic equations, so the fermionic one-forms do not propagate but instead generate a mass-like mixing between the two chiralities. The construction naturally yields Einstein-Cartan-Holst gravity with a cosmological constant, while fermions emerge from superconnection invariants that produce chirality-mixing mass terms rather than propagating fields — propagation is instead supplied by a spinorial BF term. The formulation connects to twistor geometry and residual supersymmetry, offering a novel first-order framework where gravity and matter are soldered into a single superconnection structure.

- **Categories:** gr-qc
- **Abstract:** Black holes embedded in a dark matter background represent an important candidate model that extends the standard vacuum case, characterizing the strong gravitational environments at galactic centers and the gravitational influence of dark matter. This study focuses on dark matter halo-modified Kerr black holes and systematically investigates the observational characteristics of their surrounding thin accretion disks. The dark matter halo is modeled by a general double power-law density profile, whose integrated mass $M_D(r)$ modifies the radial mass function of Kerr metric to $m(r) = M + M_D(r)$. Based on this spacetime, we analyze the observational properties of their thin accretion disk, including the radiative flux, temperature, differential luminosity, and spectral luminosity as a function of radius, with respect to the black hole spin and dark matter halo parameters. Furthermore, by combining ray-tracing methods, we simulate the bolometric image of the thin accretion disk under different black hole spins, dark matter halo parameters, and viewing inclinations. The dark matter halo enhances the gravitational potential, shifting the innermost stable circular orbit (ISCO) of the accretion disk outward, suppressing the peak radiative flux, and producing a fainter image, thereby generating signatures that are possibly observational distinguishable from standard vacuum Kerr black holes.
- **Summary:** This paper models thin accretion disks around Kerr black holes modified by a double power-law dark matter halo, then computes observable signatures including flux profiles, temperature distributions, and ray-traced bolometric images. The dark matter halo pushes the ISCO outward, suppresses peak flux, and produces fainter disk images compared to vacuum Kerr — all potentially distinguishable by future observations. The work provides a template for testing whether supermassive black holes at galactic centers are embedded in significant dark matter distributions using accretion disk spectroscopy and imaging.
- **Relevance:** Low-Medium — astrophysical modeling with observational signatures. Relevant to black hole phenomenology but not directly to quantum gravity or LQG.
