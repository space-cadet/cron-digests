## 1. Regge Dilaton Gravity from Feynman Diagrams
**Authors:** Florian Girelli, Christopher Pollack  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24691v1  
**Categories:** hep-th, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** Baratin and Freidel showed that closed Feynman diagrams in three and four Euclidean dimensions can be expressed as expectation values of observables in a Regge-type topological model \cite{baratin_hidden_2007, baratin_hidden_2007-1}. We extend their analysis to two dimensions with spherical, flat, or hyperbolic background geometry. Specifically, we show that the reduced Feynman amplitude of any closed (planar) scalar graph can be represented as an observable insertion in the partition function of either a discretized BF theory, whose gauge group is the isometry group of the background, or a homogeneous Regge discretization of linear dilaton gravity.
- **Summary:** Closed planar scalar diagrams in two dimensions are recast as observable insertions in discrete BF theory or a Regge version of linear dilaton gravity, with the background isometry group controlling the BF description. Extending the diagram-to-geometry correspondence to spherical, flat, and hyperbolic backgrounds supplies an explicit bridge between matter amplitudes and discrete gravitational partition functions.
**RELEVANCE:** Connects discrete quantum gravity to BF formulations relevant to spin foams; this is not a new LQG dynamics result.  
**TAGS:** `Regge dilaton gravity` `BF theory` `Feynman amplitudes`

## 2. The limits of magic-state cultivation
**Authors:** Llorenç Balada Gaggioli, Jakub Mareček  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24852v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph
- **Abstract:** Fault tolerance is understood through Pauli algebra, which fails at non-Clifford gates. Circuits such as magic-state cultivation, which checks an encoded T state with physical T gates, have therefore lacked an exact theory of which faults are harmful. We develop such a theory and show that, for any circuit that prepares a magic state with non-Clifford gates and postselection, one stabilizer code on the non-Clifford gates decides which faults are harmful. This ties cultivation to distillation, bounds its T-count from below and gives an exact test. For a single check on an ideally encoded state, the fault distance reduces to Pauli algebra on the code. As a use case, we analyze the S gates that have stood in for T gates in the design of cultivation. We show that this proxy can only overstate the fault distance of such a check, and that the checks in use, on color and surface codes, tolerate at most half as many faults as their code allows. The theory also yields nearest-neighbor checks that keep the full distance, certified up to code distance nine.
- **Summary:** A stabilizer code defined on non-Clifford gates gives an exact harmful-fault criterion for postselected magic-state preparation, linking cultivation to distillation and establishing T-count lower bounds. Applying it exposes optimistic distance estimates from S-for-T proxies in existing checks, while producing nearest-neighbor alternatives that retain full distance and are certified through distance nine.
**RELEVANCE:** Directly relevant to reliable non-Clifford resources and fault-tolerant computing overhead.  
**TAGS:** `magic-state cultivation` `fault distance` `non-Clifford resources`

## 3. Learning the Anyon Atlas
**Authors:** Jonah Berean-Dutcher, Clay Cordova, Julio Parra-Martinez, Richard Tuddenham, Zekai Zhu  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24752v1  
**Categories:** hep-th, cond-mat.str-el, cs.LG, math.QA
- **Abstract:** We extend our previous search for the modular data of topological quantum field theories in 2+1 dimensions from 22 to 42 anyons, identifying 150,679 new modular data. We do so by adding to our algorithm three rejection gates, two of them based on logistic regression, that discard representations of $SL(2,\mathbb{Z})$ unlikely to yield modular data. We estimate that roughly 80% of the modular data between ranks 23 and 42 has been identified after closing under known constructions. With this data, we find counterexamples to a conjecture of Geiko and Moore that the reality of the higher Gauss sums suffices for time-reversal symmetry in Chern-Simons theories, and propose a refined criterion. We demonstrate the scalability of this gated implementation by identifying prime modular data at rank 100 that is not realized by any Chern-Simons theory. The identified data through rank 42 has been made available at https://AnyonAtlas.org, to which further data will be added as it is found.
- **Summary:** A representation-search pipeline with three rejection gates, including two learned classifiers, finds 150,679 new modular data and extends the catalog to 42 anyons, with estimated rather than proven completeness. Counterexamples to a proposed time-reversal criterion and a rank-100 prime example beyond Chern-Simons realizations turn computational classification into new structural constraints.
**RELEVANCE:** Links topological matter, anyonic quantum information, and topological field theory.  
**TAGS:** `modular data` `anyon classification` `time-reversal symmetry`

## 4. Cosmological First-Order Phase Transitions with Full Nonlinear Gravity
**Authors:** Campbell Benedix, Eve Currens, John T. Giblin, Chloe Richards  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24581v1  
**Categories:** astro-ph.CO, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** Cosmological first-order phase transitions can contribute to the stochastic gravitational wave background. For decades, these first-order phase transitions have been studied using a Friedmann--Lemaître--Robertson--Walker background where the gravitational waves are treated as linear perturbations. Here, we present simulations of a first-order phase transition in the presence of fully nonlinear general relativity, using the BSSN formalism. We simulate a universe that contains a scalar field alongside a relativistic radiation fluid. We focus on GUT-scale transitions in which bubbles nucleate near the horizon size. We describe a method to construct initial data that satisfies the Hamiltonian and momentum constraints, starting from a Coleman--de~Luccia-like bubble profile and adjusting the bubble radius. We compute the gravitational wave spectrum directly from the trace-free extrinsic curvature and find a present-day peak amplitude of $Ω_{\rm GW,0}h^2 \sim 5\times 10^{-11}$, consistent with earlier results that do not include nonlinear gravity. The peak frequency depends on the symmetry-breaking scale and could fall anywhere from the LISA band to GHz frequencies. At late times, the field fragments into long-lived oscillons, which push its effective equation of state toward that of matter.
- **Summary:** BSSN simulations evolve a scalar field and radiation fluid through horizon-scale, GUT-scale bubble transitions with constraint-satisfying initial data rather than a fixed FLRW background. The present-day gravitational-wave peak is about 5×10⁻¹¹ in Ω_GW,0 h², consistent with earlier approximations, while late oscillon formation drives the field toward matter-like behavior, testing nonlinear corrections in the simulated regime.
**RELEVANCE:** Brings numerical relativity into early-universe transitions and gravitational-wave phenomenology.  
**TAGS:** `nonlinear cosmological gravity` `first-order transitions` `oscillons`

## 5. A New GUT
**Authors:** Prateek Agrawal, Michael Nee, Mario Reig  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24671v1  
**Categories:** hep-ph, hep-th
- **Abstract:** We present a new class of 4-dimensional Grand Unified Theories where the gauge sectors of the standard model have a novel embedding in the unified group that does not factor through an $SU(5)$ subgroup. The smallest example has gauge algebra $\frak{su}(12)$ and an anomaly-free spectrum that produces the three standard model generations with no additional chiral matter. The embedding predicts the axion-photon anomaly ratio $E/N=2/3$ and the weak mixing angle $\sin^2θ_W=1/2$ at the unification scale. Gauge-mediated proton decay proceeds through flipped-$SU(5)$-like operators and the minimally charged smooth monopole carries two Dirac charge units. The global gauge group can be $SU(12)$ or $SU(12)/\mathbb{Z}_2$, with the former allowing color singlets of half-integer electric charge. We develop a GUT bootstrap to constrain $SU(n)$ embeddings with three generations and no additional chiral matter. For unified fermions in up to two-index representations, we prove that all new embeddings arise from the $SU(8m+4)$ family, including flavor unified models for $m \geq 2$.
- **Summary:** An anomaly-free SU(12) construction embeds three Standard Model generations without extra chiral matter or an intermediate SU(5) embedding, predicting E/N=2/3 and a unification-scale weak mixing parameter of 1/2. A bootstrap restricted to at-most-two-index fermion representations places all new embeddings in an SU(8m+4) family, offering a constrained model-building framework rather than demonstrated phenomenological viability.
**RELEVANCE:** Relevant to high-energy unification, axion couplings, and global gauge structure.  
**TAGS:** `SU(12) unification` `GUT bootstrap` `axion anomaly`

## 6. Stabilizer models of achiral abelian topological orders
**Authors:** Marvin Qi, Wilbur Shirley  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24105v1  
**Categories:** cond-mat.str-el, hep-th, quant-ph
- **Abstract:** We construct continuous-variable stabilizer codes realizing every achiral 2D abelian topological order, both bosonic and fermionic. Starting from an arbitrary signature-zero integer $K$-matrix, we show that the corresponding topological order can be obtained by condensing a lattice of dyons with trivial pairwise mutual braiding in an $\mathbb{R}^N$ gauge theory. We implement this condensation microscopically by gauging a non-on-site 1-form symmetry of the $\mathbb{R}^N$ toric code.
- **Summary:** Starting from any signature-zero integer K-matrix, the construction obtains its achiral abelian topological order by condensing mutually trivially braiding dyons in an R^N gauge theory. Gauging a non-on-site one-form symmetry implements this microscopically as a continuous-variable stabilizer code, covering bosonic and fermionic orders and connecting topological classification to explicit code models.
**RELEVANCE:** Bridges stabilizer information, generalized symmetries, and topological phases.  
**TAGS:** `continuous-variable stabilizers` `anyon condensation` `achiral topological order`

## 7. A Nonsecular Quantum Master Equation with Detailed Balance and Second-order Error Bound
**Authors:** Ji-Hui Pei, Jin-Fu Chen  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24911v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph, cond-mat.stat-mech
- **Abstract:** An effective theory of open quantum systems should preserve the detailed balance structure inherited from microscopic reversibility and remain accurate throughout relaxation. Meeting both requirements is particularly challenging in quantum many-body systems, where dense spectra can invalidate the secular approximation even at weak coupling. Here we construct a nonsecular quantum master equation that satisfies time-reversal Kubo-Martin-Schwinger (TR-KMS) detailed balance. Under suitable conditions, we prove a time-uniform $O(λ^2)$ bound on the difference between the predicted and exact reduced density matrices, where $λ$ is the system-bath coupling strength. Our construction completes the Redfield generator by imposing detailed balance while matching its dissipative and coherent coefficients at resonance. In this approach, TR-KMS detailed balance is compatible with resonance matching, whereas its counterpart without time reversal is not. In a benchmark against the exact microscopic dynamics of two coupled oscillators, our equation maintains $O(λ^2)$ accuracy across secular and nonsecular regimes, outperforming the existing detailed-balance equations tested. These results provide a systematic route from microscopic models to thermodynamically consistent descriptions of open quantum dynamics beyond the secular approximation.
- **Summary:** Completing the Redfield generator with time-reversal KMS balance while matching resonant coefficients yields a nonsecular equation with a conditional, time-uniform O(λ²) reduced-state error bound. Exact two-oscillator benchmarks preserve this accuracy across secular and nonsecular regimes, providing a thermodynamically structured alternative when dense spectra invalidate secularization.
**RELEVANCE:** Useful for open quantum information systems and many-body relaxation.  
**TAGS:** `nonsecular master equations` `TR-KMS balance` `weak-coupling error bounds`

## 8. From geometric curvature to effective backgrounds: a dictionary for Dirac fermions in one, two and three spatial dimensions
**Authors:** David Valenzuela, Alfredo Raya  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24878v1  
**Categories:** cond-mat.str-el, gr-qc, hep-ph, hep-th, math-ph
- **Abstract:** Dirac equation on curved backgrounds can be rewritten as a flat-space equation in which geometry appears as a tensorial Fermi velocity together with a set of background fields. We derive this correspondence from a single Clifford-algebra identity and follow it through one, two, and three spatial dimensions. Once the spin connection is written in terms of Ricci rotation coefficients, only two contractions can occur. The trace produces a term with the index structure of a vector potential in every dimensionality; it enters with an imaginary factor i and is precisely the term that keeps the operator Hermitian when the Fermi velocity depends on position. The totally antisymmetric contraction produces an object that depends on the number of indices available to the Levi-Civita symbol: a trace on a line, a pseudoscalar mass on a surface, and an axial vector potential in a volume. Neither contraction is invariant under local rotations of the frame, and we give the transformation laws. On a surface, the curl of the trace term can always be removed by a frame rotation, while its divergence is proportional to the Gaussian curvature. We apply the correspondence to a Gaussian bump, where the local density of states at the center falls below its flat value and obeys a closed form at low energy; to a wire, where a nonuniform lapse binds states inside the gap and a nonuniform velocity cannot; to stationary frames on a surface, where a circulating drag shifts the Dirac mass locally with either sign; and to twisted and rotating frames in a volume, where the axial term takes the form of a chiral chemical potential produced by the frame alone, or separates Weyl nodes in a rotating sample. We separate the geometric contribution from the lattice pseudogauge field that dominates the response of strained samples
- **Summary:** A Clifford-algebra derivation translates curved-background Dirac dynamics into position-dependent Fermi velocities and dimension-specific effective fields, explicitly tracking frame dependence. Applications distinguish velocity modulation from lapse-induced binding and identify geometric mass and axial terms, providing a dictionary for geometric analogues without conflating them with lattice pseudogauge fields.
**RELEVANCE:** Directly connects relativistic geometry with condensed-matter Dirac systems.  
**TAGS:** `curved-space Dirac fermions` `frame-dependent backgrounds` `geometric analogues`

## 9. The magical memory cost of entanglement spectrum compression
**Authors:** Matias Karjula, Vilma Nieminen, Teemu Ojanen, Tapio Ala-Nissila, Moein N. Ivaki  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24710v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph, cond-mat.str-el
- **Abstract:** How much quantum memory is needed to faithfully retain the entanglement spectrum of a bipartite pure state after separating out Bell pairs through local unitaries? We derive the exact tradeoff between Bell-pair yield, residual memory, and reconstruction fidelity under local unitaries, defining an operational measure of entanglement-spectrum complexity. Smoothed entanglement spread bounds this memory cost from below, while diverging nonlocal magic forces unbounded memory at any fixed positive fidelity. Chaotic circuit dynamics exhibit a transient memory peak whose height increases with system size, followed by declining cost despite continued entropy growth. A large typical Haar-random state requires only an accuracy-dependent memory, whereas stationary fermionic Gaussian states retain a growing cost with size. In the circuits studied, the multiscale spectral structure associated with entanglement catalysis also has a practical consequence for matrix product state compression, yielding a multiplicative overhead not captured by the conventional entropy-based bond dimension estimate.
- **Summary:** An exact local-unitary tradeoff among Bell-pair extraction, residual memory, and reconstruction fidelity defines an operational entanglement-spectrum complexity, bounded using entanglement spread and nonlocal magic. Circuit and state-family comparisons show memory can decline while entropy grows and reveal matrix-product-state compression overhead beyond entropy estimates, separating spectral complexity from entanglement amount.
**RELEVANCE:** Relevant to entanglement resources, tensor networks, and many-body complexity.  
**TAGS:** `entanglement-spectrum compression` `nonlocal magic` `quantum memory`

## 10. On Reaction Depth and Fast Fault-Tolerant Boolean Logic
**Authors:** Christopher Kang, Alexander Cowtan, Adam Bene Watts, Guillaume Dauphinais, Benjamin Ide  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24687v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph
- **Abstract:** Reducing runtimes of quantum applications is critical for practical viability. On quantum computers, the runtime can depend both on the speed of quantum operations and on how often the program must wait for the classical coprocessor to determine adaptive operations. Some recent approaches to reducing this overhead have focused on minimizing the "reaction depth" of a computation, informally defined as the number of times the classical coprocessor must be consulted while running a quantum program. In this work, we formalize the notion of reaction depth. We find several subtleties in this theory. For example, we distinguish the reaction depth of a circuit from that of a circuit run, and show that the magic depth of a unitary can be higher than the reaction depth once compiled into Clifford computation and magic state injection. We then focus on the specific case of coherent Boolean logic, a common and expensive component in quantum algorithms. We use a natural gateset for fast operations with Low-Density Parity Check (LDPC) codes, which consists of homogeneous $Z$- and $X$-type Pauli Product Measurements (PPMs), alongside a supply of Toffoli states. We prove that, using this gateset, any Boolean arithmetic circuit can be implemented on a quantum computer with constant quantum depth and constant reaction depth, at the cost of exponential space and leaving some qubits "dirty". Using the same gateset we find two efficient AutoAND circuits that require only a Toffoli state and homogeneous PPMs. We then give efficient implementations of the quantum ripple-carry adder and quantum schoolbook multiplier, which require a low number of heterogeneous PPMs for uncomputation. We find implementations competitive with the state-of-the-art despite the restricted gateset.
- **Summary:** Formalizing reaction depth separates classical feedback latency from magic depth, and a Pauli-measurement plus Toffoli-state construction achieves constant-depth Boolean arithmetic only with exponential space and dirty qubits. Efficient AutoAND, ripple-carry, and schoolbook-multiplier constructions then show how restricted LDPC-friendly operations can compete with existing designs, clarifying practical latency-resource tradeoffs.
**RELEVANCE:** Targets fault-tolerant algorithm runtimes and quantum-classical control bottlenecks.  
**TAGS:** `reaction depth` `fault-tolerant arithmetic` `Pauli product measurements`

## 11. Computing $F$-Symbols from Modular Data
**Authors:** Jonah Berean-Dutcher, Clay Cordova, Julio Parra-Martinez, Richard Tuddenham  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24753v1  
**Categories:** hep-th, cond-mat.str-el, math.QA
- **Abstract:** We present a strategy for computing the $F$-symbols of unitary modular categories with a self-dual simple generator, starting from their modular data, which almost entirely avoids the non-linear solution of the pentagon equations. The algorithm climbs a ladder of braid group representations on successively more strands of the generator until enough $F$-symbols have been determined to propagate them to a complete solution. The strategy presented is successful in difficult cases through ranks 11 and 12, including $\operatorname{Z}(\mathrm{Haagerup}_3)$, and does not require the category to be multiplicity-free. Although the algorithm is implemented numerically, we describe a reliable means of recovering the exact $F$-symbols, assuming that in some gauge they lie in a given number field, and of certifying every pentagon and hexagon equation exactly. The exotic rank-11 anisotropic modular category B11-00119 (https://anyonatlas.org/data/B11-00119), with largest fusion coefficient 4, is used as a motivating example, for which the solve takes roughly 10 minutes on a laptop. We will continue to add $F$-symbol solutions to https://AnyonAtlas.org as they are completed.
- **Summary:** A ladder of braid-group representations reconstructs associativity data from modular data for categories with a self-dual simple generator, largely avoiding nonlinear pentagon solving while accommodating fusion multiplicities. Numerical solutions reach difficult rank-11 and rank-12 cases, with number-field-dependent exact recovery and certification, making otherwise costly anyon calculations tractable.
**RELEVANCE:** Computational tools for topological computing and categorical descriptions of matter.  
**TAGS:** `F-symbol reconstruction` `braid representations` `modular categories`
