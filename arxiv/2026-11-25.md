## 1. Dirichlet walls beyond JT gravity and their quantum group interpretation
**Authors:** Luca Griguolo, Jacopo Papalini, Lorenzo Russo, Domenico Seminara, Alex Tarana  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30567v1  
**Categories:** hep-th
- **Abstract:** We canonically quantize sinh dilaton gravity with finite Dirichlet boundary conditions. The reduced phase space is formulated in terms of the length of a two-sided spacelike geodesic in an effective AdS$_2$ geometry and its conjugate momentum. Canonical quantization leads to a finite-difference Schrödinger equation whose wavefunctions of the universe are given by $q$-hypergeometric functions. These furnish a quantum deformation of the hypergeometric wavefunctions of JT gravity. We further show that the finite cutoff Hamiltonian derived from the bulk analysis admits a natural interpretation as the reduced Casimir of $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R}))$ acting on hyperbolic matrix elements. The two scattering wave solutions are hyperbolic matrix elements of the modular double $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb R))\otimes U_{\tilde q}(\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb R))$, and the wavefunction of the universe is their unique combination regular at vanishing length.
- **Summary:** Canonical reduction of finite-boundary sinh dilaton gravity turns geodesic length and momentum into a finite-difference quantum Hamiltonian, with q-hypergeometric universe wavefunctions. Identifying that Hamiltonian with a quantum-group Casimir and selecting the regular modular-double combination supplies an explicit bridge between boundary quantization and deformed symmetry beyond JT gravity.
**RELEVANCE:** Directly relevant to canonical quantum gravity, quantum groups, and boundary wavefunctions; the construction is not an LQG model.  
**TAGS:** `canonical dilaton gravity` `finite Dirichlet boundaries` `modular double`

## 2. Two messages suffice for quantum interactive proof systems
**Authors:** Yupan Liu  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31236v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph, cs.CC
- **Abstract:** Quantum interactive proof systems can be parallelized to three messages, as shown by Kitaev and Watrous (STOC 2000), perhaps their most striking distinction from classical counterparts. In this work, we establish that any quantum interactive proof system can further be parallelized to two messages, with the verifier sending halves of EPR pairs (``quantum public coins'') and, after the prover's response, using only stoquastic verification. Technically, we strengthen ${\sf QIP}(3) = {\sf QIP} = {\sf PSPACE}$ of Jain, Ji, Upadhyay, and Watrous (JACM, 2011) and fully characterize two-message quantum interactive proof systems by proving \[ {\rm qq}\text{-}{\sf StoqAM} = {\rm qq}\text{-}{\sf QAM} = {\sf QIP}(2) = {\sf QIP} = {\sf PSPACE}. \] Our main technical contributions show that Close Image to Uniform (CIUniform), which is naturally in the stoquastic version of ${\rm qq}\text{-}{\sf QAM}$, is $\sf PSPACE$-hard via transforming the sum-check protocol of Shamir (JACM, 1992) and Shen (JACM, 1992) into a CIUniform instance consisting of an efficient sampler whose image distribution is exactly uniform for some input distribution when the underlying quantified Boolean formula is true.
- **Summary:** A PSPACE-hardness reduction from quantified Boolean formulas to Close Image to Uniform establishes that two-message quantum proofs already capture all of QIP. The resulting protocol needs only EPR-half messages from the verifier and stoquastic verification, sharply lowering the interaction requirements for quantum verification without claiming efficient solutions of PSPACE problems.
**RELEVANCE:** A foundational quantum-complexity result clarifying the power of entanglement-assisted verification.  
**TAGS:** `QIP(2) equals PSPACE` `stoquastic verification` `quantum public coins`

## 3. Multipartite entanglement structure of crystal quenches
**Authors:** Paul Gaudin, Clément Berthière  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30795v1  
**Categories:** cond-mat.stat-mech, cond-mat.str-el, hep-th, quant-ph
- **Abstract:** Understanding how entanglement emerges and spreads in out-of-equilibrium quantum many-body systems is a fundamental problem. In this paper, we investigate multipartite entanglement after quantum quenches. In particular, we study the entanglement structure and dynamics of quasiparticle multiplets generated by crystal quenches in the tight-binding chain. We identify zero-configurations for which a multiplet shared between three parties has no irreducible tripartite entanglement---these configurations consist entirely of pairwise distributed bipartite-entangled states. The existence of zero-configurations has important implications on the entanglement structure and dynamics of crystal quenches. For example, we show that it can further delay the production of tripartite entanglement. We build the quasiparticle picture for the Markov gap, and prove that it is a faithful witness of irreducible tripartite entanglement for crystal quenches. We discuss several key aspects of their tripartite entanglement dynamics through the lens of the Markov gap. We confront our results with exact lattice numerics and find perfect agreement in the hydrodynamic regime.
- **Summary:** A quasiparticle construction for tight-binding crystal quenches identifies configurations carrying only pairwise entanglement and proves that the Markov gap faithfully witnesses irreducible tripartite entanglement in this setting. Exact lattice calculations agree in the hydrodynamic regime, explaining additional delays in genuinely three-party entanglement that bipartite diagnostics would miss.
**RELEVANCE:** Connects many-body nonequilibrium physics with information-theoretic measures also used in holography.  
**TAGS:** `crystal quenches` `Markov gap` `tripartite entanglement`

## 4. Constraints on ultralight dark matter couplings from orbital perturbations of binary pulsars
**Authors:** Federico Huxhagen, Diana López Nacir  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30487v1  
**Categories:** astro-ph.CO, astro-ph.HE, gr-qc, hep-th
- **Abstract:** Ultralight dark matter coupled directly to ordinary matter perturbs the orbits of binary pulsars, and the perturbation grows secularly when its frequency matches a harmonic of the orbital motion. We use this effect to constrain three couplings with the wideband timing data for the $50$ binary pulsars of the NANOGrav 15 yr data set: a linear and a quadratic universal coupling of a scalar field to matter, and a vector field coupled to baryon minus lepton number ($B-L$). For every system and for masses between $10^{-23}$ and $10^{-18}\,$eV, we build the timing signature from the perturbed orbital elements. We then sample the coupling together with the local amplitude, phase and direction of the field and the red noise of the pulsar, marginalizing analytically over the timing model, and combine the single-pulsar likelihoods into $95\%$ credible upper limits at each mass. For a universal linear coupling, the orbital resonances of the eccentric systems bring the limit close to the Cassini bound. For the quadratic coupling, treating the neutron star as a point particle, the limits are the strongest available between $2\times10^{-22}$ and $2\times10^{-21}\,$eV. In the case of vector ultralight dark matter, the limit is comparable to the PPTA and Eöt-Wash bounds between $3\times10^{-22}$ and $10^{-21}\,$eV and reaches or beats the Eöt-Wash bound at isolated orbital resonances, while MICROSCOPE remains stronger over the entire mass range.
- **Summary:** Bayesian analysis of orbital perturbations in 50 NANOGrav binary pulsars constrains scalar and vector ultralight-dark-matter couplings while marginalizing field properties, timing parameters, and red noise. Orbital resonances deliver particularly strong quadratic-scalar limits under a point-particle neutron-star approximation, whereas vector limits remain weaker than MICROSCOPE overall, making the complementarity and assumptions explicit.
**RELEVANCE:** Combines cosmological dark-matter models with precision gravitational observations and statistical inference.  
**TAGS:** `ultralight dark matter` `binary-pulsar resonances` `Bayesian timing constraints`

## 5. Small-world networks yield tunable quantum codes at short circuit depth
**Authors:** Samuel Bevins, Gregory Bentsen  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31224v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph
- **Abstract:** Monitored random quantum circuits naturally generate quantum error-correcting codes. We generate quantum codes with tunable properties by implementing random Clifford circuits on Watts--Strogatz `small-world' networks and tailor code properties via three knobs: nonlocal graph rewiring, measurement rate, and circuit depth. On fully rewired networks, circuits of logarithmic depth produce codes with a finite rate and an extensive mean contiguous distance with failure probability substantially lower than codes produced from local circuits. These small-world codes form at a circuit depth growing as $\ln n$ compared to polynomial depths required for codes produced from local networks. Rewiring also moves the purification transition to higher measurement rates and changes its critical exponents. Connectivity is thus a knob for designing codes.
- **Summary:** Monitored Clifford circuits on rewired Watts–Strogatz networks generate finite-rate codes with extensive mean contiguous distance at logarithmic depth on fully rewired graphs. Varying connectivity, monitoring, and depth also shifts the purification transition, demonstrating a tunable architecture-to-code connection rather than establishing an unrestricted extensive code distance.
**RELEVANCE:** Relevant to efficient quantum-code generation and measurement-induced many-body transitions.  
**TAGS:** `small-world quantum codes` `logarithmic circuit depth` `purification transition`

## 6. Universal thermodynamics and BKT transition of the planar Bose gas from weak to strong interactions
**Authors:** Yaniss Rabahi, Ivan Balog, Adam Rançon  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30407v1  
**Categories:** cond-mat.quant-gas, quant-ph
- **Abstract:** We compute the universal thermodynamics of the dilute two-dimensional Bose gas at weak and strong coupling using the functional renormalization group (FRG). We employ an approximation that retains the full field dependence of the effective potential and spatial derivative terms, extending previous FRG calculations. We find that the equation of state is remarkably insensitive to the approximation scheme, with differences of only a few percent even at strong coupling. By contrast, the improved approximation substantially improves the description of the Berezinskii-Kosterlitz-Thouless (BKT) transition, reproducing the universal jump of the superfluid density and the critical anomalous dimension to within a few percent. At weak coupling, our results agree very well with classical-field Monte Carlo simulations, while at stronger coupling we observe deviations from classical-field scaling, valid at weak coupling. Our approach thus provides a quantitative description of the thermodynamics and superfluid density across the BKT transition, including in the strong-coupling regime where classical-field methods are no longer reliable.
- **Summary:** A functional-renormalization-group truncation retaining full field dependence in the potential and spatial derivative terms resolves planar Bose-gas thermodynamics across weak and strong interactions. While the equation of state changes little between approximations, the improved treatment captures the BKT density jump and critical anomalous dimension within a few percent and exposes strong-coupling departures from classical-field scaling.
**RELEVANCE:** Provides a nonperturbative many-body method for topological criticality beyond weak-coupling simulations.  
**TAGS:** `functional renormalization group` `BKT transition` `strongly interacting Bose gas`

## 7. Massive de Sitter correlators at one loop
**Authors:** Amara McCune  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31214v1  
**Categories:** hep-th, hep-ph
- **Abstract:** Fermions and massive fields charged under an unbroken symmetry couple to the inflaton only in pairs, so their signals in primordial correlators begin at one loop. Templates for these signals are mostly late-time estimates. Starting from a representation of massive de Sitter correlators as finite Mellin-Barnes integrals, we compute the exact squeezed coefficients of these signals and their corrections away from the squeezed limit. The leading coefficient factorizes into a hard part and a soft vertex. Late-time estimates treat the hard part exactly but approximate the soft vertex by its late-time limit, whereas we compute both exactly. Without a chemical potential, the exact coefficient differs from the late-time one by a universal factor that depends only on the masses in the loop and the coupling at the soft vertex. For the heavy Standard Model loops, the exact signals are ten to thirty times larger than their late-time estimates. With a chemical potential, the ratio depends on the model, and at our benchmarks it reaches a factor of thirty for helical vectors and about a hundred for the fermion triangle, where we agree with recent exact results. Away from the squeezed limit the leading term soon fails, mainly through a drift in the phase of the oscillation, which we derive for scalar and fermion loops.
- **Summary:** Finite Mellin–Barnes representations yield exact squeezed-limit coefficients and controlled departures from that limit for massive one-loop de Sitter correlators. Computing the soft vertex rather than replacing it by its late-time limit enhances heavy Standard Model signals by factors of ten to thirty and reveals phase drift away from squeezing, materially revising cosmological-collider templates.
**RELEVANCE:** Links inflationary observables, de Sitter quantum field theory, and otherwise inaccessible particle sectors.  
**TAGS:** `cosmological collider` `one-loop de Sitter correlators` `Mellin–Barnes integrals`

## 8. Imprints of quantum space in the ringdown phase of Kerr black holes
**Authors:** Nikola Herceg, Tajron Jurić, A. Naveena Kumara, Andjelo Samsarov, Ivica Smolić, Kumar S. Gupta  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30391v1  
**Categories:** gr-qc, hep-th
- **Abstract:** We investigate quantum-space corrections to the gravitational quasinormal modes (QNMs) of Kerr black holes within the framework of noncommutative geometry. The analysis employs a pseudo-semi-Killing Drinfeld twist, which preserves the classical Kerr geometry as an exact background solution while introducing nontrivial quantum corrections to linearized gravitational perturbations. Working to first order in the black hole spin and the quantum deformation parameter, including their mixed contribution, we derive a Schrödinger-like master equation for axial gravitational perturbations with a frequency- and spin-dependent effective potential. We compute the fundamental QNM frequencies using the WKB approximation and Leaver's continued-fraction method. Our results reveal corrections to the QNM oscillation frequencies and damping rates arising from rotation, pure quantum effects, and their interplay. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first systematic analysis of gravitational metric perturbations in a noncommutative Kerr black hole spacetime using a Drinfeld twist, extending prior work beyond the Schwarzschild case. Our findings demonstrate that quantum deformation effects alter both the oscillation frequencies and damping rates of quasinormal modes, with spin-quantum coupling introducing additional spin-dependent corrections to the Kerr QNM spectrum. Using a Kerr-anchored phenomenological prescription, we estimate the fractional frequency and damping-time shifts for selected merger remnants from the GWTC-3 and GWTC-5.0 catalogs. These estimates illustrate the potential signatures of quantum corrections in gravitational-wave ringdown.
- **Summary:** A Drinfeld-twist deformation leaves the Kerr background unchanged but modifies axial metric perturbations at first order in spin and quantum deformation, including their mixed term. WKB and continued-fraction calculations predict shifts in ringdown frequencies and damping, with catalog-based phenomenological estimates illustrating possible signatures rather than a detection or observational constraint on quantum spacetime.
**RELEVANCE:** Offers quantum-spacetime phenomenology complementary to LQG, with explicit perturbative and model-dependent limitations.  
**TAGS:** `noncommutative Kerr perturbations` `Drinfeld twist` `ringdown spectroscopy`

## 9. Exponential advantage in quantum observation with one extra qubit
**Authors:** Zhenyu Du, Xiongfeng Ma, Vlatko Vedral, Jinzhao Sun  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30876v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph, cond-mat.str-el, math-ph, physics.atom-ph
- **Abstract:** While quantum technologies promise to revolutionise how we observe the physical world, achieving substantial advantages with minimal overhead remains a central pursuit. Here, we show that augmenting a quantum machine with just one extra qubit yields an exponential advantage in probing quantum many-body dynamics. We propose an algorithm that uses this qubit to coherently interfere dynamical trajectories, simultaneously estimating exponentially many multi-time correlators with exponentially fewer evolutions than any ancilla-free scheme. Remarkably, this advantage holds even when the ancilla-free machine has unrestricted coherence time and arbitrary adaptive control, and persists even for a single correlator. We further prove that extending coherence time exponentially enhances observational power, establishing our protocol's optimality in both space and time. Finally, we numerically demonstrate our approach by predicting higher-order probe signals in a many-body system with orders-of-magnitude fewer experimental runs than previous methods. These results highlight the broad utility of our framework across quantum learning, sensing, and simulation.
- **Summary:** Coherent interference between dynamical trajectories using one auxiliary qubit yields an exponential reduction in evolutions for multi-time correlation measurements relative to ancilla-free protocols, even allowing the latter adaptive control and unrestricted coherence. Resource lower bounds and numerical higher-order-response examples establish both the space-time tradeoff and its practical motivation for observing many-body dynamics.
**RELEVANCE:** Connects quantum simulation and information-theoretic resource bounds to experimentally measurable many-body correlators.  
**TAGS:** `ancilla-assisted observation` `multi-time correlators` `quantum resource separation`

## 10. Non-invertible Duality Defects in Four-Dimensional $\mathbb{Z}_N$ Lattice Gauge Theories

**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30489v1  
**Categories:** hep-th, hep-lat
- **Abstract:** We consider four-dimensional $\mathbb{Z}_N$ lattice gauge theories with generalized plaquette actions. These theories are known to exhibit dualities. We construct topological interfaces implementing these dualities by assigning a weight to each building block and proving topological invariance. At self-dual couplings, these interfaces become duality defects representing non-invertible symmetries. We also study center symmetry defects and their junctions with the duality interfaces and derive several crossing relations.
- **Summary:** Local building-block weights construct duality interfaces in four-dimensional discrete lattice gauge theories, with topological invariance established explicitly. At self-dual couplings the interfaces become non-invertible symmetry defects; derived junction and crossing relations with center-symmetry defects provide concrete lattice data for generalized symmetry analysis.

## 11. Quark and gluon gravitational form factors of the nucleon at next-to-leading order
**Authors:** Ulaş Özdem  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30124v1  
**Categories:** hep-ph, hep-ex, hep-lat, hep-th
- **Abstract:** We compute the quark and gluon gravitational form factors $A$, $J$ and $D$ of the nucleon in three-point QCD sum rules at next-to-leading order in $α_s$ for the quark and gluon energy-momentum tensors (EMT), treating both at the same order in the $\overline{\rm MS}$ scheme. The nucleon EMT also tests the method, showing where the sum rules are reliable and why. Protected estimators of $D$ and of the trace form factor $\bar c$ require antisymmetric Borel moments, since none with equal Borel masses alone is protected. The $\mathcal{O}(α_s)$ corrections reduce $A_{\rm tot}$ by $32$--$40\%$, $J_{\rm tot}$ by $22$--$27\%$ and $|D_{\rm tot}|$ by $21$--$27\%$, since the perturbative share of the two-point function is much larger. The total form factors agree with lattice QCD for $1\le Q^2\le2.5$~GeV$^2$. At $Q^2=1.5$~GeV$^2$, $A_{\rm tot}=0.337\pm0.113$, $J_{\rm tot}=0.206\pm0.100$ and $D_{\rm tot}=-0.206\pm0.121$. $D$ is the most reliable result: its correlator is mostly perturbative, and its quark and gluon parts agree with the lattice. In $A$ and $J$, a local gluon-condensate term that does not fall with $Q^2$ is the largest, and their gluon shares are well below the lattice, possibly because the gluon EMT couples at this order only through gluon exchange and the condensate. A working region exists only near $β=1$, and the constant part of transitions cannot be separated within the Borel windows. The individual conservation identities deviate at one point by two standard deviations, consistent with a missing finite term in the $g_{μν}$ structure, and $\bar c$ is therefore given at leading order with condensates.
- **Summary:** Matched next-to-leading-order QCD sum rules for quark and gluon energy-momentum tensors reduce total nucleon gravitational form factors substantially and reproduce lattice totals over a restricted momentum window. Antisymmetric Borel moments protect selected estimators, while condensate dominance and unresolved transition terms make the D-term more trustworthy than separate A and J contributions, delimiting what this extraction can establish.
**RELEVANCE:** Probes the stress-energy structure of strongly interacting matter and the reliability of nonperturbative QCD methods.  
**TAGS:** `nucleon gravitational form factors` `NLO QCD sum rules` `D-term extraction`

## 12. Superheavy gravitational dark matter from runaway bubble collisions
**Authors:** Cheng-Wei Chiang, Van Que Tran  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30113v1  
**Categories:** hep-ph, astro-ph.CO, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** A stable particle that couples only through gravity is pair-produced in collisions of runaway bubble walls by graviton exchange. For a transition of duration $1/β$, a Dirac fermion reaches the yield $Y_X\simeq6.8\times10^{-3}(H/β)(T_{\rm reh}/M_{\rm P})^3$, where $H$ is the Hubble rate of the vacuum energy, $T_{\rm reh}$ the reheating temperature and $M_{\rm P}$ the reduced Planck mass. The yield is flat up to masses near $(H/β)M_{\rm P}/ν^2$, where $ν$ of order one sets the wall-field excursion. Gravity alone then gives dark matter up to about $10^{17}$~GeV at reheating temperatures far below its mass, where freeze-in fails.
- **Summary:** A calculation of graviton-mediated pair creation in runaway bubble-wall collisions gives a Dirac-dark-matter yield scaling with transition duration and the cube of reheating temperature in Planck units. The resulting nearly mass-independent production extends to superheavy masses around 10^17 GeV despite much lower reheating temperatures, identifying a gravitational production channel outside ordinary freeze-in reach.
**RELEVANCE:** Links early-universe phase transitions, gravitational interactions, and superheavy dark-matter phenomenology.  
**TAGS:** `bubble-wall collisions` `gravitational dark-matter production` `superheavy relics`
