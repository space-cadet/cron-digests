## 1. BMS Charges from Null Hypersurfaces
**Authors:** Martina Adamo, Luca Ciambelli, Felipe Diaz, Michael Imseis, Leonardo Sanhueza, Simone Speziale  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27488v1  
**Categories:** hep-th, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** We show how the gravitational dynamics, phase space and Noether charges of the BMS group at future null infinity $\mathscr{I}^+$ can be derived as limits of their analogues on null hypersurfaces at finite distance. The Bondi mass-loss and angular-momentum evolution equations arise as limits of the Raychaudhuri and Damour constraints. The Ashtekar--Streubel (aka Wald--Zoupas) symplectic potential arises as a specific member of a two-parameter family of null-boundary symplectic potentials, corresponding to the Dirichlet (aka canonical) polarization, while the other members differ by field-space exact terms, leading to the same radiative pre-symplectic structure but potentially different BMS fluxes. Deriving the BMS charges requires additional care, since BMS generators extended into the bulk are generally not tangent to the null hypersurfaces. After accounting for this and the associated anomalies, the renormalized canonical charges still contain an additional soft contribution. The prescription of a covariant boundary Lagrangian determines a corner improvement that removes this term and recovers the standard BMS charges.
- **Summary:** Taking finite null boundaries to future null infinity recovers Bondi evolution and the radiative symplectic structure, but obtaining the standard BMS charges additionally requires handling non-tangent generators and anomalies. A covariant boundary Lagrangian fixes a corner improvement that removes an extra soft term, clarifying how boundary polarization and charge prescriptions fit together.
**RELEVANCE:** Relevant to gravitational boundary phase spaces, asymptotic symmetries, and canonical approaches adjacent to LQG; this is not itself an LQG construction.  
**TAGS:** `BMS charges` `null-boundary phase space` `corner improvement`

## 2. Gravity-induced entanglement assisted by an additional interaction using magic angle calibration
**Authors:** Daisuke Miki, Alfred Li, Yanbei Chen  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26755v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph
- **Abstract:** Gravity-induced entanglement provides an operational test of whether gravitational dynamics are compatible with local operations and classical communication (LOCC), but the weakness of the gravitational interaction and the presence of force noise make direct entanglement generation extremely demanding. We introduce a controllable additional coherent interaction that biases two test masses close to, but within, a separability-preservation bound, so that the weak Newtonian coupling supplies only the remaining coherent contribution needed for entanglement. If the additional interaction alone is shown to be incapable of generating entanglement, while entanglement is observed when gravity is included, the gravitational interaction provides a non-LOCC contribution. Since gravity cannot be turned off, we use a magic-angle null of the projected tidal coupling to calibrate the additional interaction and force noise. If the additional dynamics is certified to preserve separability at the magic angle and entanglement is subsequently observed in a non-null configuration, the gravitational contribution is incompatible with an LOCC description within the Markovian model. The protocol trades the stringent absolute force-noise requirement of direct schemes for precision in controlling the additional coupling, its backaction, and angle-dependent systematics.
- **Summary:** An auxiliary coherent interaction is tuned just below an entangling threshold so that Newtonian gravity need supply only the remaining contribution. Magic-angle calibration is proposed to certify separability preservation of the auxiliary dynamics; within the stated Markovian model, subsequent entanglement would exclude an LOCC account of the gravitational contribution, trading force sensitivity for demanding control of backaction and angular systematics.
**RELEVANCE:** Connects quantum-gravity phenomenology with operational entanglement witnesses and the assumptions needed to interpret tabletop gravity tests.  
**TAGS:** `gravity-mediated entanglement` `magic-angle calibration` `LOCC witness`

## 3. Ancilla-Free Exact Dicke-State Synthesis with Optimal Size and Depth
**Authors:** Yunqi Huang, Wei Zi, Jianhao He, Zekun Ye, Shenggen Zheng  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27640v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph
- **Abstract:** We construct exact circuits for $n$-qubit Dicke states of any Hamming weight $k$ using $O(n)$ single-qubit gates and CNOTs in $O(\log n)$ depth without ancillary qubits. The gate count and depth are asymptotically optimal for every $0<k<n$. We compress symmetric blocks to free output qubits for weight-dependent phases. A sign correction followed by a collective rotation produces a state whose overlap with the target is at least a positive constant, so exact amplitude amplification takes $O(1)$ steps. For balanced Dicke states ($k=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$), the logarithmic depth disproves the $Ω(k)$ depth lower bound conjectured by Yuan and Zhang (2025).
- **Summary:** Symmetric-block compression, a collective rotation, and constant-round exact amplitude amplification yield ancilla-free preparation of arbitrary nontrivial Dicke states with linear gate count and logarithmic depth. The claimed asymptotically optimal construction also refutes a proposed linear-in-weight depth bound for balanced states, improving the theoretical resource baseline for symmetric-state preparation.
**RELEVANCE:** Useful for quantum algorithms and entanglement engineering where symmetric states must be prepared with minimal circuit resources.  
**TAGS:** `Dicke-state synthesis` `ancilla-free circuits` `optimal circuit depth`

## 4. Quantum Many-Body Scars from Operator Size
**Authors:** Debarghya Chakraborty, Dario Rosa  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27982v1  
**Categories:** cond-mat.str-el, hep-th, quant-ph
- **Abstract:** We present a general construction of approximate many-body scar states in two copies of a system using a bilocal size interaction that couples one site to its partner on the other copy. For an uncoupled two-sided Hamiltonian whose terms have fixed size in an operator basis, we identify polynomially many Krylov basis states that it annihilates and that, in the thermodynamic limit, become concentrated on a single size sector. The size operator splits them into a tower of equally spaced levels that gives rise to coherent revivals characterized by collective oscillations of energy across the two copies. We provide a concrete realization of this construction in two copies of a general $q$-body nearest-neighbor spin chain, and the SYK model.
- **Summary:** A bilocal operator-size coupling between two system copies resolves special Krylov states into an approximately equally spaced scar tower in the thermodynamic limit. Realizations in spin chains and SYK produce coherent inter-copy energy revivals, linking algebraic operator growth to controlled departures from thermalization.
**RELEVANCE:** Bridges many-body dynamics, quantum information, and SYK models used in quantum-gravity research.  
**TAGS:** `many-body scars` `operator size` `SYK revivals`

## 5. Beyond the Peak: Constraining early dark energy with the damping tail
**Authors:** Hao Wang, Gen Ye, Yun-Song Piao  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27069v1  
**Categories:** astro-ph.CO, hep-th
- **Abstract:** Early dark energy is commonly characterized by the amplitude and location of its primary energy-injection peak. We show that its post-peak evolution, which we call the \emph{EDE tail}, provides an additional observable probe of the underlying scalar dynamics and can materially affect its ability to alleviate the Hubble tension. We construct a simple phenomenological framework in which distinct regions of a multiscale scalar potential govern the dynamics at different field scales. As a representative realization, we consider a potential that combines axion-like and $α$-attractor-like regimes, thereby allowing the EDE tail to be reshaped through reflection and asymmetric oscillations. Confronting this framework with cosmic microwave background, baryon acoustic oscillation, and supernova observations, we find an improved fit to the high-$\ell$ CMB data together with a higher inferred bestfit $H_0$. Our results show that the EDE tail retains observable information about the underlying scalar potential that is not captured by the conventional peak parameters alone. This provides a complementary route for constructing and observationally distinguishing EDE scenarios, and potentially for further alleviating the Hubble tension.
- **Summary:** A multiscale scalar-potential framework separates early-dark-energy peak dynamics from its subsequent tail, with an axion-like/alpha-attractor realization reshaping oscillations through reflection and asymmetry. Fits to CMB, BAO, and supernova observations reportedly improve high-multipole CMB agreement while raising the best-fit Hubble constant, showing that peak parameters alone can miss observable scalar dynamics rather than establishing a resolution of the Hubble tension.
**RELEVANCE:** Targets observational cosmology, scalar-field dynamics, and model discrimination in proposed solutions to the Hubble tension.  
**TAGS:** `early-dark-energy tail` `multiscale scalar potential` `CMB damping tail`

## 6. Solving fermionic sign problems with physics-informed kernels
**Authors:** Kenji Fukushima, Friederike Ihssen, Renzo Kapust, Jan M. Pawlowski  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27768v1  
**Categories:** hep-lat, cond-mat.str-el, hep-th
- **Abstract:** We extend the generative sampling approach with physics-informed kernels (PIKs) [1,2] to a general class of physics systems with complex distributions and a structure also encountered in fermionic systems. The resolution of sign problems of generic weighted sums of complex Gaußian distributions with the PIK approach is central to this application. In a first step, PIKs map a real normal distribution to such a weighted sum, and this procedure is called the PIKfan. The PIKfan has the sampling cost of a real normal distribution. In a second step, this weighted sum is evolved with a second PIK into the distribution of the theory at hand. In combination, we reduce the sampling task for a general class of complex systems to a Gaußian one, and the main computational task is that of computing the PIK. We illustrate the approach with the one-site Hubbard model at arbitrary chemical potential and with $φ^4$-deformed extensions of it.
- **Summary:** Two physics-informed transport kernels first turn a real Gaussian into a weighted complex-Gaussian mixture and then evolve that mixture into the target theory distribution. Demonstrations on the one-site Hubbard model and deformed extensions relocate the difficult computation to learning the kernels; Gaussian-cost sampling after that step should not be read as a demonstrated efficient solution of general fermionic sign problems.
**RELEVANCE:** Relevant to nonperturbative lattice field theory and correlated-fermion simulation, with a concrete separation between sampling cost and kernel-construction cost.  
**TAGS:** `fermionic sign problem` `physics-informed kernels` `Hubbard sampling`

## 7. Pseudo-entropy in Thouless Pumps and Majorana Topological Phases
**Authors:** Nabaraj Khatri, Shuvam Chudal, Ashish Khanal  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27846v1  
**Categories:** cond-mat.stat-mech, quant-ph
- **Abstract:** We study the average excess pseudo entropy, $ΔS_{12}$, in the Rice-Mele model under Thouless pumping. We compute it for two driving schemes, the control-freak and smooth pumps, under open and periodic boundary conditions. We find that $ΔS_{12}$ distinguishes the trivial, monomer, and topological stages of the pump, and that it depends on the boundary condition, the driving scheme, and the reference time. We also find that a nonzero staggered potential breaks chiral symmetry and gaps out the SSH critical point, thereby flattening the pseudo-entropy peak at the phase boundary. We also study reference-state dependence in the open Kitaev chain, comparing a fixed even-parity target with five reference families: even free-fermion, pairing-only, vacuum, and two fixed gapped states. We find that the pseudo-entropy response depends on the reference and on the parity of the subsystem size, even when the reference remains gapped. At $μ=0$, the pairing-only reference gives the exact result $S_{\mathrm{pr}}=\ln 2$ for $L=4m+2$, while the $L=4m$ sequence has analytically determined finite-size corrections. These results show that, in both models, pseudo entropy is a property of the reference-target pair and the subsystem partition, rather than a reference-independent signature of the topological phase.
- **Summary:** Calculations in driven Rice-Mele chains and static Kitaev chains test pseudo-entropy against boundary conditions, reference states, and subsystem parity. Exact special-case results and finite-size corrections show that the diagnostic belongs to a reference-target pair rather than uniquely labeling a topological phase, placing useful limits on information-theoretic phase identification.
**RELEVANCE:** Connects topological matter with entropy-based diagnostics and cautions against interpreting reference-dependent quantities as universal order parameters.  
**TAGS:** `pseudo-entropy` `Thouless pumping` `Kitaev-chain topology`

## 8. Regularizing gravity in the presence of matter fields
**Authors:** Raúl Carballo-Rubio, Chiara Coviello, Vania Vellucci  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27367v1  
**Categories:** gr-qc, hep-ph
- **Abstract:** We investigate how matter fields affect spacetime regularity in a class of gravitational theories with second-order field equations in spherical symmetry, of which general relativity is a special case, and which may encode effective corrections from an underlying quantum theory of gravity. Focusing on spherically symmetric black holes and cosmological spacetimes in theories in which vacuum spacetimes display bounded curvature invariants, we derive the conditions under which regular geometries can be supported by static and perturbative scalar fields, electromagnetic fields, and radiation fluids. We discuss the impact of matter on the regularity requirements of curvature boundedness and geodesic completeness relative to vacuum, finding in general that coupling to matter does not eliminate the existence of theories with regular solutions.
- **Summary:** For spherically symmetric theories with second-order equations and regular vacuum geometries, the authors derive matter-dependent conditions for curvature boundedness and geodesic completeness. Scalar, electromagnetic, and radiation sources do not generically preclude regular solutions, testing whether effective quantum-gravity singularity avoidance survives beyond vacuum without claiming a universal matter-coupled cure.
**RELEVANCE:** Directly addresses robustness of effective quantum-gravity regularization in black-hole and cosmological settings, although no specific LQG derivation is supplied.  
**TAGS:** `matter-coupled regular gravity` `bounded curvature` `geodesic completeness`

## 9. Detection of genuine multipartite entanglement by means of entanglement monotones
**Authors:** Sylvain Carrozza, Johann Chevrier, Luca Lionni  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27660v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph, math-ph
- **Abstract:** We look for pure state entanglement monotones that may serve as certificates of a strong form of genuine multipartite entanglement, that we refer to as irreducible entanglement: a $D$-partite state (with $D\geq 3$) is irreducibly $D$-partite entangled if it cannot be decomposed as a product of states that are at most $(D-1)$-partite entangled. We start by establishing a no-go result proving that no reasonable genuine multipartite entanglement entropy can simultaneously obey LOCC-monotonicity and subadditivity. After relaxing the first condition to LO-monotonicity, we provide a simple method generating genuine multipartite entanglement monotones with the desired properties. Focusing on a subfamily of these monotones, we show that they are able to detect any form of genuine tripartite entanglement in three-qubit systems.
- **Summary:** A no-go theorem rules out jointly imposing LOCC monotonicity and subadditivity on a reasonable genuine multipartite entanglement entropy. Relaxing to local-operation monotonicity yields a constructive family of certificates, including a subfamily detecting every form of genuine three-qubit tripartite entanglement, exposing a structural trade-off in multipartite resource measures.
**RELEVANCE:** Relevant to entanglement structure and rigorous quantum-information measures that can also inform many-body and quantum-geometry studies.  
**TAGS:** `multipartite entanglement` `entropy no-go theorem` `LO monotones`

## 10. Cosmological Correlators with Triple Massive Exchanges
**Authors:** Shuntaro Aoki, Lucas Pinol, Zhehan Qin, Fumiya Sano, Masahide Yamaguchi, Yuhang Zhu  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27611v1  
**Categories:** hep-th, astro-ph.CO, gr-qc, hep-ph
- **Abstract:** Among all tree-level contributions to the primordial bispectrum, the triple exchange of massive scalars is the most challenging. In this paper we present an analytical formula for this bispectrum in the form of a double series, which allows the three exchanged fields to have different masses and converges throughout the whole physical region. We obtain it by expanding the mixed propagator with the softest momentum, so that the triple exchange becomes a sum of integrals over two mixed propagators. Each of these integrals satisfies a differential equation and is fixed by its value at equal momentum. We further show that the squeezed limit follows directly from this formula and takes a simple and compact form.
- **Summary:** Expanding the softest mixed propagator and solving differential equations for the remaining two-propagator integrals produces a double-series bispectrum formula for three unequal massive exchanges. Convergence over the full physical region and a compact squeezed limit make this difficult tree-level contribution analytically accessible for primordial heavy-field signatures.
**RELEVANCE:** Connects cosmological correlator techniques with inflationary probes of massive fields and high-energy physics.  
**TAGS:** `primordial bispectrum` `triple massive exchange` `cosmological collider`

## 11. Optimised 8th-Order Trotter-Suzuki Schemes for Quantum Simulation
**Authors:** Marko Maležič, Johann Ostmeyer  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27896v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph, cond-mat.stat-mech, cond-mat.str-el, hep-lat
- **Abstract:** Simulating quantum dynamics in many-body systems is one of the most promising applications of quantum computers. Product formulas are a natural choice for this task, since they map directly onto quantum gates, and increasing their order $n$ could lead to reduced gate depths, provided the parameters are chosen efficiently. So far, schemes beyond $6^{\textrm{th}}$ order have only been built as compositions of second-order schemes, while more general product formulas are known only up to $6^{\textrm{th}}$ order. In this work, we extend our optimisation framework to order $n = 8$, and find both more efficient compositions and the first $8^{\textrm{th}}$ order general schemes. Our formulas improve on the theoretical efficiency of the state-of-the-art schemes by Morales et al and we recommend two schemes explicitly. In simulations of the Heisenberg XZ chain one of our recommended schemes always outperforms those by Morales et al, though it is not always the same scheme that performs best. More generally, we observe that for Hamiltonians split into many non-commuting operators the uniformity of a Trotter decomposition becomes the main indicator of a scheme's performance. To assess the schemes' use on quantum hardware, we simulate noisy circuits with our new Qiskit plugin qiskit-omelyan. At current noise levels, the Leapfrog scheme remains the best choice, but as noise rates decrease toward fault tolerance, our recommended schemes become competitive.
- **Summary:** Numerical optimization supplies the first general eighth-order product formulas as well as improved composite schemes, benchmarked on Heisenberg XZ dynamics and noisy circuits. Their ideal efficiency beats previous eighth-order choices, but present noise still favors leapfrog integration, identifying a benefit expected nearer fault tolerance rather than an immediate hardware advantage.
**RELEVANCE:** Useful for Hamiltonian simulation of many-body and lattice-gauge systems, with explicit attention to the noise-versus-discretization trade-off.  
**TAGS:** `eighth-order product formulas` `Hamiltonian simulation` `noise-depth trade-off`

## 12. Ohmic warm inflation
**Authors:** Anish Ghoshal, Alessandro Strumia  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27986v1  
**Categories:** hep-ph, astro-ph.CO, hep-th
- **Abstract:** We show that Warm Little Inflation is anomalous, lacks a suitable inflaton potential, and cannot sustain the thermal bath, as the quasi-static approximation over-estimated the dissipated power. To address these issues, we propose a new friction mechanism and new anomaly-free models based on one fermion charged under a U(1) gauge group in one extra dimension. The four-dimensional lattice version is a ring of $N$ U(1) groups with one fermion at each site, reducing to an anomaly-free version of the warm little inflaton model for $N=2$. The inflaton is a Wilson line, protected for $N\ge5$. Large $N$ allows a suitable monodromy inflaton potential. In the continuum limit, $N\to\infty$, the inflaton velocity becomes a five-dimensional electric field and friction becomes Ohmic dissipation, which seems able to sustain warm inflation compatibly with observations.
- **Summary:** After diagnosing anomaly, potential, and energy-budget problems in Warm Little Inflation, the authors construct anomaly-free extra-dimensional U(1) models whose Wilson-line inflaton acquires friction through an Ohmic continuum limit. Large gauge-group rings support a protected inflaton and monodromy potential; sustaining an observationally viable warm phase is presented as a promising possibility, not a settled consequence.
**RELEVANCE:** Connects inflationary model building, anomaly cancellation, and extra-dimensional gauge dynamics at the cosmology/high-energy interface.  
**TAGS:** `warm inflation` `Wilson-line inflaton` `Ohmic dissipation`
