## 1. Mannheim--Kazanas Black Holes: Horizons, Temperatures and Thermodynamics
- **Authors:** Bekir Can Lütfüoğlu
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.21118
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21118
- **Categories:** hep-th, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** Static black holes in conformal Weyl gravity differ from their Einstein counterparts in a simple but important way: the Mannheim--Kazanas geometry is encoded in a Bach-flat lapse function whose radial dependence is richer than that of Schwarzschild--de Sitter. We study the de Sitter branch of this solution in the Schwarzschild gauge, keeping the characteristic linear term together with the quadratic de Sitter term. In the branch continuously connected to the positive-mass Schwarzschild--de Sitter spacetime, a regular static region between a black-hole horizon and a cosmological horizon exists precisely when \[
-\frac{1}{3}&lt;\beta\gamma&lt;\frac{2}{3},
\qquad
0&lt;\kappa&lt;\frac{1+3\beta\gamma}{27\beta^{2}}. \] In this window the singularity at the origin is hidden, the two positive horizons obey $0&lt;r_b&lt;3\beta&lt;r_c$, and the limiting endpoint is the Nariai geometry. The thermodynamic interpretation is subtler than the root structure: the two horizons generally have different temperatures, and the static patch has no asymptotic region that would select a unique normalization of time. We therefore compare the Killing, Bousso--Hawking normalized, Tolman local, and effective two-horizon temperature conventions, and we compute the corresponding Wald entropy for the pure Weyl-squared action. The result is a local horizon thermodynamic description that keeps the geometric facts separate from the ensemble-dependent choices needed for any global first law.
- **Summary:** This work systematically maps the thermodynamic landscape of Mannheim-Kazanas black holes in conformal Weyl gravity, a fourth-order derivative theory that modifies Einstein gravity at short distances. Rather than imposing a single temperature definition, the author compares four distinct prescriptions — Killing, Bousso-Hawking, Tolman local, and an effective two-horizon scheme — and derives the corresponding Wald entropy for the pure Weyl-squared action. The key insight is that horizon thermodynamics in this theory is inherently local: geometric facts about horizons and their temperatures can be stated independently of the ensemble-dependent conventions required to write a global first law. This separation clarifies longstanding ambiguities in assigning temperature and entropy to non-asymptotically flat spacetimes with multiple horizons.
- **Relevance:** Conformal Weyl gravity offers an alternative to Einstein gravity where the cosmological constant problem is softened by conformal invariance. Understanding its black hole thermodynamics informs how higher-curvature corrections modify the Bekenstein-Hawking entropy — a question central to quantum gravity approaches including string theory and loop quantum cosmology.

## 2. Spinning test particles in a weak gravitational wave and the memory effect
- **Authors:** Ritwik Acharyya, Shailesh Kumar, Sayan Kar
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.20463
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20463
- **Categories:** hep-th, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** In this work, we investigate the worldline deviation of spinning test particles in a weak gravitational wave (GW) spacetime. Within the pole-dipole approximation and the Tulczyjew spin supplementary condition, we consider the modified deviation equation, which contains the standard geodesic deviation term and an additional contribution generated by the Mathisson-Papapetrou-Dixon (MPD) force. We demonstrate how the deviation equation can be solved under viable approximations to yield a new expression for the change in the deviation vector in terms of the perturbation $h_{ij}$, its time derivative, the spin vector components and the longitudinal and transverse components of the deviation. The spin-curvature coupling is shown to play a crucial role in controlling the change in deviation. We then use this result to investigate the changes in the form and expressions of the net GW signal as well as our understanding of GW memory, in the presence of spin. We conclude with our attempts on the measurability of this spin-induced effect \emph{vis-a-vis} present GW observations.
- **Summary:** The authors extend the standard geodesic deviation framework to include spin-curvature coupling for test particles traversing weak gravitational wave spacetimes. Using the Mathisson-Papapetrou-Dixon force within the Tulczyjew spin supplementary condition, they derive a closed-form expression for the deviation vector that explicitly depends on the spin components, the metric perturbation, and its time derivative. The spin-curvature interaction is shown to substantially alter both the gravitational wave signal morphology and the memory effect — the permanent displacement between freely falling particles after a wave passes. The work closes with an assessment of whether current LIGO/Virgo sensitivity could detect this spin-induced correction.
- **Relevance:** Gravitational wave memory is a key probe of nonlinear general relativity and carries information about the total energy radiated in a merger. Spin effects in the deviation of test masses are relevant for precision gravitational wave astronomy and for testing whether compact objects carry appreciable spin angular momentum, a question tied to black hole formation and quantum hair.

## 9. Optical-area minimum method for static spherical black hole shadows
- **Authors:** Vitalii Vertogradov, Nikko John Leo S. Lobos, Ali Ovgun et al.
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.21018
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21018

- **Abstract:** We formulate a global optical-area method for shadows of static, spherically symmetric black holes. For the metric \(ds^{2}=-A(r)dt^{2}+B(r)dr^{2}+C(r)d\Omega^{2}\), spherical sections of the optical geometry have area \(\mathcal{A}_{\rm opt}=4\pi C/A\). A null ray with impact parameter \(b\) can cross a spherical section only if \(b^{2}\leq C/A\). The capture threshold is fixed by the infimum of \(C/A\) on the connected interval between the observer and the black hole horizon. Our contribution is to organize them into an observer-to-horizon global selection rule that compares all stationary candidates and relevant endpoint limits. The radial function \(B(r)\) does not affect the shadow angle, although it enters the coordinate-time instability rate, whose numerical value also depends on the normalization of the static time coordinate. - **Summary:** The authors recast black hole shadow formation for static spherically symmetric metrics into a global variational principle based on minimizing the optical area function C/A. Rather than relying on the photon sphere equation alone, their method treats the shadow boundary as determined by the infimum of C/A across the entire radial interval from observer to horizon, automatically selecting between interior minima, endpoint limits, and multiple competing stationary points. They derive compact perturbative formulas for deformed metrics and validate the construction on Reissner-Nordström, Bardeen, charged dilaton, and Kottler black holes, explicitly confirming radial-coordinate invariance. The formalism elegantly unifies local photon-sphere results into a single global selection rule.
- **Relevance:** Black hole shadows are now directly imaged by the Event Horizon Telescope and provide some of the strongest constraints on compact object geometry. A robust, coordinate-invariant method for predicting shadows across diverse metric theories is essential for comparing quantum gravity predictions — such as regular black holes or higher-curvature corrections — against observational data.

## 13. Black Holes, the Bethe Ansatz, and Elliptic Calogero--Moser Systems

- **Summary:** Chen and Chen translate Synge's world-function identity — a cornerstone of geodesic geometry in curved spacetimes — into the language of AdS/CFT. For finite bulk geodesics ending on the boundary, the Synge relation induces scale equations that control both the scalar two-point function in a Weyl frame and the entanglement entropy of disjoint spherical regions. In the planar frame, the correlator equation reduces to the familiar dilatation Ward identity, while the entanglement sector yields a new relative-scale equation governed by a transverse hyperbolic area that specializes to the known entanglement RG equation in 2D CFTs. This establishes a direct geometric origin for entanglement RG flow from classical geodesic identities.
- **Relevance:** The connection between bulk geodesics and boundary entanglement is one of the deepest results in holography. Identifying the CFT avatar of Synge's identity tightens the mathematical bridge between gravitational geometry and quantum information, with potential implications for understanding how bulk locality emerges from boundary correlations in quantum gravity.
