## 1. Light Cone Thermodynamics: Quasi-Local Formalism
**Authors:** Matheus G. Barbosa  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25477v1  
**Categories:** gr-qc, hep-th, math-ph  
- **Abstract:*** Inspired by black hole thermodynamics and other results suggesting that general relativity has some intrinsic connection with thermodynamics, this work aims to establish a quasi-local thermodynamic formalism in general spacetimes. With this purpose, we generalize the Bondi-Sachs formalism to past light cones associated with an arbitrary worldline. From this generalization, we demonstrate the emission of Hawking radiation in a quasi-local context through a few examples, suggesting that it may occur under mild conditions. In addition, we argue that an "irreversible inequality" is satisfied in typical gravitational systems, indicating that an analogue of the second law may be established. Together with a definition of internal energy derived from the Landau-Lifshitz pseudotensor, these results are used to construct a postulational framework that generalizes classical thermodynamics in order to include the dynamics of spacetime. As a consequence, it is possible to define the rest-mass density from thermodynamic variables, decoupling it from the dynamics of constituent numbers.

- **Summary:*** Barbosa extends the Bondi-Sachs null-triangle formalism from null infinity to the past light cones of arbitrary worldlines, building a quasi-local thermodynamic framework for general spacetimes in which Hawking-type emission is shown to arise under mild conditions. An "irreversible inequality" argued to hold for typical gravitational systems supplies an analogue of the second law, and pairing this with an internal energy built from the Landau-Lifshitz pseudotensor yields a postulational thermodynamics that absorbs spacetime dynamics itself. A notable consequence is that rest-mass density becomes a thermodynamically defined variable, decoupled from the conservation of constituent particle numbers.

**RELEVANCE:** A fresh quasi-local route to gravitational thermodynamics and Hawking emission that complements Deepak's quantum-gravity interests with a geometric, non-perturbative framework.
**TAGS:** `quasi-local thermodynamics` `Hawking radiation` `Bondi-Sachs formalism`

## 2. Operator-Level Quantum-Classical Correspondence in Relativistic Quantum Theory and Curved Spacetime
**Authors:** Pankaj Sheoran, Gopal Kashyap, Sanjay Siwach  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25360v1  
**Categories:** gr-qc, hep-th, quant-ph  
- **Abstract:*** In a recent work \cite{Shaikh:2026nsi}, it was shown that for a non-relativistic particle, the operator equations of motion can be cast in the same differential form as Newton's equation of motion. This establishes a correspondence between quantum operator dynamics and classical mechanics at the level of the equations of motion. In this work, we investigate whether this correspondence extends to quantum field theory and linearized gravity. We explicitly show that the Heisenberg equations of motion for quantum field operators reproduce the corresponding classical equations of motion for a broad class of field theories. The Klein-Gordon equation, Maxwell's equations, Yang-Mills equations, and linearized Einstein equations are shown to arise at the operator level without taking the limit $\hbar\rightarrow0$. We then extend the analysis to curved spacetime and show that the quantum field operators satisfy the corresponding covariant field equations at the operator level. In the weak-field approximation, we show that the Heisenberg evolution of the metric-perturbations reproduces the linearized Einstein equations at the operator level. For a quantum particle in curved spacetime, the Heisenberg equations for position and momentum reproduce classical geodesic motion in the semiclassical limit, thereby extending the operator-level quantum-classical correspondence from fields to particle dynamics.

- **Summary:*** This paper extends a recently proposed operator-level quantum-classical correspondence — where Heisenberg equations for a non-relativistic particle literally take Newton's form — to relativistic quantum field theory and linearized gravity. The authors show that the Heisenberg equations for quantum field operators reproduce the Klein-Gordon, Maxwell, Yang-Mills, and linearized Einstein equations exactly at the operator level, without any $\hbar \to 0$ limit, and that the correspondence survives covariantization on curved spacetime. For particles, the position-momentum Heisenberg equations reduce to classical geodesic motion in the semiclassical limit, so the entire ladder from field equations to geodesics holds at the level of operator dynamics.

**RELEVANCE:** Directly bridges quantum theory and classical gravity at the operator level — a structural result about the quantum-to-classical transition relevant to quantum foundations in curved spacetime.
**TAGS:** `quantum-classical correspondence` `Heisenberg equations` `semiclassical gravity`

## 3. $\mathsf{BQP} \subseteq \mathsf{IP}$ Does Not Relativize
**Authors:** Adam Bouland, Andrew Huang, Anand Natarajan, Itay Shalit, Avishay Tal  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25680v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph, cs.CC  
- **Abstract:*** We construct an oracle relative to which $\mathsf{BQP} \not\subseteq \mathsf{IP}$, resolving a long-standing open question in quantum complexity theory. Together with recent work due to Aaronson et al., our work also gives the first oracle separation between $\mathsf{IP}$ and $\mathsf{MIP}$, answering a question dating back to Fortnow's thesis. Our separation is based on the Forrelation problem, where given Boolean functions $f$ and $g$, the goal is to determine if $f$ is correlated with the Fourier spectrum of $g$. While this task is solvable by a query-efficient quantum algorithm, we show that it admits no classical interactive protocol with polynomial communication and a polynomial-query verifier.
  Our proof is based on (i) a new structural result showing how to approximate Avg-Max circuits (which are well-known to capture the power of interactive proofs in the oracular setting) by convex functions with small first and second derivatives and (ii) a novel analysis establishing that the Forrelation distribution suggested by Aaronson and Ambainis fools such functions.
  Our results imply that any prover-efficient classical interactive protocol for $\mathsf{BQP}$ must rely on non-relativizing techniques. This might serve as a partial explanation for the lack of progress towards doubly-efficient, unconditionally sound classical verification of quantum computation.

- **Summary:*** The authors construct an oracle relative to which BQP is not contained in IP, settling a long-standing open problem in quantum complexity theory and, combined with recent work of Aaronson et al., yielding the first oracle separation between IP and MIP. The separation is built on the Forrelation problem: query-efficient for quantum algorithms, yet shown here to admit no classical interactive protocol with polynomial communication and a polynomial-query verifier. The proof rests on approximating Avg-Max circuits — which capture oracular interactive proofs — by smooth convex functions, and showing the Aaronson-Ambainis Forrelation distribution fools them. The upshot is that any prover-efficient classical verification of BQP must use non-relativizing techniques, explaining the stalled quest for unconditionally sound, doubly-efficient verification of quantum computation.

**RELEVANCE:** A landmark quantum-complexity result with direct consequences for verifiable quantum computing — the question of classically certifying quantum advantage that underpins quantum-information research.
**TAGS:** `quantum complexity` `interactive proofs` `oracle separation`

## 4. Exponential Quantum Advantage in Testing Fourier Dimensionality
**Authors:** Kenny Chen  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25816v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph, cs.CC, cs.DS  
- **Abstract:*** A boolean function $f$ has Fourier dimension $k$ if its nonzero Fourier coefficients span a subspace of dimension $k$. We consider the property testing task of determining whether a function has Fourier dimension at most $k$, or is $ε$-far from being so. We show that there is a $Θ(k)$ quantum property tester for this problem. With Gopalan \etal's classical lower bound of $Ω(2^{k/2})$ for this task, this demonstrates an exponential quantum advantage for this task. We complement this result with a $\tilde{O}(2^{k/2}/ε)$ classical tester, improving the best known upper bound and thus showing the prior lower bound is essentially tight.

- **Summary:*** Chen considers the property-testing problem of deciding whether a Boolean function has Fourier dimension at most $k$, or is $\varepsilon$-far from that property, and gives a quantum tester using only $\Theta(k)$ queries. Matched against Gopalan et al.'s $\Omega(2^{k/2})$ classical lower bound, this is an exponential quantum advantage for a natural, well-studied testing task. A new $\tilde{O}(2^{k/2}/\varepsilon)$ classical tester improves the best known upper bound, showing the earlier lower bound was essentially tight and pinning down the classical complexity of the problem.

**RELEVANCE:** A clean exponential quantum speedup for a natural property-testing problem — the kind of provable advantage that anchors the case for quantum computing within Deepak's quantum-information interests.

## 5. Thermodynamic Proof of Quantumness without Structure
**Authors:** Florian Meier, Hayata Yamasaki  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25211v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph  
- **Abstract:*** Demonstrating that a machine performs genuinely quantum operations is a central challenge in quantum information processing. Existing proofs of quantumness typically rely on computational tasks that are infeasible for classical machines under assumptions such as computational hardness, or explicit bounds on classical runtime, memory, or oracle queries. Here we introduce an alternative paradigm: a thermodynamic proof of quantumness based on energy consumption. Using the search problem of Yamakawa and Zhandry with a structureless uniformly random oracle, we show that any classical machine solving the task with constant success probability must generate entropy that grows exponentially with the security parameter. By Landauer's principle, this implies an exponential lower bound on heat dissipation and hence on the energy consumption of any cyclic classical implementation. In contrast, the corresponding quantum prover solves the task with polynomial query and gate complexity, giving polynomial energy consumption under standard assumptions for quantum implementations. Thus, in the random-oracle setting, sufficiently low energy consumption in a successful implementation certifies quantumness through a macroscopic thermodynamic observation of energy consumption.

- **Summary:*** Meier and Yamasaki propose certifying quantumness not by computational hardness but by thermodynamics: in the Yamakawa-Zhandry search problem with a structureless random oracle, any classical machine with constant success probability must generate entropy growing exponentially in the security parameter. Landauer's principle then forces exponential heat dissipation for any cyclic classical implementation, while the quantum prover runs with polynomial query and gate complexity and hence polynomial energy cost. A macroscopic measurement of energy consumption therefore itself certifies quantumness — a proof of quantumness that needs no trapdoor structure and no computational assumptions.

**RELEVANCE:** Reframes proofs of quantumness on thermodynamic grounds — connecting quantum information, thermodynamics, and the foundations of quantum certification in a way that resonates with Deepak's cross-disciplinary interests.
**TAGS:** `proof of quantumness` `thermodynamics` `Landauer principle`

## 6. Anyon Crystallization by Statistics
**Authors:** Zohar Komargodski, Xuzixiang Lou, Ivri Nagar, Domenico Orlando, Susanne Reffert, Amit Sever  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25249v1  
**Categories:** hep-th, cond-mat.quant-gas, cond-mat.str-el  
- **Abstract:*** We revisit the long-standing problem of the anyon gas. We study the solvable near-bosonic limit of a large number of anyons $N$ at fixed $N/k$, where $1/k$ characterizes the statistical interaction. In this limit, the ground state is a crystal with order $N/k$ lattice sites. The statistical interactions, mediated by a long-range gauge field, are completely screened within the crystalline phase. The resulting state is a conventional crystal without superfluidity. We characterize this anyon crystal by determining its unit-cell size, ground-state energy, acoustic excitations, angular momentum crossings, and other properties.
  Our results indicate a quantum phase transition between the crystal at $k>k_c$ and a superfluid at $k<k_c$, with a critical value $k_c\simeq2\text{--}5$. These results imply that an anyon gas with only statistical interactions is not necessarily a superfluid, contrary to common lore.
  We also present a detailed analysis of the renormalization-group evolution of anyon--anyon interactions, identifying many new potential fixed points and elucidating finite-size effects.

- **Summary:*** Komargodski and coauthors solve the large-N anyon gas in the near-bosonic limit at fixed $N/k$ and find the ground state is a crystal with order $N/k$ lattice sites — not a superfluid. The statistical interactions, mediated by a long-range gauge field, are completely screened inside the crystal, which is characterized by its unit-cell size, ground-state energy, acoustic excitations, and angular-momentum crossings. Their analysis indicates a quantum phase transition at $k_c \simeq 2$–$5$ between this crystal and a superfluid, overturning the common expectation that a statistical-interaction anyon gas must superfluid, and an RG study of anyon-anyon interactions uncovers many new potential fixed points.

**RELEVANCE:** Settles a long-standing question about anyonic matter with exact solvable results, sitting at the hep-th/condensed-matter boundary and informing topological-quantum-matter interests.

## 7. Nullspace-guided Adaptive Bootstrap of Quantum Many-body Systems
**Authors:** Xu-Cheng Wang, Yang Qi  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25834v1  
**Categories:** cond-mat.str-el, quant-ph  
- **Abstract:*** We introduce a nullspace-guided adaptive (NGA) bootstrap method that improves the energy lower bounds of quantum many-body ground states by refining the bootstrap basis in a dynamic and incremental way. At each iteration, the optimized moment matrix reveals a nullspace of saturated positivity directions, which is intuitively interpreted as annihilators of the approximate ground-state subspace. The NGA bootstrap then prunes operators with small nullspace leverage and grows the basis along descendants of these null directions. By applying the NGA bootstrap to the transverse-field Ising chain, we obtain nearly exact energy lower bounds because the algorithm automatically discovers the eigenoperator structure in terms of Jordan-Wigner fermions from a minimal local bootstrap basis. For the Hubbard chain, it improves upon state-of-the-art energy lower bounds by up to two orders of magnitude, reaching errors ranging from $10^{-3}$ down to $10^{-5}$ in the strongly correlated regimes. We further show that the NGA framework can be used to improve the certified two-sided bounds on general observables. In addition, the bootstrap error decreases approximately as a power law with increasing computational resources. These results suggest that our method provides a practical and scalable route toward accurate bootstrap of general quantum many-body systems.

- **Summary:*** Wang and Qi introduce a nullspace-guided adaptive bootstrap that reads the nullspace of saturated positivity directions from the optimized moment matrix — interpreted as annihilators of the approximate ground-state subspace — and uses it to prune low-leverage operators and grow the basis along their descendants. On the transverse-field Ising chain the method automatically discovers the Jordan-Wigner eigenoperator structure from a minimal local basis and returns nearly exact energy lower bounds; on the Hubbard chain it beats state-of-the-art lower bounds by up to two orders of magnitude, reaching $10^{-3}$–$10^{-5}$ errors in strongly correlated regimes. The framework also sharpens certified two-sided bounds on general observables and shows power-law convergence in resources, marking a practical, scalable route to accurate many-body bootstrapping.

**RELEVANCE:** A methodological advance in the many-body bootstrap — an increasingly important non-perturbative tool for strongly correlated quantum systems at the heart of condensed-matter physics.
**TAGS:** `many-body bootstrap` `strongly correlated systems` `semidefinite programming`

## 8. Quantum Mpemba effect from Stark localization
**Authors:** Nico Albert, Masudul Haque, Shovan Dutta  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24993v1  
**Categories:** cond-mat.stat-mech, cond-mat.quant-gas, quant-ph  
- **Abstract:*** In classical systems, rugged potential energy landscapes provide a transparent mechanism for the celebrated Mpemba effect, in which hotter initial states cool down faster. This picture generally does not survive in quantum systems. Here we show how to design a quantum energy landscape with local dissipation leading to an Mpemba effect with parametrically separated timescales. Our approach uses Stark localization to design an energy landscape and localized incoherent hopping as cooling mechanism. The hops are triggered by rare "detection" events whose rate grows with energy, allowing hotter states to cool faster and producing super-exponentially separated cooling rates for localized initial states. We further show that the effect is dramatically enhanced by collective hopping of bound pairs in the presence of attractive on-site interactions. These findings have clear experimental signatures accessible in present-day setups.

- **Summary:*** Albert, Haque, and Dutta design a genuinely quantum Mpemba effect — hotter states cooling faster — using Stark localization to engineer an energy landscape and localized incoherent hopping as the cooling mechanism. Cooling proceeds through rare "detection" events whose rate grows with energy, so hotter states cool faster and localized initial states acquire super-exponentially separated cooling rates. Attractive on-site interactions dramatically enhance the effect through collective hopping of bound pairs, and the predicted signatures are within reach of present-day experiments.

**RELEVANCE:** A concrete, experimentally accessible realization of the quantum Mpemba effect connecting nonequilibrium quantum dynamics, localization, and thermalization — core themes in modern condensed-matter and quantum-statistical physics.
**TAGS:** `Mpemba effect` `Stark localization` `nonequilibrium quantum dynamics`

## 9. Cosmological stasis and the coupled dark sector of the Dark Dimension
**Authors:** Alexander Stewart  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25230v1  
**Categories:** hep-ph, astro-ph.CO, gr-qc, hep-th  
- **Abstract:*** We study cosmological stasis in coupled dark matter and dark energy models in which a scalar field drives the dark energy and sets the dark matter mass, with the Dark Dimension as the motivating realization. Treating dark matter, dark energy, baryons, and radiation as an autonomous dynamical system, we show that exact stasis requires exponential slopes along the trajectory, and that running slopes give exact stasis or a drifting quasi-stasis depending on whether the running is integrable. The constant abundance solutions are the stationary solutions of coupled quintessence, which we read as stasis epochs with computable lifetimes and exits. Anchoring the cosmology to the measured abundances at matter-radiation equality and today, we show that the stasis region cannot accelerate for any initial conditions, and that the total equation of state is never phantom although the effective dark energy of a $Λ$CDM fit can appear so. For the Dark Dimension, capture into stasis in the future is excluded for geometric sources of the potential once dark fifth-force bounds are imposed, and dark matter production from the Standard Model brane supports a further stasis which the standard scenario does not reach. The stasis solutions provide analytic exclusions of the parameter space which do not depend on initial conditions.

**Categories:** hep-th, astro-ph.CO, gr-qc, hep-ph  
- **Abstract:*** We establish a direct quantum Clausius relation for conformal fields inside the apparent horizon of a quasi-de Sitter spacetime. Our results provide a quantum field theoretic realization of dynamical horizon thermodynamics beyond exact de Sitter equilibrium.

## 11. Functional Dimensional Regularization
**Authors:** Piero Beretta, Alessandro Codello  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25238v1  
**Categories:** hep-th, cond-mat.stat-mech, math-ph  
- **Abstract:*** We introduce and develop Functional Dimensional Regularization (FDR), a novel functional renormalization group (RG) framework that extends the principles of dimensional regularization beyond the perturbative $\varepsilon$-expansion. The key insight is to define the FDR beta functions, valid in any continuous dimension $d$ and characterized by threshold functions, as a sum over all critical dimensions of the corresponding scheme-independent beta functions computed in DR. We demonstrate that this formalism exhibits all the hallmarks of a fully-fledged functional RG. Within the local potential approximation, we perform a general fixed point analysis, recovering the $\varepsilon$-expansion for all multi-critical models and establishing a direct connection to functional scaling solutions, spike plots, and eigen-perturbation spectra. Extending the framework to the second order of the derivative expansion, we compute the critical exponents for the Ising universality class in $d=3$, providing estimates for the anomalous dimension $η$ and RG spectrum that converge rapidly and show favorable agreement with state-of-the-art results. We present a detailed comparison with non-perturbative and proper-time RG approaches, highlighting structural similarities and key differences. In particular, we introduce a ``distillation'' procedure that allows one to systematically derive the FDR flow from any other one-loop-exact RG scheme. Our results establish FDR as a self-contained and competitive RG framework that combines the technical simplicity of dimensional regularization with the versatility of the functional approach.

- **Summary:*** Beretta and Codello introduce Functional Dimensional Regularization, a functional RG framework that defines beta functions valid in any continuous dimension $d$ — complete with threshold functions — as sums over all critical dimensions of scheme-independent DR beta functions. Within the local potential approximation they recover the $\varepsilon$-expansion for all multi-critical models and connect to functional scaling solutions and eigen-perturbation spectra, and at second order of the derivative expansion they compute $d=3$ Ising critical exponents converging rapidly to state-of-the-art values. A "distillation" procedure lets one derive the FDR flow from any one-loop-exact RG scheme, establishing FDR as a self-contained framework marrying the technical simplicity of dimensional regularization with functional-RG versatility.

**RELEVANCE:** A new non-perturbative RG framework connecting dimensional regularization to functional methods — a methodological contribution relevant to renormalization and critical phenomena across high-energy and condensed-matter physics.
**TAGS:** `renormalization group` `dimensional regularization` `critical phenomena`
