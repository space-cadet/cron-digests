## 1. An algebraic quantization of gravity from the null cone
**Authors:** Venkatesa Chandrasekaran  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34924v1  
**Categories:** hep-th, gr-qc, math-ph
- **Abstract:** We propose a background independent quantization of vacuum general relativity on four dimensional Lorentzian spacetimes. Starting from first order gravity, we solve the gravitational constraints on intersecting null cones of a bulk subregion and obtain the reduced phase space at finite $G_N$. We promote its canonical Poisson brackets to commutation relations and use deformations of the reduced action to define an algebra of observables. Equipped with this, we use Gaussian wavefunctions of canonical pairs to construct a nonperturbative Hilbert space representation, for which this algebra generates a type I factor. By composing amplitudes between states prepared on null cones, we then obtain a Lorentzian path integral in which each sum over intermediate states converges absolutely. As a check, we compute the amplitude for graviton pair creation and annihilation on a null cone nonperturbatively in $G_N$ and find a finite closed form expression, even though expanding first in $G_N$ yields a logarithmic divergence. We next use diffeomorphism invariance of the path integral to show that a macroscopic causal diamond with a given maximum size has a finite dimensional Hilbert space. From the Lorentzian trace we obtain the Bekenstein Hawking entropy for marginally trapped surfaces and calculate its logarithmic correction for large Schwarzschild black holes, recovering at one loop the coefficient Sen obtained through Euclidean methods. We end by deriving the semiclassical Einstein equations to one loop as the low energy limit of this theory.
- **Summary:** Solving first-order gravitational constraints on intersecting null cones, the author constructs a reduced observable algebra and a nonperturbative Lorentzian amplitude prescription. The construction yields a finite graviton-pair amplitude where a premature perturbative expansion diverges, and reports finite causal-diamond state counting, horizon entropy, and one-loop Einstein dynamics, making it a substantial proposed framework rather than an established quantization.
**RELEVANCE:** Directly relevant to Deepak's background-independent quantum gravity and boundary phase-space interests; comparison with LQG is natural, but this is not an LQG construction.  
**TAGS:** `null-cone quantization` `reduced phase space` `Lorentzian amplitudes`

## 2. Bound magic in multi-qubit systems
**Authors:** Jonas Kitzinger, Jens Eisert, Ravi Kunjwal  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34872v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph
- **Abstract:** Magic captures nonstabilizerness, a resource required for quantum computational speedups. We prove the existence of multi-qubit nonstabilizer states from which no pure magic can be distilled, even with arbitrarily many copies and rounds of stabilizer operations. Such bound magic states have previously been only known to exist in odd Hilbert-space dimensions, where positivity of discrete Wigner functions can be used analogously to the positive partial transpose (PPT) criterion for bound entanglement. We introduce a PPT analogue for magic in multi-qubit systems that is closed under tensor products and stabilizer operations and reduces to the stabilizer polytope for one and two qubits. This enables the proof of multi-qubit bound magic and the explicit construction of bound magic states for $n \geq 3$ qubits.
- **Summary:** A tensor-product-stable analogue of the PPT criterion identifies multiqubit nonstabilizer states that remain unable to yield pure magic under arbitrarily many stabilizer-processing rounds. Explicit examples begin at three qubits, extending bound-magic phenomena beyond odd-dimensional systems and separating possession of computational resources from their distillability.
**RELEVANCE:** Connects Deepak's quantum-information foundations with the resource bottlenecks of fault-tolerant quantum computing.  
**TAGS:** `bound magic` `stabilizer operations` `resource distillation`

## 3. Neural Network Decoders For Qubit Loss
**Authors:** J. Wilson Staples, Winston Fu, Yihe Dong, Jeff D. Thompson  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34891v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph
- **Abstract:** Qubit loss is a significant source of error in many quantum hardware platforms, requiring circuits that periodically replace or reset qubits to maintain fault-tolerance. When the reset process includes loss-resolving measurements, this information can improve decoding and substantially reduce overhead compared to the case where this information is not available. Prior work has demonstrated loss-tolerant circuits for the surface code that show significantly improved thresholds and subthreshold scaling by incorporating loss information. However, the computational complexity of loss simulation and decoding, which falls outside of the usual stabilizer framework, has limited extending these studies to high-rate qLDPC codes, which are already challenging to decode in the absence of loss. In this work, we present a loss-aware convolutional neural-network (CNN) decoder framework that circumvents this challenge. Training is enabled by a novel sampler, \texttt{slim}, which achieves a throughput for circuits with Pauli noise and loss that is comparable to the state-of-the-art for pure Pauli noise. On the surface code with loss, the decoder matches the accuracy of computationally expensive most-likely error (MLE) decoders at much lower latency. For the $[[144,12,12]]$ bivariate bicycle (Gross) code, we study a walking syndrome extraction circuit with Pauli and loss errors. For physical error rates $p<1\%$, we observe a more than $10^3$-fold lower logical error rate for a pure loss model, relative to a pure Pauli error model at the same physical error rate, along with significantly enhanced sub-threshold error rate scaling. This demonstrates that loss-dominated error models can also significantly improve performance in a high-rate qLDPC code, and provides a simulation and decoder training platform that can be extended to other codes and algorithmic gadgets.
- **Summary:** A fast loss-and-Pauli sampler trains convolutional decoders that use loss flags, matching expensive surface-code decoding accuracy at lower latency. In a walking circuit for the 144-qubit Gross code, pure-loss noise below 1% gives over a thousandfold lower logical error than equal-rate pure Pauli noise, demonstrating a hardware-noise-dependent route to better high-rate error correction.
**RELEVANCE:** Relevant to Deepak's interest in practical fault tolerance and how physical noise structure changes qLDPC performance.  
**TAGS:** `loss-aware decoding` `bivariate bicycle codes` `neural decoders`

## 4. Data-enhanced self-learning projective quantum Monte Carlo with Rydberg-atom measurements
**Authors:** Vichayuth Imchitr, Luca Brodoloni, Sebastiano Pilati, Estelle Maeva Inack  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34636v1  
**Categories:** cond-mat.stat-mech, cond-mat.quant-gas, quant-ph
- **Abstract:** We introduce a data-enhanced self-learning projective quantum Monte Carlo (PQMC) method that incorporates projective measurements from programmable quantum simulators into the guiding wavefunction of a ground-state projection. We demonstrate the approach for a two-dimensional Rydberg-atom array using restricted Boltzmann machine (RBM) and recurrent neural network (RNN) guiding wavefunctions. Experimental pretraining reduces finite-population effects and accelerates self-learning convergence, allowing unbiased estimation of ground-state properties to be obtained with fewer walkers and fewer self-learning iterations. The improvement is particularly pronounced for the RBM, while the more accurate RNN guiding wavefunction exhibits a smaller, though still observable, benefit from pretraining. Simulations across different system sizes suggest that the relative advantage of experimental pretraining may increase with system size. Improvements are also observed for the checkerboard order parameter, despite deviations of the experimental measurements from the equilibrium numerical results. Finally, finite-size scaling with self-learning PQMC without experimental pretraining yields the critical point and critical exponents of the disordered-to-checkerboard transition in agreement with previous numerical studies. Our results show that limited and imperfect experimental measurements can provide an effective initialization for self-learning PQMC, while imaginary-time projection combined with subsequent unsupervised learning progressively refines the guiding wavefunction toward the ground state.
- **Summary:** Projective measurements from a Rydberg simulator initialize neural guiding wavefunctions for self-learning ground-state Monte Carlo, after which imaginary-time projection refines the estimate. Tests reduce walker-population bias and convergence effort, especially for restricted Boltzmann machines, showing how imperfect experimental samples can assist unbiased many-body computation rather than replace it.
**RELEVANCE:** Bridges Deepak's condensed-matter and quantum-simulation interests through a concrete experiment-assisted classical method.  
**TAGS:** `projective Monte Carlo` `Rydberg arrays` `neural guiding states`

## 5. A Genetic Algorithm Approach to Reconstructing Scalar-Tensor Lagrangians
**Authors:** Mikel Artola, George Alestas, Dimitrios Efstratiou, Evangelos Achilleas Paraskevas  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34512v1  
**Categories:** astro-ph.CO, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** Recent DESI baryon acoustic oscillation (BAO) data, combined with cosmic microwave background (CMB) and Type Ia supernovae observations, have been interpreted as evidence for dynamical dark energy whose equation of state crosses the phantom divide $w=-1$. Such a crossing cannot be realized smoothly by a minimally coupled scalar field $\varphi$, but can arise naturally in scalar-tensor theories. We present a data-driven framework that reconstructs the scalar-tensor Lagrangian directly from observations, using a genetic algorithm to evolve the symbolic expressions for the nonminimal coupling $F(\varphi)$ and the scalar potential $U(\varphi)$. Each candidate is evaluated through a forward model that self-consistently determines the expansion history, the effective gravitational coupling, and the growth of structure. Candidates are ranked by a joint likelihood of DESI DR2 BAO, Pantheon+ supernovae calibrated with SH0ES Cepheids, compressed CMB distance priors, and redshift-space distortion measurements of $fσ_8$. We restrict to the unscreened branch of the theory and impose Solar System constraints on the post-Newtonian parameter $γ$ and on the time variation of the gravitational coupling, together with a Big Bang nucleosynthesis bound on its evolution. Across 64 independent runs, the best reconstruction improves the fit relative to $Λ$CDM by $Δχ^2=-16.6$, compared with $Δχ^2=-10.6$ for the CPL dark-energy parametrization. The preferred coupling is well described by $F(\varphi)=c_1\varphi^2+e^{\varphi}(1-c_2\varphi)$. The reconstructed theory shows phantom behavior at $z\gtrsim0.5$, but it remains close to $w=-1$ at low redshift.
- **Summary:** Symbolic genetic search reconstructs both scalar-tensor coupling and potential through a forward model constrained by expansion, growth, and gravitational tests. Across 64 runs the best fit improves chi-squared by 16.6 over Lambda-CDM and exhibits higher-redshift phantom behavior, providing a candidate reconstruction whose fit gain alone does not establish a preference after model-complexity penalties.
**RELEVANCE:** Pertinent to Deepak's cosmology and modified-gravity interests, especially observational reconstruction subject to local-gravity constraints.  
**TAGS:** `scalar-tensor reconstruction` `genetic algorithms` `dynamical dark energy`

## 6. Massive Triangle in Cosmological Correlator beyond Conformal Coupling
**Authors:** Taushif Ahmed, Syed Mehedi Hasan  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34892v1  
**Categories:** hep-th, gr-qc, hep-ph
- **Abstract:** A massive scalar coupled in pairs to the inflaton generates a one-loop primordial bispectrum whose mass corrections at generic momenta have lacked closed analytic expressions beyond conformal coupling. We obtain two leading mass corrections at arbitrary external momenta in terms of multiple polylogarithms of uniform weights three and four. Auxiliary-energy dressings preserve rational time integrals, revealing unexpected simplicity despite elliptic intermediate geometry. Independent full-loop evaluations, including blinded predictions, validate the closed forms. We also determine a subleading light-field singularity and recover the heavy-mass effective theory.
- **Summary:** Auxiliary-energy dressings keep time integrations rational and produce two leading mass corrections to a one-loop primordial bispectrum in weight-three and weight-four multiple polylogarithms at arbitrary external momenta. Independent loop checks support the formulas, which also recover a light-field singularity and the heavy-mass effective limit, improving analytic control of inflationary particle signatures.
**RELEVANCE:** Links Deepak's high-energy field theory and cosmology interests through explicit loop-level primordial observables.

## 7. Vacuum correlation measures as CFT partition functions
**Authors:** Hewei Frederic Jia  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34922v1  
**Categories:** hep-th, cond-mat.stat-mech, math-ph, quant-ph
- **Abstract:** We show that in any $d$-dimensional CFT, the vacuum sandwiched Rényi mutual information (SRMI) of integer order $n$ between two disjoint balls maps to a CFT partition function on a manifold of topology $\#^{n-1}(S^1 \times S^{d-1})$, with moduli fixed by the ball configuration and single-ball modular flow. At $n=2$, it is given by the thermal partition function on $S^{d-1}$, upper-bounding the von Neumann mutual information. For $n>2$, SRMI diverges at finite separation due to a hot-spot limit of the partition function. Explicit CFT evaluations agree well with density matrix computations.
- **Summary:** Modular flow turns integer-order sandwiched Renyi mutual information between CFT balls into partition functions on connected-sum geometries. The second-order quantity becomes a thermal partition function bounding ordinary mutual information, whereas higher orders develop finite-separation divergences, exposing both a calculational bridge and a limitation of these correlation diagnostics.
**RELEVANCE:** Relevant to Deepak's entanglement, field-theory, and quantum-gravity interests through a geometric representation of vacuum correlations.  
**TAGS:** `sandwiched Renyi information` `CFT partition functions` `modular flow`

## 8. Noise threshold for learning with quantum memory
**Authors:** Miaojiang Chen, Wenjing Xiao, Haiqiang Chen, Jiarong Liang, Anfeng Liu, Zhenrong Zhang, Athanasios V. Vasilakos, Saif Al-Kuwari, Ahmed Farouk, H. Herbert Song  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34474v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph, cs.AI
- **Abstract:** A quantum memory can reduce exponentially the number of experiments needed to learn a physical process, but real memories are noisy. Here we show that, for Pauli memory noise with eigenvalues $λ_X,λ_Y,λ_Z$, the advantage is governed by $λ_X^2+λ_Y^2+λ_Z^2$. We prove that no protocol with an entangled probe gains an exponential advantage when this sum is at most one, reduce the general case to the additivity of a Rényi-2 coherent information, and find coded memories that retain a growing advantage within 1% of this threshold. On a superconducting processor, nine simultaneously operated Bell pairs show an advantage that grows or shrinks exponentially with system size, reversing where a parameter-free model predicts. A three-qubit repetition code turns a shrinking advantage into a growing one with all processor and readout errors included, and under dephasing its memory performs better than Bell sampling in any memory frame on a perfect processor.
- **Summary:** The squared Pauli-noise eigenvalues of a memory determine a threshold below which entangled-probe learning cannot retain exponential advantage; coded constructions approach that boundary, while the general characterization is tied to a coherent-information additivity question. Nine Bell pairs on a superconducting device exhibit the predicted reversal of scaling, and a repetition code restores growing advantage, connecting a theoretical noise criterion to an experimental intervention.
**RELEVANCE:** Addresses Deepak's quantum-computing interest in operational advantage under realistic memory noise rather than idealized resource counts.  
**TAGS:** `noisy quantum memory` `learning advantage` `coded Bell sampling`

## 9. Prethermalization of entanglement entropy in the mixed-field Ising model
**Authors:** Miguel Gonzalez, Budhaditya Bhattacharjee, Alexei Andreanov, Barbara Dietz, Sergej Flach  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34060v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph
- **Abstract:** We study how weak integrability breaking affects the dynamics of the half-chain entanglement entropy of random product states in the mixed-field Ising model. The associated Hamiltonian depends on two parameters $g$ and $h$ determining the sizes of the longitudinal and transverse fields and has two integrable limits: the classical Ising chain $(h=0)$ and the transverse-field Ising model $(g=0)$. Weak perturbations of these limits give rise to the short-range network (SRN) and long-range network (LRN) regimes, respectively. We characterize the departure from integrable dynamics and the emergence of intermediate entanglement plateaus by melting times $T_M$ at prescribed entropy thresholds and analyze their dependence on the perturbation strength. For the SRN parameters examined away from $g=2$, we find $T_M\propto h^{-2}$ over the thresholds tested. At $g=2$, the relaxation splits into two stages: an initial departure from the integrable plateau with $T_M\propto h^{-1}$, followed by the melting of a well-defined intermediate prethermal plateau with $T_{M_2}\propto h^{-2}$. In the LRN regime, by contrast, the inverse of $T_M$ scales with exponents of $g$ close to either one or two, depending on the parameter values, the system size, and the entropy threshold, and no well-defined prethermal plateaus are observed. Our analysis distinguishes the melting of integrable dynamics from the subsequent decay of a prethermal plateau, which is observed in the SRN regime.
- **Summary:** Tracking half-chain entropy after product-state quenches distinguishes two weakly nonintegrable limits of the mixed-field Ising chain. Near the classical limit, a special parameter point separates inverse-linear initial melting from inverse-quadratic prethermal decay, while the transverse-field limit lacks clear plateaus in the tested regime, showing why a single relaxation exponent can obscure distinct dynamical stages.
**RELEVANCE:** Relevant to Deepak's nonequilibrium many-body physics and entanglement-growth interests, with explicit scaling diagnostics near integrability.  
**TAGS:** `Ising thermalization` `entanglement plateaus` `integrability breaking`

## 10. Unified preparation of exact and asymptotic quantum many-body scars from Dicke states
**Authors:** Yuki Asari, Masaya Kunimi, Tetsuro Nikuni  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33987v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph, cond-mat.quant-gas, cond-mat.stat-mech, cond-mat.str-el
- **Abstract:** We propose a unified quantum algorithm for preparing exact and asymptotic quantum many-body scar (QMBS) states in the spin-1 {\it XY}, domain-wall-conserving, and Onsager scar models. Model-dependent isometries map the exact scar states to Dicke states and the target asymptotic QMBS states to gapless magnon excitations of the spin-1/2 ferromagnetic Heisenberg chain. Exact QMBS states are prepared by applying the inverse isometries to Dicke states. For asymptotic QMBS states, block encoding first prepares the magnon excitations from Dicke states to a prescribed fidelity, after which the inverse isometries map the prepared states back to the physical chains. At fixed target fidelity, the block-encoding success probability has a positive lower bound independent of system size and scar index, implying that a constant number of amplitude amplification rounds suffices. Our algorithm enables systematic studies of nonergodic dynamics on programmable quantum simulators.
- **Summary:** Model-specific isometries turn exact scar preparation into Dicke-state preparation and asymptotic scars into magnon-excitation preparation followed by inverse mapping. For the block-encoded asymptotic construction, fixed-fidelity success remains bounded below independently of size and scar index, allowing constant-round amplitude amplification and offering a systematic route to studying nonergodic chains.
**RELEVANCE:** Combines Deepak's quantum-algorithm and many-body interests through constructive access to atypical eigenstate families.  
**TAGS:** `many-body scars` `Dicke-state preparation` `block encoding`

## 11. Inverted Pendulum in the Sky: Floquet Stabilization of Cosmological Axions
**Authors:** Tokiro Numasawa, Masahito Yamazaki  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34439v1  
**Categories:** hep-ph, astro-ph.CO, cond-mat.str-el, hep-th
- **Abstract:** Many cosmological scenarios require a scalar field to sit near the top of its potential, which usually requires a fine-tuned initial condition. We ask whether the hilltop of an axion potential can be dynamically stabilized by a rapidly oscillating coupling, as for the inverted pendulum of Kapitza, and to what extent this can reduce the fine-tuning. Using Floquet theory, we determine the stability window of the hilltop and analyze several realizations: an oscillating coupling, a spinning or oscillating second field, and a gyroscopic analogue, an inverted spinning top realized by a sigma model on a squashed three-sphere. In an expanding Universe the stabilized phase lasts for a number of $e$-folds logarithmic in the hierarchy of scales, independently of the cosmological background. Classifying the stabilizers by how their effective frequency redshifts, we show that in these realizations the hilltop cannot both attract the field and leave it frozen by the Hubble friction during radiation or matter domination, and that during inflation the attraction is limited by the Hubble friction and the energy budget. Thus the simple realizations studied here do not generically remove the hilltop fine-tuning. They can instead delay the onset of the oscillation and enhance the relic abundance as $n_φ/n_{φ,{\rm std}}\propto(\tilde m/H_c)^{3/2}$ in radiation domination. We discuss the implications for axion dark matter, inflation, quintessence and curvatons.
- **Summary:** Floquet analysis tests whether rapidly driven axion couplings can stabilize potential hilltops in an expanding universe. The examined mechanisms generally fail to eliminate initial-condition tuning because expansion, attraction, and the energy budget restrict stabilization, but delayed oscillation can enhance relic abundance, distinguishing transient control from a solution to the hilltop problem.
**RELEVANCE:** Relevant to Deepak's early-universe and particle-cosmology interests, especially the limits of dynamical explanations for axion initial conditions.  
**TAGS:** `axion hilltops` `Floquet stabilization` `relic abundance`

## 12. Coherent quantum-circuit simulation of Mueller dipole branching at small $x$: multiplicity fluctuations and DIS phenomenology
**Authors:** Wei Kou, Xurong Chen  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33979v1  
**Categories:** hep-ph, hep-th, quant-ph
- **Abstract:** Mueller dipole branching at small $x$ is a stochastic process in which splitting rates depend on transverse geometry. We construct a coherent quantum circuit for a regulated finite-state version of this process. Poisson-clock uniformization controls the number of updates, while orthogonal event-history registers retain the splitting paths, including those leading to the same final configuration. The circuit prepares transition amplitudes from local splitting rates rather than loading a precomputed evolved distribution. Its statevector multiplicity probabilities reproduce the truncated-clock Markov result to numerical precision and agree with the full continuous-time Markov-chain benchmark within approximately $7\times10^{-4}$. To examine fluctuations beyond the small circuit, we independently simulate the Mueller cascade with continuous transverse coordinates. At the largest rapidity studied, its normalized fourth factorial moment is $1.81$, compared with $2.84$ for an equal-mean Yule process. We also determine multiplicity entropies and correlations between dipole-size sectors. Applying a fixed negative-binomial dipole-to-charged-particle response reproduces the rising trend of the H1 charged-particle multiplicity entropy toward smaller $x$, but does not fully describe the measured multiplicity distributions. These results demonstrate an explicit coherent encoding of finite-state dipole branching and clarify its relation to continuous-cascade fluctuations and DIS phenomenology.
- **Summary:** Poisson-clock uniformization and explicit history registers encode regulated Mueller dipole branching coherently from local splitting rates, reproducing a continuous-time benchmark within about seven parts in ten thousand. Separate continuous-coordinate cascade simulations find suppressed fourth-order multiplicity fluctuations relative to a Yule process and only partial agreement with H1 distributions, making this a concrete QCD circuit demonstration rather than evidence of quantum speedup.
**RELEVANCE:** Connects Deepak's high-energy phenomenology and quantum-simulation interests, with unusually explicit checks against classical stochastic dynamics.  
**TAGS:** `small-x dipole branching` `coherent simulation` `DIS multiplicities`
