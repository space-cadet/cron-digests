## 1. Riemannian geometry and quantum gravity on trees
**Authors:** Shahn Majid  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23103v1  
**Categories:** math.QA, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** We show that the tree graphs that admit a quantum Riemannian geometry (QRG) with a positive ratio metric are exactly the Dynkin graphs of finite type $A,D,E$ and affine type $\widetilde D,\widetilde E$. A metric in this context is an assignment of a nonzero real value to each arrow and positive ratio requires that the two directions on any edge have the same sign. It turns out that the metric value in one direction can be chosen freely and the other is then determined. For finite $A, D, E$ type, the construction involves $q$-integers where $q=e^{\imathπ/h}$ with $h$ the Coxeter number and there is a conjugate pair of $*$-preserving quantum Levi-Civita connections (QLCs). In the case of type $A$, these are necessarily contained in a previously known 1-parameter space of connections. For affine $\widetilde D,\widetilde E$ type, there is only one QLC, with real coefficients. Using the quantum Riemannian geometry, we construct a natural Einstein--Hilbert action and hence new quantum gravity models on $A_3,D_4,\widetilde D_4,\widetilde D_5,\widetilde D_6,\widetilde E_6$ in a functional integral approach, other than the 4-star $\widetilde D_4$ which we revisit.
- **Summary:** Classifying positive-ratio quantum metrics singles out finite ADE and affine D/E trees, with quantum Levi-Civita connections controlled by Coxeter data in the finite case. The resulting Einstein–Hilbert actions support functional-integral gravity models on several small graphs, supplying concrete discrete geometries rather than a continuum or LQG quantization.
**RELEVANCE:** Directly relevant to discrete quantum geometry and graph-based gravitational path integrals; this is noncommutative quantum Riemannian geometry, not an LQG result.  
**TAGS:** `quantum Riemannian geometry` `Dynkin graphs` `discrete gravity`

## 2. Teleportation of Logical Qubits using Lattice Surgery with Superconducting Circuits
**Authors:** Luca Hofele, Yongxin Song, Áron Márton, Ian Hesner, Ilya Besedin, Jakob Ekert, Jonathan Trebst, François Swiadek, Felix Henrich, Dante Colao Zanúz, Mohsen Bahrami Panah, Lukas Bödeker, Nicholas Gialouris, Chrysander D. Hagen, Marek Pechal, Jean Claude Besse, Markus Müller, Christoph Hellings, Andreas Wallraff  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23165v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph
- **Abstract:** Demonstrating a quantum advantage when solving large-scale computational problems on a quantum computer requires the correction of errors that occur due to limitations in control accuracy and coherence. Using the concept of quantum error correction, fault-tolerant logical qubits can be realized and manipulated with logical single- and two-qubit gates. In this paper, we experimentally analyze fault-tolerant lattice-surgery, which is a key primitive that enables operations between logical qubits realized in the surface-code. Using this primitive, we demonstrate the teleportation of logical states between two distance-three logical qubits realized in a superconducting circuit composed of 41 physical qubits. Throughout this process, we study the step-by-step transfer of logical information from a source to a target logical qubit by characterizing the time evolution of logical observables. We find that repeated execution of stabilizer measurements, after merging two surface-code patches, restores fault tolerance and yields increased fidelity of the teleported state. When optimizing the protocol in terms of gate count and sequence duration, we observe further improvements in fidelity. Our work offers new insights into the optimization of fault-tolerant lattice-surgery operations, while also providing a comprehensive description of the physical device and gate sequences used. We expect the efficient implementation of increasingly complex logical circuits to enhance the performance of future fault-tolerant quantum computers.
- **Summary:** A 41-physical-qubit superconducting device teleports logical states between distance-three surface-code patches using lattice surgery, tracking the information transfer through logical observables. Repeating stabilizer extraction after the merge restores fault tolerance and improves teleportation fidelity, with further gains from shorter, lower-gate-count sequences, making inter-logical operations a concrete optimization target.
**RELEVANCE:** An experimental step from logical memories toward executable fault-tolerant quantum circuits.  
**TAGS:** `logical teleportation` `lattice surgery` `superconducting qubits`

## 3. Non-equilibrium topological order from Floquet anyon proliferation in the toric code
**Authors:** Francesco Petiziol  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23132v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph, cond-mat.quant-gas
- **Abstract:** We analyze the dynamics of Kitaev's toric code in Floquet quenches where the drive coherently creates and propagates fermionic $f$ anyons. We show that the long-time state departs from pure-state topological order but retains long-range entanglement, thereby realizing a Floquet analog of intrinsic mixed-state topological order. As a key result enabling this analysis, we derive decompositions of the entanglement entropy and logarithmic negativity that separate the ground-state value, including topological terms, from a contribution given by the driven quasiparticles. For a drive inducing non-interacting $f$ dynamics, these decompositions allow us to leverage numerical methods for free fermions and to link the emergence of the proposed long-time ordered state to the equilibration of $f$ anyons to a periodic Gibbs ensemble. For drives inducing Floquet eigenstate thermalization of $f$, our results suggest a scenario where Floquet heating of the $f$ sector stabilizes a genuine non-equilibrium topological phase, in contrast with the usual view of heating as an obstacle to order in periodically driven systems.
- **Summary:** Entropy and logarithmic-negativity decompositions isolate driven-anyon contributions in Floquet toric-code quenches, allowing free-fermion numerics to characterize a long-time state with persistent long-range entanglement but without pure-state topological order. Equilibration to a periodic Gibbs ensemble explains this mixed-state-order analog; the interacting thermalizing case motivates, rather than establishes, a phase stabilized by Floquet heating.
**RELEVANCE:** Connects topological quantum information with nonequilibrium many-body dynamics and the distinction between pure- and mixed-state order.  
**TAGS:** `Floquet toric code` `anyon dynamics` `mixed-state topology`

## 4. CFTs with Large Virasoro Primary Gaps
**Authors:** Ziming Ji, Jiahua Tian  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23231v1  
**Categories:** hep-th, math-ph
- **Abstract:** We construct the first explicit examples of compact unitary bosonic full conformal field theories (CFTs) in two dimensions with a unique vacuum and an all-spin Virasoro-primary gap greater than two. Our constructions use asymmetric orbifolds of lattice CFTs built from even unimodular lattices of sufficiently large minimum norm, ensuring that no charged state lies below the desired gap. The gauged symmetries combine a common orthogonal action on the left- and right-moving currents with independent sign reversals; the latter project out the dimension-two scalar primary $\sum_i J_L^iJ_R^i$ that survives when the symmetry acts identically on both chiral halves. We prove that the gauging is anomaly-free and determine the exact gaps by analyzing the invariant untwisted spectrum and all twisted sectors. From lattices constructed using ternary quadratic-residue codes, we obtain an infinite family with gap three at $(c_L,c_R)=(p+1,p+1)$ for primes $p\ge71$ with $p\equiv23\pmod{24}$. From Barnes-Wall lattices, we obtain an infinite family with gap four at $(c_L,c_R)=(2^m,2^m)$ for odd $m\ge7$. We also obtain two additional examples, with gap three at $(c_L,c_R)=(48,48)$ and gap four at $(c_L,c_R)=(512,512)$.
- **Summary:** Anomaly-free asymmetric orbifolds of lattice CFTs remove the dimension-two current bilinear while controlling both twisted and untwisted spectra. The construction produces infinite families with exact all-spin Virasoro-primary gaps of three and four, giving explicit compact unitary two-dimensional examples beyond gap two and sharper benchmarks for sparse-spectrum CFT questions.
**RELEVANCE:** Relevant to high-energy conformal field theory and sparse spectra that inform holographic quantum-gravity questions.  
**TAGS:** `Virasoro primary gaps` `asymmetric orbifolds` `lattice CFT`

## 5. Probing Lorentz-violating electrodynamics with CMB polarization. II. Updated constraints and forecasts for the Simons Observatory and LiteBIRD
**Authors:** Margherita Lembo, Luca Caloni, Martina Gerbino, Serena Giardiello, Giulia Gubitosi, Massimiliano Lattanzi, Luca Pagano, Nicolò Raffuzzi  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23082v1  
**Categories:** astro-ph.CO, gr-qc, hep-ph
- **Abstract:** We update the constraints on Lorentz-violating electrodynamics from the polarization of the cosmic microwave background (CMB). Within the minimal Standard Model Extension, we consider the renormalizable CPT-odd and CPT-even operators of the photon sector, which source cosmic birefringence and circular polarization, respectively. Combining Planck, Atacama Cosmology Telescope (ACT) and South Pole Telescope (SPT) temperature and $E$-mode data with $B$-mode measurements from BICEP/Keck, SPT-3G D1 and SPTpol, we constrain the CPT-odd coefficients to $|k^{(3)}_{(V)00}|<7.75\times10^{-45}$ GeV and $|\mathbf{k_{AF}}|<3.82\times10^{-45}$ GeV at 95% credible level. These are the strongest bounds to date from any probe, and are driven by the SPT-3G D1 $B$-mode data. For the first time in this type of analysis, we also include the Planck PR4 $EB$ spectrum, which yields a mild preference for a non-zero $|k^{(3)}_{(V)00}|$. For the CPT-even coefficient, we find $k_{F,E+B}<5.99\times10^{-32}$. Including the latest circular polarization measurements from the Cosmology Large Angular Scale Surveyor (CLASS) tightens this bound by about 7%. This is the first time that CMB circular polarization data contribute to constraining this coefficient. Finally, we forecast the sensitivity of the Simons Observatory (SO) and LiteBIRD. With linear polarization only, the two experiments are complementary, with SO tightening the CPT-even bound by about 30% and LiteBIRD the CPT-odd ones by 40 to 45%. Exploiting the sensitivity to circular polarization provided by the non-idealities of its half-wave plate, LiteBIRD could also improve the CPT-even bound by more than an order of magnitude.
- **Summary:** A joint analysis of CMB polarization data within the minimal Standard Model Extension constrains CPT-odd photon coefficients below 7.75×10⁻⁴⁵ and 3.82×10⁻⁴⁵ GeV at 95% credibility, while CLASS circular polarization improves the CPT-even bound by about 7%. Forecasts separate the complementary reach of Simons Observatory and LiteBIRD, including a possible order-of-magnitude CPT-even improvement from LiteBIRD circular-polarization sensitivity; the reported EB preference remains mild.
**RELEVANCE:** Observationally anchors Lorentz-symmetry tests relevant to quantum-gravity phenomenology without equating a mild preference with detection.  
**TAGS:** `CMB birefringence` `Lorentz violation` `circular polarization`

## 6. Expressivity of two-dimensional Gaussian fermion tensor network states
**Authors:** Pok Man Tam, William Musk, Zhehao Dai  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23227v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph, cond-mat.mes-hall, cond-mat.str-el, cond-mat.supr-con
- **Abstract:** Tensor network states (TNS) provide a family of efficiently compressed approximations to physically relevant many-body quantum states. An important quantity that characterizes the computational resources required by a TNS is its bond dimension. While in one dimension an area law for the Renyi-0 entropy guarantees an exact matrix product state representation with a bond dimension independent of the system size, an analogous statement does not hold for general states in two dimensions (2D). This leaves open the question of how the expressivity of 2D TNS is related to the entanglement properties of the states they represent. In this work, we focus on two families of 2D translation-invariant Gaussian fermionic states: single-band BCS superconductors and half-filled two-band insulators (including semimetals). For each family, we prove that an area law for the Renyi-0 entropy is equivalent to having an exact Gaussian fermionic TNS representation with a finite bond dimension determined by the Renyi-0 entropy. We also establish equivalent characterizations in terms of compactly supported Wannier-type orbitals and the momentum-space wavefunction, relating the spatial extent of these orbitals and the structure of the wavefunction to the required bond dimension.
- **Summary:** For single-band BCS states and half-filled two-band Gaussian insulators in two dimensions, the authors prove equivalence between a Renyi-0 area law and an exact finite-bond-dimension Gaussian tensor-network representation. Equivalent compact-orbital and momentum-space criteria translate entanglement structure into representation resources, establishing a controlled result for these families rather than arbitrary two-dimensional states.
**RELEVANCE:** A rigorous bridge between entanglement, many-body wavefunctions, and the resources needed for tensor-network simulation.  
**TAGS:** `Gaussian tensor networks` `Renyi-0 area law` `compact Wannier orbitals`

## 7. Complete Cosmological Collider Signatures from Right-handed Neutrino Loops
**Authors:** Linghao Song, Jingtao You, Ming Cheng, Hong-Jian He, Xingang Chen  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23220v1  
**Categories:** hep-ph, astro-ph.CO, hep-th
- **Abstract:** We study the complete cosmological collider (CC) signatures from right-handed neutrino loops with chemical-potential enhancement. These CC signals are induced by the inflaton-right-handed-neutrino interaction and can be extracted in the collinear squeezed configuration, which allows the right-handed-neutrino triangle loop to be factorized into the hard and soft parts. We precisely compute the soft part of the fermion loop for the first time by using the complete mode functions and the full loop integral, and treat the hard part as a tree-level subdiagram. We subtract the large-momentum asymptotic terms of the soft loop integrand and retain their finite contributions to the oscillation. We then evaluate the full spinor-helicity and Schwinger-Keldysh sums to obtain a finite, closed analytic formula for the complex clock coefficient. We further derive a compact formula for the leading CC signals in the large-chemical-potential expansion. We analyze the structure of the soft-part contribution by examining the sum of residues of the Mellin poles of the fermion propagators. For this, we demonstrate that the first local pole on each soft line contributes half of the asymptotic coefficient on one Schwinger-Keldysh path and the remaining local poles on the second soft line supply the other half.
- **Summary:** Factorizing a squeezed-limit neutrino triangle into hard and soft pieces, the calculation retains complete soft mode functions and finite subtraction terms before performing spinor-helicity and Schwinger–Keldysh sums. This yields a finite analytic complex clock coefficient and a large-chemical-potential expansion, improving theoretical templates for inflationary heavy-neutrino signatures rather than reporting an observational signal.
**RELEVANCE:** Links early-universe quantum fields, neutrino physics, and precision predictions for cosmological-collider observables.  
**TAGS:** `cosmological collider` `right-handed neutrinos` `inflationary loops`

## 8. Strange-Metal Magnetotransport without a Strange-Metal Ground State
**Authors:** Yongjun Ahn, Matteo Baggioli, Hyun-Sik Jeong, Keun-Young Kim  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22985v1  
**Categories:** hep-th, cond-mat.str-el
- **Abstract:** Strange metals display a linear-in-temperature resistivity, $ρ_{xx}\sim T$, and a quadratic inverse Hall angle, $\cotΘ_H\sim T^2$. These seemingly universal laws are often attributed to quantum criticality, yet no controlled theory realizes both at an asymptotic zero-temperature fixed point, and experimentally they persist only over finite temperature windows. Here, we show that these properties can arise from an intermediate critical regime that does not govern the ground state. Using a minimal class of holographic models, we construct a fully backreacted realization of both scalings, which appear within a finite temperature window controlled by an emergent intermediate scaling geometry. The window is bounded by relevant deformations of the corresponding fixed point: momentum relaxation sets the upper edge, while the magnetic field $B$ closes it from below at $T_{\min}\sim B^{2/3}$, up to logarithmic corrections. At $T_{\min}$, strange-metal transport, the scaling geometry, and critical thermodynamics break down together. Our results point to intermediate criticality as a distinct route to strange-metal transport, and suggest that it can be identified by the simultaneous breakdown of transport and thermodynamic scaling under a magnetic field.
- **Summary:** Fully backreacted holographic models realize linear resistivity and a quadratic inverse Hall angle in an intermediate scaling geometry rather than at the ground-state fixed point. Relevant deformations bound this finite-temperature window, with its lower edge scaling as B^(2/3) up to logarithms, suggesting simultaneous transport and thermodynamic breakdown as a diagnostic of intermediate criticality.
**RELEVANCE:** Bridges gravitational dual methods and correlated-electron phenomenology while challenging the need for a strange-metal ground state.  
**TAGS:** `holographic transport` `intermediate criticality` `strange metals`

## 9. Local interference algorithms for observables in large random circuits
**Authors:** Philippe Suchsland, Brayden Ware, Vladislav D. Kurilovich, Vadim Smelyanskiy, Kostyantyn Kechedzhi  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23207v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph, cond-mat.str-el
- **Abstract:** In quantum dynamics, it is generally believed that interference effects lead to a sign problem for classical Monte Carlo sampling methods, rendering sampling ineffective. In this paper we show that this is generically not the case for the task of simulating the evolution of observables in random instances of circuit dynamics. Specifically, we consider two examples of observables which give large signals despite the randomness of the dynamics: first order out-of-time-ordered correlators (OTOCs) in unstructured random circuits without conservation laws and time-ordered charge-charge correlators (TOCs) in particle number-preserving random circuits. We show that these observables can be approximated by classical stochastic processes and that these approximations can be systematically improved with a series expansion that incorporates coherent corrections associated with clusters of gates. We design and implement algorithms that combine Monte Carlo sampling and tensor network methods to approximate the series. We observe numerically that the computational complexity of approximating TOCs and OTOCs scales polynomially in the circuit size and in the target precision. We study the accuracy of these algorithms by directly mapping their ensemble-averaged errors to partition functions of replica statistical models, allowing for perturbative analysis and numerics in $1$d brickwork geometries. Our theoretical analysis explains the empirical observation of polynomial complexity of these algorithms and is consistent with polynomial complexity extending to arbitrarily small precision and to the thermodynamic limit.
- **Summary:** A gate-cluster expansion adds coherent corrections to stochastic approximations of OTOCs and charge correlators, combining Monte Carlo sampling with tensor-network contractions. Numerical polynomial resource scaling is supported by replica-model error analysis in one-dimensional brickwork settings, identifying tractable scrambling observables without establishing efficient simulation of arbitrary quantum circuits.
**RELEVANCE:** Important for quantum-information scrambling and for judging which proposed quantum-simulation tasks remain classically accessible.  
**TAGS:** `random-circuit simulation` `OTOC algorithms` `replica error analysis`

## 10. Non-Invertible Quantum Cellular Automata I: Microscopic Definition of Non-Invertible Symmetries
**Authors:** Sahand Seifnashri, Shu-Heng Shao  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22928v1  
**Categories:** cond-mat.str-el, hep-th, math-ph, quant-ph
- **Abstract:** We introduce non-invertible quantum cellular automata (QCAs), defined as locality-preserving quantum channels that are dualizable and pure. This provides an operator-algebraic definition of non-invertible symmetries of quantum lattice systems, formulated in terms of their action on local operators. Focusing on quantum spin chains, we motivate these defining conditions and conjecture that every such quantum channel admits a realization by dualizable topological defects or, equivalently, by dualizable sequential quantum circuits. We construct a broad class of examples, including on-site non-invertible symmetries that are anomalous, as well as higher-dimensional examples. In Part II of this work, we reconstruct topological defects whose boundary algebras yield an index theory that classifies non-invertible QCAs up to blend equivalence.
- **Summary:** Defining non-invertible cellular automata as pure, dualizable locality-preserving quantum channels gives lattice symmetries a microscopic operator-algebraic formulation. Examples include anomalous on-site symmetries and higher-dimensional constructions, while equivalence to topological-defect or sequential-circuit realizations is explicitly conjectured, setting up a framework rather than a completed classification here.
**RELEVANCE:** Connects generalized symmetries in high-energy theory with local quantum channels and many-body lattice systems.  
**TAGS:** `non-invertible symmetries` `quantum cellular automata` `topological defects`

## 11. Using the 0i Einstein equation to break dark-sector degeneracies
**Authors:** Camille Bonvin  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22498v1  
**Categories:** astro-ph.CO, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** Over the past decade, cosmological observations have started to challenge the $Λ$CDM model. If these tensions persist, they immediately raise one fundamental question: what new physics could be responsible for them? More specifically, do these deviations arise from a modification of gravity, from an evolving or clustering dark energy component, or from dark matter properties beyond the cold dark matter paradigm? In this paper we show that, with current methods, any observed deviation can always be attributed to more than one sector, preventing us from determining if the tension is due to gravity, dark energy or dark matter. The main reason is that current tests rely on unobservable quantities. These quantities are then either related to observables by assuming the validity of specific equations, or compared with theoretical predictions computed within a given class of theories. Both approaches inevitably break the model independence of the tests, leading to degeneracies between gravity, dark energy, and dark matter. To overcome this limitation, we construct a set of three tests that rely only on observable quantities and that, when combined, can distinguish between the three sectors. We show that the $0i$ Einstein equation -- which is never tested in standard analyses -- adds crucial information to that provided by the gravitational slip equation and the Euler equation.
- **Summary:** Three observable-based consistency tests combine the momentum-constraint, gravitational-slip, and Euler equations to distinguish modified gravity, dark energy, and nonstandard dark matter. The contribution is a diagnostic framework exposing assumptions hidden in tests built from unobservable variables, with the usually unused 0i Einstein equation supplying the extra discriminating information rather than a claimed resolution of existing tensions.
**RELEVANCE:** Directly useful for separating cosmological signatures of gravitational dynamics from dark-sector modeling assumptions.  
**TAGS:** `dark-sector degeneracies` `cosmological consistency tests` `momentum constraint`

## 12. Gravitational waves from cosmological first-order phase transitions with fluctuation-induced friction
**Authors:** Jun-Ye Chen, Dongdong Wei, Zong-Kuan Guo  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22266v1  
**Categories:** gr-qc, astro-ph.CO, hep-ph
- **Abstract:** We study gravitational-wave production in a cosmological first-order phase transition with fluctuation-induced friction using three-dimensional lattice simulations of two coupled scalar fields. Kinetic and gradient energy in the additional field $s$ accumulates near the expanding bubble walls of $φ$ and affects their motion. The contribution from $s$ exceeds that from $φ$ at the spectral peak and has a slope similar to that of the bulk flow model over a finite interval above the peak. In the high-wave-number (UV) direction, $φ$ dominates and the total spectrum develops a broad feature. For the larger coupling, slowly contracting false vacuum remnants persist long after bubble merger.
- **Summary:** Three-dimensional lattice simulations of two coupled scalar fields track how fluctuation-induced friction changes bubble-wall motion and gravitational-wave production in a first-order transition. The extra field dominates near the spectral peak while the transition field controls the ultraviolet, producing a broad feature and, at larger coupling, long-lived false-vacuum remnants, showing why friction microphysics matters for signal templates.
**RELEVANCE:** Connects early-universe field dynamics with gravitational-wave probes beyond simplified bubble-collision models.  
**TAGS:** `phase-transition gravitational waves` `lattice simulations` `fluctuation-induced friction`
