## 1. The Cosmological Constant as a Clock and the Vilenkin Wavefunction as a Resonance
**Authors:** Stephon Alexander, Marcell Howard  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32987v1  
**Categories:** gr-qc, hep-th
- **Abstract:** We address the problem of time in quantum gravity using a covariant formulation of unimodular gravity. As a result, the cosmological constant is canonically conjugate to a global four-volume variable, which serves as a clock and leads to a time-dependent Wheeler--DeWitt equation. We construct exact, background-independent solutions related to the full Chern--Simons--Kodama state and demonstrate unitary evolution in minisuperspace. The Chern--Simons functional also provides an intrinsic gravitational clock. These solutions reveal that the Vilenkin wavefunction is a gravitational Gamow resonance, in which its non-normalizability follows from the outgoing boundary condition. The tunneling probability into the expanding branch increases with the positive cosmological constant. These results give a dynamical interpretation of the wavefunction of the universe and clarify the role of its boundary conditions.
- **Summary:** Using covariant unimodular gravity, the authors promote four-volume to a clock conjugate to the cosmological constant and construct exact solutions linked to the Chern–Simons–Kodama state. Their minisuperspace evolution is unitary, while the outgoing Vilenkin state becomes a Gamow resonance rather than a normalizable bound state, connecting boundary conditions to the interpretation of cosmological tunneling.
**RELEVANCE:** Directly relevant to Deepak's canonical quantum-gravity and quantum-cosmology interests, especially the problem of time and connection-based states.  
**TAGS:** `unimodular clock` `Kodama state` `cosmological resonance`

## 2. Classical algorithms for sparse random Hamiltonians via free probability
**Authors:** Matthew Ding, Robbie King, Thomas Schuster  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33128v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph, cs.DS
- **Abstract:** The search for quantum advantage in average-case problems is a pressing open question in physics and computer science. The widest conjectured example of such an advantage is in simulation of quantum many-body systems, where sign problems and entanglement growth cause the breakdown of many classical methods. Nonetheless, theoretical evidence for or against such an advantage remains scarce. Here, we investigate this in sparse random non-local Hamiltonians. We design a classical algorithm for such Hamiltonians that enables accurate computation of spectral fluctuations, thermal values, and time-ordered and out-of-time-order correlation functions. For any subexponential precision $\varepsilon$, our algorithm runs in time $(1/\varepsilon)^{2+o(1)}$, nearly identical to a quantum simulation. Crucially, this applies even at late evolution times, low temperatures, and near critical points, where existing classical methods require exponential time. Rather than directly simulating a quantum state or operator, our algorithm first computes the model's mean behavior and then a short hierarchy of instance-dependent corrections. These corrections predict long-time behavior based on short-time data using free probability theory.
- **Summary:** Free-probability techniques replace explicit state evolution with ensemble means and a short hierarchy of sample-specific corrections for sparse random nonlocal Hamiltonians. The resulting classical computation of spectra, thermal observables, and dynamical correlators costs nearly the same precision-dependent runtime as quantum simulation, including difficult late-time regimes, narrowing a proposed quantum-advantage setting rather than solving generic many-body dynamics.
**RELEVANCE:** A substantial classical benchmark for Deepak's interests in quantum simulation, scrambling, and the computational limits of many-body physics.  
**TAGS:** `free probability` `sparse random Hamiltonians` `classical simulation`

## 3. Multiscale Zoom-in Lattice Simulation for Inflation
**Authors:** Xinpeng Wang, Angelo Caravano, Leander Thiele, Misao Sasaki  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32753v1  
**Categories:** astro-ph.CO, gr-qc, hep-ph
- **Abstract:** Lattice simulations are a powerful tool for studying the nonlinear evolution of inflationary fluctuations. The exponential expansion, however, makes it challenging to cover a broad range of scales relevant for the dynamics, limiting how many e-folds a single fixed grid can follow. We develop a three-dimensional zoom-in lattice method to follow inflationary fluctuations over a broad range of scales from the adiabatic subhorizon regime through horizon crossing. Every few e-folds, the grid is refined into one or more subgrids, with small-scale power injected and large-scale fluctuations inherited from previous generations. Each zoom shifts the resolved band while keeping its dynamic range fixed. This extends the duration over which lattice simulations can follow inflationary fluctuations from a few e-folds to tens of e-folds. In a Starobinsky benchmark evolved for 50 e-folds, the curvature power spectrum agrees with linear theory. In a model with an oscillatory feature, the zoom-in simulations recover the peak seen in a high-resolution fixed-grid run and show scale-dependent non-Gaussianity.
- **Summary:** A three-dimensional lattice scheme repeatedly refines subgrids, carrying forward long-wavelength fluctuations while adding new short-scale power to follow inflation across many more e-folds than a fixed grid. A 50-e-fold Starobinsky benchmark matches the linear power spectrum, and a featured model reproduces a fixed-grid peak with scale-dependent non-Gaussianity, supporting extended nonlinear studies of primordial fluctuations.
**RELEVANCE:** Offers Deepak a concrete numerical method connecting inflationary theory with primordial spectra and non-Gaussian observables.  
**TAGS:** `multiscale inflation` `zoom-in lattices` `primordial non-Gaussianity`

## 4. Horizon dynamical entropies and phase space topology
**Authors:** Matteo Bruno, Simone Speziale  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32444v1  
**Categories:** gr-qc, hep-th, math-ph
- **Abstract:** We study phase space topologies induced by infinite-dimensional norms as a means to solve the problem of 'integrability' of Hamiltonian generators corresponding to diffeomorphisms of a null hypersurface, with the specific goal in mind to compare different prescriptions for the dynamical entropy of black holes, which replaces the area in non-stationary regimes. We first show that for a scalar field, there exists a family of norms that would provide integrability for different polarizations. We then show that a single member of this family applies to the gravitational field, and it is given by the Sobolev norm already considered in the literature. The obstruction to the other candidates lies in the semi-positivity of the metric.
- **Summary:** Examining which infinite-dimensional phase-space norms make null-surface diffeomorphism generators integrable, the authors contrast a family of admissible scalar-field polarizations with gravity's much tighter restrictions. Only the previously studied Sobolev choice survives for the gravitational field because of metric semipositivity, constraining the phase-space foundations used to compare nonstationary horizon entropies.
**RELEVANCE:** Closely aligned with Deepak's gravitational phase-space, boundary-charge, and horizon-entropy interests; it addresses underlying geometry rather than an explicit LQG quantization.  
**TAGS:** `null-surface charges` `Sobolev phase space` `dynamical horizon entropy`

## 5. Exploiting SU(2) Symmetry for Efficient Quantum State Preparation of Matrix Product States
**Authors:** Etienne D'Argenlieu, Alessandro Summer, François Jamet, David P. Tew, Lachlan P. Lindoy  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32805v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph
- **Abstract:** Quantum computing offers a promising route to simulating strongly correlated systems, particularly for dynamical and spectral quantities whose classical computation can become prohibitively expensive. Fault-tolerant algorithms for these tasks, including real-time dynamics and phase estimation, assume efficient preparation of the initial many-body state, making state preparation an important bottleneck. Matrix product states obtained classically with the density matrix renormalization group can be compiled sequentially into fault-tolerant circuits. We exploit non-Abelian spin SU(2) symmetry in this compilation. By the Wigner-Eckart theorem, only the reduced tensors, which carry the variational information, need generic unitary synthesis. The magnetic structure is restored by Clebsch-Gordan transformations with analytically known angles. We benchmark the method on representative strongly correlated models, including the one-dimensional Hubbard model, quasi-one-dimensional Hubbard geometries, and Anderson impurity models, and perform fault-tolerant resource estimates using the Toffoli count as the primary cost metric. Across the systems considered, exploiting non-Abelian SU(2) symmetry reduces the Toffoli cost roughly threefold relative to U(1)-adapted compilation and by more than an order of magnitude relative to the standard dense compilation method. These results demonstrate how non-Abelian symmetries can substantially reduce the cost of transferring classically tractable many-body ground states to quantum computers, facilitating their use in quantum dynamics and energy estimation.
- **Summary:** The authors compile matrix product states into fault-tolerant circuits by synthesizing only SU(2)-reduced tensors and restoring magnetic indices through known Clebsch–Gordan rotations. Hubbard and impurity benchmarks show roughly threefold Toffoli savings over U(1)-adapted compilation and more than tenfold savings over dense synthesis, reducing the state-loading bottleneck for subsequent quantum dynamics and spectroscopy.
**RELEVANCE:** Connects Deepak's quantum-computing and correlated-matter interests through non-Abelian symmetry and explicit resource estimates.  
**TAGS:** `SU(2) compilation` `matrix product states` `Toffoli resources`

## 6. Entanglement Asymmetry and the Quantum Mpemba Effect in Inhomogeneous Quenches
**Authors:** Maëlle Olivier, Filiberto Ares, Pasquale Calabrese  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32934v1  
**Categories:** cond-mat.stat-mech, quant-ph
- **Abstract:** Entanglement asymmetry is a powerful probe of symmetry breaking and its dynamical restoration following quantum quenches. While extensively studied for homogeneous initial states, its evolution from inhomogeneous initial configurations remains largely unexplored. We investigate a spin-$1/2$ XX chain whose two halves are initially prepared in tilted ferromagnetic states with different tilt angles. Combining generalized hydrodynamics (GHD) and the quasiparticle picture with exact fermionic correlation-matrix calculations, we study the dynamics of entanglement asymmetry. We find good agreement between the two approaches for generic initial configurations and observe the quantum Mpemba effect, whereby states with stronger initial symmetry breaking can restore the symmetry faster. This effect now depends on the subsystem position: while the homogeneous behavior is recovered when the subsystem lies entirely within one of the initially tilted halves, the entanglement-asymmetry curves for subsystems straddling the interface can exhibit multiple crossings or no crossings at all. A notable exception occurs for subsystems entirely within an initially non-tilted half. In this case, GHD predicts vanishing asymmetry, but the exact dynamics generates a small, nonzero asymmetry that can develop a plateau before decaying toward zero. This feature reveals contributions beyond the leading-order hydrodynamic description.
- **Summary:** Generalized hydrodynamics and quasiparticle predictions are tested against exact fermionic correlation matrices for an XX chain joined from differently tilted halves. Symmetry-restoration crossings depend on subsystem position, and an initially untilted region develops asymmetry missed by leading hydrodynamics, showing both the spatial dependence of the quantum Mpemba effect and a specific limit of coarse-grained transport theory.
**RELEVANCE:** Relevant to Deepak's interests in nonequilibrium many-body physics, entanglement diagnostics, and emergent hydrodynamics.  
**TAGS:** `entanglement asymmetry` `inhomogeneous quenches` `quantum Mpemba effect`

## 7. The double copy and perturbative teleparallel gravity
**Authors:** Georg Stettinger  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33146v1  
**Categories:** hep-th, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** In this paper we analyze the double copy between gluon and graviton amplitudes within the context of teleparallel gravity. We first set up an effective Lagrangian based on minimal assumptions which reproduces the on-shell amplitudes stemming from the double-copy procedure. By straightforwardly computing the three- and four-point amplitude, this double-copy Lagrangian can be constrained up to a few real parameters when expanding to quartic order. For a specific choice of parameters, we find an exact equivalence with perturbative teleparallel gravity. This suggests that the teleparallel formulation might be well suited to make the double copy more transparent from a Lagrangian viewpoint.
- **Summary:** Matching an effective gravitational Lagrangian to double-copy gluon amplitudes at three and four points constrains its quartic expansion to a small parameter family. One parameter choice reproduces perturbative teleparallel gravity exactly at the studied order, suggesting that torsion-based variables may expose the gauge–gravity amplitude relation more directly without establishing an all-order equivalence.
**RELEVANCE:** Connects alternative gravitational variables with high-energy scattering methods, a useful intersection for Deepak's quantum-gravity interests.

## 8. Probing the Rung-Mott Insulator in Bosonic Two-Leg Ladders
**Authors:** Christopher Parsonage, Lennart Koehn, Lorenzo Carfora, Ben Watson, Timon Hilker, Elmar Haller, Peter Kirton, Arthur La Rooij, Stefan Kuhr  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32680v1  
**Categories:** cond-mat.quant-gas, physics.atom-ph, quant-ph
- **Abstract:** Mott insulators typically appear at integer densities, but certain lattice geometries or non-local interactions can stabilise Mott insulators at fractional densities. In this work, we study strongly interacting two-leg ladders using ultracold bosonic atoms around half-filling, and identify the rung-Mott insulating (RMI) phase through its characteristic rung occupation in site-resolved measurements. We probe its excitation spectrum via lattice modulation spectroscopy, and interpret the observed spectra using numerical simulations that include the driven dynamics, coupling to multiple excited states, and energy offsets. As with other Mott insulators, the RMI phase is characterized through its incompressibility, which we probe via a site-resolved analysis of densities and chemical potentials. Our results provide direct insight into the excitation dynamics of a strongly correlated ladder system, and pave the way for studying transport and further dynamical properties in low-dimensional quantum systems with programmable lattice geometries.
- **Summary:** Site-resolved measurements of ultracold bosons in two-leg ladders identify a half-filled rung-Mott phase and probe its incompressibility through local densities and chemical potentials. Modulation spectra are interpreted using driven multistate simulations including energy offsets, providing experimental access to fractional-density insulating excitations in a programmable many-body geometry.
**RELEVANCE:** Adds an experimental many-body anchor to Deepak's digest, with direct relevance to quantum simulation and low-dimensional correlated phases.  
**TAGS:** `rung-Mott insulator` `bosonic ladders` `modulation spectroscopy`

## 9. A Koashi--Imoto Criterion for Holevo-Rate Visible Compression
**Authors:** Anthony M. Polloreno  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33129v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph, cs.IT
- **Abstract:** For a finite memoryless quantum source whose encoder knows the sequence of classical labels, we determine when the Holevo rate is achievable with shared classical randomness and no shared entanglement. In the maximal Koashi--Imoto decomposition of the signal ensemble, this occurs exactly when each signal is pure on the quantum part of every sector it occupies. For qubits, this means that the signals are all pure or commute pairwise. If any occupied quantum part is mixed, every code with sufficiently small mean local error has rate bounded away from the Holevo information, uniformly in blocklength. To prove the converse, we apply Fawzi--Renner recovery to a hypothetical sequence of visible codes approaching the Holevo rate at vanishing local error, then invoke a decoupling bound of Baghali Khanian and Winter for blind compression.
- **Summary:** A Koashi–Imoto decomposition gives an exact criterion for visible quantum-source compression to attain the Holevo rate using shared classical randomness but no entanglement: every occupied quantum sector must be pure. Recovery and decoupling arguments establish a uniform rate gap when this condition fails, turning a resource-dependent achievability question into a structural test on the signal ensemble.
**RELEVANCE:** A rigorous quantum-information result relevant to Deepak's interests in compression, recovery, and distinctions between classical and entangled resources.  
**TAGS:** `visible compression` `Koashi–Imoto decomposition` `Holevo-rate criterion`

## 10. Exact solution of the Bose-Fermi mixture along the unnesting line
**Authors:** Soham Chandak, Aleksandra Petković, Zoran Ristivojevic  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32804v1  
**Categories:** cond-mat.quant-gas, math-ph, quant-ph
- **Abstract:** A one-dimensional model of a Bose-Fermi mixture with contact repulsions is exactly solvable via nested Bethe ansatz. That formal solution is here simplified along a special line where the Bethe-ansatz equations can be unnested. The model solution is expressed in terms of the known solutions for the Lieb-Liniger model and the Yang-Gaudin model of attractive spin-$\frac{1}{2}$ fermions in the balanced case. We find explicit analytical results for the ground-state energy, the velocities of excitations, and the Drude weights at weak and strong interactions. At weak interactions, the special line corresponds to the parameter values where the isolated systems of free fermions and weakly interacting bosons have equal velocities.
- **Summary:** Along a special unnesting line, the nested Bethe equations of a repulsive one-dimensional Bose–Fermi mixture reduce to known Lieb–Liniger and balanced attractive Yang–Gaudin solutions. This yields analytical weak- and strong-coupling energies, excitation velocities, and Drude weights, providing tractable benchmarks for transport in interacting mixtures rather than just a formal integrability statement.
**RELEVANCE:** Useful to Deepak's many-body and mathematical-physics interests as an exact reference for quantum-gas thermodynamics and transport.

## 11. Perturbative unitarity bounds from qubit entanglement in scalar scattering and QED
**Authors:** Shiuli Chatterjee, Kamila Kowalska, Enrico Maria Sessolo  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32842v1  
**Categories:** hep-ph, hep-th, quant-ph
- **Abstract:** In the framework of $2\to 2$ relativistic scattering described by the perturbative $S$-matrix, we perform a comprehensive analysis of the properties of quantum channels obtained by tracing out the momentum from the scattered state. We work in two distinct scenarios with qubit degrees of freedom: flavored scalar contact interactions and QED, focusing in particular on initial-state leptons with transverse polarization. We identify the conditions that make the quantum channel unitary, calculate the entanglement of the qubits through the concurrence, and use its maximally allowed value to extract perturbativity upper bounds on the coupling constants. We find that the limits are often more constraining than the corresponding partial-wave expansion bounds, and that they allow us to bypass some of the obstacles associated in the latter with the singular behavior of forward scattering.
- **Summary:** Tracing momentum out of relativistic scalar and QED scattering produces qubit channels whose unitarity conditions and concurrence can be computed perturbatively. Enforcing the maximum allowed concurrence yields coupling bounds often tighter than partial-wave estimates and avoids some forward-scattering singularity obstacles, linking information-theoretic consistency to practical perturbativity diagnostics.
**RELEVANCE:** Directly bridges Deepak's high-energy-physics and quantum-information interests through scattering entanglement and consistency bounds.  
**TAGS:** `scattering quantum channels` `concurrence bounds` `perturbative QED`

## 12. Prospects and limitations of adaptive variational quantum algorithms for lattice field theories: a case study in 2+1 dimensions
**Authors:** Nico Dichter, Andrea Bulgarelli, Lena Funcke, Stefan Kühn  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33138v1  
**Categories:** hep-lat, hep-th, quant-ph
- **Abstract:** The variational quantum algorithm ADAPT-VQE has previously been used to prepare the vacuum of lattice gauge theories in $1+1D$. In this paper, we study whether it extends to higher dimensions. We identify two general limitations of ADAPT-VQE. First, if the qubit Hamiltonian and the initial state are real, i.e., invariant under qubit-level time-reversal, all real-valued pool operators have vanishing gradients and are never selected. Second, the algorithm can converge to excited eigenstates. We illustrate both limitations, also providing examples from literature, and present a first application of ADAPT-VQE to a lattice field theory in $2+1D$, namely free Wilson fermions, where both obstacles occur. Adding charge conjugation symmetry to the operator pool improves the results, and combining different pools with suitable initial states recovers the ground and first excited states over the full mass range. These findings give practical guidelines for applying ADAPT-VQE to lattice field theories beyond $1+1D$.
- **Summary:** A study of ADAPT-VQE for free Wilson fermions in 2+1 dimensions exposes two failure modes: time-reversal symmetry can force candidate gradients to vanish, and optimization can terminate at excited eigenstates. Symmetry-aware operator pools combined with suitable starting states recover the lowest two states across the mass range, giving concrete design guidance for extending variational field-theory simulation beyond one spatial dimension.
**RELEVANCE:** Relevant to Deepak's quantum-computing and high-energy interests, with actionable limitations for lattice-field state preparation.  
**TAGS:** `ADAPT-VQE limitations` `2+1D Wilson fermions` `symmetry-aware pools`
