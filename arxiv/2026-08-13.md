## 1. Robustness of the primordial power spectrum in hybrid loop quantum cosmology to approximations near the bounce
- **Authors:** Kristina Giesel, Almudena Guillén, Guillermo A. Mena Marugán et al.
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.12181
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12181
- **Categories:** hep-th, gr-qc, astro-ph.CO
- **Abstract:** We prove the robustness of the analytic approximation used in (hybrid) loop quantum cosmology to compute the primordial power spectrum of the cosmological perturbations for a recently proposed vacuum state (the non-oscillatory state with asymptotic Hamiltonian diagonalization). To investigate this, we study different approximations to the effective mass of these perturbations near the bounce, showing that the Pöschl-Teller potential employed in previous works leads to indistinguishable power spectra compared to other estimations of the mass, provided that they successfully capture the characteristic scale of the bounce, which translates into a scale of power suppression.
- **Summary:** This work tests a key assumption in hybrid loop quantum cosmology: that the Pöschl-Teller potential approximation used to compute primordial power spectra near the bounce captures the essential physics. By systematically comparing different approximations for the effective perturbation mass, the authors demonstrate that the resulting power spectra are essentially identical as long as the approximation correctly captures the bounce scale — the single parameter that determines power suppression. This validates earlier analytic results and shows they are not artifacts of a specific mathematical ansatz.
- **Relevance:** Critical validation for LQC cosmological predictions. The robustness of the power spectrum calculation strengthens confidence in observable signatures from loop quantum cosmology, such as the characteristic suppression of power at large scales that could be tested against CMB data.
- **Tags:** loop-quantum-gravity, quantum-cosmology, CMB

## 2. Why There is No Memory Burden in Holographic Space-time Models of Black Hole Formation and Evaporation
- **Authors:** T. Banks
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.12139
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12139
- **Categories:** hep-th, gr-qc, hep-ph
- **Abstract:** Recent papers\cite{dvali} have claimed that general quantum information considerations put macroscopic constraints on the properties of large black holes, which can affect their lifetimes in cosmologically and astrophysically interesting ways. We examine black hole evaporation in Holographic Space Time (HST) models\cite{hstbh}, which appear to have the {\it memory burden} effect responsible for these deviations from semi-classical expectations, and explain why, in those models, no such effect exists. It is basically a consequence of Fermi\'s Golden Rule/The Principle of Detailed Balance, but depends crucially on both the definition of energy in HST models and the way in which causality is implemented.
- **Summary:** Banks tackles the "memory burden" problem — the claim that quantum information constraints should modify the evaporation of large astrophysical black holes. In Holographic Space-Time (HST) models, which naively appear to exhibit this effect, Banks shows that careful application of Fermi's Golden Rule and detailed balance eliminates any macroscopic memory burden. The resolution hinges on how energy is defined in HST and the model's implementation of causality, suggesting that the information-theoretic constraints on black hole lifetimes may be model-dependent rather than universal.
- **Relevance:** Directly addresses black hole information paradox and the validity of semiclassical expectations for astrophysical black holes. Important for understanding whether quantum gravity effects could observably alter black hole evaporation on cosmological timescales.
- **Tags:** black-holes, holography, information-paradox

- **Categories:** hep-th, quant-ph, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** Ryu-Takayanagi established how boundary entanglement encodes bulk area. We provide the metric counterpart: boundary mutual information imposes a rigorous lower bound on bulk geodesic separation that diverges logarithmically as correlations vanish. As entanglement decreases, the bound on spatial separation diverges logarithmically, effectively preventing bulk disconnection. - **Relevance:** Fundamental advance in holographic entanglement and the geometry of spacetime. The quantitative link between entanglement reduction and spatial separation growth provides a precise manifestation of "spacetime from entanglement" relevant to quantum gravity research.

## 4. Geometry of Noisy Quantum Many-Body Dynamics with Continuous Symmetries: Entanglement and Correlations
- **Authors:** Marco Lastres, Sanjay Moudgalya
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.11297
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11297
- **Categories:** hep-th, math-ph, cond-mat.stat-mech, cond-mat.str-el, quant-ph
- **Abstract:** We study unitary quantum dynamics in noisy Brownian models with global continuous symmetries, such as $U(1)$ and $SU(2)$, focusing on Rényi entanglement entropies and hydrodynamic and non-hydrodynamic correlators. By mapping the averaged late-time dynamics to the low-energy physics of effective replica Hamiltonians, we find that the evolution is controlled by the quantum geometry of their ground-state manifolds, which is directly related to the geometry of $k$-commutants---the symmetry algebra of $k$ replicas of the system. In interacting systems, these $k$-commutants are generically determined solely by the symmetries of the system, independent of microscopic details of the noisy evolution. This allows us to use the time-dependent variational principle (TDVP) to provide simple geometric explanations for the sub-ballistic Rényi entanglement growth and the anomalous decay of non-hydrodynamic correlators in interacting systems with continuous symmetries. We find this behavior to be intimately connected to singularities within the $k$-commutant manifolds, arising from frozen ``void\'\' states in the Hilbert space that exist due to continuous on-site symmetries. This also demystifies the important role of voids in the dynamics of these observables, previously identified in $U(1)$ symmetric systems. We compare these behaviors in interacting systems with Abelian and non-Abelian continuous symmetries and in free-fermion systems, which differ in the geometry of their $k$-commutants. Ultimately, this work provides a general geometric framework for systematically studying observables in noisy systems with continuous symmetries, including Haar-random circuits.
- **Summary:** The authors develop a unified geometric framework for understanding entanglement growth and correlation decay in noisy quantum systems with continuous symmetries. By mapping the problem to the quantum geometry of replica Hamiltonian ground-state manifolds, they explain why entanglement grows sub-ballistically and correlators decay anomalously — phenomena traced to singular structures in the symmetry algebra (k-commutants) created by "void" states. The framework applies across Abelian and non-Abelian symmetries and explains previously mysterious void-state effects.
- **Relevance:** Bridges quantum many-body physics, entanglement dynamics, and symmetry-constrained systems. The geometric perspective on noisy dynamics is valuable for understanding thermalization and information scrambling in quantum systems, with connections to tensor networks and quantum computing.
- **Tags:** many-body, entanglement, quantum-dynamics

## 5. Static multipolar Einstein-vector-Gauss-Bonnet black holes
- **Authors:** Burkhard Kleihaus, Jutta Kunz
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.11900
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11900
- **Categories:** hep-th, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** We construct and analyze static multipolar black holes in Einstein-vector-Gauss-Bonnet theory with a quadratic coupling function. The vectorized solutions bifurcate from the Schwarzschild black hole at discrete values of the Gauss-Bonnet coupling, obtained from a perturbative eigenvalue problem on the Schwarzschild background and labelled by the angular multipole number $\ell$. We restrict to the fundamental radial branches. The electric sector contains the known spherically symmetric $\ell=0$ branch as well as axisymmetric branches with $\ell&gt;0$. These electric branches extend to larger values of the coupling. The magnetic sector features a sequence of axisymmetric branches, including the previously found magnetic dipole branch at $\ell=0$. The magnetic branches instead exist only on finite intervals of the coupling, ending at critical solutions. Away from the bifurcation point, nonlinearities generate additional multipoles, but even-$\ell$ and odd-$\ell$ moments remain separated.
- **Summary:** New black hole solutions with vector hair and multipolar deformations are constructed in Einstein-vector-Gauss-Bonnet theory. The solutions emerge from Schwarzschild via spontaneous vectorization at discrete coupling values labeled by angular multipole number. The authors find a striking difference between electric-type solutions (which persist to large coupling) and magnetic-type solutions (which terminate at critical points), with nonlinear effects generating higher multipoles while preserving parity separation. This extends the landscape of known hairy black holes in modified gravity.
- **Relevance:** Adds to the growing family of black hole solutions beyond General Relativity. Multipolar structure in these solutions could produce distinct gravitational wave signatures, making them relevant for both theoretical quantum gravity and observational astrophysics.
- **Tags:** black-holes, modified-gravity, Gauss-Bonnet

## 6. Squeezed quantum states and partner modes in the moving mirror model of black hole evaporation
- **Authors:** Kuan-Nan Lin, Pisin Chen, Michael R.R. Good et al.
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.11957
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11957
- **Categories:** hep-th, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** The standard moving mirror model in (1+1)-dimensional spacetime is known to reproduce several quantum aspects of black hole evaporation. A perfectly reflecting, accelerating mirror can emit radiation whose frequency spectrum resembles that of Hawking radiation, although this correspondence holds only under certain approximations. Moreover, the standard in-out formulation does not provide a natural notion of the partner modes associated with the Hawking radiation. In this paper, we reformulate the moving mirror model in terms of Rindler/Milne modes. This formulation not only attributes the origin of the required approximations to mode squeezing effects but also naturally incorporates the notion of partner modes. Furthermore, as a consequence of these mode squeezing effects, the radiation received by an inertial observer at future null infinity exhibits additional nontrivial quantum correlations, even though its frequency spectrum approximately follows a Bose--Einstein or Fermi--Dirac distribution.
- **Summary:** The moving mirror model — a key toy model for Hawking radiation — is reformulated using Rindler/Milne modes, which naturally reveals why the Hawking spectrum only emerges as an approximation. The new framework shows that mode squeezing is the underlying mechanism, and crucially, it incorporates partner modes (the interior modes paired with exterior Hawking quanta) that were absent in the standard formulation. The analysis also predicts extra quantum correlations in the radiation beyond the thermal spectrum, offering a richer picture of black hole evaporation.
- **Relevance:** Clarifies the relationship between the moving mirror and Hawking radiation, resolving long-standing questions about partner modes. Important for black hole information paradox research, as partner modes are central to understanding how information might escape from evaporating black holes.
- **Tags:** black-holes, Hawking-radiation, quantum-field-theory

- **Abstract:** We derive an effective scalar field theory for matter in group field theory condensate cosmology, starting from the fundamental quantum-gravity dynamics in a fully relational framework and encompassing both early- and late-universe regimes. ## 8. Aspects of Carrollian field theory from holography
- **Authors:** Hare Krishna, Vaishnavi Patil
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.12241
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12241
- **Categories:** hep-th, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** We study holography for three-dimensional asymptotically flat spacetimes in which the bulk gravitational dynamics is conjectured to be dual to a two-dimensional Carrollian (equivalently BMS) conformal field theory. Such theories are known to arise as ultrarelativistic ($c\rightarrow0$) contractions of relativistic 2d conformal field theories. Under this contraction, the Virasoro algebra becomes the conformal Carrollian algebra and the Brown-Henneaux central charges become $c_L=0$, $c_M=\frac{3}{G}$. In this article, we recover these central charges directly from holography. We first construct the holographic quasilocal stress tensor for asymptotically flat spacetimes carrying Bondi mass and angular momentum. Under BMS$_3$ transformations, the stress tensor acquires an inhomogeneous term, the ``BMS Schwarzian\", and extracting the central charges from it reproduces the algebraic result exactly. We then obtain the action for the boundary BMS Schwarzian modes. Finally, we show that the holographic stress-tensor correlators satisfy the expected Ward identity, while stress-tensor conservation obeys flux-balance laws.
- **Summary:** The authors derive the central charges of flat-space holography (c_L=0, c_M=3/G) directly from the bulk gravitational theory by constructing the holographic quasilocal stress tensor for asymptotically flat 3D spacetimes. The stress tensor transforms with a "BMS Schwarzian" under BMS3 symmetry, from which the central charges are extracted. They further verify that stress-tensor correlators satisfy the expected Ward identities and flux-balance laws, providing a concrete holographic realization of the BMS/CFT correspondence for Carrollian field theories.
- **Relevance:** Strengthens the foundations of flat-space holography, which is less developed than AdS/CFT but crucial for understanding gravity in asymptotically flat spacetimes — the setting for most astrophysical black holes and gravitational wave sources.
- **Tags:** holography, BMS-symmetry, flat-space

## 9. Horizon flux-balance laws in the multiscale perturbation
- **Authors:** Ali Seraj
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.10093
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10093
- **Categories:** hep-th, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** Black-hole event horizons obey a set of evolution equations governing their intrinsic and extrinsic geometry. We study these equations in a \textit{two-timescale} perturbative expansion about a Kerr background and derive strong constraints on the coarse-grained horizon dynamics. At leading order, the coarse-grained linear shear vanishes, while the horizon exhibits an \textit{adiabatic rigidity}: the leading corrections to its angular velocity and inaffinity remain uniform on each horizon cut, while evolving on the slow timescale. We then provide a systematic procedure for transforming a perturbative bulk solution, given for example in Lorenz gauge, to an ingoing Newman--Unti gauge adapted to the horizon, allowing the perturbed horizon geometry to be extracted directly from the bulk metric. Finally, we formulate black-hole conservation laws associated with horizon symmeties, including energy, dynamical entropy, and angular momentum. We expand the charges through second order and their fluxes through third order in perturbation theory, providing a framework for future applications to horizon absorption and backreaction in extreme mass-ratio inspirals.
- **Summary:** A systematic two-timescale perturbative framework for black hole horizon dynamics is developed, expanding around Kerr. The key results are "adiabatic rigidity" of the horizon (leading corrections to angular velocity and inaffinity remain uniform) and a practical gauge-transformation procedure to extract horizon geometry from bulk perturbations in Lorenz gauge. Conservation laws for energy, entropy, and angular momentum are derived through third order, establishing a rigorous foundation for studying horizon absorption and backreaction in extreme mass-ratio inspirals.
- **Relevance:** Provides essential theoretical infrastructure for gravitational wave physics, particularly for extreme mass-ratio inspirals where horizon backreaction is significant. The gauge-transformation technique is a practical tool for numerical relativity applications.
- **Tags:** black-holes, gravitational-waves, perturbation-theory

- **arXiv ID:** 2608.10593
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10593
- **Categories:** hep-th, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** We derive the gravitational radiation generated by a localised matter source in de Sitter spacetime at octupolar order in the multipolar expansion, working in generalised harmonic gauge in the future Poincaré patch. The scalar, vector, and tensor perturbation equations are solved explicitly, taking into account the cosmological tail. This constitutes the first extension of the de Sitter multipolar expansion beyond quadrupolar order. A key technical ingredient is a universal multipole moment differentiation identity, which unifies the relations between multipole moments into a single recursive formula and enables a controlled extension to higher multipoles. Using the symmetric trace-free tensor formalism of Blanchet, Damour, and Iyer, we further develop a systematic translation of the radiative solution into a spherical harmonic basis, and apply it to both the quadrupolar and octupolar metric perturbations. This representation renders the angular structure of the radiation manifest, simplifies the verification of the linearised field equations, and facilitates direct comparison with Bondi-gauge and other perturbative treatments of de Sitter spacetime. Our results constitute a step toward a systematic ``multipolar post-de Sitter formalism\'\'.
- **Summary:** For the first time, gravitational radiation from localized sources in de Sitter spacetime is computed beyond the quadrupole approximation, reaching octupolar order. The authors solve the full perturbation equations (scalar, vector, and tensor) in the future Poincaré patch and develop a recursive multipole differentiation identity that enables systematic extension to even higher orders. The results are translated into spherical harmonics using the Blanchet-Damour-Iyer formalism, paving the way for a complete "multipolar post-de Sitter" framework for gravitational waves in expanding universes.
- **Relevance:** Foundation for precision gravitational wave physics in cosmological backgrounds. As we enter the era of cosmological gravitational wave detection, understanding higher-multipole corrections in de Sitter becomes essential for accurate source modeling.

## 11. Quasinormal modes in effective loop quantum gravity and consequences for isospectrality
- **Authors:** Alejandro García-Quismondo, Guillermo A. Mena Marugán, Alvaro Torres-Caballeros
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.11088
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11088
- **Categories:** gr-qc
- **Abstract:** We investigate the quasinormal mode spectra of axial and polar perturbations about the effective Ashtekar, Olmedo, and Singh black hole geometry within the hybrid approach to loop quantum gravity. We also compare our results with those previously obtained by del-Corral and Olmedo for the dressed metric approach. Starting from mode equations that are straightforward effective counterparts of the classical equations, we compute these spectra by means of a high-order WKB method with Padé resummation. The violation of isospectrality between axial and polar perturbations that was claimed to exist in the dressed metric approach persists with the hybrid quantization, with deviations of the same order of magnitude as in the former effective case, although slightly larger for the hybrid prescription. In both situations, the isospectrality breaking decreases with the cubic root of the squared black hole mass in Planck units, consistently recovering the classical Schwarzschild limit. - **Summary:** The authors compute quasinormal mode spectra for the Ashtekar-Olmedo-Singh loop quantum black hole using the hybrid quantization approach, finding that the previously reported breaking of isospectrality (equality between axial and polar modes) persists here too. The deviation scales as (Planck mass / black hole mass)^(2/3), vanishing for large black holes as expected. A careful WKB validity analysis identifies where the method is reliable and where it fails, providing a benchmark for future loop quantum black hole spectroscopy studies.
- **Relevance:** Loop quantum black holes are one of the most concrete quantum gravity predictions for astrophysical objects. Isospectrality breaking could serve as an observational discriminator between LQG and classical black holes, making this work relevant for gravitational wave asteroseismology.

- **Categories:** hep-th, quant-ph
- **Abstract:** We construct a toy model of an evaporating black hole using approximately local degrees of freedom acting on ``overlapping\" qubits in which a version of black hole complementarity arises naturally. The operators corresponding to the radiation and the interior are identified as two distinct representations of the same fundamental algebra, thereby preventing the exact factorization of the Hilbert space into interior and exterior and avoiding the conventional no-cloning violations. - **Relevance:** Proposes an alternative resolution to the black hole information paradox that preserves unitarity without invoking firewalls. The "overlap" mechanism could inspire new approaches to quantum gravity where exact locality is emergent rather than fundamental.

## 14. Classical fractons with cosmological fixed points
- **Authors:** Akash Singh, Dileep P. Jatkar, S. L. Sondhi et al.
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.07672
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07672
- **Categories:** gr-qc, hep-th, physics.class-ph, cond-mat.stat-mech, cond-mat.str-el
- **Abstract:** Classical fractons are Hamiltonian systems that can develop attractors after projection onto configuration or shape variables, although the full phase space admits none. We study a scale-invariant, dipole-conserving two-parameter family of fracton Hamiltonians $H_{\alpha,\beta}$. By separating coordinates into scale and shape, we obtain autonomous shape dynamics that admit fixed points which leave a purely scale evolution of the form $R(t)\propto |t|^{\alpha/(\alpha-\beta)}$. The shape fixed points, which determine the distribution of the expanding particles, are central configurations of power-law Riesz potentials. The distinguished model $(\alpha,\beta)=(-2,1)$ is unique: its scale evolution takes the Einstein-de Sitter form $R(t)\propto |t|^{2/3}$, its fixed-point equation is the equal-mass Newtonian central-configuration, its large-$N$ distribution is a homogeneous ball, and its homothetic trajectories admit a zero-energy Newtonian gravitational dual. The fixed points are locally stable, and simulations at moderate $N$ approach them from random initial data. Large $N$ simulations reveal a richer class of fixed-points: bound clusters of approximately fixed physical size retain internal motion, while their centers approach unequal-mass Newtonian central configurations and preserve large-scale homogeneity. A scale-separation conjecture yields an effective unequal-mass fracton dynamics for the centers and a corresponding zero-energy Newtonian gravitational dual. Trajectories generically exhibit a bidirectional arrow of time: scale and shape complexity grow away from a Janus point, while Boltzmann entropy grows logarithmically. Together, these features reproduce the salient structure of a flat matter-dominated cosmology. In the distinguished fracton model, all these cosmological analogues emerge as attractor properties, making it a toy model for cosmological dynamics without fine-tuning.
- **Summary:** A remarkable connection between fracton physics and cosmology is uncovered: a specific dipole-conserving fracton Hamiltonian reproduces the Einstein-de Sitter expansion (R ~ t^(2/3)) as an attractor, without any fine-tuning. The system exhibits a bidirectional arrow of time from a Janus point, with large-N simulations showing cluster formation that mimics large-scale structure. The distinguished model has a zero-energy Newtonian gravitational dual, suggesting that cosmological dynamics might emerge from condensed matter-like systems with conservation laws.
- **Relevance:** Fascinating bridge between condensed matter (fractons) and cosmology. The emergence of FLRW-like expansion from a non-gravitational Hamiltonian system raises deep questions about whether cosmic dynamics could have roots in more fundamental statistical/quantum mechanisms.
- **Tags:** fractons, cosmology, emergent-gravity
