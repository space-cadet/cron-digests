## 1. Quantum-Corrected Black Holes in Higher Dimensions: From Gravitational Collapse to Remnants, WGC-Like Behavior and Accretion Signatures
- **Authors:** Saeed Noori Gashti, Umair Anwar, Abdul Jawad et al.
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.16369
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16369
- **Categories:** hep-th, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** We study the gravitational collapse of a homogeneous dust sphere in higher-dimensional spacetime, with the loop quantum gravity correction entered through a modified Friedmann equation. Its most striking feature is an endpoint: at a finite horizon radius the temperature vanishes and evaporation stops, leaving a remnant. Reading \(\alpha\) as an effective charge, we find behavior reminiscent of the Weak Gravity Conjecture (WGC), although no \(U(1)\) gauge field is present in the model. Solving the degeneracy conditions with the untruncated metric function, rather than with a mass expansion that is not uniform near the endpoint, we obtain closed-form expressions for the remnant radius and mass in \(D=4,5,6,7\). The unscaled ratio \(\alpha/M_{\text{remnant}}^2\) is constant only in four dimensions and grows as \(\alpha^{4-D}\) elsewhere, but it carries dimension \(L^{8-2D}\), so that growth is an artifact of the units. The dimensionless combination \(\tilde{\mathcal{R}} = (\alpha/M^2) r_0^{2D-8}\) settles instead on a finite \(\alpha\)-independent number in every dimension, decreasing from \(27/64\) in four dimensions to \(13824/(15625\pi^4)\) in seven. We also deform the quantum parameter, \(\alpha \to \alpha + \varepsilon\), and evaluate \(-T(\partial S/\partial \varepsilon)_M\) in the remnant limit. Because \((\partial M/\partial r)_\alpha\) vanishes there, the fixed-entropy derivative reduces exactly to a fixed-radius one, and the resulting combination \(\mathcal{U}\) is finite in each dimension but depends on \(\varepsilon\) except for \(D=5\). What is invariant is the product \(\mathcal{R}\,\mathcal{U}\,M_{\text{remnant}} = (D-3)/2\), which we verify numerically. Lastly, we study Bondi accretion onto the quantum-corrected black hole and obtain the Eddington luminosity for each dimension.
- **Summary:** The authors incorporate loop quantum gravity corrections into the gravitational collapse of a homogeneous dust sphere in spacetimes of dimension \(D = 4\) through \(7\), using a modified Friedmann equation. Rather than relying on truncated mass expansions that break down near the endpoint, they solve the full degeneracy conditions analytically and obtain exact closed-form expressions for the remnant mass and radius. A key finding is a dimensionally universal invariant \(\mathcal{R}\,\mathcal{U}\,M_{\text{remnant}} = (D-3)/2\), verified numerically across dimensions, alongside a Weak-Gravity-Conjecture-like behavior arising without any \(U(1)\) gauge field.
- **Relevance:** This work directly extends loop quantum gravity phenomenology to higher dimensions, deriving exact remnant properties that are central to resolving the black hole information paradox in LQG. The closed-form results for remnant mass and radius, and the unexpected WGC-like structure, provide concrete predictions that could inform future work on Planck-scale physics and black hole evaporation.
- **Tags:** Loop Quantum Gravity, Black Hole Remnants, Higher Dimensions

## 2. Extremal non-rotating black holes have no fermionic Love
- **Authors:** Xiankai Pang, Yu Tian, Hongbao Zhang
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.15479
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15479
- **Categories:** hep-th, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** The static tidal Love numbers (TLNs) of \(4\)-dimensional black holes vanish for bosonic perturbations but are generically nonzero for fermions, with rare exceptions. In this paper, we show that for static, spherically symmetric black holes, fermionic TLNs vanish if and only if the black hole is extremal, in the sense that its horizon is degenerate. This follows from a closed formula for the static fermionic TLN of any asymptotically flat black hole, obtained by solving the static massless Dirac equation exactly on an arbitrary such spacetime and imposing regularity at the horizon. As applications, we analyze the Simpson--Visser regular black hole and the loop-quantum-gravity remnant black holes, whose extremal configurations lead to vanishing fermionic yet nonvanishing bosonic TLNs.
- **Summary:** By solving the static massless Dirac equation exactly on arbitrary asymptotically flat black hole spacetimes, the authors derive a closed formula for fermionic tidal Love numbers. They prove that fermionic TLNs vanish precisely when the horizon is degenerate—that is, for extremal black holes—while remaining generically nonzero otherwise. The result is applied to both Simpson–Visser regular black holes and loop quantum gravity remnant black holes, revealing that their extremal limits exhibit vanishing fermionic but nonvanishing bosonic Love numbers.
- **Relevance:** The Love number analysis of LQG remnant black holes is particularly significant: it provides a novel observable signature that could distinguish quantum-gravitational remnants from classical black holes. The closed formula for fermionic TLNs is a powerful technical tool that may find broader application in quantum gravity phenomenology and gravitational-wave physics.
- **Tags:** Black Hole Physics, Tidal Love Numbers, Loop Quantum Gravity

## 3. Quantum Stability of the Fuzzy Sphere Black Hole Horizon
- **Authors:** Chong-Sun Chu
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.16374
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16374
- **Categories:** hep-ph, hep-th, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** We establish the perturbative stability of the fuzzy-sphere black-hole horizon in the large-$N$ matrix quantum mechanics of \cite{Chu:2024qil}. Large-$N$ counting shows that the leading quantum correction to the fluctuation spectrum is the planar bosonic one-loop contribution, while higher-loop bosonic effects and the leading fermionic one-loop contribution are parametrically suppressed. The classical spectrum contains a tachyonic \((1,2)\) mode and a marginal \((2,3)\) mode; we show that both acquire positive quantum curvature and are stabilized. For the general spectrum, we find that a factorization structure of the Hessian controls the leading quantum curvature and renders it non-negative. Quantum effects dominate the stabilization of generic low-angular-momentum modes, whereas classical curvature dominates at high angular momentum. The two effects become comparable in the crossover regime \(L \sim \sqrt{N}\), where both must be retained. Under smoothness and non-saturation assumptions supported by finite-$N$ numerical tests, positivity of the quantum fluctuation spectrum is thus established for sufficiently large but finite \(N\). This local curvature stability is compatible with non-perturbative monopole tunneling. Intriguingly, the same \(L\sim\sqrt{N}\) mesoscopic angular momentum range also dominates the tunneling process, suggesting a distinguished IR--UV crossover regime in the quantum dynamics of the black-hole horizon.
- **Summary:** The author demonstrates perturbative quantum stability of a fuzzy-sphere black hole horizon modeled within large-$N$ matrix quantum mechanics. Classical instabilities—a tachyonic mode and a marginal mode—are shown to be cured by planar bosonic one-loop quantum corrections, with a factorization structure of the Hessian ensuring non-negative curvature for the full spectrum. The stabilization mechanism is dominated by quantum effects at low angular momentum and by classical curvature at high angular momentum, with a notable crossover at \(L \sim \sqrt{N}\) that coincides with the regime governing non-perturbative monopole tunneling.
- **Relevance:** This work bridges matrix-model approaches to quantum gravity with black hole horizon stability, a problem central to understanding the quantum nature of spacetime. The fuzzy-sphere construction and its large-$N$ stability analysis offer a concrete framework where quantum gravitational effects on horizon dynamics can be computed explicitly, with direct parallels to loop quantum gravity's own treatment of horizon degrees of freedom.
- **Tags:** Quantum Gravity, Black Hole Stability, Matrix Models

## 4. On the (Non)Unitarity with respect to the Clock of a Dynamical Local Observer and the Einstein Equivalence Principle
- **Authors:** Kengo Shimada
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.16762
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16762
- **Categories:** hep-th, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** We investigate the unitarity of quantum evolution relative to an internal time defined by a local observer's clock. The observer is modeled as a relativistic particle carrying both a clock and a matter-field detector, analyzed first on a fixed curved background and subsequently within a fully diffeomorphism-invariant theory of dynamical gravity. In the former case, we find that evolution with respect to the internal clock time is generally nonunitary, implying a violation of the Einstein equivalence principle at the quantum level. In contrast, in the latter case, diffeomorphism invariance allows us to adopt observer-centric coordinates without loss of generality. On the resulting partially-reduced phase space, one of the diffeomorphism generators becomes linear in the clock Hamiltonian, generating a relational evolution that is consistent with the remaining diffeomorphism constraints. Assuming that an effective quantum field theory exists to be consistent with the diffeomorphism invariance, these features ensure unitary evolution relative to the internal clock, thereby preserving the equivalence principle even in the quantum regime. Our results highlight the fundamental role of diffeomorphism invariance in shaping relational unitary evolution from the perspective of a local observer.
- **Summary:** The author examines whether quantum evolution remains unitary when time is defined by a local observer's internal clock, modeling the observer as a relativistic particle equipped with a clock and detector. On a fixed curved background, evolution relative to the internal clock is generically nonunitary, signaling a quantum-level violation of the Einstein equivalence principle; however, in a fully diffeomorphism-invariant theory of dynamical gravity, relational evolution generated by a constraint-linear clock Hamiltonian restores unitarity. The result underscores that diffeomorphism invariance is not merely a technical redundancy but a physical requirement for preserving the equivalence principle at the quantum scale.
- **Relevance:** The problem of time and relational observables is a foundational issue in quantum gravity, shared by canonical approaches including loop quantum gravity. This work provides a rigorous argument that diffeomorphism invariance is the key ingredient ensuring unitary relational evolution, offering conceptual support for the relational frameworks employed in LQG and related approaches.
- **Tags:** Quantum Gravity, Problem of Time, Diffeomorphism Invariance

## 5. New metric useful for studies of gravitational radiation from accelerating black holes of type D with any cosmological constant
- **Authors:** Jiri Podolsky
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.14919
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14919
- **Categories:** hep-th, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** We apply a convenient parametrization of all Plebanski-Demianski black holes of algebraic type D with the Kerr rotation, NUT twist, acceleration, electric and magnetic charges, and any value of the cosmological constant \(\Lambda\), recently found by Astorino. This represents a complete class of exact solutions to the Einstein-Maxwell-\(\Lambda\) equations such that the (non-null) electromagnetic field is aligned with both the (geodesic, shear-free, double) principal null directions of the Weyl tensor. We derive a novel appropriate metric form and the corresponding canonical frames naturally adapted to both the de~Sitter-like (\(\Lambda>0\)) and the anti-de~Sitter-like (\(\Lambda<0\)) conformal infinity, and we demonstrate that these geometric objects are useful for investigation of radiation properties. In particular, using the Fernandez-Alvarez-Senovilla criterion, it confirms that the gravitational radiation is present if, and only if, these black holes are accelerating.
- **Summary:** Podolsky constructs a new metric form for the full Plebański-Demiański class of type-D black holes—encompassing Kerr rotation, NUT charge, acceleration, electromagnetic charges, and arbitrary cosmological constant—using a recent parametrization by Astorino. The novel coordinate system and canonical frames are tailored to both de Sitter and anti-de Sitter conformal infinity, enabling a rigorous analysis of gravitational radiation. Applying the Fernández-Álvarez-Senovilla criterion, the work confirms that gravitational radiation is emitted if and only if the black hole is accelerating.
- **Relevance:** Exact solutions in general relativity remain essential benchmarks for testing quantum gravity predictions, and the Plebański-Demiański family is among the most important. The new metric's natural adaptation to conformal infinity provides a cleaner framework for studying asymptotic radiation properties, which could be valuable for comparing semiclassical and quantum gravitational descriptions of black hole radiation.
- **Tags:** Black Hole Physics, Exact Solutions, Gravitational Radiation

## 6. Exceptional points of the Schwarzschild spectrum under local potential perturbation: The Princess and the Pea
- **Authors:** Mohamed Ould El Hadj, Sam R. Dolan
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.16283
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16283
- **Categories:** astro-ph.HE, hep-th, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** Black-hole quasinormal modes (QNMs) may coalesce at exceptional points (EPs) in the frequency spectrum. We study EPs and their phenomenology for gravitational perturbations of a Schwarzschild black hole whose Regge-Wheeler potential is perturbed by a localized delta-function of amplitude \(\epsilon\) at radius \(r_0\). For real \(\epsilon\), we find infinite families of discrete EPs. Each family approaches a distinct unperturbed QNM overtone frequency as \(r_0\) grows large. Under perturbation at fixed \(r_0\), we find that neighboring QNM overtones migrate in the complex plane to meet at these EPs, with the higher overtone moving further. We derive large-\(r_0\) asymptotics, finding that EPs have (asymptotically) equal spacing in the tortoise coordinate, and that EP amplitudes \(\epsilon\) can be exceedingly small, decaying exponentially according to a rule governed by the complex frequency of the unperturbed QNM. Using quadratic approximations around EPs and high-precision numerical methods, we investigate a range of phenomena in the model: square-root splittings, avoided crossings and hyperbolae, hysteresis and geometric phases generated by encircling an EP, adiabatic invariants, deformed Cassini ovals and cusp-like trajectories, as well as the lemniscate geometry of the excitation factors. We then replace the delta-function perturbation by a finite-width Gaussian, verify the persistence of the EP structure, and show that varying the width extends isolated EPs into exceptional lines in parameter space. Finally, we study the response to initial data and find that, although a weak perturbation can substantially reconfigure the QNM spectrum, the corresponding time-domain response remains perturbative. This contrast is captured by a re-interpretation of the tale of The Princess and the Pea.
- **Summary:** The authors introduce a toy model in which a localized delta-function perturbation to the Schwarzschild Regge-Wheeler potential generates infinite families of exceptional points where quasinormal modes coalesce. They derive asymptotic formulae showing that these EPs are exponentially sensitive to the perturbation amplitude and exhibit rich spectral phenomenology—including square-root splittings, geometric phases, and hysteresis—while the time-domain ringdown remains perturbatively stable. Remarkably, replacing the delta-function with a finite-width Gaussian broadens isolated EPs into continuous exceptional lines in parameter space.
- **Relevance:** Quasinormal modes are among the most important observational probes of black hole geometry, and exceptional points represent a novel regime where the standard mode-sum description breaks down. Understanding how EPs respond to environmental perturbations is directly relevant to interpreting gravitational-wave ringdown signals and to assessing the stability of quantum-corrected black hole geometries.
- **Tags:** Black Hole Physics, Quasinormal Modes, Exceptional Points

## 7. Quasi-local Hamiltonian for generalized Kerr-Schild black holes in the Iyer-Wald formalism
- **Authors:** M. A. Jaraba, T. L. Campos, M. C. Baldiotti
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.16534
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16534
- **Categories:** hep-th, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** Grounded in the covariant phase space approach of the Iyer-Wald formalism, we propose a quasi-local Hamiltonian for generalized Kerr-Schild (GKS) black holes. Evaluated for spherically symmetric spacetimes over a flat Minkowski background, our formulation recovers the Misner-Sharp mass. Because this framework relies solely on the GKS structure, it provides a straightforward extension of the Misner-Sharp mass to rotating geometries. We further extend our analysis to asymptotically anti-de Sitter (AdS) black holes, where a renormalization procedure to subtract the background AdS energy naturally emerges. By employing this technique, we establish a geometric quasi-local formulation of Kerr-AdS thermodynamics.
- **Summary:** Using the Iyer-Wald covariant phase space formalism, the authors construct a quasi-local Hamiltonian valid for the entire class of generalized Kerr-Schild black holes. In spherical symmetry the construction reduces to the familiar Misner-Sharp mass, while for rotating geometries it yields a natural generalization without additional assumptions; applied to asymptotically AdS spacetimes, the framework automatically produces a background-subtraction renormalization and a geometric formulation of Kerr-AdS thermodynamics.
- **Relevance:** Quasi-local energy definitions are central to loop quantum gravity, where the isolated horizon framework and quasi-local Hamiltonians underpin the derivation of black hole entropy. This work extends quasi-local mass constructions to a broad class of rotating black holes and establishes a clean thermodynamic framework for Kerr-AdS, providing tools that may inform quantum gravitational descriptions of rotating horizon boundaries.
- **Tags:** Black Hole Thermodynamics, Quasi-Local Energy, Kerr-Schild Geometry

## 8. Quasinormal frequencies and greybody factors for axial perturbations of dilaton-Euler-Heisenberg de Sitter black holes
- **Authors:** Ming Zhang, Guo-Xin Chen, Xufen Zhang et al.
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.15111
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15111
- **Categories:** hep-th, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** We investigate the quasinormal modes (QNMs) and greybody factors of dilaton-Euler-Heisenberg (dEH) de Sitter (dS) black holes in string-inspired Euler-Heisenberg gravity. Since the axial gravitational and electromagnetic perturbations decouple, we treat them independently. By applying the asymptotic iteration method (AIM) alongside a sixth-order WKB approximation, we compute the quasinormal frequencies and find excellent agreement between the two approaches. We also find that the QNM spectra depend sensitively on the magnetic charge \(Q_{\text{m}}\), cosmological constant \(\Lambda\), and nonlinear coupling \(\epsilon\), with a notable topological anomaly appearing in the electromagnetic frequency trajectories. Additionally, larger values of \(Q_{\text{m}}\) or the multipole number \(l\) generally suppress wave transmission, while the electromagnetic sector with \(\epsilon=1\) exhibits an anomalous response.
- **Summary:** The authors compute quasinormal modes and greybody factors for axial perturbations of dilaton-Euler-Heisenberg black holes in de Sitter space, using both the asymptotic iteration method and sixth-order WKB. The spectra reveal a sensitive dependence on magnetic charge, cosmological constant, and nonlinear coupling, including a topological anomaly in electromagnetic frequency trajectories. Larger magnetic charge or multipole number typically suppresses transmission, though the electromagnetic sector shows an anomalous enhancement at unit nonlinear coupling.
- **Relevance:** Black hole perturbation theory in modified gravity theories provides crucial benchmarks for distinguishing quantum gravity effects from classical extensions of general relativity. The string-inspired Euler-Heisenberg framework and its interplay with de Sitter cosmology are particularly timely given observational interest in both primordial black holes and dark energy.
- **Tags:** Black Hole Perturbations, Modified Gravity, Quasinormal Modes

## 9. Zero-point theorems in quantum many-body physics
- **Authors:** Yuan Yao
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.15542
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15542
- **Categories:** math-ph, cond-mat.str-el, quant-ph, hep-th, cond-mat.stat-mech
- **Abstract:** We propose several zero-point type arguments based on the inevitable zero point(s) of a spectral gap in the quantum spin system phase diagrams in various dimensions. We consider multi-parameter families of Hamiltonian extending the conventional zero-point theorem that includes only one parameter. Analogously to the zero-point theorem, we only impose model-independent transformation relations along the parameter boundary, rather than specifying any low-energy dynamics or response. We further give a series of conjectures, which generalize our statements in a uniform way. Our results give powerful and universal model-independent constraints on the possible relevant operators for critical phenomena in quantum spin models in arbitrary high dimensions.
- **Summary:** The paper generalizes the zero-point theorem—an exact constraint on spectral gap closure in quantum spin systems—from single-parameter to multi-parameter families of Hamiltonians. By relying solely on model-independent symmetry transformations along parameter boundaries, the author derives universal constraints on allowed critical operators without invoking any specific low-energy effective theory. A series of conjectures extends these results to arbitrary dimensions, offering a powerful tool for ruling out candidate phases in quantum many-body systems.
- **Relevance:** The zero-point perspective on quantum phase transitions offers a novel, model-independent approach to constraining critical behavior in strongly correlated systems. While rooted in condensed matter physics, the emphasis on spectral gaps and universal constraints resonates with broader questions in quantum gravity about phase transitions in quantum geometry and the emergence of spacetime from quantum degrees of freedom.
- **Tags:** Many-Body Physics, Quantum Phase Transitions, Critical Phenomena

## 10. Non-exotic traversable wormholes in Einstein-Chern-Simons gravity
- **Authors:** Mauricio Cataldo, Fernando Goméz, Paola Meza et al.
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.16723
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16723
- **Categories:** hep-th, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** We investigate traversable wormhole solutions in five-dimensional Einstein-Chern-Simons (EChS) gravity, a gauge-theoretic extension of General Relativity that introduces a higher-curvature correction parametrized by the combination \(\alpha l^2\), where \(\alpha\) is a dimensionless coupling and \(l\) is a length scale. Working with the Morris-Thorne metric and an anisotropic fluid, we derive exact expressions for the energy density, radial pressure, and lateral pressure at the wormhole throat. We show that the radial Null Energy Condition (NEC) is satisfied at the throat if and only if \(\alpha l^2\leq-r_0^2\), being saturated at \(\alpha l^2=-r_0^2\) and strictly satisfied for \(\alpha l^2<-r_0^2\), independently of the shape function. In the latter regime, the energy density is strictly positive and the full NEC, Weak, Strong, and Dominant Energy Conditions can be simultaneously satisfied. These results are established analytically for arbitrary shape functions and illustrated concretely for the power-law family \(b(r)=r_0(r_0/r)^n\), \(\Phi=0\), for which all four standard energy conditions are satisfied at the throat for all \(n>0\) when the EChS coupling is sufficiently negative. We further show that the radial pressure is negative and geometrically fixed at the throat, independently of \(\alpha\), \(b(r)\), and \(\Phi(r)\). Using the Volume Integral Quantifier (VIQ), we establish that the EChS correction reduces the magnitude of the negative integrated NEC contribution relative to GR, and derive an exact closed-form critical coupling \(\alpha l^2_{\rm crit}\) at which the VIQ vanishes. The hierarchy \(\alpha l^2_{\rm crit}<-r_0^2<0\) shows that NEC satisfaction at the throat and a non-negative global VIQ are compatible but distinct conditions, both achievable within EChS gravity. All results reduce continuously to those of GR in the limit \(l\to0\), confirming the consistency of the framework.
- **Summary:** The authors demonstrate that five-dimensional Einstein-Chern-Simons gravity admits traversable wormholes satisfying all standard energy conditions—including the Null Energy Condition—without requiring exotic matter. For a broad class of shape functions, the higher-curvature correction parametrized by \(\alpha l^2\) enables NEC satisfaction at the throat when the coupling is sufficiently negative, and the Volume Integral Quantifier reveals a critical coupling at which the global exoticity vanishes. Remarkably, the radial pressure at the throat is fixed purely by geometry, independent of the specific matter content or EChS parameters.
- **Relevance:** Traversable wormholes without exotic matter have long been sought as theoretical laboratories for quantum gravity, and this work achieves them within a well-defined higher-curvature extension of general relativity. The analytical control over energy conditions and the explicit critical coupling for global exoticity provide a concrete setting for studying how quantum gravitational corrections might modify causal structure without violating classical energy bounds.
- **Tags:** Wormholes, Modified Gravity, Energy Conditions
