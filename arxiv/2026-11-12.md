## 1. Solving the SU(2) BFSS model
**Authors:** Kabir Bajaj, Henry W. Lin  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14512v1  
**Categories:** hep-th
- **Abstract:** We numerically solve the ground state wavefunction of SU(2) BFSS matrix quantum mechanics. We compare ground correlators against islands obtained using the matrix bootstrap and find agreement up to five significant figures. The power law tail of this wavefunction determines the three-point amplitude, which agrees with the computation of Herderschee and Maldacena. Beyond the ground state, we extract the non-perturbative four-point scattering amplitude using Lüscher's method. In the appropriate regime, we find agreement with the Born-Oppenheimer approximation that yields the well-known $v^4/r^7$ interaction. We also find explicit bound states with positive energies in various SO(9) irreps. These are closely related to the bound states whose existence was recently discovered by OpenAI. Finally, we identify metastable resonances in the spectrum.
- **Summary:** Bajaj and Lin numerically solve the SU(2) BFSS ground-state wavefunction and benchmark its correlators against matrix-bootstrap islands to five significant figures. Its asymptotic tail reproduces a known three-point amplitude, while Lüscher analysis extracts a nonperturbative four-point amplitude matching the Born–Oppenheimer $v^4/r^7$ interaction; the calculation also reveals positive-energy SO(9) bound states and metastable resonances. This supplies unusually detailed nonperturbative data for matrix quantum mechanics and its holographic interpretation.
**RELEVANCE:** A technically substantial matrix-model calculation connecting numerical quantum mechanics, scattering, and holographic quantum gravity.  
**TAGS:** `BFSS matrix model` `nonperturbative scattering` `quantum gravity`

## 2. Nonperturbative string dynamics in a (2+1)D lattice gauge theory on a superconducting quantum processor
**Authors:** Sanziana Stelea, Anthony Gandon, Francesco Tacchino, Jad C. Halimeh, Ivano Tavernelli  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14484v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph
- **Abstract:** Electric flux strings underpin confinement in gauge theories. In two spatial dimensions, their evolution involves competing processes of charge motion, pair creation, and transverse deformation. Accessing these dynamics beyond perturbative limits challenges classical approaches, as entanglement growth restricts the times accessible to tensor-network simulations. Here we use quantum simulation to investigate a (2+1)D Z2 lattice gauge theory with dynamical matter and an explicit magnetic plaquette interaction, spanning strongly confined and nonperturbative deconfined regimes. Using 63 superconducting qubits, including 12 ancillas, we evolve initially localized strings with static or dynamical endpoint charges. Spatially resolved measurements reveal rapid charge and flux spreading in the deconfined regime, enhanced matter occupation in the string interior near the pair-creation resonance, and suppressed spreading at strong electric fields. String-path occupations further probe transverse rearrangements between configurations connected by plaquette operations. Retaining separate matter and gauge degrees of freedom makes local Gauss-law constraints directly accessible for error detection. Combining these constraints with ancilla checks and noise rescaling, we measure dynamics in circuits containing up to 15 second-order Trotter steps. Our results establish quantum simulation as a practical approach to exploring nonperturbative string dynamics in two spatial dimensions, with gauge redundancy serving as an experimental resource.
- **Summary:** Using 63 superconducting qubits, the authors simulate a $(2+1)$-dimensional $mathbb Z_2$ gauge theory with dynamical matter across confined and deconfined regimes. Spatially resolved string measurements show charge/flux spreading, pair-creation enhancement, and field-controlled suppression, while explicit matter and gauge registers expose Gauss-law violations for error detection; ancilla checks and noise rescaling extend the experiment to 15 second-order Trotter steps. The work demonstrates a practical hardware route to nonperturbative gauge-string dynamics.
**RELEVANCE:** Directly combines quantum computing with lattice gauge theory, with experimentally useful treatment of gauge constraints.  
**TAGS:** `lattice gauge theory` `quantum simulation` `confinement`

## 3. The Higgs boson, naturalness, and the Universe
**Authors:** Mikhail Shaposhnikov  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14370v1  
**Categories:** hep-ph, hep-th
- **Abstract:** The Higgs boson -- the only known fundamental scalar particle -- may have profound implications for both particle physics and cosmology. This review examines how the Higgs field may have influenced the Universe from its earliest moments to its present state. The synthesis organizes how one measured scalar can influence physics from the early Universe through present vacuum stability, making clear which open problems are particle-physics puzzles and which are cosmological opportunities. It is a useful map of the Higgs-centered interface between high-energy theory and cosmology.

## 4. Multiloop Unitarity from Airy Zeros
**Authors:** Laurentiu Rodina  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14359v1  
**Categories:** hep-th
- **Abstract:** Hidden zeros are non-trivial cancellations that make amplitudes or integrands vanish on specific kinematic loci away from propagator poles. They have been shown to uniquely determine tree amplitudes and one-loop surface integrands in $\Tr(φ^3)$ theory, without assuming factorization. Here we extend both the zeros and the uniqueness results to two, three and four loops at arbitrary multiplicity. We show that two types of zeros naturally enter the all-loop problem. First, tree-level zeros extend to all loop orders and fix relative weights within families of subgraphs attached to vacuum ``loop cores.'' We then show that Kontsevich's matrix Airy function can be used to generate a rich structure of loop level zeros that fix the relative weights of the loop cores themselves. The relative factorization and cut data required by unitarity therefore follow from the combination of tree and Airy loop zeros. These results bring within reach the emergence of unitarity to all orders in perturbation theory.
- **Summary:** Rodina extends hidden-zero and uniqueness methods in $\mathrm{Tr}(φ^3)$ theory from trees and one loop to two, three, and four loops at arbitrary multiplicity. Tree zeros fix weights within subgraph families, while zeros generated by Kontsevich’s matrix Airy function determine the relative weights of vacuum loop cores; together they recover the factorization and cut data required by unitarity. This suggests an algebraic route toward deriving all-order perturbative unitarity without taking factorization as an input.
**RELEVANCE:** A novel amplitudes approach to perturbative QFT structure, relevant to Deepak’s high-energy-theory interests.  
**TAGS:** `scattering amplitudes` `multiloop unitarity` `matrix Airy function`

## 5. Edge quantum weight of finite Chern flakes and its topological floor
**Authors:** Rohit Narula  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14271v1  
**Categories:** cond-mat.mes-hall, quant-ph
- **Abstract:** The zero-point energy of a circuit measures the polarization fluctuation of an insulator coupled to it. In a crystal this is the bulk quantum weight $K_\mathrm{bulk}$, which the Chern number $C$ bounds below by $|C|$. We show that a finite Chern flake adds an edge term that survives as the flake grows. The low-frequency transitions of its chiral edge carry a dipole weight, the sum of their squared dipole matrix elements, $K_\mathrm{edge}\geq|C|$ per unit area, a floor that follows from the positive absorption of both circular polarizations by every transition and the known cancellation of circular dichroism between edge and bulk. A weakly coupled circuit slower than the edge modes therefore reads $K_\mathrm{bulk}+K_\mathrm{edge}$, averaged over two field directions, while a faster one below the bulk gap approaches $K_\mathrm{bulk}$, so which quantum weight is measured depends on whether the flake is made large or the circuit slow first. Exact free-fermion calculations on lattice flakes bear out the floor and this dependence on the circuit frequency. The result holds at weak coupling, which a growing flake at fixed coupling eventually leaves. Above the floor, the edge weight of one channel is a number fixed by the shape of the boundary and the edge velocities, which square and rectangular lattice flakes follow to within 0.003. Each further edge channel adds its own such number, while the circular dichroism of the edge counts only the net chirality.
- **Summary:** Narula shows that a finite Chern flake contributes a nonvanishing edge dipole weight even as its area grows, with a topological lower bound $K_\mathrm{edge}\ge |C|$ per unit area. Exact free-fermion flakes establish that a slow circuit measures bulk plus edge weight, whereas a faster subgap circuit isolates the bulk, so the thermodynamic and low-frequency limits do not commute. Boundary shape and edge velocities control the excess above the topological floor, while circular dichroism measures net chirality.
**RELEVANCE:** Connects topological edge physics, measurable response, and quantum fluctuations in finite systems.  
**TAGS:** `Chern insulators` `edge states` `topological response`

## 6. Disorder-Induced Logarithmic Spreading and Localization of Quantum Fluctuations
**Authors:** Ang Yang, Jingxuan Zhang, Lei Ying  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14200v1  
**Categories:** cond-mat.dis-nn, cond-mat.quant-gas, quant-ph
- **Abstract:** Disorder can localize quantum particles, even in the presence of interactions, but its influence on quantum fluctuations beyond mean--field dynamics remains poorly understood. Here, we investigate the propagation of quantum fluctuations in weakly interacting disordered bosonic systems by comparing the Bose--Hubbard model with the Gross--Pitaevskii equation. We find that, in contrast to ballistic spreading in the disorder--free system, quantum fluctuations exhibit logarithmic spreading at intermediate times under both random and quasiperiodic disorder, before eventually saturating. The spreading rate decreases algebraically with increasing disorder strength, and this behavior persists across different particle numbers and extends to two dimensions. Using a time--dependent Bogoliubov--de Gennes framework, we identify the localized mean--field wavefunction as a time--dependent pairing background that generates quantum fluctuations. The exponential suppression of fluctuation--generation rates with distance provides a physical mechanism for the logarithmically advancing fluctuation front. Our results reveal a distinct regime of disorder--controlled quantum--fluctuation dynamics and establish disorder as a means of controlling their spreading.
- **Summary:** Comparing the disordered Bose–Hubbard model with Gross–Pitaevskii dynamics, the authors find that quantum fluctuations spread logarithmically at intermediate times rather than ballistically, then saturate. A time-dependent Bogoliubov–de Gennes treatment identifies the localized mean-field state as a pairing background and explains the front through exponentially suppressed distant fluctuation generation; the trend survives random/quasiperiodic disorder, varying particle number, and two dimensions. Disorder thus becomes a controllable knob for fluctuation propagation beyond mean field.
**RELEVANCE:** A clear many-body result on localization and nonequilibrium quantum dynamics.  
**TAGS:** `many-body localization` `Bogoliubov theory` `quantum fluctuations`

## 7. Landau Theory of Black-Brane Phase Transitions from Kasner Singularities
**Authors:** Hong-Da Lyu  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14075v1  
**Categories:** hep-th, cond-mat.str-el, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** We develop a Landau theory of a continuous black-brane phase transition using the geometry of the black-hole interior. The normal phase has a Reissner--Nordström Cauchy horizon, whereas the scalarized phase ends at a Kasner singularity. A conserved radial charge determines the grand potential from either endpoint and permits a direct phase comparison. At the critical point, we show analytically that the quadratic correction to the conserved radial charge vanishes. Using the inverse Kasner scalar velocity as an interior order parameter, we then construct a Landau potential whose coefficients are determined entirely from the terminal singularity. The Iyer--Wald first law relates this potential to the conventional boundary Landau theory and fixes its quartic coefficient. For two models with different scalar self-interactions, the resulting theory predicts the critical scaling of the Kasner order parameter and the grand-potential difference between the phases, in agreement with fully backreacted numerical solutions.
- **Summary:** Lyu constructs a Landau description of a continuous black-brane transition using an interior order parameter, the inverse Kasner scalar velocity. A conserved radial charge supplies the grand potential, while the Iyer–Wald first law relates the singularity-based potential to boundary thermodynamics and fixes its quartic term; fully backreacted solutions in two models confirm the predicted critical scaling and free-energy difference. The result turns Kasner interior data into quantitative phase-transition information.
**RELEVANCE:** A striking bridge between black-hole interiors, holographic thermodynamics, and critical phenomena.  
**TAGS:** `black-brane transitions` `Kasner singularities` `holography`

## 8. Non-singular cosmology from effective LQG-inspired black holes
**Authors:** V. F. C. Belo, F. G. S. Menezes, S. M. Rupf, I. P. R. Baranov, H. A. Borges  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14001v1  
**Categories:** gr-qc, hep-th
- **Abstract:** We propose a non-singular primordial cosmology derived from matching an effective Loop Quantum Gravity-inspired black hole geometry to an expanding Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker universe via Darmois-Israel junction conditions. The resulting modified Friedmann equation replaces the singularity with a quantum bounce in the Planckian regime. Mapping the surface energy-momentum tensor of the dynamic thin shell onto a homogeneous scalar field reveals a transient phantom-like behavior. For any equation of state of the shell satisfying $ω> -1$, its effective energy density is diluted with cosmic expansion. Extending the framework to a two-scalar-field system provides a mechanism to bridge the energy gap between the Planckian bounce and the sub-Planckian scale. The pre-inflationary phase is brief, enabling the second scalar field to take over to drive the standard slow-roll inflation.
- **Summary:** The authors match an effective LQG-inspired black-hole geometry to an expanding FLRW region with Darmois–Israel junction conditions, producing a modified Friedmann equation with a Planckian bounce. Interpreting the shell stress tensor as scalar matter reveals transient phantom-like behavior, while a two-field extension bridges the bounce to sub-Planckian slow-roll inflation. The construction offers a concrete nonsingular pre-inflationary scenario, though its phenomenology depends on the effective geometry and matching assumptions.
**RELEVANCE:** Directly relevant to LQG-inspired singularity resolution and its cosmological consequences.  
**TAGS:** `loop quantum gravity` `quantum bounce` `nonsingular cosmology`

## 9. Lattice Hamiltonians for Chiral Gauge Theories
**Authors:** Dorota M. Grabowska  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13886v1  
**Categories:** hep-lat, hep-th, quant-ph
- **Abstract:** We present a lattice Hamiltonian for chiral gauge theories on the boundary of a slab. The formulation is gauge-fixed, so the gauge variables are the physical modes of the boundary field, and Gauss's law holds exactly. The bulk gauge field is determined by the boundary field and an integer label, which is the topological charge that the boundary generates and the bulk stores as a localized instanton. The bulk enters as a dielectric, and both gauge anomaly conditions become the question of whether the boundary satisfies Gauss's law on its own. For background gauge fields, we show that sectors that differ by a unit of bulk charge are degenerate after Pauli-Villars subtraction and the mirror fermions decouple. The boundary theory has no vacuum angle. Every term in the Hamiltonian is defined in terms of the boundary gauge variables, the integer label and the fermion modes, a starting point for quantum circuit construction.
- **Summary:** Grabowska formulates a gauge-fixed lattice Hamiltonian for chiral gauge theories living on a slab boundary, with exact Gauss law and a bulk integer storing the boundary’s topological charge as a localized instanton. Anomaly cancellation is recast as the boundary’s ability to satisfy Gauss law independently; Pauli–Villars subtraction makes sectors differing by bulk charge degenerate and decouples mirror fermions. Because every term is expressed in boundary variables, the construction is also a candidate starting point for quantum-circuit implementations.
**RELEVANCE:** Addresses a central obstacle in chiral lattice gauge theory while keeping quantum simulation in view.  
**TAGS:** `chiral gauge theory` `lattice Hamiltonian` `anomalies`

## 10. Assessing the expressive power of a gauge-invariant PEPS ansatz across a topological phase transition
**Authors:** Etsuko Itou, Tsuyoshi Okubo  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13852v1  
**Categories:** hep-lat, cond-mat.str-el, hep-th, quant-ph
- **Abstract:** We investigate the expressive power of the gauged Gaussian fermionic projected entangled pair state (GGFPEPS) ansatz, which is a manifestly gauge-invariant tensor network ansatz for $(2+1)$-dimensional lattice gauge theories. In the GGFPEPS ansatz, the gauge field on each link is represented by coupling its configuration to the fermion number of virtual fermions placed at both ends of the link. This ansatz has been studied by variational Monte Carlo (VMC) methods in previous work~\cite{Emonts:2022yom}. However, the statistical error of VMC makes a precise determination of the variational parameters difficult, so that the expressive power of the ansatz could not be examined accurately. In this work, we reformulate the computation as a Grassmann tensor network (TN), in which the whole network is contracted deterministically, and introduce the higher-order tensor renormalization group (HOTRG), which allows us to perform calculations in large volumes up to $L=34$. We also evaluate the infinite volume system using the corner transfer matrix renormalization group (CTMRG). We find that the ansatz with only eight complex variational parameters accurately describes the pure $\mathbb{Z}_2$ gauge theory including the region of the topological phase transition, not only in the energy but also in the topological entanglement entropy. The critical coupling obtained from the peaks of their susceptibilities agrees quantitatively with the one predicted by continuous-time quantum Monte Carlo simulations~\cite{wu2012phase}. Furthermore, near the transition point, we show that the ansatz represents a state outside the plaquette product family, which is a one-parameter family of states assumed in the construction of the ansatz as a simple interpolation between the strong and weak coupling limits.
- **Summary:** Itou and Okubo replace noisy variational Monte Carlo optimization of a gauge-invariant GGFPEPS ansatz with deterministic Grassmann tensor-network contraction, using HOTRG up to $L=34$ and CTMRG in the thermodynamic limit. An ansatz with only eight complex parameters reproduces both energy and topological entanglement entropy across the pure $mathbb Z_2$ transition, and its susceptibility locates the critical coupling consistently with quantum Monte Carlo. Near criticality it also leaves the assumed plaquette-product family, demonstrating nontrivial expressive power.
**RELEVANCE:** A rigorous tensor-network route to gauge-invariant many-body states and topological transitions.  
**TAGS:** `PEPS` `tensor networks` `topological phase transition`
