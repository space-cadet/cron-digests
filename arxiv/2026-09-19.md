## 1. The linear instability of Kasner spacetimes
**Authors:** Oliver Petersen  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20809v1  
**Categories:** math.AP, gr-qc, math.DG  
- **Abstract:*** We prove linear stability of Kasner spacetimes in the direction of the Big Bang, up to an explicit finite dimensional space of non-decaying self-similar solutions. The fastest growing solution is the linearization of Taub's explicit Bianchi II solution, describing a Kasner transition. All other non-decaying solutions are inhomogeneous analogues of this, and the linearized Kasner metric. The key novelty is a notion of quasinormal modes on Kasner spacetimes, similar to quasinormal modes on stationary black holes spacetimes.
  All quiescent (as opposed to oscillatory) vacuum Big Bang spacetimes of dimension 4 are asymptotic to a Kasner spacetime from the perspective of a single observer at the Big Bang. They are expected to be highly unstable due to the oscillations conjectured by Belinski, Khalatnikov and Lifschitz. This paper provides a complete description of this instability on a linear level without symmetry assumptions.

- **Summary:*** Petersen establishes linear stability of Kasner spacetimes toward the Big Bang except for an explicit finite-dimensional family of non-decaying self-similar modes, the dominant one being the linearized Taub Bianchi II transition. The proof introduces quasinormal-mode theory on Kasner backgrounds, transplanting black-hole spectral techniques to cosmological singularities. This gives the first complete symmetry-free linear description of the instability driving the Belinski–Khalatnikov–Lifshitz oscillatory approach to the Big Bang.

**RELEVANCE:** A rigorous mathematical-physics cornerstone for quantum gravity and the structure of cosmological singularities.
**TAGS:** `Kasner spacetime` `BKL instability` `cosmological singularities`

## 2. Looking inside a quantum black hole
**Authors:** Chiara Coviello, Ansh Gupta, Robie A. Hennigar, Kai Shi, Andrew Svesko  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20788v1  
**Categories:** hep-th, gr-qc  
- **Abstract:*** Quantum effects are expected to modify a black hole's interior structure, particularly near the singularity. We show how to extract the scaling exponent of the singularity from the quasinormal mode (QNM) spectrum of a massless scalar probe in the asymptotic large-overtone limit. We apply our method to a family of exact quantum black holes in (2+1)-dimensional anti-de Sitter (AdS) space and explicitly uncover a transition deep in the interior when quantum effects become dominant.

- **Summary:*** The authors demonstrate that the large-overtone quasinormal-mode spectrum of a scalar probe encodes the singularity's scaling exponent, turning QNM data into a probe of black-hole interiors. Applied to exact quantum-corrected black holes in AdS$_3$, the method reveals an explicit transition deep inside where quantum effects take over the classical singularity structure. This links a spectroscopic observable to interior quantum geometry, a rare measurable handle on quantum gravity.

**RELEVANCE:** Connects quantum-corrected black holes, holography, and observable QNM signatures — central to Deepak's quantum-gravity interests.

## 3. Toward a Unique Filter for the Gravitational Path Integral
**Authors:** Marc S. Klinger  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20697v1  
**Categories:** hep-th  
- **Abstract:*** In recent work, McNamara and Wang demonstrated that the failure of factorization for a gravitational path integral (GPI) can be understood as an incompleteness of its spectrum of states by constructing a unitary quantum field theory (QFT) into which the GPI embeds. We examine this construction from an algebraic point of view, and use it to verify a conjecture of the author concerning the existence and uniqueness of a unitary completion for the GPI by means of the theory of conditional expectations. We explore how the unitary QFT can be regarded as a single coherent theory in which the quantitative signatures of ensemble averaging emerge purely from projecting to the underlying, smooth GPI. The conditional expectation relating the two theories may therefore be interpreted as a filter in the sense of Liu. The factorizing theory differs from a more standard QFT in the sense that it is reducible, meaning the Hilbert space associated with the empty set is non-trivial. We explain how the structure of reducible QFTs are generically encoded in weak Hopf algebraic symmetries and demonstrate how the weak structure allows for generalized $\alpha$-sectors and half wormholes to coexist within a single Hilbert space.

- **Summary:*** Klinger recasts the McNamara–Wang unitary completion of the gravitational path integral in the language of conditional expectations, proving the author's conjecture on existence and uniqueness of the completion. The projection from the factorizing unitary theory to the smooth GPI is interpreted as a filter, with ensemble-averaging signatures emerging from that projection alone. Reducible QFTs are shown to be generically governed by weak Hopf algebra symmetries, letting $\alpha$-sectors and half wormholes live in one Hilbert space — a sharp algebraic clarification of factorization in quantum gravity.

**RELEVANCE:** Factorization, wormholes, and ensemble averaging are live problems at the quantum-gravity/quantum-information interface.
**TAGS:** `gravitational path integral` `factorization` `weak Hopf algebras`

## 4. The Page Transition in the Multi-Time Correlations of Hawking Radiation
**Authors:** Tushar Waghmare  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20456v1  
**Categories:** hep-th  
- **Abstract:*** The Page curve and the island formula describe a reorganization of quantum information in Hawking radiation across the Page time. We study how this reorganization appears in correlations among emissions collected at several retarded times. The late radiation is treated as a covariant multi-time process, or process tensor, and $\chi_{\mathrm{island}}=\mathcal{N}_{\varnothing}-\mathcal{N}_{R}$ measures the temporal memory removed when the early radiation register $R$ is supplied. Operationally, this quantity is the corresponding change in the optimal distinguishability of the radiation process from a Markovian reference. We exhibit evaporation processes whose single-bin states and all process marginals below a finite order $k_\ast\geq 3$ remain fixed and coincide with a CP-divisible reference, while $\chi_{\mathrm{island}}$ changes by order one at order $k_\ast$. The Page transition can therefore appear as a transition in the Markov order of the radiation process. When the interior mediates the late-time memory, entanglement-wedge reconstruction of the predictive interior from $R$ Markovianizes the late process; conversely, when the late process has a minimal predictive memory and its future-affecting information is mediated by that memory, Markovianization by $R$ supplies a simulator of its influence. A Hayden--Preskill evaporation comb and an evaporating-JT calculation realize this mechanism. The late radiation retains temporal memory across the transition, while the early radiation becomes sufficient for predicting its future multi-time statistics.

- **Summary:*** Waghmare reformulates the Page transition in the process-tensor language of multi-time quantum correlations, showing the island information reorganization appears as a jump in the Markov order of Hawking radiation. Strikingly, all process marginals below a finite order can remain CP-divisible and fixed while the island channel changes by order one at order $k_*$, so low-order statistics alone cannot detect the transition. Hayden–Preskill and evaporating-JT realizations confirm that early radiation Markovianizes the late process via entanglement-wedge reconstruction, giving an operational, non-entropy signature of the Page curve.

**RELEVANCE:** Bridges black-hole information, quantum channels, and process tensors — directly at the Deepak's QI/QG overlap.
**TAGS:** `Page curve` `process tensor` `Hawking radiation`

## 5. Environmental Effects in Post-Minkowskian Dynamics: Effective Field Theory, Feynman Rules, and Ward Identities for Compact Objects in Relativistic Fluids
**Authors:** Zvi Bern, Samuel Degen, Enrico Herrmann, Mikhail P. Solon, Anna M. Wolz  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20742v1  
**Categories:** hep-th, gr-qc, hep-ph  
- **Abstract:*** We develop a hydrodynamic effective field theory for the gravitational dynamics of compact objects moving through an inviscid fluid environment. The fluid is described by the effective theory of perfect fluids, whose Goldstone phonons are kept as explicit fields coupled to gravity. We establish a consistent power counting for the resulting Feynman rules and derive the propagators and interaction vertices through four points in $D$ dimensions, together with the generalized on-shell Ward identities they obey. These identities relate amplitudes with an external graviton to those with the graviton replaced by a phonon, and thus provide nontrivial checks on higher-order calculations. As an application, we recover the leading-order relativistic dynamical-friction force from the tree-level amplitude for single-phonon emission. This work is a first step towards a toolkit for incorporating environmental effects into the scattering-amplitude pipeline used in post-Minkowskian calculations.

- **Summary:*** The authors build a hydrodynamic EFT in which perfect-fluid Goldstone phonons are dynamical fields coupled to gravity, deriving Feynman rules through four points with consistent power counting. New on-shell Ward identities swap external gravitons for phonons and provide strong checks on higher-order amplitudes. As a first payoff, the standard relativistic dynamical-friction force is recovered from a single-phonon tree amplitude, opening a scattering-amplitude route to environmental effects in post-Minkowskian gravitational-wave physics.

**RELEVANCE:** Modern amplitude methods applied to gravitational dynamics and environments — high-energy theory with gravitational-wave applications.
**TAGS:** `post-Minkowskian` `effective field theory` `gravitational waves`

## 6. Statistics, 't Hooft Anomaly, and the Else-Nayak Index: a careful comparison of concepts
**Authors:** Hanyu Xue  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20813v1  
**Categories:** cond-mat.str-el, hep-th  
- **Abstract:*** Generalized symmetries and topological excitations, as well as symmetry anomalies and the statistics of topological excitations, are widely believed to be related. There are, however, pitfalls in how this relation is established. A lattice truncation of a symmetry transformation to a finite patch gives a symmetry patch operator that creates symmetry defects at its boundary. This geometric picture resembles a hopping operator creating topological excitations at the boundary of its support, but does not provide well-defined statistics, let alone guarantee agreement with the symmetry anomaly. A more natural and robust relation is that the hopping operators of topological excitations are symmetric: they commute with symmetry transformations. Under suitable assumptions, this condition yields a one-to-one correspondence between statistics and anomalies. We further couple boundary matter to a DW gauge field in one higher dimension to explain this relation from the perspective of gauging. A hopping operator is, in essence, a gauge-invariant operator acting on the physical degrees of freedom after gauging. Once the gauge-field background is fixed, global symmetry comes from gauge transformations that preserve that background, while the symmetric condition on hopping is precisely the remaining gauge invariance. This distinction clarifies potential misconceptions in the literature and provides a more reliable framework for comparing symmetries and topological excitations.

- **Summary:*** Xue identifies a common pitfall: patch operators creating symmetry defects resemble hopping operators for topological excitations geometrically, but do not define statistics or match anomalies. The robust relation is instead that hopping operators commute with the symmetry, which under mild assumptions yields a one-to-one statistics–anomaly correspondence, made transparent by coupling to a higher-dimensional Dijkgraaf–Witten gauge field. The result corrects literature misconceptions and grounds the Else–Nayak index-style analysis of topological order on firmer footing.

**RELEVANCE:** Generalized symmetries, anomalies, and topological order sit at the condensed-matter/high-energy interface Deepak follows.
**TAGS:** `generalized symmetry` `anomaly` `topological order`

## 7. Lyapunov-controlled thermalization: an exact real-time example
**Authors:** Jonas Loy, Jan C. Louw  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20597v1  
**Categories:** cond-mat.str-el, hep-th, quant-ph  
- **Abstract:*** A verified Kubo-Martin-Schwinger (KMS) relation, after a drive has ceased, is not sufficient evidence to prove equilibrium. We demonstrate this in a large-$N$ large-$q$ Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev (SYK) quench protocol. Although all post-quench fermion correlators are exactly thermal, a second quench back to the initial Hamiltonian reveals hidden memory of the initial state. It is encoded in correlators connecting to times before the first quench. This memory is an extensive nonequilibrium (NEQ) witness with its decay rate being the Lyapunov exponent $λ_L$. Thus $λ_L$ sets the rate at which the state becomes effectively indistinguishable from a Gibbs state. Despite being a unitary interacting many-body system, the complete NEQ real-time evolution is obtained exactly in the large-$N$, large-$q$ limit, making the setup ideal for analytically studying thermalization.

- **Summary:*** In an exactly solvable large-$N$, large-$q$ SYK quench protocol, all single-time correlators after the quench are precisely thermal, yet a return quench exposes hidden non-equilibrium memory in multi-time correlators reaching back before the drive. This memory is extensive and decays at the Lyapunov exponent, so chaos sets the timescale for true thermalization. The work gives a sharp, analytically controlled demonstration that KMS checks alone cannot certify equilibration.

**RELEVANCE:** SYK, quantum chaos, and thermalization are core to many-body quantum dynamics and holographic duality.
**TAGS:** `SYK model` `thermalization` `Lyapunov exponent`

## 8. Coherent and ultra-low-power EDSR with a flopping-mode spin qubit in germanium
**Authors:** Alexei Orekhov, Wonjin Jang, Pan Zhang, Konstantinos Tsoukalas, Fabian Oppliger, Franco De Palma, Elena Acinapura, Younghun Ryu, Inga Seidler, Lisa Sommer, Leonardo Massai, Felix J. Schupp, Matthias Mergenthaler, Stefano Bosco, Patrick Harvey-Collard, Pasquale Scarlino  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20775v1  
**Categories:** cond-mat.mes-hall, quant-ph  
- **Abstract:*** Hole spin qubits in semiconductor quantum dots (QDs) enable high-fidelity all-electric control, but conventional electric dipole spin resonance (EDSR) can require substantial rf drive power at the low magnetic fields that are favorable for qubit coherence and readout. In planar Ge hole spin qubits, this can reach -27 dBm at the device, posing challenges for scalable architectures due to heating and crosstalk. Here, we demonstrate a flopping-mode (FM) qubit in Ge, where a single spin is delocalized in a double QD, combining first-order protection against charge noise with exceptionally efficient electric driving. By mapping out coherence sweet-spots as a function of magnetic field orientation we achieve $T_2^*= 1.4μ\mathrm{s}$, $T_2^{\mathrm{Hahn}}= 11.5 μ\mathrm{s}$, $T^{φ, \mathrm{CPMG32}}_2= 130 μ\mathrm{s}$, and $T_1= 226 μ\mathrm{s}$, and a single-qubit gate fidelity of up to 99.76$%$ for a gate time $t_{Xπ} = 88$ ns. Importantly, these results are obtained at a nearly in-plane magnetic field of 5 mT using only -52 dBm drive power at the device. We further find that qubit relaxation in this regime is consistent with a two-photon Orbach process, providing a route for further optimization. Our results demonstrate that FM-EDSR supports ultra-low-power, high-fidelity single-qubit operations, improvements that could benefit scalable hole-spin-based architectures and hybrid spin-photon interfaces.

- **Summary:*** A flopping-mode qubit delocalizing a single Ge hole spin across a double quantum dot delivers first-order charge-noise protection together with highly efficient electric driving. The device reaches 99.76% single-qubit fidelity at 5 mT using only −52 dBm of drive power — orders of magnitude below conventional EDSR — with coherence up to 130 μs under CPMG. Relaxation consistent with a two-photon Orbach process points to a further optimization path, making the scheme attractive for scalable semiconductor spin qubit architectures.

**RELEVANCE:** State-of-the-art experimental quantum computing hardware with strong performance metrics relevant to scalable qubit platforms.
**TAGS:** `spin qubits` `germanium` `electric dipole spin resonance`

## 9. All causally separable quantum processes are quantum circuits with classical control of causal order
**Authors:** Julian Wechs, Alastair A. Abbott, Cyril Branciard  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20774v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph  
- **Abstract:*** The concept of causal (non)separability describes whether the causal order between parties that perform local quantum operations is well-defined or indefinite. Causal (non)separability in the general multipartite setting was introduced and studied in [Oreshkov and Giarmatzi, New J. Phys. 18, 093020 (2016); Wechs, Abbott, and Branciard, New J. Phys. 21, 013027 (2019)]. We resolve an open problem from these earlier works by showing -- using a novel "coherent teleportation technique" -- that a sufficient condition for causal separability identified in [Wechs, Abbott, and Branciard, New J. A consequence of this result is that all causally separable processes admit a realisation as generalised quantum circuits in which the order between the operations is classically controlled, known as "quantum circuits with classical control of causal order".

- **Summary:*** The authors settle a long-standing open problem with a "coherent teleportation" argument proving that a previously known sufficient condition for multipartite causal separability is in fact necessary. This yields a complete characterization of causally separable processes and establishes that every such process can be realized as a quantum circuit with only classical control of causal order. The result sharply delineates what indefinite causal order genuinely adds beyond classical ordering of quantum operations.

## 11. The return of Palatini inflationary attractors: Universal mapping of observables
**Authors:** Christian Dioguardi, Francesco Gianesello, Antonio Racioppi  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20622v1  
**Categories:** gr-qc, astro-ph.CO, hep-ph  
- **Abstract:*** We study single-field slow-roll inflation in the context of Palatini gravity for the class of non-minimally coupled $\xi$-attractors, i.e., models where the same function $f(\varphi)$ fixes both the non-minimal coupling, $1+\xi f(\varphi)$, and the inflationary potential, $V(\varphi) = V_0 f(\varphi)^2$. As is well-known, for an arbitrary $f(\varphi)$, the number of $e$-folds is, at leading order, independent of $\xi$. Thanks to this, we provide an immediate mapping between the observables of the non-minimally coupled setup and those of the minimally coupled one. In the strong coupling limit, the tensor-to-scalar ratio is, as usual, suppressed, while the scalar spectral index is shifted towards larger values, with the magnitude of the shift depending only on the tensor-to-scalar ratio associated with the original potential $V(\varphi)$.

- **Summary:*** For Palatini $\xi$-attractor models where one function $f(\varphi)$ sets both the non-minimal coupling and the potential, the $e$-fold count's leading-order independence of $\xi$ enables a direct universal map between Palatini and minimally coupled inflationary observables. Strong coupling suppresses the tensor-to-scalar ratio while shifting the spectral index upward by an amount fixed solely by the minimal model's tensor amplitude. This gives a simple, general diagnostic for confronting Palatini attractors with CMB data.

**RELEVANCE:** Inflation model building and modified-gravity cosmology are active areas at the gravity–cosmology boundary.
**TAGS:** `Palatini gravity` `inflation` `xi-attractors`

## 12. Asymmetric phase transitions in random noncommutative geometries
**Authors:** Benedek Bukor, Masoud Khalkhali, Samuel Kováčik, Adam Kubiś, Katarína Magdolenová, Nathan Pagliaroli, Juraj Tekel  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19898v1  
**Categories:** math-ph, hep-th, math.QA  
- **Abstract:*** In this paper, we study the asymmetric phases of the quartic type (0, 1) and (1, 0) Dirac ensembles via three approaches: the Riemann-Hilbert approach, bootstrapping with positivity, and Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (HMC) simulations. The focus of this work is on asymmetric solutions to the Schwinger-Dyson and saddle point equations of these models, whose solution spaces prove deeply intricate. Via the Riemann-Hilbert approach, we are able to give explicit formulae for the eigenvalue distributions and free energy of various solutions. Using Hamiltonian Monte Carlo simulations, we are able to reconstruct the phase structure. Lastly, using bootstrapping with positivity, we are able to reconstruct the eigenvalue distribution of these models from their bootstrapped moments. All three methods show excellent agreement for a large matrix size.

- **Summary:*** The quartic Dirac ensembles of random noncommutative geometry are analyzed in their asymmetric phases using three independent methods — Riemann–Hilbert analysis, positivity-constrained bootstrap, and Hamiltonian Monte Carlo — which agree closely at large matrix size. Explicit eigenvalue distributions and free energies are obtained for solutions of the Schwinger–Dyson equations, revealing an intricate solution landscape. The multi-method consistency establishes these matrix models as a controlled arena for phase structure in spectral-geometry approaches to quantum gravity.

**RELEVANCE:** Random noncommutative geometry and spectral action models are close cousins of LQG-style quantum-gravity approaches.
**TAGS:** `noncommutative geometry` `matrix models` `phase transitions`
