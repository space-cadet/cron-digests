## 1. Holography as an Information Principle in Quantum Gravity
- **Authors:** Sebastian De Haro, Enrico Cinti, Aude Corbeel
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.26282
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26282
- **Categories:** hep-th, gr-qc, physics.hist-ph
- **Abstract:** We draw on 't Hooft's seminal formulation of the holographic principle to analyse the methodological and conceptual role of information in quantum gravity. We argue that, in 't Hooft's work and in later developments, information functions as a substantive guiding principle. We distinguish three aspects of this role: First, holographic bounds on the amount of information that can be stored in a region function as theory selection criteria that constrain viable quantum gravity theories. Second, holography functions as a principle of theoretical equivalence: the bulk and boundary theories must describe the same physical content, even though 't Hooft privileges a more fundamental, lower-dimensional, and potentially deterministic boundary description. Third, the distribution and encoding of information connect 't Hooft's proposals to contemporary work on bulk reconstruction, holographic quantum error correction, and ER=EPR, where emergent spacetime structure is tied to patterns of entanglement and redundancy. On the basis of these three roles, we argue that purely epistemic or Shannon-style conceptions of information are inadequate in this context: we instead outline a distinction between what we call maximal and intermediate conceptions that aim to capture the methodological and interpretative roles of information in holographic quantum gravity. We therefore suggest that, in the context of holography and quantum gravity, a more systematic philosophical treatment of the role of information as an interpretation-guiding principle would be desirable.
- **Summary:** The authors develop a systematic conceptual analysis of how information functions as a substantive principle in quantum gravity, tracing three interconnected roles through 't Hooft's original holographic principle and its modern descendants. Rather than treating information as merely epistemic or Shannon-theoretic, they argue that holographic bounds serve as theory-selection criteria, that holography enforces a principle of theoretical equivalence between bulk and boundary descriptions, and that the encoding of information links 't Hooft's deterministic vision to contemporary work on bulk reconstruction and ER=EPR. They propose a distinction between "maximal" and "intermediate" conceptions of information to better capture the interpretative work that information does in guiding quantum gravity research.
- **Relevance:** Directly addresses foundational questions in quantum gravity that lie at the intersection of holography, information theory, and the emergence of spacetime. The analysis of how information functions as a principle rather than a derived quantity resonates with ongoing efforts in loop quantum gravity and related approaches to understand the microscopic degrees of freedom of spacetime.

## 4. Quantizing the guiding center: do quantization and coarse graining commute?
- **Authors:** Rodrigo Andrade e Silva, Ted Jacobson
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.26277
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26277
- **Categories:** hep-th, math-ph, gr-qc, quant-ph
- **Abstract:** We develop a nonperturbative, group-theoretic quantization of the effective guiding center theory of a charged particle drifting in a magnetic field in two spatial dimensions, and compare the resulting quantum theory with a corresponding coarse graining of the underlying microscopic theory. In the classical effective theory, the small gyro motion is not resolved, while the motion of the center of the gyro orbit remains observable. The reduced phase space is the physical space itself, so quantization leads to noncommuting spatial coordinates, and the effective theory loses access to the metric structure of physical space, retaining only its area structure. By formulating a prescription to match between the microscopic and effective quantum theories, we find that the predictions of the quantized effective theory are generally consistent with those of the microscopic theory. However, for closed isomagnetic contours the effective theory predicts a quantization of ``radius'', a spatial discreteness absent from the microscopic theory. This illustrates that quantization of an effective theory may yield spurious nonperturbative predictions, even if that quantum theory shows no internal signs of breakdown.
- **Summary:** The authors rigorously quantize the guiding center effective theory for a charged particle in a two-dimensional magnetic field using group-theoretic methods, then compare the result with a direct coarse-graining of the full microscopic quantum theory. They find broad agreement between the two approaches, but discover a striking exception: for closed isomagnetic contours, the quantized effective theory predicts a discretization of radius that has no counterpart in the underlying microscopic description. This exposes a subtle but important pitfall in effective field theory methodology—quantizing after coarse-graining can generate spurious nonperturbative effects that appear mathematically consistent within the effective theory itself.
- **Relevance:** The interplay between coarse-graining and quantization is a foundational issue in quantum gravity, where effective theories of geometry are routinely quantized. Jacobson's involvement and the explicit demonstration of spurious discreteness from effective-theory quantization carry direct lessons for approaches that derive discrete spectra from effective descriptions of gravitational systems.

## 6. The Capacity of Entanglement and Holographic Entropies at Finite Resources
- **Authors:** Ning Bao, Jacob March
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.26377
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26377
- **Categories:** quant-ph, hep-th
- **Abstract:** The Ryu-Takayanagi formula equates the area of a minimal surface with the von Neumann entropy of a boundary subregion, and leaves two things about that identification open. The first is how sharply a geometry fixes an entropy. Fannes-Audenaert answers with a Hilbert-space dimension, which diverges as the cutoff is removed however close the two states are. We replace it with the capacity of entanglement, the variance of the modular energy, whose square root grows like the square root of the entangling area where the dimensional factor grows like the regulated volume. The bound is dimension-free and saturated, and it makes the ambiguity of the entropy subextensive for any perturbation whose capacity is small compared with S_vN² times the trace norm. The second is what the area means for a single state, since compression and dilution rates are defined only for many copies while a geometry describes one. When a single replica saddle dominates near α = 1, every smooth Rényi entropy at fixed α > 1 agrees with S_vN to O(√S_vN), as do the smooth min- and max-entropies. The minimal surface therefore fixes every one-shot entropy of the state at once, with large central charge playing the role of large copy number in the asymptotic equipartition property. As a consequence we bound how far outside the holographic entropy cone a holographic state can appear to fall, leaving estimation and certification open.
- **Summary:** The authors resolve two long-standing conceptual gaps in the Ryu-Takayanagi holographic entropy formula. First, they replace the divergent Hilbert-space-dimension bound on entropy precision with the capacity of entanglement—measuring the variance of modular energy—which yields a dimension-free, saturable bound scaling with the square root of entangling area rather than regulated volume. Second, they prove that for holographic states where a single replica saddle dominates, all smooth Rényi entropies and one-shot min/max-entropies converge to the von Neumann entropy up to O(√S_vN), meaning the minimal surface geometry simultaneously determines every operational entropy measure for a single copy of the state.
- **Relevance:** Holographic entanglement entropy is a cornerstone of the AdS/CFT correspondence and increasingly influential in understanding how spacetime emerges from quantum information. These results sharpen the geometric meaning of entropy in holography and have direct implications for bulk reconstruction and the black hole information problem.

- **Abstract:** The pursuit of a quantum theory of gravity, aiming to unify general relativity and quantum mechanics, remains one of the most enduring challenges in physics. By deriving exact solutions incorporating the full three-dimensional particle paths, the author demonstrates that this approximation can qualitatively alter predictions—producing false null results in regimes where entanglement should exist and missing configurations where entanglement actually vanishes.
