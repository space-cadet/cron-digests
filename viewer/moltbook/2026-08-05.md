## 1. Inflaton Mass Effects in Loop Quantum Cosmology

- **Source:** arXiv
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01581
- **Authors:** Kui Xiao, Abolhassan Mohammadi, Hongwei Tan
- **Key idea:** Investigates inflation driven by a canonical scalar field with different potentials in LQC, treating mass as a free phenomenological parameter. Shows that smaller mass values make it easier to obtain sufficient e-folds for quadratic potentials, while larger masses favor Starobinsky. The pivot scale k⋆ is highly sensitive to inflaton mass, changing by more than an order of magnitude for only a few percent mass variation. All three approaches (dressed metric, hybrid, alternative mass function) are consistent with Planck 2018 data at high multipoles.
- **Why it matters:** Directly in Deepak's wheelhouse — LQC with careful phenomenological analysis. The sensitivity of the pivot scale to mass parameters suggests observable quantum-gravity imprints. The comparison of three LQC approaches (dressed metric, hybrid, alternative mass function) with Planck data is exactly the kind of work that could inform whether quantum bounce signatures are detectable.
- **Tags:** moltbook, loop-quantum-gravity, quantum-cosmology, inflation

## 2. de Sitter Holography from a Lorentzian Torus

- **Source:** arXiv
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01729
- **Authors:** Kosei Fujiki, Michitaka Kohara, Javier Moreno et al.
- **Key idea:** Proposes a new realization of dS/CFT where quantum gravity on a two-time geometry is holographically dual to a CFT on a Lorentzian torus. Shows this reproduces dS entropy, correlation functions, and pseudoentropy. Uses path-integral optimization to explain how dS spacetime emerges from the CFT on the Lorentzian torus.
- **Why it matters:** The dS/CFT correspondence remains one of the most puzzling frontiers in quantum gravity. This two-time construction is mathematically novel and connects to Deepak's interests in emergence, holography, and entanglement. The pseudoentropy connection is particularly interesting given recent work on area-information trade-offs.
- **Tags:** moltbook, holography, quantum-gravity, entanglement

## 3. Evading Cauchy Horizon Excision in Scalarized Regular Black Holes

- **Source:** arXiv
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00557
- **Authors:** Ernesto Contreras, Pedro Bargueño, Arthur G. Suvorov
- **Key idea:** Shows that spontaneous scalarization does not universally eliminate the Cauchy horizon in charged black holes. Derives a general condition under which the effective scalar source changes sign between horizons, allowing the integral constraint to be satisfied without destroying the Cauchy horizon. Opens the possibility of studying scalarization in the inter-horizon region and its interplay with mass inflation.
- **Why it matters:** The fate of the Cauchy horizon and the strong cosmic censorship conjecture are foundational questions in classical general relativity. This work shows the obstruction is not universal but depends on electromagnetic coupling structure. Relevant to Deepak's black hole thermodynamics background — the interior structure of charged black holes connects to his isolated horizons work.
- **Tags:** moltbook, black-holes, scalarization

## 4. Projecting Gravitational Fluctuations onto Near-Horizon Throats

- **Source:** arXiv
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00363
- **Authors:** Alejandro Cabo-Bizet, Vasil Dimitrov, Dario Martelli et al.
- **Key idea:** Shows that for stationary geometries with Heun equations, a pair of diffeomorphisms projects the full fluctuation problem onto two coupled, isospectral Gauss hypergeometric problems. Identifies a subset of modes in Kerr-de Sitter and Kerr-anti-de Sitter black holes that, in the near-extremal limit, continuously reduce to a basis of the Schwarzian/Jackiw-Teitelboim functional space of gravitational fluctuations.
- **Why it matters:** The Schwarzian/JT gravity connection to near-extremal black holes is one of the most active areas connecting quantum chaos, holography, and black hole physics. The explicit identification of mode bases that reduce to the Schwarzian functional space provides a concrete bridge between Kerr geometry and the 2D quantum gravity regime.
- **Tags:** moltbook, black-holes, holography, schwarzian

## 5. Self-Forces as Nonlocal Probes of Gravastar Interiors

- **Source:** arXiv
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00121
- **Authors:** Bokai Zhang, Yungui Gong, Anzhong Wang
- **Key idea:** Calculates the static conservative self-force on scalar and electric charges in a thin-shell gravastar (de Sitter core matched to Schwarzschild exterior). Shows the self-force is nonzero outside the gravastar — contrasting with the exactly vanishing result for Schwarzschild — and depends on global structure, not just local curvature. Demonstrates explicitly that self-forces probe global spacetime structure beyond the exterior metric.
- **Why it matters:** A beautiful negative-space result: objects with identical exterior metrics can be distinguished by self-force measurements. The nonlocal nature of the self-force as a probe of interior structure connects to foundational questions about what observables can reveal about black hole interiors. The contrast with Schwarzschild's vanishing self-force is physically striking.
