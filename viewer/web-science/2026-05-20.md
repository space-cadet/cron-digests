## 1. What Do Gödel's Incompleteness Theorems Truly Mean?
- **Source:** Quanta Magazine / Mathematics
- **URL:** https://www.quantamagazine.org/what-do-godels-incompleteness-theorems-truly-mean-20260518/
- **Summary:** In 1931, the 25-year-old Austrian logician Kurt Gödel proved a pair of theorems that transformed the landscape of knowledge and truth. By turning logic on itself, Gödel established that no formal system of mathematics — no finite set of axioms from which everything is supposed to follow — can ever be complete. There will always be true mathematical statements that do not logically follow from those axioms. Quanta columnist Natalie Wolchover unpacks the enduring implications of this proof for the nature of truth, knowledge, and the limits of formal reasoning.

## 3. The Haystack 37m Telescope: A new era of astrophysical research
- **Source:** MIT News / Radio Astronomy
- **URL:** https://news.mit.edu/2026/haystack-37m-telescope-new-era-astrophysical-research-0519
- **Summary:** MIT Haystack Observatory's legendary 37-meter radio telescope has returned to front-line astronomical research after an extended period of system upgrades. First lit in 1964, the telescope supported NASA's Apollo lunar landings, mapped Venus by radar, tested Einstein's general relativity, and pioneered very long baseline interferometry (VLBI). On Dec. 8, 2025, Haystack scientists observed the supermassive black hole system M87* using VLBI in collaboration with the Very Long Baseline Array and the Greenland Telescope — probing the energetic jet launched from a black hole 6.5 billion times the mass of our sun and extending thousands of light-years into intergalactic space.
- **Relevance:** 🔥 High — The return of a historically significant instrument to active VLBI science strengthens global efforts to image and understand black hole jets and tests of general relativity
