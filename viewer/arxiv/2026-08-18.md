## 1. Regular Black Holes from Collapsing Dust in a Dark Energy Background
- **Authors:** Qazi Abdul Ghafoor
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.13619
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13619
- **Categories:** gr-qc
- **Abstract:** The central singularity in gravitational collapse represents a fundamental breakdown of classical general relativity, yet its resolution remains an open challenge. While conventional regular black hole models invoke exotic matter or nonlinear electrodynamics, the physical origin of such regularization mechanisms remains obscure. In this work, we demonstrate that a radially varying interaction between dust and dark energy can naturally resolve the central singularity. We systematically investigate three cases: no interaction, constant interaction, and radially varying interaction. Only when the interaction grows sufficiently toward the center can the divergent behavior of the density and the central mass contribution simultaneously be eliminated near the center. This yields a mass function scales as $M \sim \chi^3$, a finite Kretschmann scalar ($\lim_{\chi \to 0} K < \infty$), and a de Sitter-like core for the physical regime $w \leq -1$ (with $w=-1$ corresponding to pure de Sitter core). At the center, the resulting configuration satisfies the null, weak, and dominant energy conditions, while the strong energy condition is violated. Unlike charge-based models, our interaction parameter $\alpha(t)$ stabilizes at a non-zero value after the collapse, allowing the regular core to persist in the late-time configuration. This provides a physically motivated alternative to conventional regular black hole models, grounded in the modern cosmological understanding of dark energy rather than exotic fields or charges.
- **Summary:** This work proposes a novel mechanism for singularity resolution in gravitational collapse: a radially varying interaction between dust and dark energy that grows toward the center, replacing the singularity with a regular de Sitter-like core. Unlike conventional regular black hole models that rely on exotic matter or electric charge, this approach uses dark energy — a component already required by cosmological observations — to achieve finite density and curvature throughout the collapse. The resulting late-time configuration preserves the regular core without fine-tuning, offering a physically motivated pathway from gravitational collapse to a nonsingular black hole interior.
- **Relevance:** The resolution of spacetime singularities is one of the central challenges that quantum gravity must address. This work provides a semiclassical mechanism for singularity avoidance via dark energy, conceptually parallel to bounce scenarios in loop quantum cosmology and black hole interiors. The emergence of a de Sitter-like core from dynamical collapse connects to ongoing discussions about black hole interiors in quantum gravity, where regular cores and bounce behavior are expected features.
- **Tags:** Regular Black Holes, Singularity Resolution, Dark Energy

## 2. Quasinormal modes as exterior probes of black hole interiors in our Universe
- **Authors:** Sašo Grozdanov, Vita Movrin, Samuel Valach
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.13643
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13643
- **Categories:** hep-th, astro-ph.HE, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** We consider a scenario in which a black hole is enclosed in a reflecting timelike cavity and linearly perturbed. For the resulting quasinormal spectra of gravitational, electromagnetic, and other perturbations, in conjunction with our arXiv:2606.11297, we establish a thermal product formula and derive a universal asymptotic expression for high quasinormal overtones. Both results hold in asymptotically flat, de Sitter, and Anti-de Sitter spacetimes (known previously). This spectral signature directly encodes information about the black hole interior and its singularity, and is, at least in principle, accessible to an observer outside the event horizon. We then propose a concrete observational setup in which accreting plasma plays the role of a partially transmissive mirror. For small, hypothetical, primordial black holes, we argue that one may thereby be able to extract concrete information about the black hole interior from electromagnetic waves in the radiowave spectrum.
- **Summary:** The authors show that the quasinormal mode spectrum of a black hole enclosed in a reflecting cavity contains universal signatures that encode information about the interior singularity structure, making the interior indirectly observable from outside the horizon. By proposing that accreting plasma around primordial black holes can act as a natural partially-reflecting mirror, they transform this theoretical construction into a potentially testable scenario using radio wave observations. This bridges abstract spectral physics with a concrete observational proposal for probing the most extreme regime of spacetime.
- **Relevance:** Understanding black hole interiors and singularities is at the heart of quantum gravity. The possibility of extracting interior information from exterior observations — even if mediated by a mirror-like boundary — resonates deeply with holographic principles and the black hole information problem. For researchers working on quantum gravity phenomenology, this offers a novel angle on how Planck-scale physics might leave imprints on observable spectra.
- **Tags:** Black Hole Interior, Quasinormal Modes, Primordial Black Holes

## 3. Asymptotic flatness beyond General Relativity
- **Authors:** David Maibach
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.14204
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14204
- **Categories:** gr-qc, hep-ph
- **Abstract:** The asymptotic symmetry group of asymptotically flat spacetimes gives rise to balance flux equations that constrain, fully non-perturbatively, the asymptotic strain measured by gravitational-wave detectors. Such constraints are sharp tools for identifying features such as the memory effect. As detector sensitivities improve, it becomes imperative to place the most promising beyond-GR candidates on the same footing. Whether the asymptotically flat framework applies to such theories at all is far from obvious and requires careful analysis of the additional degrees of freedom reaching future null infinity. In this work, we address this question. We integrate the Bondi-Sachs hierarchy in the presence of an arbitrary stress-energy tensor and extract the falloff conditions its components must satisfy for the standard metric decay to close. We then feed the most general scalar-vector-tensor theory with second-order equations of motion through this framework. Recasting the field equations in the effective Einstein form $G_{\mu\nu}=T^{\rm eff}_{\mu\nu}/G_4(\Phi,X)$ and evaluating every operator of $L_i$ against the falloff table, we condense the outcome into a constraint table for the coupling functionals and their derivatives at the asymptotic vacuum, sharpened by the scalar and vector equations of motion and vacuum stability. Remarkably few conditions survive. The scalar potential must vanish to cubic order at the asymptotic vacuum, the asymptotic Newton constant must be finite and positive, the scalar and vector modes must be canonically normalized, and the conformally coupled sector carries a frame subtlety. Every other coupling functional is protected by the structure of the theory. The constraint table thus provides a diagnostic for screening beyond-GR models against asymptotic flatness and establishes the BMS group as the asymptotic symmetry group across the entire admissible SVT class.
- **Summary:** This work systematically extends the Bondi-Sachs asymptotic framework to the most general scalar-vector-tensor theories of gravity, deriving a compact diagnostic table that determines which beyond-GR models can admit asymptotically flat spacetimes. Surprisingly, most coupling functionals require no additional constraints beyond those already implied by the theory's structure; only a handful of conditions on the scalar potential, Newton constant, and mode normalization survive. This establishes the BMS group as the universal asymptotic symmetry for a vast class of modified gravity theories, providing a rigorous foundation for comparing gravitational-wave predictions across competing models.
- **Relevance:** The asymptotic structure of spacetime and the BMS group have become increasingly important in quantum gravity, particularly in understanding soft hair, memory effects, and their connections to the black hole information paradox. Extending this framework to beyond-GR theories is essential for testing quantum gravity phenomenology with next-generation gravitational wave detectors like LISA and Cosmic Explorer.
- **Tags:** Asymptotic Flatness, BMS Group, Modified Gravity

## 4. C-infinity Compact-Support Wormholes with Exact Schwarzschild Exterior in Trace-Coupled Gravity
- **Authors:** Mushtaq Ahmad, M. Farasat Shamir, Ahdab K. Althukair
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.14259
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14259
- **Categories:** gr-qc
- **Abstract:** We study static, spherically symmetric traversable wormholes in trace-coupled gravity with action density f(R,T) = R + lambda T^2. The spacetime is built from C-infinity deformations of Schwarzschild that vanish identically outside a finite core, so the exterior is exactly Schwarzschild and no thin shell or junction surface is needed. Because f_R = 1, the metric equations remain second order and the matter problem reduces to a local algebraic reconstruction. For an anisotropic source, we derive unified inversion formulas for (rho, p_r, p_t) in terms of the effective source and the dimensionless trace variable chi = lambda T/(4 pi), and show that the admissible matter branch is the vacuum-connected real root of a cubic equation. The resulting parameter space contains regular positive-density and negative-density throat regimes, a critical boundary where the reconstruction degenerates, and a nonadmissible branch-failure sector. The geometry itself is branch-independent, but the reconstructed matter depends on the matter-Lagrangian prescription. On the admissible branch the radial NEC still fails at the throat, so the model localizes exoticity rather than removing it. The Ricci support and reconstructed matter remain confined to a finite interval, while the exterior tidal field is exactly Schwarzschild with ADM mass M. The results should be read as controlled existence and viability statements within the displayed ansatz families, not as a stability proof.
- **Summary:** The authors construct smooth wormhole geometries in f(R,T) gravity where the matter and curvature deviations are confined to a finite region, leaving an exact Schwarzschild exterior without any thin-shell junction. By deriving explicit inversion formulas that reconstruct the anisotropic matter from the geometry, they map out the full parameter space of solutions and identify where the null energy condition violation — the hallmark of wormhole throat stability — becomes localized rather than distributed. This provides a controlled mathematical laboratory for studying how modified gravity can support traversable wormholes while preserving the familiar exterior observational signatures of ordinary black holes.
- **Relevance:** Traversable wormholes and their connection to quantum entanglement (ER=EPR) have become a central theme in quantum gravity research. The f(R,T) framework used here offers a tractable modified gravity setting where the relationship between geometry, matter, and energy conditions can be studied in detail, with potential implications for understanding how quantum effects might effectively modify the gravitational action near Planck-scale structures.
- **Tags:** Wormholes, Modified Gravity, Energy Conditions

## 5. Investigation of the black-hole quantum atmosphere from the effective proper temperature
- **Authors:** Sojeong Cheong, Wontae Kim
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.13943
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13943
- **Categories:** hep-th, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** Hawking radiation may be regarded as originating at the event horizon; however, its spatial origin can instead be distributed over a finite region outside the horizon. In this paper, using an effective proper temperature, we investigate the quantum atmosphere within a tractable model based on the two-dimensional radial sector of D-dimensional Schwarzschild black holes. In the Hartle-Hawking state, the proper temperature is derived from the first law of thermodynamics in the presence of the conformal anomaly associated with Hawking radiation. In the Unruh state, we decompose the proper temperature into two chiral temperatures, T_L and T_R, associated with the ingoing and outgoing fluxes, respectively. Demonstrating T_R as the effective proper temperature characterizing the outgoing Hawking flux with the proper temperature in the Hartle-Hawking state, we define the atmospheric radius effectively as the radial position at which the effective proper temperature attains its maximum. We then numerically compute the atmospheric radius of the quantum atmosphere for various spacetime dimensions and show that it decreases monotonically as the number of spacetime dimensions increases. In the large-dimensional limit, we find that the atmospheric radius remains separated from the horizon by a finite radial factor, indicating that the quantum atmosphere can persist as an extended exterior region in any dimension.
- **Summary:** This work develops a thermodynamic characterization of the "quantum atmosphere" — the finite region outside a black hole horizon where Hawking radiation is effectively produced. By defining an atmospheric radius through the maximum of the effective proper temperature, the authors show that this region shrinks with increasing spacetime dimension but never collapses onto the horizon, even in the large-D limit. The framework unifies the Hartle-Hawking and Unruh thermal states through a chiral decomposition, providing a concrete measure of where the quantum-to-classical transition in black hole radiation occurs geometrically.
- **Relevance:** The quantum atmosphere concept challenges the traditional view of Hawking radiation as purely horizon-localized, with implications for understanding the information paradox and firewall debates. The thermodynamic approach developed here, incorporating the conformal anomaly, connects to broader questions about how quantum field theory in curved spacetime should be consistently formulated near horizons — a key concern for any quantum gravity program including loop quantum gravity.
- **Tags:** Hawking Radiation, Quantum Atmosphere, Black Hole Thermodynamics

## 6. Radiative Matching Across a Projector Bridge
- **Authors:** Medeu E. Abishev, Daulet Z. Berkimbayev
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.13634
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13634
- **Categories:** gr-qc, hep-th
- **Abstract:** Two distinct Einstein-Hilbert sectors are related by a covariant correspondence bridge that maps stress-energy through bitensor kernels. The Bianchi identities enforce conservation, with bridge stresses balancing cross-sector exchange. For positive gravitational couplings and paired Minkowski backgrounds, a fixed metric-independent bridge leaves the quadratic action block diagonal, so the vacuum contains two massless spin-2 fields and no Fierz-Pauli mass term. A reciprocal first-order transport law generates a flux-preserving rotation of the canonical outgoing data. This divides the emitted power between visible and hidden channels while preserving the total quadrupole luminosity and the general-relativistic chirp phase. The reduced visible amplitude produces a systematic standard-siren distance shift, whose present sensitivity can be estimated from gravitational-wave catalog cosmology combined with an independent distance calibration. A source-level rescaling is physically inequivalent: it changes radiation reaction and requires compensating bridge energy flow. The construction therefore describes amplitude transfer between massless tensor channels, distinct from massive-graviton dispersion and propagation-induced oscillations.
- **Summary:** The authors construct a novel theoretical framework in which two separate gravitational sectors communicate through a "projector bridge" that transfers stress-energy via bitensor kernels, creating a hidden channel for gravitational radiation. Unlike massive graviton scenarios, the hidden gravitational waves are massless and travel at the speed of light, but their existence would manifest as a systematic dimming of standard siren luminosity distances without altering the chirp phase. This provides a new class of beyond-GR signatures that current gravitational-wave cosmology can already constrain, while remaining fundamentally distinct from conventional massive gravity or oscillation scenarios.
- **Relevance:** Multi-sector gravity models and hidden gravitational channels are motivated by string theory landscapes and braneworld scenarios. The distinctive signature — amplitude suppression without phase distortion — offers a novel target for gravitational wave astronomy, and the bitensor-based bridge formalism may find applications in understanding how different quantum gravity sectors could interact or decouple.
- **Tags:** Gravitational Waves, Beyond-GR, Hidden Sectors

## 7. Open system probes of renormalization group flow
- **Authors:** Andrew Keefe, Brenden Bowen, Saptarshi Biswas, Albion Lawrence, Nishant Agarwal, Archana Kamal
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.13664
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13664
- **Categories:** quant-ph, cond-mat.mes-hall, cond-mat.stat-mech, hep-th
- **Abstract:** Open system probes can provide an efficient means to characterize quantum many-body systems by employing them as engineered environments. The key idea is to map long-range spatial correlations of the environment onto dynamical correlations in the evolution of a simple quantum probe. Using the example of a qubit coupled to a transverse-field Ising model, we show how the non-Markovian rate or spectral flow can be used to identify stable and unstable fixed points, infer scaling dimensions of relevant fields, and deduce the renormalization group flow induced by deformations around any fixed point.
- **Summary:** This paper demonstrates that a single qubit coupled to a quantum critical system can serve as a powerful probe of renormalization group flow, using the probe's non-Markovian dynamics as a spectroscopic tool. By mapping the spatial correlations of the critical environment onto temporal correlations in the qubit's evolution, the authors show how to extract fixed point locations, scaling dimensions, and the direction of RG flow under deformations — all from measuring a simple two-level system. This effectively turns an open quantum system into a microscope for universal critical behavior, with potential applications across quantum simulation and condensed matter platforms.
- **Relevance:** Renormalization group methods are fundamental tools in quantum field theory and quantum gravity. The ability to extract RG flow information from simple quantum probes has implications for quantum simulation of critical phenomena and may inspire analogous approaches for probing quantum gravitational degrees of freedom in tabletop or near-term quantum computing experiments. The connection between non-Markovian open dynamics and critical behavior also resonates with recent work on quantum channels in holography.
- **Tags:** Renormalization Group, Quantum Probes, Critical Phenomena
