## 1. Quantum Gravity Beyond the Bulk
- **Authors:** Jorge Gamboa, Natalia Tapia-Arellano
- **arXiv ID:** 2605.15251
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15251
- **Categories:** gr-qc, hep-th
- **Summary:** An infrared and asymptotic formulation of quantum gravity adapted to external observers. In an asymptotic proper-time gauge, the physical generator of evolution reduces to the Regge–Teitelboim boundary charge, so that quantum dynamics is governed by infrared gravitational configurations at spatial infinity. In the weak-field regime this leads naturally to a Born–Oppenheimer separation between slow asymptotic data and fast bulk fluctuations. Integrating out the latter induces a Berry connection on the space of admissible configurations, whose holonomy characterizes infrared-dressed gravitational states. Observable evolution is then governed by an infrared effective Hamiltonian obtained after integrating out fast bulk fluctuations, with the geometric (Berry) contribution entering as part of the resulting evolution operator on the space of asymptotic configurations. Tracing over unresolvable degrees of freedom induces a reduced density matrix whose entropy is dominated by global infrared holonomy sectors rather than local bulk dynamics.
- **Relevance:** A novel infrared perspective on quantum gravity that shifts focus from bulk dynamics to boundary charges and Berry phases. The entanglement structure being dominated by infrared holonomy sectors rather than local bulk dynamics is a significant conceptual shift. Awarded an Honorable Mention in the 2026 Gravity Research Foundation Essay Competition.
- **Tags:** Quantum Gravity, Infrared Physics, Berry Phases, Black Hole Information

## 2. Loop Quantum Kaluza-Klein Cosmology and Inflation
- **Authors:** Shengzhi Li, Yongge Ma, Faqiang Yuan, Xiangdong Zhang
- **arXiv ID:** 2605.15858
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15858
- **Categories:** gr-qc
- **Summary:** Five-dimensional loop quantum Kaluza-Klein cosmology based on the symmetric reduction of the connection formulation of the full theory. The effective scalar constraint for the geometric sector is derived by systematic semi-classical analysis in both canonical and path-integral formulations, incorporating quantum fluctuations as a subleading-order correction. The resulting effective scalar constraint exhibits the correct classical limit and serves as the basis for investigating three distinct effective scenarios: (i) vacuum, (ii) minimally coupling with a scalar field, and (iii) coupling with dust. In all three scenarios, the big bang and potential past big rip singularities in the classical model are naturally resolved by including the leading-order quantum correction of holonomies. The visible universe undergoes a super-inflationary phase after overcoming the classical big bang singularity, during which the phenomenologically desired 55 e-folds can be achieved by appropriate initial conditions. When the subleading-order quantum fluctuation term is included as a constant, the evolutions in all three scenarios not only achieve sufficient inflation in the visible dimensions, but also exhibit re-collapse behaviors at certain large scales.
- **Relevance:** Concrete bridge between loop quantum gravity and higher-dimensional cosmology. The resolution of both big-bang and big-rip singularities via holonomy corrections, combined with achieving sufficient e-folds of inflation, makes this a strong contribution to quantum cosmology phenomenology. The re-collapse behavior at large scales is an interesting prediction.
- **Tags:** Loop Quantum Gravity, Quantum Cosmology, Kaluza-Klein, Inflation

## 3. Generating Rotation in a Snap
- **Authors:** Soumangsu Chakraborty, Pierre Heidmann, Gela Patashuri
- **arXiv ID:** 2605.15258
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15258
- **Categories:** hep-th, gr-qc
- **Summary:** A new purely algebraic technique to generate rotation from arbitrary static solutions that asymptote to four- or five-dimensional Minkowski spacetime. The method does not require solving Einstein equations. It proceeds by transforming the static solution to AdS×S asymptotics, performing a coordinate shift to a uniformly rotating frame, and then transforming back to asymptotically flat spacetime. Implemented in five-dimensional minimal supergravity, though it applies more broadly to any framework admitting AdS×S geometries and relevant sigma-model transformations. As a first application, the Kerr and Myers-Perry black holes are recovered directly from Schwarzschild black holes. Applied to the linear class of static Weyl solutions, it yields the first linear ansatz describing an arbitrary number of non-extremal rotating and charged sources.
- **Relevance:** Elegant algebraic shortcut for generating rotating solutions — recovering Kerr from Schwarzschild without solving field equations is a powerful demonstration. The extension to multi-source configurations suggests broad applicability for constructing complex rotating geometries.
- **Tags:** Black Holes, Rotating Solutions, Supergravity, Solution-Generating Techniques

## 4. Kerroll Black Holes
- **Authors:** Florian Ecker, Daniel Grumiller, Lucas Hörl, Mattéo Leturcq–Daligaux, Alfredo Pérez
- **arXiv ID:** 2605.15269
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15269
- **Categories:** hep-th
- **Summary:** Rotating black holes in Carroll gravity constructed via two distinct approaches. In one, the freedom in the Carroll compatible connection is exploited to encode rotation — constructing rotating solutions in magnetic Carroll gravity by dressing the Carroll-Schwarzschild black hole with a rotational charge. This solution is intrinsically Carrollian with no Lorentzian analog. In the other approach, an extension of magnetic Carroll gravity from general relativity in an odd-power expansion in the speed of light is constructed. This theory contains magnetic Carroll gravity as a subsector but has more physical degrees of freedom. It admits a Carroll analog of the Kerr black hole, referred to as the "Kerroll black hole," whose rotation appears as an intrinsically odd-power effect in the Carroll data. Conserved charges are computed for both theories.
- **Relevance:** The "Kerroll" pun aside, this is a genuinely interesting exploration of Carrollian gravity — a non-relativistic limit of GR that has gained attention in holography and flat-space gravity. The existence of a Carroll analog of Kerr suggests deeper structure in the Carrollian limit than previously appreciated.
- **Tags:** Carroll Gravity, Black Holes, Non-Relativistic Gravity, Kerr Geometry

## 5. A General Proof of Integer Rényi QNEC
- **Authors:** Tanay Kibe, Pratik Roy
- **arXiv ID:** 2605.15272
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15272
- **Categories:** hep-th, math-ph, math.OA, quant-ph
- **Summary:** Proof of the Rényi quantum null energy condition for all integer Rényi parameters n ≥ 2 for von Neumann algebras carrying a half-sided modular inclusion structure. The only assumption on the excited state is finiteness of its sandwiched Rényi divergence (SRD) relative to the vacuum. Concretely, for any σ-finite von Neumann algebra with such an inclusion, log-convexity is proved under the associated null-translation semigroup of the Kosaki Lⁿ norm of any normal positive functional with finite Lⁿ norm.
- **Relevance:** Technical but important. The QNEC is a fundamental constraint in quantum field theory and quantum gravity. Extending it to integer Rényi indices with minimal assumptions strengthens the toolbox for proving energy conditions in infinite-dimensional settings.
- **Tags:** Quantum Field Theory, Quantum Inequalities, Operator Algebras, Rényi Entropy

## 6. Dimensional Reduction of AdS₃ Chern-Simons Gravity
- **Authors:** Goffredo Chirco, Lucio Vacchiano, Patrizia Vitale
- **arXiv ID:** 2605.15293
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15293
- **Categories:** hep-th, gr-qc
- **Summary:** A symmetry-reduced sector of AdS₃/ℤ₂ gravity formulated as an SO(2,2) Chern–Simons theory on a 3D manifold with toroidal boundary. The reduction is implemented by requiring a globally defined symmetry and restricting to the sector where the gauge connection is invariant along the symmetry flow. The resulting theory reduces to a two-dimensional BF-like model together with an induced one-dimensional boundary action. Two inequivalent boundary sectors arise from different boundary conditions for the parent 3D theory at the level of the variational principle. On the boundary subspace A_τ = Φ, the universal one-dimensional action reproduces the standard Drinfel'd–Sokolov reduction in JT gravity capturing Schwarzian boundary dynamics. On the generalized boundary A_τ = λ'Φ + u⁻¹∂_τ u, the same action yields a deformed Schwarzian functional with affine residual symmetry, naturally associated with a non-extremal or Rindler-type regime.
- **Relevance:** Clean derivation of how 3D Chern-Simons gravity reduces to 2D BF + 1D boundary theory. The two boundary sectors — standard Schwarzian vs. deformed Schwarzian with affine symmetry — provide a nice organizing framework for understanding different near-boundary regimes.
- **Tags:** Chern-Simons Gravity, AdS/CFT, Schwarzian, JT Gravity, Dimensional Reduction

## 7. Anomalous Transport from Effective Field Theory
- **Authors:** Rémy Larue, Amaury Marchon, Jérémie Quevillon, Diego Saviot
- **arXiv ID:** 2605.15968
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15968
- **Categories:** hep-th, cond-mat.stat-mech, hep-ph
- **Summary:** Systematic study of chiral effects using an Effective Field Theory framework. By integrating out a massive Dirac fermion at finite temperature in presence of vector and axial background fields, the currents and their anomalies are computed from the path-integral. Chiral effects previously considered separately naturally arise in a unified computation, including new mass corrections. The link between each anomalous transport effect and the anomalies is clearly established beyond the identification of their coefficients. These effects are sourced by the anomalous nature of the theory even in configurations where the anomaly itself vanishes. Both consistent and covariant anomalies are encapsulated in master formulae for the currents. At finite temperature, the physical currents cannot be inferred simply from the Chern-Simons terms whose divergence reproduce the chiral anomalies.
- **Relevance:** Comprehensive EFT treatment of chiral anomalies and transport. The unified derivation with mass corrections, and the clarification that anomalous transport persists even when the anomaly vanishes locally, are valuable conceptual advances.
- **Tags:** Chiral Anomalies, Effective Field Theory, Transport Phenomena, Finite Temperature

## 8. Synthesis and Optimization of Encoding Circuits for Fault-Tolerant Quantum Computation
- **Authors:** Tom Peham, Matthew Steinberg, Robert Wille, Sascha Heußen
- **arXiv ID:** 2605.15266
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15266
- **Categories:** quant-ph, cs.ET
- **Summary:** Methods for synthesizing efficient encoders for arbitrary stabilizer codes. Encoder synthesis is formulated as a search over stabilizer tableaus, with greedy and rollout-based algorithms exploiting the freedom among stabilizer-equivalent realizations of the same encoding isometry. For code families with modular structure (generalized concatenated and holographic codes), large encoders are assembled from optimized local constituent encoders. SMT-based exact synthesis obtains optimal local circuits for small instances. Evaluated on holographic and quantum LDPC codes, obtaining improvements of up to 43% in two-qubit gate count and up to 70% in depth compared to recent methods and existing constructions. All methods are openly available as part of the Munich Quantum Toolkit.
- **Relevance:** Encoder synthesis is a critical bottleneck for fault-tolerant quantum computing. The 43% reduction in gate count and 70% reduction in depth are substantial practical improvements. Open-source availability in the Munich Quantum Toolkit is a plus.
- **Tags:** Quantum Computing, Fault Tolerance, Stabilizer Codes, Circuit Optimization

## 9. Towards Deploying Optimistic Quantum Fourier Transforms
- **Authors:** Pedro L. S. Lopes
- **arXiv ID:** 2605.15297
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15297
- **Categories:** quant-ph
- **Summary:** Architecture-algorithm co-design study of the Optimistic Quantum Fourier Transform (OQFT) under a surface-code fault-tolerant execution model for reconfigurable neutral-atom hardware. A hot-zone architecture decouples data storage from processing and dynamically routes mobile resource packages (magic-state factories, bridge qubits, and phase-gradient registers) to stationary data regions. Rotation insertions are routed via catalytic phase-gradient addition, and ripple-carry adders are heuristically micro-scheduled to patch-level moves. Under this model, Gidney and Cuccaro adders have similar space-time volume but require different parallelism levels. The five-layer OQFT shows a tunable parallelism/latency trade-off: two hot zones match serial-QFT latency, four hot zones roughly halve runtime, and additional hot zones asymptotically approach constant-time execution at substantial resource cost. Across 256-2048 bit instances, half-time performance requires about 500 additional logical ancillae and peak parallelism of 128 logical qubits.
- **Relevance:** Concrete co-design study bridging algorithmic structure with hardware architecture for a core quantum subroutine. The explicit resource estimates (500 ancillae, 128 logical qubits for half-time at 2048 bits) are valuable for planning.
- **Tags:** Quantum Computing, Quantum Fourier Transform, Surface Code, Neutral Atoms

## 10. Beyond Unitary Quantum Simulation
- **Authors:** Michael Marthaler, Elias Zapusek, Florentin Reiter
- **arXiv ID:** 2605.15277
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15277
- **Categories:** quant-ph
- **Summary:** Review discussing a broader perspective on quantum simulation motivated by the observation that naturally occurring quantum systems are rarely isolated and often reach physically relevant states only through relaxation, decoherence, and thermalization. Examines what is and is not known about exponential quantum advantage in chemistry, emphasizing that coherent Hamiltonian simulation provides the clearest formal case for speed-up while many open questions remain for realistic problems. Discusses how dissipation might be integrated into quantum chemistry on a fault-tolerant quantum computer, using recent proposals for chemically motivated dynamical simulation as a guiding vision. - **Relevance:** A timely corrective to the overemphasis on unitary ground-state simulation. The perspective that dissipation and thermalization are physically realistic and algorithmically useful is increasingly important as the field matures.

## 11. Measurement-Efficient VQLS for Carleman-Linearized Nonlinear Dynamics
- **Authors:** Yunya Liu, Pai Wang
- **arXiv ID:** 2605.15366
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15366
- **Categories:** quant-ph, physics.comp-ph
- **Summary:** Hybrid quantum-classical pipelines for solving the Duffing equation leveraging Carleman linearization and the Variational Quantum Linear Solver (VQLS). Carleman linearization accurately approximates the weakly nonlinear Duffing equation with errors diminishing as truncation order increases. VQLS is deployed across IBM and Xanadu platforms with symmetry-grouped Hadamard Test evaluations under both global and local cost formulations. Distinct Hermitianizations are compared within a common cost framework, and hardware-efficient ansatz architectures are benchmarked under a fixed Hermitianization. Near-unity fidelity and vanishing relative residuals are achieved across block-banded test cases. The results show that topology-agnostic ansatz, optimized Hermitianization, and efficient cost formulation enable VQLS to recover quantum states proportional to classical solutions for Carleman-structured systems.
- **Relevance:** Practical demonstration of solving nonlinear dynamics via quantum-classical hybrid methods. The cross-platform deployment (IBM and Xanadu) and near-unity fidelity results are promising for quantum-in-the-loop simulation.
- **Tags:** Quantum Computing, Variational Algorithms, Nonlinear Dynamics, Hybrid Quantum-Classical

## 12. Unveiling Magnetic Frustration via the Elastocaloric Effect
- **Authors:** Eric C. Andrade, Pedro M. Cônsoli, Matthias Vojta
- **arXiv ID:** 2605.15274
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15274
- **Categories:** cond-mat.str-el, cond-mat.stat-mech
- **Summary:** The thermodynamic response of frustrated magnets to uniaxial strain is examined. Ising and Heisenberg models on spatially anisotropic triangular (and kagome for Ising) lattices are studied. The entropy as a function of temperature and strain is determined, and the elastic Grüneisen ratio η is computed. The Ising models can be strain-tuned into and out of classical spin-liquid phases, and η can become arbitrarily large at low temperature near the point of maximal frustration — a universal hallmark of an extensive ground-state entropy. In contrast, the spin-1/2 Heisenberg model is moderately frustrated and displays multiple T=0 phase transitions. These transitions dominate η at low T while the intermediate-T behavior is similar to the Ising model.
- **Relevance:** The divergence of the Grüneisen ratio near maximal frustration is a clean thermodynamic signature that could guide experiments. The comparison between Ising and Heisenberg cases highlights how quantum fluctuations modify the frustration physics.
- **Tags:** Frustrated Magnetism, Spin Liquids, Thermodynamics, Elastocaloric Effect

## 13. Disorder-Driven Symmetry Suppression in a Magnetic Topological Insulator
- **Authors:** Rikkie Joris, Heyi Xia, Ana Beatriz Pedro Fontes, Seul-Ki Bac, Sara Bey, Jiaqi Zhou, Zviadi Zarkua, Ahmed Samir Lotfy, Muhammed Saad, Philippe Ohresser, Margriet van Bael, Clement Merckling, Xinyu Liu, Francisco Molina-Lopez, Jean-Christophe Charlier, Jin Won Seo, Badih A. Assaf, Lino M. C. Pereira
- **arXiv ID:** 2605.15332
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15332
- **Categories:** cond-mat.mtrl-sci
- **Summary:** Ion irradiation of the van der Waals magnetic topological insulator MnBi₂Te₄ modifies its magnetic and topological properties via collision cascades without chemical doping. Two regimes: at low fluence, cation antisite disorder leads to near-complete redistribution of Bi over cation sites while preserving long-range crystallographic order, accompanied by a transition from p-type to n-type transport. At high fluence, cation-anion intermixing drives formation of a previously unreported layer-disordered phase with a high density of van der Waals-specific planar defects including swapped bilayers. Despite significant structural disorder, partial periodic order persists. Magnetometry and X-ray spectroscopy show that the Mn high-spin state and antiferromagnetic interactions persist while magnetic anisotropy is strongly reduced. The anomalous Hall response is suppressed by over an order of magnitude, far exceeding the change in magnetization, indicating direct modification of Berry curvature.
- **Relevance:** Direct Berry-curvature engineering via defect creation in a magnetic topological insulator. The suppression of the anomalous Hall response by >10× while preserving the magnetic ground state is a remarkable degree of control.
- **Tags:** Topological Insulators, Berry Curvature, Defect Engineering, van der Waals Materials

## 14. Multifractal and Ergodic Properties of Conductance Fluctuations under Strong Disorder
- **Authors:** Marcos A. A. de Sousa, Heitor R. Publio, Henrique A. de Lima, Adauto J. F. de Souza, Fernando A. Oliveira, Anderson L. R. Barbosa
- **arXiv ID:** 2605.15447
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15447
- **Categories:** cond-mat.mes-hall, cond-mat.stat-mech
- **Summary:** Investigation of the multifractal and ergodic properties of the fictitious time series of conductance in two-dimensional tight-binding models under varying Anderson disorder. Using standard multifractal analysis, conductance fluctuations exhibit a transition from non-ergodic to ergodic behavior as disorder strength increases, as evidenced by the decay of the conductance correlation function. Multifractality persists in both regimes but becomes insensitive to shuffling in the strong-disorder (ergodic) regime, suggesting that distributional effects dominate temporal organization. In the weakly disordered (non-ergodic) regime, long-range correlations play a significant role. Results are robust against changes in lead geometry.
- **Relevance:** The persistence of multifractality across the ergodic transition, combined with the shift from temporal-correlation-dominated to distribution-dominated behavior, provides a nuanced picture of how quantum transport statistics evolve with disorder.
- **Tags:** Anderson Localization, Multifractality, Quantum Transport, Mesoscopic Physics

## 15. Fractal Analysis of Disordered Magnetic Systems
- **Authors:** Klaus P. Hermann, Ismael S. S. Carrasco, Jairo R. L. de Almeida, Fernando A. Oliveira
- **arXiv ID:** 2605.15610
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15610
- **Categories:** cond-mat.stat-mech
- **Summary:** Systematic investigation of magnetization, correlation functions, order parameter, and fractal dimensions in the Edwards-Anderson Hamiltonian as a function of disorder. The autocorrelation function for order-parameter fluctuations provides a framework for understanding the second-order phase transition at equilibrium. Recent work demonstrated that at T_c a fractal analysis is necessary for a complete description of the correlation function. Monte Carlo simulations validate analytical results and show how disorder alters critical exponents, giving rise to different universality classes. A subgroup of critical exponents and fractal dimensions is invariant with disorder, heralding a "strong universality class."
- **Relevance:** The coexistence of disorder-dependent critical exponents (weak universality) with a disorder-invariant subgroup (strong universality) is a subtle and important result for understanding the taxonomy of universality in disordered systems.
- **Tags:** Spin Glasses, Critical Phenomena, Fractals, Universality Classes
