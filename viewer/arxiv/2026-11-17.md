## 1. Geometric Quantization of the Giant Graviton Master Moduli Space
**Authors:** Kasia Budzik, Davide Gaiotto  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18539v1  
**Categories:** hep-th, math-ph
- **Abstract:** We describe a remarkable reorganization of the space of gauge invariant operators of free or twisted $U(N)$ gauge theories as combinations of single-trace and determinant operators modulo certain "open loop equations". The reorganization has a transparent holographic interpretation, describing how states at finite $N$ can be reproduced by imposing certain $N$-independent gauge constraints on the combination of the closed strings Fock space and a geometric quantization of the moduli space of Giant Graviton D-branes, with quantization parameter $N^{-1}$. The moduli space of Giant Graviton D-branes is reproduced from tachyon condensation of auxiliary D-brane/anti-D-brane pairs which are "Koszul dual" to the D-brane system which supports the original gauge theory. We describe how to impose the open loop equations via certain BRST complexes and conjecture that their homology recovers the finite $N$ BPS Hilbert space in the limit of large auxiliary rank. The BRST construction conjecturally categorifies Murthy's Giant Graviton Expansion of indices counting $U(N)$-invariant BPS operators.
- **Summary:** Budzik and Gaiotto reorganize finite-N gauge-invariant operators into trace and determinant sectors constrained by open-loop equations, giving a holographic description in terms of closed strings and geometrically quantized giant-graviton moduli. Their BRST construction proposes a route to recovering the BPS Hilbert space and categorifying the giant-graviton expansion, with the homological identification still conjectural.
**RELEVANCE:** Finite-N state counting and geometric quantization connect to Deepak’s quantum-gravity interests; this is holographic rather than loop quantum gravity.  
**TAGS:** `giant gravitons` `geometric quantization` `BRST cohomology`

## 2. Local Quantum Metrology: Precision Limits for Observers in QFT
**Authors:** Markus Fröb, Albert Much  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17948v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph, hep-th, math-ph
- **Abstract:** How precisely can an observer estimate a parameter related to a quantum field? This question is answered for quantum-mechanical systems using the SLD--Bures--Fisher metric and the quantum Cramér--Rao bound. However, local algebras in relativistic quantum field theory are of type III: they carry no trace and normal states have no density matrices, so the SLD quantum Fisher information and the quantum Cramér--Rao bound cannot be defined in the usual way. We define the SLD (Bures) quantum Fisher information on arbitrary von Neumann algebras through Uhlmann's transition probability and prove a quantum Cramér--Rao bound for collective measurements. We also show that the bound is sharp, and equals the largest classical Fisher information obtainable by measurements in the region.
- **Summary:** Extending estimation theory beyond density matrices, this work defines Bures quantum Fisher information on arbitrary von Neumann algebras using Uhlmann transition probability. It proves a sharp collective-measurement Cramér–Rao bound equal to the maximal classical Fisher information accessible within a region, making precision limits meaningful for type-III local QFT algebras.
**RELEVANCE:** Directly bridges quantum information and relativistic QFT, including operational questions about what local observers can measure.  
**TAGS:** `type-III algebras` `quantum Fisher information` `local metrology`

## 3. Anyon Proliferation at Self-Triality: From a Lattice Model to Adjoint QCD$_3$
**Authors:** Yuxuan Guo  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17724v1  
**Categories:** cond-mat.str-el, hep-th
- **Abstract:** We study how competitive proliferation among three types of anyons in a self-trial $\mathbb{Z}_2\times\mathbb{Z}_2$ gauge theory can drive symmetry breaking while preserving part of the topological order. We construct a deformation of the honeycomb color code, a spin model rooted in quantum error correction, with an $S_3$ symmetry that permutes the competing anyons, together with time-reversal and fermionic $\mathbb{Z}_2^{(1)}$ one-form symmetries. We propose two dual continuum descriptions of this transition. The bosonic description is a $\mathrm{PSU}(3)_3\times\mathrm{Spin}(8)_{-1}$ Chern-Simons-Higgs theory with a real Higgs field in the $(\mathbf{8}_{\mathrm{adj}},\mathbf{8}_v)$ representation. Fermion-boson duality gives a particularly simple fermionic dual: $\mathrm{PSU}(3)_{3/2}$ QCD$_3$ with a single adjoint Majorana fermion and gauged fermion parity. Both descriptions match the lattice model's unitary symmetries and proposed phase diagram, connecting double-toric-code order to a symmetry-broken phase with residual toric-code order. The transition is described by Higgs condensation and by a Majorana mass changing sign, respectively. The continuum theories are proposed to recover lattice time reversal at criticality. This connection to adjoint QCD$_3$ points to a possible continuous $S_3$-breaking transition from competing anyons, in contrast to the conventional first-order three-state Potts transition from competing local order parameters.
- **Summary:** A deformed honeycomb color code supplies a lattice setting where competition among three anyons can break permutation symmetry while retaining toric-code order. Proposed Chern–Simons–Higgs and adjoint-Majorana QCD3 duals match the unitary symmetries and phase structure, suggesting a potentially continuous transition unlike the conventional three-state Potts case; critical time-reversal recovery remains a proposal.
**RELEVANCE:** An unusually direct connection between topological quantum codes, anyon condensation, and strongly coupled high-energy field theory.  
**TAGS:** `anyon proliferation` `adjoint QCD3` `color codes`

## 4. Reconstruction of Dynamical Dark Energy and Gravity from latest cosmological data
**Authors:** Maria Luisa Gallavotti, Alessandra Silvestri  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18558v1  
**Categories:** astro-ph.CO, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** Recent cosmological observations have renewed interest in non-standard late-time evolution. We investigate whether departures from the standard expansion history are accompanied by modifications of gravitational dynamics through a joint, non-parametric reconstruction of the effective dark energy density, $Ω_{\rm X}(a)$, and the gravitational response functions $μ(a)$ and $Σ(a)$. Combining CMB, DESI DR2 BAO, Pantheon+, and large scale structure observations, including DES Year 3 $6\times2$pt data, we perform reconstructions with and without a theory motivated correlation prior derived from stable Horndeski models. We find a persistent maximum in $Ω_{\rm X}$ at $z\simeq0.5$--$0.7$, corresponding to a crossing of the effective phantom divide. This feature is already present in the CMB+BAO combination and remains robust when modifications to clustering and lensing are allowed. The reconstructed gravitational response exhibits suggestive enhancements in $μ$ and $Σ$ over approximately the same redshift range, although these departures are less significant. Including LSS information instead favours a reduced lensing response, $Σ<1$, while leaving the background feature largely unchanged. An interpretation within Generalized Brans-Dicke theories illustrates how modifications to the effective gravitational coupling and a scalar-mediated fifth force may contribute to these trends. Our results indicate that current departures from $Λ$CDM are most significant in the expansion history, while leaving open the possibility of correlated modifications to gravitational dynamics that future Stage IV surveys can test.
- **Summary:** A joint nonparametric reconstruction using CMB, DESI BAO, supernovae, and large-scale structure separates evolving dark energy from changes in clustering and lensing gravity, with and without a stable-Horndeski correlation prior. A dark-energy-density maximum near redshift 0.5–0.7 persists, whereas gravitational-response anomalies are weaker and lensing shifts downward with structure data, locating the strongest departure from ΛCDM in the expansion history rather than establishing modified gravity.
**RELEVANCE:** Connects observational cosmology to scalar–tensor gravity while carefully distinguishing robust background features from tentative gravitational signatures.  
**TAGS:** `dark-energy reconstruction` `Horndeski gravity` `large-scale structure`

## 5. Real-time simulation of scattering in $φ^4$ theory: localized excitation, scalable wave packet preparation, and peak-generating artifacts
**Authors:** Boyu Gao, Natalie Klco  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18552v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph, hep-lat, hep-ph, nucl-th
- **Abstract:** Probing real-time dynamics is essential for understanding non-perturbative scattering and interactions in quantum field theory. A common challenge in real-time simulation is preparing accurate asymptotic incoming states. Through efficiently solvable eigenvalue problems, we jointly optimize a superposition of single particle momentum eigenstates and a single site interpolating operator to maximize preparation fidelity from the interacting vacuum. This leads to a localized excitation in the interacting theory analogous to a position eigenstate. Weighted sums of the resulting interpolating operator over lattice sites prepare wave packets of arbitrary spatial extent. To ensure that this construction produces the correct continuum limit, relative phases among single particle momentum eigenstates in this superposition are calibrated using a relation derived from Lorentz symmetry. Analyzing single particle wave packet propagation through the position space profile reveals oscillatory Airy distortions, which originate from interference between saddle point contributions at different momenta that share the same lattice group velocity. These oscillations systematically weaken when approaching the continuum limit, and reliable regimes are identified as a function of simulation time and wave packet momentum profile. Two-particle scattering simulations in interacting scalar field theory are also implemented, revealing artificial inelasticity arising from coarse local field digitization or deviation from the Nyquist-Shannon sampling point. With these artifacts under control, present simulations provide emerging evidence for physical inelastic processes in $φ^4$ theory. The constructions introduced in this work may provide new understanding of state preparation for the quantum simulation of scattering.
- **Summary:** Optimizing interacting one-particle superpositions together with local interpolating operators gives a scalable wave-packet preparation scheme for real-time scalar-field scattering, with relative phases fixed using Lorentz symmetry. Propagation and collision simulations isolate lattice-induced Airy distortions and digitization-induced false inelasticity, identifying controlled regimes in which evidence for physical inelastic scattering begins to emerge.
**RELEVANCE:** Links quantum simulation to nonperturbative high-energy dynamics, emphasizing state preparation and artifact control needed for credible scattering calculations.  
**TAGS:** `quantum field simulation` `wave-packet preparation` `lattice artifacts`

## 6. Transfer-Matrix Calculus for Infinite Matrix Product States
**Authors:** Qi Yang, Philippe Corboz  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18427v1  
**Categories:** cond-mat.str-el, quant-ph
- **Abstract:** Long-range interactions, from the Coulomb repulsion between electrons to the power-law interactions of trapped ions and Rydberg atoms, are widespread in quantum many-body physics. For infinite systems, however, MPS algorithms, the standard classical reference in one dimension, conventionally rely on a computational compromise: replacing these interactions by fitted sums of exponentials. We develop transfer-matrix calculus (TMC), a unified framework for deriving infinite-MPS algorithms. In TMC, an interaction is written as a function of the transfer matrix, and one set of contraction rules then yields the effective Hamiltonians of DMRG and VUMPS, the tangent-space excitation action, and the energy variance, without an MPO. Long-range interactions thus enter with their prescribed distance dependence. For the half-filled lattice Coulomb chain with a $1/d$ interaction of strength $V$, theory predicts CDW order for all $V>0$, but at weak coupling this order is too weak for earlier numerics to resolve. Using TMC, we obtain infinite-MPS ground states at $V=2$ directly in the thermodynamic limit, with an energy variance per site of $2\times10^{-10}$. Our data, extrapolated to zero energy variance, indicate a small nonzero CDW amplitude, $m_0=0.002213(14)$, consistent with this prediction. The complete $V=2$ optimization workflow across the full series of bond dimensions takes less than a day on a single AMD MI250X GPU die. For an exactly solvable long-range hopping model, TMC reproduces the nonanalytic $|k|$ cusp of the one-particle dispersion that any fixed finite sum of decaying exponentials rounds off. We also use TMC to optimize first-quantized MPS directly in the thermodynamic limit. For half-filled free fermions, our entanglement entropy data show scaling consistent with central charge $c=1$ over the studied bond dimensions, as for second-quantized MPS.
- **Summary:** Transfer-matrix calculus represents long-range interactions as functions of the infinite-MPS transfer matrix, deriving ground-state, excitation, and variance contractions without fitted exponential interactions or an MPO. For the half-filled Coulomb chain at V=2 it resolves a small CDW amplitude of 0.002213(14), while a solvable hopping benchmark retains a nonanalytic dispersion cusp, demonstrating why preserving the interaction tail matters numerically.
**RELEVANCE:** A substantive many-body computational method for long-range systems, useful for classical benchmarks of quantum simulators and tensor-network studies.  
**TAGS:** `infinite MPS` `long-range interactions` `charge-density waves`

## 7. Efficient representations by PEPS: critical free-fermion and chiral topological states
**Authors:** Ignacio Cirac, Rahul Trivedi  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17936v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph
- **Abstract:** We construct projected entangled-pair state (PEPS) approximations to various many-body states in 2D lattices with global error at most $ε$ and bond dimension polynomial in the system size and $1/ε$. They include critical free-fermion models in two dimensions, as well as chiral topological states. For the two-dimensional nearest-neighbor tight-binding model at half filling, our construction combines Gaussian wavelet transformations with matrix product state approximations of the one-dimensional chains, as well as selected fermionic swap operations. For arbitrary uniformly gapped, finite-range quadratic Hamiltonians, we give a different construction based on repeated one-particle filtering, which yields Gaussian PEPS in any spatial dimension. Those include, for instance, $p+ip$ models or a Chern-insulator in a square lattice, for which we give an explicit construction. Finally, we prove that Gutzwiller projections of a fixed number of identical Gaussian PEPS approximations to free-fermion states at arbitrary filling do not amplify considerably the approximation error. This provides a polynomial-bond PEPS approximations to the resulting states, including familiar lattice chiral-spin-liquid states.
- **Summary:** Constructive tensor-network schemes give global-error-controlled, polynomial-bond PEPS approximations for critical two-dimensional free fermions and chiral topological states. Wavelet-based constructions and one-particle filtering cover distinct model classes, while an error-stability result for Gutzwiller projection extends the representations to familiar chiral spin liquids; the result concerns efficient representations, not a general efficient contraction algorithm.
**RELEVANCE:** Connects tensor-network expressivity, critical entanglement, and chiral topological matter across condensed matter and quantum information.  
**TAGS:** `PEPS approximations` `chiral spin liquids` `Gaussian filtering`

## 8. Structure makes quantum circuits trainable
**Authors:** Wenbo Xie, Wojciech Szpankowski, Ananth Grama  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18378v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph, cs.LG
- **Abstract:** The scalability of variational quantum algorithms is limited by barren plateaus, where gradients vanish exponentially in system size. This worst-case result raises fundamental questions about the trainability of quantum AI models. We present a major advance towards resolving this trainability question by answering whether a given quantum circuit has a barren plateau. We do this using an analytical framework that bounds the gradient variance for arbitrary input states $ρ$ and observables $\mathcal{M}$ by decomposing the unitary representation of the associated compact Lie group. When the circuit segments on both sides of a gate form $2$-designs, we obtain the variance exactly and prove a sufficient condition for trainability: if $ρ^{\otimes 2}$ and $\mathcal{M}^{\otimes 2}$ are sufficiently concentrated on the same irreducible invariant subspace of polynomial dimension, and the differentiated gate moves this subspace by an inverse-polynomial amount, then the gradient variance is at least inverse-polynomial. We further present a sampling algorithm that estimates the gradient variance from runs of the ansatz, without constructing the representation-theoretic projectors. Our algorithm decides whether the variance exceeds a tolerance $τ$ with the number of runs polynomial in $1/τ$, including cases in which the bounds are inconclusive. This resolves the question of the trainability of the circuit before expensive training procedures.
- **Summary:** A compact-Lie-group decomposition bounds circuit gradient variance for general input states and observables and makes it exact when the circuit segments satisfy two-design assumptions. Shared weight in a polynomial-dimensional invariant sector, together with sufficient gate action, yields a sufficient trainability condition; a sampling procedure also tests variance against a tolerance with cost polynomial in its inverse, providing an ansatz diagnostic rather than a guarantee of successful optimization.
**RELEVANCE:** Relevant to variational quantum computing and the structural origin of barren plateaus, with explicit assumptions separating rigorous guarantees from broad training claims.  
**TAGS:** `barren plateaus` `representation theory` `gradient variance`

## 9. Gravitational Corrections to OPE Coefficients
**Authors:** Ameya Chavda, Alessandro Podo, John Staunton  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18415v1  
**Categories:** hep-th
- **Abstract:** The short-distance singular behavior of correlation functions of quantum field theories in curved spacetime is a universal property shared by all well-behaved states (known as Hadamard states). For two-point functions this takes the form of the Hadamard tail of free scalars and its generalizations to spinning fields and interacting theories. In this work, we compute similar universal curvature corrections to curved-space OPE coefficients in a short-distance expansion for interacting quantum field theories in general dimensions. We work at leading order in the curvature but for arbitrary backgrounds. Examples include weakly coupled scalar field theories with cubic and quartic interactions as well as strongly coupled conformal field theories. The gravitationally dressed OPE coefficients allow us to test explicitly, and confirm, the validity of the smearing-in-time procedure discussed by Witten in the construction of the algebra of observables near an observer's worldline in curved spacetime.
- **Summary:** A leading-curvature short-distance expansion computes universal OPE corrections on arbitrary backgrounds for interacting theories, including weakly coupled scalars and strongly coupled conformal examples. These coefficients explicitly validate the proposed time-smearing procedure for observables near an observer’s worldline, tying curved-spacetime ultraviolet structure to an operational algebra construction.
**RELEVANCE:** Relevant to quantum fields on curved spacetime and observer-local observables in gravity; it is not a direct LQG calculation.  
**TAGS:** `curved-space OPE` `Hadamard structure` `observer algebras`

## 10. General $δN$ formalism in Single Field Inflation and Graph Theory
**Authors:** Amin Nassiri-Rad, Hassan Firouzjahi  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17978v1  
**Categories:** astro-ph.CO, gr-qc, hep-th
- **Abstract:** We study higher order correlation functions in single field inflation using the $δN$ formalism and methods from graph theory. By representing connected tree level contributions as graphs, we systematically classify their independent topologies, momentum shapes and amplitudes. We show that the amplitudes of arbitrary trees factorize into products of primary amplitudes, providing a general framework for relations among higher order correlators, including the Suyama-Yamaguchi equality. As the number of external legs increases, the number of distinct tree topologies grows exponentially, whereas the number of algebraically distinct amplitude products grows considerably more slowly. As an application, we consider ultra-slow-roll inflation followed by a transition to a slow-roll attractor. For transitions without a potential step, we derive an explicit all-order expression for the vertex amplitudes and establish their positivity and dependence on the sharpness of the transition. When a potential step is included, we prove that all nonlinear vertex amplitudes remain positive for upward steps, whereas downward steps allow cancellations at specific parameter values. These cancellations can eliminate entire families of tree contributions, including configurations in which the leading local bispectrum and trispectrum vanish simultaneously while higher order correlations remain nonzero.
- **Summary:** Graph-theoretic organization of tree-level δN contributions shows that higher-point amplitudes factor into primary amplitudes, reducing the independent algebraic data despite exponentially many graph topologies. Applying the framework to ultra-slow-roll transitions gives all-order vertex results and shows that downward potential steps can cancel the leading local bispectrum and trispectrum while leaving higher correlations nonzero, exposing signatures missed by low-order diagnostics.
**RELEVANCE:** Provides systematic tools for primordial non-Gaussianity and inflationary model discrimination beyond the usual three- and four-point functions.  
**TAGS:** `delta-N formalism` `primordial non-Gaussianity` `ultra-slow-roll inflation`

## 11. Linear monitoring-feedback Model for Linearized gravity
**Authors:** Daisuke Miki, Alfred Li, Yiqiu Ma, Yubao Liu, Yanbei Chen  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17642v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** Whether gravity has quantum degrees of freedom is a question of fundamental importance. We formulate a linear monitoring-feedback framework for gravitational interactions that includes local classical-record models of semiclassical gravity and, when quantum resources are supplied in correlated ancilla inputs, can reproduce linearized quantum-gravity dynamics. Auxiliary systems monitor the positions of massive bodies, and their records are fed back through a chosen gravitational response kernel. At the level of linear input-output theory, this construction makes the deterministic response explicit while exposing the force noise and correlations associated with the auxiliary sector. We organize the allowed ancilla correlations into three regimes. In Regime-I, independent ancillas have unsqueezed, uncorrelated sensing and back-action quadratures. A single object then has a minimum classical-gravity noise (CGN), S_F^min = 2 hbar M Omega_SN^2, where Omega_SN is the Schrodinger-Newton frequency set by the microscopic mass distribution. In Regime-II, arbitrary local sensing--back-action correlations are allowed, but the ancillas associated with different objects remain independent. The single-object CGN is then unrestricted, while the joint force-noise matrix obeys a determinant bound that is equivalent to the local classical-channel/no-entanglement bound. In Regime-III, cross-object correlations and a common nonlocal mediator are allowed. The commuting force noises can then be jointly squeezed, entanglement between the masses can occur, and--with a suitable retarded response and input state--the monitoring-feedback system can reproduce the noise and commutators of linearized quantum gravity, including non-Markovian quantum fluctuations.
- **Summary:** A linear monitoring-and-feedback description separates a gravitational response kernel from auxiliary force noise and classifies models by allowed ancilla correlations. Independent local ancillas impose single-body or joint noise bounds depending on sensing correlations, whereas nonlocal quantum resources can permit entanglement and reproduce linearized quantum-gravity noise and commutators, clarifying which conclusions genuinely require a quantum mediator.
**RELEVANCE:** Directly addresses experimental tests of gravity’s quantum character and the assumptions behind classical-channel entanglement bounds, without claiming a full quantum-gravity theory.  
**TAGS:** `gravity-mediated entanglement` `measurement feedback` `force-noise bounds`

## 12. Chip-Based Discrete-Modulated Continuous-Variable Quantum Key Distribution with Uncertainty-Principle-Based Composable Security
**Authors:** Jiale Mi, Yiming Bian, Zhengyu Li, Xuesong Xu, Song Yu, Yichen Zhang, Hong Guo  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18288v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph
- **Abstract:** Continuous-variable quantum key distribution offers a promising platform for scalable quantum networks through its compatibility with standard telecommunications technology and photonic integration. High-order discrete modulation facilitates practical implementation while approaching the key-rate performance of ideal Gaussian modulation, but its finite-size composable security analysis entails substantial computational overhead. Here, we develop an uncertainty-principle-based security framework that enables finite-size composable key-rate evaluation against collective attacks for constellations up to 256-QAM. By introducing a heterodyne-measured ancillary mode into the entanglement-based source description, we incorporate additional source correlations into the security analysis that allow the Heisenberg uncertainty principle alone to provide a tight bound for the unknown correlations in a covariance matrix characterizing the whole system. Matrix symmetrization further reduces the key-rate optimization to a one-dimensional search, while subsequent mode reduction yields an analytical lower bound requiring no numerical search. Experimental demonstrations using 256-QAM on discrete-component and silicon-photonic-receiver platforms achieve finite-size composable secret key rates of 17.436 and 0.519 Mbps, respectively, over a 50 km fiber link. This approach facilitates the practical deployment of high-speed, real-time and compact quantum key distribution systems. Beyond quantum key distribution, the principle of tightening uncertainty-constrained bounds via source-mode expansion offers a paradigm for exploring the information-theoretic properties of complex non-Gaussian systems.
- **Summary:** An ancillary-mode extension of the source description turns uncertainty constraints into tractable finite-size security bounds for discrete-modulated continuous-variable QKD, reducing optimization to one dimension or an analytic lower bound. Experiments with 256-QAM report composable secret-key rates of 17.436 Mbps on discrete components and 0.519 Mbps with a silicon-photonic receiver over 50 km, under collective-attack assumptions, connecting a security-analysis improvement to integrated hardware.
**RELEVANCE:** Balances foundational quantum-information results with a concrete photonic quantum-network demonstration and clearly specified security scope.  
**TAGS:** `continuous-variable QKD` `composable security` `silicon photonics`
