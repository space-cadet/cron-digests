## 1. Spectral Localization Principle for Entanglement Harvesting
- **Authors:** Hao Xu
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.13449
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13449
- **Categories:** gr-qc, hep-th, quant-ph
- **Abstract:** We propose a unified physical principle for entanglement harvesting: the entanglement that two localized detectors can extract from a quantum field is determined solely by how localized the field's effective spectral density is. We demonstrate this in an analytically solvable model of two qubits coupled to a leaky single-mode cavity, which in turn couples to a continuous electromagnetic bath, and derive the maximum harvestable concurrence in closed form, $\mathcal{C}_{\max}(Q)=2e^{-\pi/(2Q)}(1+e^{-\pi/(2Q)})/(1+3e^{-\pi/Q})$, where $Q\equiv|\Delta|/\kappa$ is the ratio of the qubit-cavity detuning $\Delta$ to the cavity linewidth $\kappa$. In the high-$Q$ limit, $\mathcal{C}_{\max}\simeq1-\pi^{2}/(16Q^{2})$, so the entanglement is robust against cavity loss; in the low-$Q$ limit it decays exponentially to zero, consistent with the irreversible-reservoir character of a continuous field, where maximal entanglement is unattainable. Since $Q$ is proportional to the inverse participation ratio (IPR) of the effective spectral density, it is the single dimensionless parameter governing the crossover from deterministic gate-based entanglement ($Q\to\infty$) to vacuum harvesting ($Q\to0$). Our framework operationalizes the Reeh-Schlieder theorem by quantifying the fraction of vacuum correlations accessible to localized detectors. It also reveals a formal correspondence of the maximal concurrence with the IPR, analogous to the conductivity-participation-ratio relation in Anderson localization. The predicted $\mathcal{C}_{\max}(Q)$ curve is, in principle, directly observable in superconducting circuit QED experiments.
- **Summary:** This work identifies a single dimensionless parameter — the inverse participation ratio of the effective spectral density — as the universal controller of how much entanglement two localized detectors can extract from a quantum field. By solving an analytically tractable cavity-bath model exactly, the author derives a closed-form expression for the maximum concurrence and shows that it interpolates between deterministic gate-based entanglement (when the spectral density is sharply peaked) and vacuum harvesting (when it is broad). The result transforms the Reeh-Schlieder theorem from an abstract statement about vacuum correlations into a quantitative bound that can be tested in circuit QED experiments.
- **Relevance:** Entanglement harvesting and the Reeh-Schlieder theorem are foundational concepts for understanding quantum correlations in curved spacetime and quantum gravity. The connection to Anderson localization via the participation ratio suggests deep structural parallels between quantum field theory and condensed matter that may inform quantum geometry approaches.
- **Tags:** Entanglement Harvesting, Quantum Field Theory, Reeh-Schlieder Theorem

## 2. Thermal false vacuum decay near black holes is aspherical
- **Authors:** D. S. Gorbunov, D. G. Levkov, V. E. Maslov
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.12469
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12469
- **Categories:** gr-qc, hep-th, astro-ph.CO, hep-ph
- **Abstract:** We study decay of a scalar field false vacuum near a (3+1)-dimensional Schwarzschild black hole equilibrated at Hawking temperature with the environment. Our scalar field model has negative quartic self-coupling and thereby resembles Higgs sector of the Standard Model in the large-field limit. We demonstrate that if the black hole is not too small, the false vacuum in this model decays aspherically with regard to the black hole center: via formation of expanding true vacuum bubbles emerging on the outer side of the event horizon. More specifically, we identify three regimes of the decay. For the largest and coldest black holes, the main mechanism is quantum tunneling described by an infinitesimally thin bounce sitting at some point of the horizon. In the intermediate-mass regime, the vacuum is destroyed by thermal fluctuations creating aspherical critical bubbles in the horizon vicinity. Finally, near the smallest black holes thermal fluctuations still guide the decay but the dominant critical bubble is spherically symmetric and covers the entire horizon.
- **Summary:** The authors show that false vacuum decay near a Schwarzschild black hole is fundamentally aspherical, with true vacuum bubbles preferentially nucleating on the exterior side of the event horizon rather than forming symmetrically around it. They map out three distinct regimes controlled by black hole mass and temperature: quantum tunneling dominates for large cold black holes, thermal fluctuations create aspherical bubbles for intermediate masses, and only for the smallest black holes does spherical symmetry return. This means that a black hole acts as a catalyst that breaks the spatial symmetry of vacuum decay — a dramatic departure from flat-space Coleman-de Luccia bubbles.
- **Relevance:** False vacuum decay in strong gravitational fields is a key process in early universe cosmology and may have observational consequences for primordial black holes. The aspherical nature of the decay near horizons has implications for understanding how quantum fields behave in the strong-field regime, which is central to quantum gravity phenomenology.
- **Tags:** False Vacuum Decay, Black Hole Physics, Quantum Tunneling

## 3. Dymnikova Black Hole Tidal Forces
- **Authors:** M. H. Macêdo, A. A. M. Silva, R. R. Landim
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.12495
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12495
- **Categories:** gr-qc, hep-th
- **Abstract:** In this work we investigate the tidal properties of the Dymnikova regular black hole and their effects on massive particles in radial free-fall. Starting from Dymnikova static, spherically symmetric solution, we derive the equations governing timelike radial geodesics and construct an orthonormal tetrad adapted to a free-falling observer. We then obtain the radial and angular components of the tidal tensor and analyze their dependence on the black hole mass and the characteristic length scale of the de Sitter core. At large radial distances, tidal forces recover Schwarzschild behavior, whereas near the regular center, both components remain finite, reflecting the non-singular nature of the spacetime. We show that the radial and angular tidal forces vanish and change sign at characteristic radii inside the event horizon, indicating transitions between stretching and compression regimes. A particle released from rest outside the event horizon reaches a turnaround point located inside the Cauchy horizon, rather than reaching the regular center. We also solve the geodesic deviation equations for two sets of initial conditions and examine the evolution of the radial and transverse components of the deviation vector. Although the solutions asymptotically reproduce Schwarzschild behavior, they differ significantly in the inner region: the deviation vector components remain finite up to the turnaround point, whereas the corresponding radial component in the Schwarzschild case diverges at the singularity. These results demonstrate how the de Sitter core regularizes the tidal dynamics of extended falling bodies.
- **Summary:** The authors compute the tidal tensor for free-falling observers in the Dymnikova regular black hole — a model with a de Sitter core that replaces the singularity with a smooth interior. They find that tidal forces remain finite everywhere, vanish and change sign at characteristic radii inside the horizon (creating transitions between stretching and compression), and cause infalling particles to reach a turnaround point rather than the center. Geodesic deviation remains bounded throughout, in stark contrast to Schwarzschild where the radial deviation diverges at the singularity. This provides a concrete dynamical signature distinguishing regular black holes from singular ones.
- **Relevance:** Regular black holes with de Sitter cores are motivated by semiclassical considerations and share qualitative features with black hole interiors in loop quantum gravity, where bounce and regularization effects are expected. The tidal dynamics computed here offers observable predictions that could distinguish regular from singular spacetimes in future gravitational wave or black hole imaging observations.
- **Tags:** Regular Black Holes, Tidal Forces, Singularity Resolution

## 4. Quantum-Corrected Thermodynamics and Phase Structure of AdS Euler-Heisenberg Black Hole
- **Authors:** Siddhartha Sankar Borah, Dhruba Jyoti Gogoi, Kalyan Bhuyan
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.13378
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13378
- **Categories:** gr-qc, hep-th
- **Abstract:** We investigate the thermodynamic behaviour of an AdS black hole arising from nonlinear electrodynamics-corrected gravity, incorporating quantum effects through thermal fluctuations. From the Einstein--Euler--Heisenberg framework, we consider the modified black hole solution and analyse its thermodynamic properties in the extended phase space. Logarithmic and inverse-area corrections to the entropy are obtained, leading to modified expressions for enthalpy, internal energy, Helmholtz free energy and Gibbs free energy. The corrected specific heat exhibits multiple divergences and sign changes, signalling genuine second-order phase transitions and revealing a quantum-stabilized microscopic phase followed by universal macroscopic instability. Our results demonstrate that thermal fluctuations qualitatively restructure the thermodynamic phase space and highlight the dominant role of quantum corrections in governing the black hole stability.
- **Summary:** This study examines how quantum thermal fluctuations modify the thermodynamics of an AdS black hole in Einstein-Euler-Heisenberg gravity. By including logarithmic and inverse-area corrections to the Bekenstein-Hawking entropy, the authors find that the specific heat develops multiple divergences and sign changes, indicating a sequence of second-order phase transitions. A striking result is the emergence of a quantum-stabilized microscopic phase that is absent classically, followed by a universal macroscopic instability. The quantum corrections thus fundamentally restructure the phase diagram, suggesting that black hole thermodynamics is far richer than the classical picture admits.
- **Relevance:** Quantum corrections to black hole thermodynamics are a central frontier in quantum gravity. The phase structure revealed here — with quantum-stabilized phases and multiple transitions — has direct parallels to ongoing investigations of black hole phase transitions in AdS/CFT and may inform how quantum geometry modifies thermodynamic behavior in loop quantum gravity.
- **Tags:** Black Hole Thermodynamics, Quantum Corrections, Phase Transitions

## 5. Quasitopological Gravity with Matter: Modified Double-Copy Approach
- **Authors:** Valeri P. Frolov
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.12596
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12596
- **Categories:** gr-qc, hep-th
- **Abstract:** We extend the recently proposed modified double-copy formalism to quasitopological gravity (QTG) coupled to matter. For spherically symmetric configurations, the QTG field equations in $D-$dimensional curved spacetime with a broad class of matter sources are mapped to equations for an auxiliary nonlinear gauge field in a flat $(D+1)$-dimensional spacetime. The nonlinear electrodynamics governing this auxiliary field is determined entirely by the generating function $h(p)$ that specifies the QTG model, while the corresponding current is determined by the matter stress-energy tensor. Restricting the auxiliary solution to a $D$-dimensional hyperplane and applying the modified double-copy prescription yields the Kerr--Schild metric solving the QTG equations. We show that Maxwell and nonlinear electrodynamics, as well as a broad class of spherically symmetric Yang--Mills fields, provide physical matter sources compatible with this construction. In the absence of null currents, the resulting solutions satisfy a generalized Birkhoff theorem and are static, whereas null charged currents naturally generate Vaidya-type solutions. In the Einstein limit, $h(p)=p$, the auxiliary nonlinear electrodynamics reduces to Maxwell theory.
- **Summary:** The author extends the color-kinematics double-copy framework — originally connecting Yang-Mills theory to gravity — to quasitopological gravity coupled to matter. The key construction maps QTG field equations in D dimensions to a nonlinear gauge theory in a flat (D+1)-dimensional auxiliary spacetime, from which Kerr-Schild metrics are recovered by restriction. This provides a systematic way to generate exact solutions in higher-curvature gravity from known gauge theory solutions, and naturally produces both static geometries (via a generalized Birkhoff theorem) and radiating Vaidya-type solutions.
- **Relevance:** The double-copy program is one of the most promising structural insights connecting gauge theory and gravity. Extending it to quasitopological gravity — a higher-curvature theory with cleaner quantum properties than generic f(R) models — opens new avenues for constructing exact solutions and understanding how the gravity-gauge theory correspondence generalizes beyond Einstein gravity.
- **Tags:** Double-Copy, Quasitopological Gravity, Exact Solutions

## 6. Holographic Renormalization for String-Derived Lovelock--Horndeski Theory
- **Authors:** Tianhao Wu
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.13319
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13319
- **Categories:** gr-qc, hep-th
- **Abstract:** String-derived higher-curvature scalar--tensor gravities encode microscopic coupling data in boundary response, raising the question of whether holographic observables can reconstruct the underlying higher-dimensional parameters. We answer this question for the five-dimensional string-derived Lovelock--Horndeski (SDLH) theory on its exact linear-dilaton asymptotically locally AdS branch, constructing the renormalized generating functional for an arbitrary boundary metric and spacetime-dependent scalar source. A boundary-covariant radial hierarchy unifies the variational problem, local backreaction, logarithmic obstruction, finite one-point functions, and Ward identities. Two response determinants organize the recursion, resonant obstructions, and metric--scalar mixing. The Weyl anomaly condenses into an Euler density, a Weyl-squared density, and a single curvature--scalar square whose paired variations generate the metric and scalar obstructions. The resulting renormalized functional carries string-selected coupling data into boundary geometry, operator response, anomaly coefficients, and a calculable interface with gravitational observables. On the regular branch, four scalar-normalization-invariant holographic combinations admit a global rational inverse to the continuous reduced couplings. At fixed compactification dimension the map has maximal rank, while the curvature-anomaly sum reconstructs the higher-dimensional Gauss--Bonnet coefficient without sign ambiguity. Holographic response thus provides an explicit, overdetermined boundary fingerprint of the underlying string reduction.
- **Summary:** This work constructs the complete holographic renormalization for a five-dimensional string-derived Lovelock-Horndeski theory and shows that boundary observables — including the Weyl anomaly, one-point functions, and response coefficients — contain enough information to reconstruct the higher-dimensional coupling constants. The key result is that four scalar-normalization-invariant holographic combinations admit a rational inverse to the bulk couplings, and the Gauss-Bonnet coefficient can be unambiguously extracted from the curvature-anomaly sum. This establishes that holographic response serves as a "fingerprint" of the underlying string compactification.
- **Relevance:** Holographic renormalization in higher-curvature scalar-tensor theories is essential for connecting string theory compactifications to observable boundary physics. The reconstruction of bulk parameters from boundary data has implications for understanding how quantum gravity imprints itself on low-energy physics, with relevance to both AdS/CFT and emergent gravity approaches.
- **Tags:** Holographic Renormalization, Higher-Curvature Gravity, String Theory

## 7. On Super-Hyperbolic Wormhole Geometries and their Deformations
- **Authors:** Subrat Parida
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.12412
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12412
- **Categories:** gr-qc, hep-th, math-ph
- **Abstract:** In this study, we developed a unified geometric and relativistic framework for a new family of traversable wormholes constructed from a super-hyperbolic deformation of the classical catenoid, whose geometric embedding combines an explicit local curvature parameter $\kappa$, which secures a regular throat, with the Mittag-Leffler-based super-hyperboloid deformation function ${\mathfrak{S}C}_{\alpha} (v)$, where the deformation parameter $\alpha>1$ governs the radial onset and far-field growth of the embedding beyond the throat, continuously approaching the classical catenoidal geometry in the boundary limit $\alpha\rightarrow1^{+}$. Allowing a non-constant redshift $\Phi(r)$, we systematically evaluate closed analytical expressions for the shape function, curvature tensors, matter sources, and junction conditions, culminating in an exact thin-shell equation of motion and stability criterion. This work establishes a mathematically consistent and physically viable framework for the extension of wormhole theory, providing a new avenue for exploring exotic geometries within general relativity and identifying the conditions under which traversable configurations remain locally stable.
- **Summary:** The author constructs a new family of traversable wormholes by deforming the classical catenoid embedding using a Mittag-Leffler-based super-hyperbolic function, introducing a deformation parameter that controls the throat geometry and asymptotic behavior. For this family, exact analytical expressions are derived for the shape function, curvature tensors, matter sources, and junction conditions, leading to a closed-form thin-shell equation of motion and a stability criterion. The construction is mathematically consistent and provides a tunable framework for studying exotic geometries while identifying parameter regimes where traversability and stability coexist.
- **Relevance:** Traversable wormholes remain a theoretical laboratory for understanding exotic matter, topological effects in gravity, and the limits of classical general relativity. The Mittag-Leffler deformation introduces fractional-calculus-inspired structures that may connect to ongoing work on non-local gravity and quantum gravitational effective descriptions.

## 8. A Lapse in the Cosmological Constant Problem with Bulk Dynamics
- **Authors:** Justin Khoury, Benjamin Muntz, Antonio Padilla
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.12460
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12460
- **Categories:** gr-qc, hep-th
- **Abstract:** We have previously proposed a new approach to the cosmological constant problem based on anisotropic scaling in a compact extra dimension. In the ultra-local $z=0$ limit, the interplay between a projectable lapse, foliation-preserving diffeomorphisms, and higher-form fluxes renders the gravitational field equations insensitive to radiative corrections to the matter vacuum energy. Here we extend this framework beyond the ultra-local limit by introducing $z=1$ deformations that restore dynamics along the compact direction. We show that vacuum-energy cancellation persists at the level of the background equations, while generic extrinsic-curvature couplings propagate an additional scalar ghost. A healthy quadratic spectrum selects the Fierz-Pauli relation between these couplings. This motivates a particularly simple realization in terms of five-dimensional Einstein gravity, a top-form flux, and a spacelike khoron scalar field that dynamically defines a preferred projectable foliation. In this covariant formulation, the global constraint arises from an auxiliary einbein on the space of khoron leaves, and constant shifts of the renormalized matter vacuum energy cancel algebraically from the intrinsic Einstein equations. Finally, allowing matter to propagate around the compact dimension generates finite, topology-sensitive Casimir contributions that are not automatically removed by the mechanism. Suppressing these contributions requires additional spectral conditions on the propagating bulk degrees of freedom.
- **Summary:** The authors extend their novel approach to the cosmological constant problem — which uses anisotropic scaling in a compact extra dimension to render Einstein equations insensitive to vacuum energy — by introducing dynamical deformations that restore propagation along the compact direction. They show that vacuum-energy cancellation survives at the background level, but avoiding a scalar ghost requires the Fierz-Pauli tuning of extrinsic-curvature couplings. The construction is realized covariantly via a five-dimensional Einstein-gravity-plus-flux model with a spacelike "khoron" field that dynamically selects a preferred foliation, making the vacuum-energy cancellation manifest as an algebraic cancellation in the intrinsic equations.
- **Relevance:** The cosmological constant problem remains one of the deepest unsolved problems in theoretical physics. This framework offers a genuinely new mechanism — distinct from supersymmetry, anthropic reasoning, or modified gravity — and its extension to dynamical bulk theories brings it closer to physical realizability. The connection to Horava-Lifshitz-type anisotropic scaling may also inform approaches to quantum gravity with emergent Lorentz symmetry.
- **Tags:** Cosmological Constant Problem, Extra Dimensions, Anisotropic Scaling

## 9. Cosmological Vacuum Decays from Schwinger-Keldysh Formalism
- **Authors:** Zi-Yan Yuwen
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.12736
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12736
- **Categories:** gr-qc, hep-th, hep-ph
- **Abstract:** In this work, we establish a systematic framework to describe the vacuum decays in a radiation-dominated FLRW universe from the Schwinger-Keldysh formalism. By splitting the phase transition field $\Phi$ into the mean field $\phi$ and the short-wavelength modes $\sigma$ and tracing over the latter as the environment, we obtain a classical Langevin-type equation-of-motion for the mean field $\phi$, where the quantum effects are encoded in a non-Markov memory kernel and a non-Gaussian noise. As a phenomenological example, we consider a polynomial potential and study the structure of the memory kernel as well as the correlation functions of the noise term. With a less restrictive scale split by allowing $\phi$ to carry spatial dependence, further extensions remain possible to describe the whole dynamics of cosmological first-order phase transitions via numerical simulations.
- **Summary:** The author develops a Schwinger-Keldysh-based open quantum systems framework for cosmological vacuum decay, in which the phase-transition field is split into a mean field and short-wavelength fluctuations that are traced over as an environment. The resulting effective dynamics for the mean field takes the form of a classical Langevin equation with a non-Markovian memory kernel and non-Gaussian quantum noise, capturing how quantum correlations feed back into the classical evolution of the order parameter. This provides a systematic starting point for simulating first-order phase transitions in the early universe beyond the usual Markovian approximations.
- **Relevance:** First-order phase transitions in the early universe are important for baryogenesis, gravitational wave production, and primordial black hole formation. The Schwinger-Keldysh approach offers a more rigorous treatment of quantum effects during these transitions than standard effective potential methods, with potential applications to quantum field theory in curved spacetime.
- **Tags:** Vacuum Decay, Schwinger-Keldysh, Cosmological Phase Transitions

## 10. Microscopic Interaction versus Purely Gravitational Coupling in Strange Quark Stars Admixed with Dark Matter: A One-Fluid and Two-Fluid Comparison
- **Authors:** J. Sedaghat, G. H. Bordbar, M. Haghighat, S. M. Zebarjad
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.12383
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12383
- **Categories:** gr-qc, hep-th, hep-ph, astro-ph.HE
- **Abstract:** In this study, we investigate strange quark stars (SQSs) admixed with scalar dark matter (DM), focusing on the role of microscopic interactions versus purely gravitational coupling. Our results demonstrate that while the interacting one-fluid model yields viable configurations with M_TOV > 2M_sun that satisfy current pulsar M-R measurements alongside the tidal deformability (Lambda) constraints from GW170817, the noninteracting two-fluid model more robustly meets both existing GW170817 limits and the tighter Lambda bounds anticipated from next-generation gravitational-wave detectors.
- **Summary:** The authors compare two approaches to modeling strange quark stars mixed with scalar dark matter: an interacting one-fluid model where dark matter and quark matter are microscopically coupled, and a noninteracting two-fluid model where they only interact gravitationally. While both models can produce massive stars exceeding two solar masses, the noninteracting two-fluid model better satisfies the tidal deformability constraints from GW170817 and is more compatible with the tighter bounds expected from next-generation detectors. This suggests that gravitational coupling alone may be sufficient to describe dark matter admixture in compact stars without invoking additional microscopic interactions.
- **Relevance:** Compact stars admixed with dark matter represent a novel astrophysical probe of dark matter properties. The comparison between interacting and noninteracting models provides guidance for interpreting future gravitational wave and pulsar observations, and connects to broader questions about how dark matter behaves in strong gravitational fields.
- **Tags:** Strange Quark Stars, Dark Matter, Gravitational Wave Constraints
