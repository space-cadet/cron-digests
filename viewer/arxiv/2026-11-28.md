## 1. Rényi multi-entropy from Riemann surfaces
**Authors:** Kenya Tasuki  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34715v1  
**Categories:** hep-th, cond-mat.stat-mech, math-ph, quant-ph
- **Abstract:** We compute vacuum tripartite Rényi multi-entropy in a two-dimensional compact-boson conformal field theory (CFT). For every integer Rényi index $n\geq2$, we evaluate the partition function on the replica Riemann surface for disjoint intervals, obtaining the twist four-point function in terms of a ratio of theta functions. An independent calculation of the partition function on a Fermat curve yields the adjacent three-point coefficient. In the fusion limit, the Riemann surface for disjoint intervals degenerates into two Fermat curves, and the residue of the four-point function reproduces the squared three-point coefficient, as required by the operator product expansion. The genuine multi-entropy therefore approaches twice its strictly adjacent value. The CFT predictions agree with lattice calculations for the critical XXZ and Ising spin chains and a harmonic chain. We also exhibit a unitary CFT in which the canonical replica construction yields complex Rényi multi-entropy.
- **Summary:** Replica-surface partition functions give exact integer-index tripartite multi-entropies in the compact-boson CFT, with independent Fermat-curve calculations checking the fusion coefficients. Agreement with critical-chain numerics supports the construction, while a complex-valued example exposes a limitation of interpreting canonical replica multi-entropy as an ordinary real correlation measure.
**RELEVANCE:** Connects Deepak's quantum-information and many-body interests through exact CFT geometry and lattice benchmarks.  
**TAGS:** `replica Riemann surfaces` `tripartite multi-entropy` `critical spin chains`

## 2. High-Throughput GPU Decoding of Quantum Bivariate Bicycle Codes under Circuit-Level Noise
**Authors:** Aditya Sodhani, Keshab K. Parhi  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34481v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph, cs.AR
- **Abstract:** Bivariate bicycle (BB) codes reduce the physical-qubit overhead of quantum error correction compared to other codes such as Surface codes, but circuit-level faults create dependencies across measurement rounds that limit decoding throughput. We present a parallel-window GPU decoder that combines reusable fault patterns, compact shared-memory state, and persistent GPU workers using min-sum with Relay-BP recovery. On an RTX 5090, the proposed decoder keeps pace with one syndrome round every 1 $μ$s across all four evaluated codes, up to [[144,12,12]] with 63-144 $μ$s median commit delays. This rate has not been demonstrated in prior circuit-level GPU decoding studies and it provides $120\times$ higher decoding throughput than sequential sliding-window decoding. Checking neighboring detector rounds before accepting a decoding result reduces failure rates by up to 65%. For [[144,12,12]] at $p=0.002$, our proposed decoder achieves $7.58\times 10^{-6}$ failures per round, 36% lower than the published belief propagation (BP) with syndrome flipping (BP-SF) result.
- **Summary:** A parallel-window decoder combines reusable circuit-fault patterns with persistent GPU workers and min-sum decoding supplemented by Relay-BP recovery. On an RTX 5090 it sustains one syndrome round per microsecond through the 144-qubit bicycle code, with 63–144 microsecond median commit delays, separating high throughput from feedback latency while improving reported failure rates.
**RELEVANCE:** Relevant to Deepak's fault-tolerant computing interests, especially the classical-processing bottleneck of high-rate quantum codes.  
**TAGS:** `bivariate bicycle codes` `GPU decoding` `circuit-level noise`

## 3. Gravitational collapse to extremal black holes with $Λ>0$: counterexamples to the third law without charge
**Authors:** Mónica Tapia del Moral  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34542v1  
**Categories:** gr-qc, math-ph, math.AP, math.DG
- **Abstract:** We study the dynamical formation of the extremal Schwarzschild-de Sitter solution, in both its time orientations, and the related Nariai solution. We prove that there exist spherically symmetric, regular Cauchy data for the Einstein-scalar field system with $\mathbb S^3$ topology, for any scalar field mass $\mathfrak{m}\geq 0$, that lead to the formation of an exactly extremal Schwarzschild-de Sitter black hole in finite time, and that moreover the apparent horizon of the solution is exactly isometric to that of the subextremal Schwarzschild-de Sitter solution at earlier times. We also show that if the scalar field mass satisfies $\mathfrak{m}>\sqrtΛ/2$, an analogous result holds having the extremal Schwarzschild-de Sitter white hole or the Nariai solution in place of the black hole. In the black hole case, the exactly extremal Schwarzschild-de Sitter region is bounded to the future by two event horizons and can be seen as the analogue of the exterior region in the asymptotically flat case. We moreover establish that these two black hole horizons that bound the exactly extremal Schwarzschild-de Sitter region are the only event horizons present in the spacetime, something non-trivial given the existence of multi-black hole configurations when $Λ> 0$. Consequently, the solutions arising from these data can be thought to constitute, in particular, regular counterexamples to the third law of black hole thermodynamics with $Λ>0$ arising from the collapse of physically plausible matter which is moreover uncharged. Our approach to construct the Cauchy data relies on $C^k$ characteristic gluing results for the Einstein-scalar field system in spherical symmetry, in the spirit of [KU25].
- **Summary:** Characteristic gluing constructs regular spherical Einstein–scalar initial data that reach exactly extremal Schwarzschild–de Sitter black holes in finite time without electric charge. The construction supplies counterexamples to a positive-cosmological-constant version of the third law and identifies the complete event-horizon structure, sharpening the classical premises behind horizon thermodynamics.
**RELEVANCE:** Directly engages Deepak's gravitational foundations and horizon-thermodynamics interests, without presenting a quantum-gravity or LQG model.  
**TAGS:** `extremal collapse` `characteristic gluing` `de Sitter horizons`

## 4. Slow mixing of Gibbs sampling for the quantum Curie-Weiss model
**Authors:** Xiaoyang Huang  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34682v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph, cond-mat.stat-mech, cond-mat.str-el
- **Abstract:** Hamiltonian evolution can spread local operators across a dense quantum magnet. We show that this global support does not remove the bottleneck to thermalization in the quantum Curie-Weiss model: at fixed low temperature and weak transverse field, the Chen-Kastoryano-Gilyen Gibbs sampler [arXiv: 2311.09207] has an exponentially large mixing-time lower bound. A dynamical quantum bottleneck theory that generalizes the classical notion of conductance has been proposed alongside existing frameworks that rely on spatial locality. We also give a complementary interpretation of slow mixing using an effective Fokker-Planck equation.
- **Summary:** A dynamical bottleneck analysis establishes exponentially slow mixing for the Chen–Kastoryano–Gilyen Gibbs sampler in a low-temperature, weak-transverse-field Curie–Weiss magnet. An effective Fokker–Planck description complements the bound, showing why Hamiltonian-induced global operator support need not bypass metastable barriers in quantum thermal-state preparation.
**RELEVANCE:** Links Deepak's many-body thermalization interests to rigorous limitations on quantum Gibbs-state algorithms.  
**TAGS:** `quantum Gibbs sampling` `Curie–Weiss model` `mixing-time lower bounds`

## 5. Intrinsically nonlinear nanomechanical resonator toward the single-phonon Kerr regime
**Authors:** S. Klingel, T. Althuon, C. Wassmer, C. Sürgers, T. Cubaynes, W. Wernsdorfer  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34345v1  
**Categories:** cond-mat.mes-hall, quant-ph
- **Abstract:** Manipulating a physical system at the single-quantum level typically requires an anharmonic energy spectrum. Nanomechanical resonators, however, are commonly admitted to be linear objects in the single-phonon regime, thus motivating most efforts to employ external sources of nonlinearity at the cost of added relaxation and decoherence channels. Here, we take a radically different approach by directly exploiting strong intrinsic mechanical nonlinearities. We quantify this nonlinearity using a novel method based on the coupling between bending modes. Using this technique, we establish an upper bound of 34.0 +/- 9.7 phonons for the onset of intrinsically mechanical bistability. In addition, we highlight a new tuning mechanism based on a mechanical polarization angle of an upward-buckled nanotube resonator allowing us to reach an optimal working point. Extrapolating this level of intrinsic nonlinearity to realistically shorter resonators shows that the single-phonon Kerr regime is within reach, allowing for the realization of purely mechanical Schrödinger-cat states and highly coherent mechanical qubits.
- **Summary:** Intermode bending coupling measures the intrinsic anharmonicity of a buckled nanotube resonator and places the onset of bistability at no more than 34.0 ± 9.7 phonons. Mechanical-polarization tuning optimizes the operating point; extrapolation to shorter devices suggests single-phonon Kerr physics, but that regime is a prospect rather than an experimental achievement here.
**RELEVANCE:** Bridges Deepak's condensed-matter and quantum-hardware interests through intrinsic mechanical routes to nonclassical states.  
**TAGS:** `nanotube mechanics` `intrinsic anharmonicity` `single-phonon Kerr`

## 6. Ultralight Dark Matter Spectrum with Gravitational Focusing and Doppler Motion
**Authors:** Shao-Feng Ge, Yong Tang, Yue-Hui Yao  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34464v1  
**Categories:** hep-ph, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** Ultralight dark matter can produce signals in experiments through its couplings with the Standard Model particles. The resulting signal spectrum is determined by the phase-space distribution of dark matter, which encodes information about its production mechanism and the history of the local universe. However, the local environmental effects can modify the observed spectrum and should be included in signal modeling to avoid biased parameter estimates. In this work, we investigate how the gravitational focusing by Sun and the Doppler effect due to the detector's motion modify the signal spectrum and affect inferring the underlying dark matter parameters. We analytically estimate the signal significance and perform Bayesian analysis on simulated data to demonstrate their impact.
- **Summary:** Analytic significance estimates and Bayesian inference on simulated data incorporate solar gravitational focusing and detector-motion Doppler shifts into ultralight-dark-matter spectra. The analysis shows how local environmental distortions affect recovery of underlying dark-matter parameters, making them part of signal modeling rather than automatically attributing spectral structure to primordial physics.

## 7. Time-weighted energy correlators are infrared unsafe in gauge theories
**Authors:** Siqing Yu  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34886v1  
**Categories:** hep-th, hep-ph
- **Abstract:** Energy correlators are infrared safe because each detector weights a parton by its energy, but a weight in the arrival time makes them infrared unsafe in gauge theories. For the state created by a scalar operator that makes two partons at lowest order in a weakly coupled conformal gauge theory, we show that the correlator of two time-weighted energy detectors (the light-ray operators $\int\!du\,u\,T_{uu}$) at separated directions diverges at two loops like the logarithm of the time window. The logarithm does not come from a single soft gluon, but from a soft parton pair with comparable energies, one parton in each detector; its coefficient is a closed-form function of the angle for any matter content. In $N=4$ super Yang-Mills theory, we recover this coefficient from the two-gluon Regge term of the known four-point function and derive a simple relation to the logarithm in the charge correlator. In the planar theory the leading logarithms resum to a power of the window, set by the BFKL intercept.
- **Summary:** A two-loop light-ray calculation identifies a logarithmic observation-window divergence from comparable-energy soft parton pairs in time-weighted energy correlations, with an angular coefficient valid for arbitrary matter content. An independent super-Yang–Mills correlator check and planar BFKL resummation connect the divergence to Regge physics, delimiting which detector observables inherit infrared safety.
**RELEVANCE:** Relevant to Deepak's high-energy theory interests through light-ray observables, infrared structure, and resummed gauge dynamics.  
**TAGS:** `time-weighted correlators` `infrared unsafety` `BFKL resummation`

## 8. Sharp Transitions of Copy Complexity in Local Certification of Typical Pure States
**Authors:** Yimeng Cao, Ge Bai  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34742v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph
- **Abstract:** We determine the asymptotic copy complexity of $n$-qudit state certification with tensor-product measurements. For given acceptance and rejection fidelities $a$ and $b$ ($b<a$), the optimal copy complexity is $Θ(n^r)$. For each qudit dimension $d$, $r$ is a piecewise constant function of $g=(a-b)/(1-b)$. This scaling holds for a family of sets of pure target states whose Haar measures tend to one as $n\to\infty$. We characterize the range of $g$ corresponding to each optimal exponent and provide an algorithm for computing its endpoints exactly. The optimal exponent grows logarithmically with $1/g$ as $g$ tends to zero. The upper bound is achieved by a nonadaptive protocol using only single-qudit projective measurements and classical postprocessing. The matching lower bound is established by constructing pairs of states that are difficult to distinguish under arbitrary tensor-product measurements. Our result can thus guide the design of certification tests when the number of available copies is limited.
- **Summary:** Matching constructive protocols and indistinguishability lower bounds determine the optimal polynomial copy cost for certifying typical pure qudit states with tensor-product measurements. The exponent jumps across fidelity-gap thresholds and grows logarithmically as the normalized gap closes, providing a precise resource map for certification using only local projective measurements.
**RELEVANCE:** Addresses Deepak's quantum-information interests in verification and the operational limits imposed by restricted measurements.  
**TAGS:** `local state certification` `copy complexity` `fidelity-gap thresholds`

## 9. Cosmological rigidity in $3$ spacetime dimensions
**Authors:** Gregory J. Galloway, Eric Ling, Argam Ohanyan  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34800v1  
**Categories:** math.DG, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** We establish rigidity theorems for cosmological spacetimes in $3$ spacetime dimensions. In the setting of the Bartnik Splitting Conjecture, under the additional assumptions of null geodesic completeness and the dominant energy condition, we show that the spatial topology is either spherical or the spacetime splits as a product. Our methods rely on the theory of marginally outer trapped surfaces, known rigidity results in the presence of a timelike or null line, and more recent rigidity results in the context of Penrose's singularity theorem due to the first two authors, from which the existence of a foliation by totally geodesic null hypersurfaces can be concluded. Our second main result closes the argument by establishing a splitting theorem for cosmological and null geodesically complete $3$-dimensional spacetimes which satisfy the dominant energy condition and which admit a foliation of the aforementioned type. In its proof, we make use of the local Kundt structure of the metric and construct a global parallel generator for the null foliation.
- **Summary:** Marginally trapped-surface rigidity and null-foliation arguments constrain three-dimensional cosmological spacetimes under additional completeness and energy assumptions. In the Bartnik setting, the authors obtain spherical spatial topology or product splitting, with a Kundt-based global parallel generator closing the splitting argument; this is a conditional rigidity result rather than a general cosmological classification.
**RELEVANCE:** Relevant to Deepak's cosmology and spacetime-geometry interests, providing rigorous lower-dimensional constraints rather than observational expansion-history results.  
**TAGS:** `cosmological rigidity` `Bartnik splitting` `null completeness`

## 10. The Price of Quantum Advantage in Computational Chemistry
**Authors:** Haejung Koh, Katerina Gratsea, Hao Zhang, Matthew Otten  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34889v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph
- **Abstract:** As the fault-tolerant era approaches, the question shifts from ``is it possible'' to ``is it worthwhile.'' To assess practical quantum advantage against reliable classical baselines, we examine 26 benchmark instances including FeMoco, iron-sulfur clusters, cytochrome P450, and ruthenium catalysts using two selected configuration interaction solvers, SHCI and TrimCI. Comparing classical workloads with fault-tolerant resource estimates, we calculate the break-even whole-QPU hourly price. Even at an optimistic price of US\$7{,}000 per whole-QPU hour, and with mid-range CPU clusters, classical computation remains cheaper for six of seven systems. FeMoco alone remains sensitive to the classical method and extrapolation strategy. Wavefunction analysis further reveals how orbital choice affects the determinant support required for target-state overlap in quantum phase estimation (QPE). Our results highlight the importance of reliable classical references and trial-state characterization, and quantify the economic conditions under which quantum computing can become competitive with classical alternatives.
- **Summary:** Classical selected-configuration-interaction workloads for 26 chemistry benchmarks are compared with fault-tolerant resource estimates to derive break-even whole-QPU prices. At the assumed USD 7,000 hourly price, classical methods remain cheaper for six of seven systems, while FeMoco and orbital-dependent trial-state support expose how baseline and state-preparation choices can shift the advantage assessment.
**RELEVANCE:** Useful for Deepak's quantum-computing interests as a quantitative check on practical advantage claims and phase-estimation assumptions.  
**TAGS:** `quantum chemistry economics` `fault-tolerant resources` `trial-state overlap`
