## 1. De Sitter Gravity and Emergent Time from Fusion Matrix Product States and SYK
**Authors:** Shinsei Ryu, Yu-ki Suzuki  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17045v1  
**Categories:** hep-th, cond-mat.str-el, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** We propose a quantum-mechanical model dual to two-dimensional de Sitter (dS) gravity. It is a quantum channel, defined by a matrix product state that fuses one spin at each step, and is related to a limit of the Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev model without chord crossings. Its simplest part is a random walk with an emergent $SL(2,\mathbb R)$ symmetry, which we identify with the symmetry of the future boundary of dS. It selects a time variable in which the walk obeys the Schwarzian action and yields the disk amplitude of dS Jackiw-Teitelboim gravity. As the simplest case, we consider free massless scalar fields in the fixed background of dS$_2$. We find the agreement of the two-point function in the Bunch-Davies vacuum and the matching of symmetry. Our construction provides a concrete model for the dS/CFT correspondence for two-dimensional de Sitter gravity.
- **Summary:** Ryu and Suzuki build a spin-fusing matrix-product-state quantum channel whose random-walk sector develops SL(2,R) symmetry and a preferred time governed by the Schwarzian action. The construction reproduces the de Sitter JT disk amplitude and, for a free massless scalar on fixed dS2, the Bunch–Davies two-point function and symmetry, offering a concrete but restricted candidate for de Sitter holography.
**RELEVANCE:** Connects emergent time, tensor networks and cosmological quantum gravity without relying on black-hole examples.  
**TAGS:** `de Sitter holography` `emergent time` `matrix product states`

## 2. Observation of Genuine Multi-Time Quantum Memory
**Authors:** Konstantin Beyer, Valentin Link, Surihan Sean Borjigin, Qian Cao, Orion Lee, Kater Murch, Igor Pikovski, Philip Taranto  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17268v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph
- **Abstract:** Memory effects in open quantum systems do not by themselves imply quantum memory, since non-Markovian dynamics can often be reproduced by classical-memory models. Known criteria certify the presence of quantum memory in the environment by comparing the evolution of the system at two different times. We show that quantum memory can remain hidden from all such pairwise tests and become detectable only when three or more times are considered jointly. We derive an analytical three-time witness and use it to certify quantum memory in a minimal qubit example in which every pair of times has a classical-memory explanation, thus establishing a strict hierarchy of quantum memory effects. Genuine multi-time quantum memory does not rely on fine-tuned time-dependent Hamiltonians or gate-based evolution. We experimentally demonstrate the hierarchy of quantum memory in a time-continuous realization on a superconducting-qubit platform with time-independent couplings.
- **Summary:** An analytical three-time witness identifies environmental quantum memory that cannot be certified by any pairwise temporal test, establishing a hierarchy beyond ordinary non-Markovianity. A superconducting-qubit experiment with continuous evolution and fixed couplings realizes the minimal example, showing that genuinely quantum temporal correlations can evade standard two-time diagnostics.
**RELEVANCE:** A foundational and experimentally grounded result on quantum information distributed across time.  
**TAGS:** `quantum memory` `multi-time correlations` `superconducting qubits`

## 3. Lattice response theory for subsystem symmetry-protected topological phases
**Authors:** Yuki Furukawa, Takuya Okuda  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17182v1  
**Categories:** cond-mat.str-el, hep-th, quant-ph
- **Abstract:** We develop a framework to study subsystem symmetry-protected topological (SSPT) phases using lattice response theory. We begin with a systematic construction of spacetime lattice gauge fields from general microscopic $X$-type symmetry actions on qudit systems, including global symmetries, subsystem symmetries, and more general modulated symmetries. Given a gauge-invariant response action, we then construct a corresponding entangler and explicitly show that it implements stacking with the SSPT phase described by the response action. We show that a broad class of SSPT phases can be described by intersection pairings and realized by cluster entanglers. We also study SSPT phases beyond intersection pairings, including those described by subsystem analogues of type-II group cocycles involving the Bockstein homomorphism. We construct a new 3+1d SSPT phase protected by two 2-foliated planar subsystem symmetries and a global 0-form symmetry, whose response action is a subsystem analogue of a type-III cocycle. On its boundary, the 0-form symmetry acts by stacking with a 2+1d linear SSPT phase, signaling a mixed 't Hooft anomaly.
- **Summary:** Furukawa and Okuda turn microscopic qudit symmetry actions into spacetime lattice gauge fields, then translate gauge-invariant response actions into entanglers that stack subsystem-protected phases. Beyond intersection-pairing constructions, they exhibit a new 3+1-dimensional phase with planar and global symmetries whose boundary stacking action exposes a mixed 't Hooft anomaly, connecting constructive many-body models to response theory.
**RELEVANCE:** Bridges topological matter, lattice gauge structures and anomalies across condensed matter and high-energy theory.  
**TAGS:** `subsystem SPT` `lattice response` `mixed anomaly`

## 4. Relational Cosmology: Covariant and Canonical Dressings, and the Hamiltonian of Internal Time
**Authors:** Francesco Sartini  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16864v1  
**Categories:** gr-qc
- **Abstract:** We compare covariant and canonical relational dressings with gauge-fixing reductions for the flat FLRW minisuperspace with a homogeneous massless scalar. We distinguish covariance of the dressing map from symmetries of the relational theory: fixing a clock makes its frame fields background structures, so its reorientations act as vector fields on relational field space rather than as spacetime diffeomorphisms. This loss of relational-time reparametrization covariance permits a nontrivial relational Hamiltonian, distinct from the undressed and dressed Hamiltonian constraints, which vanish on the constraint surface. We identify the conditions under which the dressed equations reproduce the original constrained dynamics. The relational generator defines a Hamiltonian flow even when relational-time translations are not symmetries of the dressed action. In each clock frame, it corresponds to the canonical complete observable and reduced Hamiltonian on the appropriate solution space, through the Legendre map and relational evaluation. The classical descriptions are compatible but retain distinct variational principles and intermediate phase spaces; their quantum equivalence remains an open question.
- **Summary:** For scalar-clock flat FLRW minisuperspace, Sartini compares covariant dressings, canonical complete observables and gauge-fixed reductions, isolating a nonvanishing relational Hamiltonian from constraints that vanish on shell. Clock-frame structures explain why its flow need not be an action symmetry; the classical formulations agree under specified conditions but retain different variational structures, leaving quantum equivalence unresolved.
**RELEVANCE:** Directly relevant to the problem of time and relational observables in canonical gravity and LQG-related cosmology; this is not an LQG calculation.  
**TAGS:** `relational observables` `internal time` `FLRW minisuperspace`

## 5. Bound States of Two D0-Branes and the Three-Graviton Coupling
**Authors:** Sergio Sanjurjo, Themistocles Zikopoulos  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17325v1  
**Categories:** hep-th
- **Abstract:** We numerically solve for the ground state of the $SU(2)$ BFSS matrix model for M-theory. This state corresponds to the zero-energy bound state of two D0-branes or, by the BFSS conjecture, to the eleven-dimensional graviton with two units of momentum along the M-theory circle. The wavefunction exhibits a strongly coupled core, as well as an asymptotic power-law tail where the D0-branes are well separated. The coefficient of this power-law decay is the three-graviton coupling, previously computed by Herderschee and Maldacena, which our numerical solution reproduces with high accuracy. We also show that this coupling is protected by supersymmetry in the matrix model, and derive its value from the non-renormalized one-loop interaction of the two D0-branes. Lastly, we provide a simple representation-theoretic argument regarding the recently proposed excited bound states of the model.
- **Summary:** A numerical SU(2) BFSS ground-state solution extracts the three-graviton coupling from the asymptotic tail of the two-D0-brane bound-state wavefunction and accurately reproduces an existing amplitude calculation. A separate supersymmetry argument derives the coupling from the non-renormalized one-loop D0 interaction, tying a strongly coupled quantum-mechanical bound state to protected eleven-dimensional gravitational scattering.
**RELEVANCE:** A complementary follow-up to yesterday's BFSS selection, emphasizing supersymmetric protection and the graviton-coupling interpretation.  
**TAGS:** `BFSS matrix model` `D0-brane bound states` `three-graviton coupling`

## 6. Long-lived light-driven bimagnons at finite momentum in NiO
**Authors:** Xian P. Yang, Christopher T. Parzyck, Martin Cross, Ella Blake, Octave Duros, Yinchuan Lv, Hari Paudyal, Che-Min Lin, Sharon S. Philip, Lingjia Shen, Daniel Jost, Giacomo Coslovich, Joshua J. Turner, Choongwon Seo, Elyse Schriber, Michael E. Flatté, Xiaoqin Li, Yuri Suzuki, Durga Paudyal, Harold Y. Hwang, Georgi L. Dakovski, Matthias C. Hoffmann, Wei-Sheng Lee, Alexander H. Reid  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17335v1  
**Categories:** cond-mat.str-el, cond-mat.mtrl-sci, physics.optics, quant-ph
- **Abstract:** Light can manipulate magnetic order and its excitations on ultrafast timescales, including through resonantly driven phonons. In antiferromagnets, light can also create correlated magnon pairs, or bimagnons. Unlike single magnons, whose damping has been measured and modeled in detail, little is known about the lifetime, interactions and decay of such correlated pairs: prior two-magnon measurements show broad linewidths or coherences that dephase within 200 fs. Here we pump the phonon-assisted two-magnon absorption of NiO with mid-infrared pulses, far below its bandgap, and probe the resulting magnetic dynamics at finite momentum with time-resolved resonant inelastic X-ray scattering (RIXS). Upon photoexcitation, the one-magnon linewidth and energy are essentially unchanged, indicating that the long-range magnetic order is preserved without the photocarrier-induced demagnetization characteristic of above-bandgap excitation. A pump-induced two-magnon signal appears on the energy-gain (anti-Stokes) side of the spectrum. Its spectral weight peaks 4 ps after the pump, well beyond our 0.5 ps time resolution, indicative of a delayed growth likely due to momentum redistribution of the light-induced magnon pairs across the Brillouin zone. This two-magnon signal decays with a time constant of $28\pm11$ ps, exceeding the previously reported two-magnon dephasing time by more than two orders of magnitude. These results establish long-lived light-driven bimagnon correlations beyond the one-magnon state, akin to the higher-order correlations measured in ultracold gases.
- **Summary:** Mid-infrared excitation followed by time-resolved finite-momentum RIXS isolates a NiO two-magnon anti-Stokes signal while leaving the single-magnon energy and linewidth essentially unchanged. The signal peaks after 4 ps and decays over 28 ± 11 ps, distinguishing long-lived pair correlations from previously measured rapid dephasing and opening a route to manipulate magnetic correlations without photocarrier-driven demagnetization.
**RELEVANCE:** A substantive experimental many-body result separating correlated excitation lifetimes from coherence times.  
**TAGS:** `bimagnons` `time-resolved RIXS` `antiferromagnetic dynamics`

## 7. Persistent-mediator architecture for universal fault-tolerant quantum computation with color codes
**Authors:** Zhi-Cheng He, Jingbo Wang, Dong E. Liu  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16691v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph
- **Abstract:** Two-dimensional color codes provide transversal Clifford gates, whereas suitable three-dimensional codes provide a transversal $T$ gate. Representative cross-dimensional schemes either switch the computational encoding or consume a 3D encoded state. Reusing the same protected 3D block would remove repeated auxiliary-state preparation and terminal readout, raising a central question: can the 3D module remain encoded and protected while mediating successive $T$ gates on a separate 2D register? Here we introduce a cross-dimensional persistent-mediator architecture that retains the 2D data register and 3D mediator as distinct encoded modules at each cycle boundary, coupled only through a transient protected subsystem. We construct a membrane-like joint-logical measurement and combine it with a matched cross-dimensional $CZ$, complementary string-like surgery, and feed-forward to close a deterministic logical $T$-gate cycle. The cycle avoids post-selection and destructive mediator readout and restores the 3D block to its initial logical state. Stabilizer analysis establishes the logical action and preserved spatial distance. Phenomenological and circuit-level simulations of a Clifford surrogate show finite-distance scaling close to the matched 3D memory benchmark, while sequential tests show approximately linear logical-error accumulation in the low-error regime. Persistent mediation enables repeated access to higher-dimensional non-Clifford capability while keeping the computational register in its 2D encoding.
- **Summary:** The proposed color-code architecture uses protected joint measurements, cross-dimensional coupling and feed-forward to implement deterministic logical T cycles while restoring a reusable 3D mediator and retaining a separate 2D data register. Stabilizer analysis establishes the logical operation and spatial distance, while Clifford-surrogate simulations show memory-like finite-distance scaling and roughly linear sequential error growth; these benchmarks support the architecture without constituting full non-Clifford circuit validation.
**RELEVANCE:** Targets the resource cost of universal fault-tolerant computing through reusable higher-dimensional encoded modules.  
**TAGS:** `color codes` `fault tolerance` `persistent mediator`

## 8. Gauge Theory Bootstrap with varying $N_c$
**Authors:** Rafael Cordoba, Yifei He, Martin Kruczenski, Derek Ping, Jignesh Mohanty, Emil Alegria  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16824v1  
**Categories:** hep-th, hep-lat, hep-ph
- **Abstract:** We use the Gauge Theory Bootstrap to study asymptotically free gauge theories with a varying number of colors $N_c$. The parameter $N_c$ enters the computation through the sum rules and asymptotic form factors, allowing us to tune this microscopic parameter directly and examine the corresponding low-energy strongly coupled dynamics. For $3\le N_c\le 12$, we extract the scattering lengths, effective ranges, and low-energy constants $\bar{l}_i$, and study their $N_c$ dependence. We also obtain the $σ$, $ρ$, and $f_2$ resonances for the same range of $N_c$. The $ρ$ and $f_2$ masses depend only weakly on $N_c$, and their widths decrease as $N_c$ increases, consistent with the $1/N_c$ scaling expected for conventional $q\bar{q}$ mesons. In contrast, the $σ$ meson mass and width both increase with $N_c$, in agreement with a previous unitarized $χ$PT analysis. These results provide a further test of the Gauge Theory Bootstrap for studying asymptotically free gauge theories.
- **Summary:** Scanning the color number from 3 to 12 through bootstrap sum rules and asymptotic form factors yields low-energy scattering parameters and sigma, rho and f2 resonance trajectories. The rho and f2 narrow with nearly stable masses, consistent with conventional meson scaling, whereas both sigma mass and width grow, making the method a nonperturbative probe of contrasting hadron structure.
**RELEVANCE:** Adds strongly coupled particle physics and scattering methods to the digest's gravity and quantum-information coverage.  
**TAGS:** `gauge theory bootstrap` `large-Nc dynamics` `hadron resonances`

## 9. Efficient Standard Siren Cosmological Inference with Posterior Reuse
**Authors:** Alexander Papadopoulos, Michael J. Williams, Christian E. A. Chapman-Bird, Rachel Gray, Christopher Messenger  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16697v1  
**Categories:** astro-ph.CO, astro-ph.HE, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** Standard siren cosmology depends on several modelling assumptions, including the underlying source population, galaxy catalogue, and detection selection function. Quantifying the effect of each choice typically requires a full, independent analysis. As gravitational-wave catalogs grow incrementally, much computational effort is spent re-analysing largely overlapping datasets. We apply the recently developed ASPIRE (Accelerated Sequential Posterior Inference via Reuse) sampler to the joint estimation of cosmological and population parameters with dark sirens. ASPIRE updates existing posterior samples into the posterior under a new hypothesis without restarting from the prior. It combines normalising flows with a sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) scheme to produce accurate posteriors and unbiased evidence estimates. Applying ASPIRE to a number of dark siren analysis cases with GWTC-5.0, we recover posteriors consistent with nested sampling, while recording a performance improvement of between 6-28 times over the set of analyses when using an existing posterior. This enables efficient exploration of modelling choices and rapid updates as catalogs grow, without duplicating existing computational effort.
- **Summary:** Normalising flows combined with sequential Monte Carlo repurpose an existing dark-siren posterior for a changed cosmological or population hypothesis instead of restarting inference from the prior. In the reported GWTC-5.0 cases, ASPIRE matches nested-sampling posteriors while delivering 6–28-fold speedups and unbiased evidence estimates, making modelling-systematics studies and incremental catalogue updates less expensive.
**RELEVANCE:** A practical cosmology methodology for extracting robust expansion-history information from gravitational-wave standard sirens.  
**TAGS:** `standard sirens` `posterior reuse` `cosmological inference`

## 10. Near-Optimal Multiparameter Pure-State Metrology with a Constant Number of Bases
**Authors:** Bowen Xiao, Zhiyao Yang, Huangjun Zhu  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17281v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph, math-ph
- **Abstract:** Multiparameter pure-state metrology is limited by the incompatibility of measurements optimized for different parameters. We characterize uniform sensitivity by the Fisher floor, the smallest fraction of the quantum Fisher information (QFI) retained over all pure states and parameter directions. We prove that a constant number of fixed orthonormal bases suffices for a nearly optimal Fisher floor, independently of the Hilbert-space dimension $d$. With high probability, five independent Haar bases already achieve a positive, dimension-independent floor, and larger constant families achieve approximate universal Fisher symmetry: they retain nearly one half of the QFI in every direction, the largest uniform fraction permitted by measurement incompatibility. The required number of bases is optimal up to a logarithmic factor in inverse accuracy. In contrast, $d+1$ mutually unbiased bases, when they exist, can minimize mean Fisher anisotropy among families of $d+1$ bases yet have a Fisher floor of at most $1/(d+1)$, showing that optimal average balance does not ensure uniform sensitivity.
- **Summary:** A worst-direction Fisher-information criterion reveals that a dimension-independent number of random measurement bases can deliver uniform pure-state multiparameter sensitivity: five Haar bases already give a positive floor with high probability. Larger constant families approach the incompatibility-limited one-half QFI fraction, whereas mutually unbiased bases can have a floor no better than 1/(d+1), separating average isotropy from reliable sensitivity in every direction.
**RELEVANCE:** A rigorous measurement-design result with implications for scalable quantum sensing and information geometry.  
**TAGS:** `multiparameter metrology` `Fisher information` `random measurement bases`

## 11. Automatic O(a) Improvement for Wilson Twisted Mass Fermions in the Hamiltonian Formulation
**Authors:** Carsten Urbach  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16658v1  
**Categories:** hep-lat, quant-ph
- **Abstract:** We apply the recently developed Symanzik effective Hamiltonian framework to Wilson twisted mass fermions. The effective Hamiltonian is constructed solely based on the symmetries of the lattice Hamiltonian without recourse to the Symanzik effective action in Euclidean space-time. We use it to prove automatic $O(a)$ improvement at maximal twist, as well as $O(a)$ improvement via so-called mass and Wilson average. Moreover, we derive the structure of $O(a)$ counterterms pre-requisite for clover on-shell improvement of Wilson fermions. We show how to remove on-shell redundant contributions using canonical transformations, and find that the well known clover term in isotropic Euclidean space time splits into two terms with independent coefficients.
- **Summary:** Using symmetries of the lattice Hamiltonian rather than a Euclidean effective action, Urbach constructs a Symanzik description proving automatic linear-spacing-error removal at maximal twist and improvement through mass or Wilson averaging. Canonical transformations eliminate redundant on-shell terms and reveal two independently weighted Hamiltonian clover counterterms, providing discretization-control tools for Hamiltonian lattice gauge calculations.
**RELEVANCE:** Connects precision lattice field theory with Hamiltonian methods relevant to quantum simulation.

## 12. Noise-Protected Superconducting Qudits from Higher-Harmonic Josephson Junctions
**Authors:** Hamed Vakili  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16563v1  
**Categories:** cond-mat.supr-con, cond-mat.mes-hall, quant-ph
- **Abstract:** Qudits, which generalize qubits to $d$ levels, store more information per circuit element than qubits. As with qubits, however, their sensitivity to noise must be overcome before they can be used for fault-tolerant quantum computing. Here we show that, by exploiting the second harmonic of the current--phase relation, a superconducting loop of three Josephson junctions can host qutrits strongly protected against relaxation, while dephasing limits their coherence. When the second harmonic is positive, a simple loop with a small asymmetry between the junctions provides a tunable, symmetry-protected qutrit. When it is negative, a protected qutrit can be realized by adding a small symmetry-breaking term, such as a weak Josephson diode effect. The two designs involve different trade-offs: the first has an exact charge selection rule, whereas the second is nearly insensitive to charge noise and its quasiparticle errors can be corrected. The preferable choice therefore depends on the materials used. Both require a sizable second harmonic to begin with, and we identify altermagnetic Josephson junctions as a candidate platform. Finally, microscopic calculations show that a gate voltage selects the sign of the second harmonic in these junctions, although gate-voltage noise then becomes the leading source of dephasing. These findings establish the sign of the second harmonic as a design principle for protected superconducting qudits.
- **Summary:** A theoretical three-junction loop exploits the sign of a sizable second Josephson harmonic to realize two different relaxation-protected qutrit designs, with contrasting charge-noise and correctability trade-offs. Microscopic calculations identify gate-tunable altermagnetic junctions as a candidate implementation, but voltage-noise dephasing remains a limiting factor, making the result a materials-dependent design proposal rather than demonstrated hardware protection.
**RELEVANCE:** Unites superconducting-device physics, multilevel quantum information and realistic noise mechanisms.  
**TAGS:** `protected qutrits` `Josephson harmonics` `altermagnetic junctions`
