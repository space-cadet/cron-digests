## 1. Twisting IKKT with branes
**Authors:** Fabian Hahner, Natalie M. Paquette  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17270v1  
**Categories:** hep-th, math-ph
- **Abstract:** Motivated by the conjectural relationship between the IKKT matrix model and type IIB string theory, we classify supersymmetric twists of the matrix model, allowing for both dynamical and kinematical supercharges. Beyond the two twists available at any finite $N$, which we studied in our previous work, we find five additional classes at infinite rank which require non-commuting backgrounds associated with D-branes in IIB supergravity. Together, these twists realize representatives of every equivalence class of twisting supercharges in flat-space type IIB supergravity recently classified by the first author; however, the precise form of the orbit stratification differs. This difference is related to a higher central extension of the IIB superalgebra that appears in our treatment of the IKKT model in the BV formalism. Expanding around the non-commutative backgrounds gives worldvolume theories on products of A- and B-branes, including four-dimensional BF theory on an emergent D3-brane. We relate all these twists to deformations of BCOV theory (the closed string field theory of the topological B-model) using the open-closed duality of classical string field theory. For one of the twists with Spin$(7)$ symmetry, we also show the localization of the IKKT partition function by Moore, Nekrasov, and Shatashvili can be directly reconstructed with the BV formalism, with their auxiliary field completion encoded by quadratic antifield terms. Throughout, we provide self-contained treatments of the formal techniques we employ.
- **Summary:** Hahner and Paquette classify supersymmetric twists of IKKT, finding five infinite-rank classes beyond the two finite-matrix twists by allowing noncommutative D-brane backgrounds. BV methods and open–closed string duality connect the resulting worldvolume theories, including emergent four-dimensional BF theory, to deformations of BCOV theory and reconstruct a known localization prescription. This sharpens the structural correspondence between matrix-model and type-IIB descriptions of quantum gravity.
**RELEVANCE:** A substantive matrix-model route to emergent geometry and BF theory, adjacent to Deepak’s quantum-gravity and LQG interests rather than a direct LQG result.  
**TAGS:** `IKKT matrix model` `supersymmetric twists` `emergent geometry`

## 2. Non-interferometric tests of gravitational decoherence models with mechanical oscillators
**Authors:** Sandro Donadi, Iarley P. Lobo, Matteo Fadel  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17260v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** Phenomenological models of quantum-gravity-induced decoherence can leave observable effects even in experiments that do not rely on interferometric measurements. We study three such models, based respectively on deformed relativistic symmetries, stochastic metric fluctuations, and a stochastic generalized uncertainty principle, all of which induce decoherence in the momentum basis. For a free particle, the models preserve momentum distribution and mean energy but produce position diffusion and momentum-space decoherence with model-specific momentum dependences. For a harmonic oscillator, the noncommutativity of the collapse operators with the oscillator Hamiltonian results in anomalous heating, with the three models leading to qualitatively different energy dynamics. The first gives heating at a constant rate, the second at a rate proportional to the kinetic energy, which can lead to exponential growth, and the third at a rate growing cubically with excitation. We further derive the stationary position power spectral density and show that all three models produce the same leading-order excess lineshape in the weak-noise regime, distinct from that of force noise. Comparing with existing mechanical platforms, we find that displacement-noise measurements can already constrain the fluctuation time of the first model below the Planck time, while occupation measurements could improve the bounds on the second model by up to fourteen orders of magnitude over those from ground-state heating of levitated particles. Summing the heating over all phonon modes of a solid, the residual heat release of low-temperature experiments constrains the first model far below the Planck time and the second at the Planck time. Our results establish heating and continuous measurements as complementary non-interferometric probes of gravity-related decoherence.
- **Summary:** Donadi and collaborators derive oscillator heating and displacement spectra for three momentum-decohering quantum-gravity models, showing that their energy dependence distinguishes them even though their leading weak-noise excess lineshapes coincide. Comparisons with mechanical and low-temperature platforms yield strong model-dependent constraints, with occupation measurements potentially improving one existing bound by fourteen orders of magnitude. The analysis makes non-interferometric experiments a concrete complement to coherence-based tests of gravity-related noise.
**RELEVANCE:** Connects phenomenological quantum gravity to experimentally accessible quantum mechanics, with explicit discriminants and sensitivity estimates.  
**TAGS:** `gravitational decoherence` `mechanical oscillators` `quantum-gravity tests`

## 3. Scalable Pauli propagation through $\mathbb{F}_2$-linear partitioning
**Authors:** Alev Orfi, Andrew Kille, J. Clayton Peacock, Jack Dunham, Lukas Devos  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17262v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph
- **Abstract:** Pauli propagation simulates quantum circuits and many-body dynamics by evolving observables in the Heisenberg picture as sparse sums of Pauli strings. As Pauli-string operations reduce to bit manipulations, its performance is limited by memory bandwidth and capacity rather than computational throughput. Pooling the memory of many cores and nodes is hindered by the deduplication step after every gate, which can require global synchronization. We avoid this bottleneck by partitioning the strings with an $\mathbb{F}_2$-linear hash function, which guarantees that a $k$-qubit channel couples each partition to at most $4^k$ partitions fixed by the gate, decomposing gate application into independent tasks with bounded communication. Benchmarks of our open-source implementation demonstrate efficient parallel scaling within and across compute nodes, enabling simulations with over $10^{11}$ Pauli strings and extending the accessible timescales of quantum many-body dynamics.
- **Summary:** An F2-linear partition of Pauli strings turns gate application into bounded-communication tasks, avoiding the globally synchronized deduplication that limits distributed Heisenberg-picture simulation. The open-source implementation scales across cores and nodes and handles more than 100 billion strings, extending the simulated duration of many-body dynamics. Its contribution is a memory-and-communication strategy rather than a new quantum algorithm, making it directly useful for classical benchmarking of quantum circuits.
**RELEVANCE:** A practical computational advance for studying quantum many-body dynamics and validating quantum processors.  
**TAGS:** `Pauli propagation` `distributed simulation` `many-body dynamics`

## 4. Higher-Group Statistics on Lattices
**Authors:** Chen Yang, Yu-An Chen  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16962v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph, cond-mat.str-el, hep-th, math.QA
- **Abstract:** Crossing a domain wall can permute anyons, and intersecting strings can create a particle. These couplings between defects of various dimensions are specified by higher groups. We develop a lattice framework defining higher-group statistics as Berry phases of closed sequences of hopping operators, invariant under local modifications of the operators. Our central example is a closed Majorana chain, a 1-dimensional topological superconductor, embedded in 3 dimensions. We construct a loop-flip process with phase $+i$, refining the usual $\pm 1$ loop-flip invariant through intermediate transfer of fermion parity between the chain and physical fermions. In two dimensions, charge-wall statistics distinguish two $\mathbb Z_4$ toric codes enriched by $\mathbb Z_2$ charge-conjugation symmetry, with identical anyon statistics but distinct gauged theories: the quantum doubles of the dihedral group $D_4$ and the quaternion group $Q_8$. We provide two complementary constructions of statistical processes. The first extends the Smith normal form algorithm in [Phys. Rev. X $\textbf{16}$, 011010 (2026), arXiv:2412.01886] to higher groups, generating processes from lattice geometry and prescribed local moves. For a finite higher group $\mathbb G$, we prove that statistical processes on a $d$-sphere are classified by $H_{d+2}(B\mathbb G,\mathbb Z)$. The second constructs processes directly from homology classes without examining exponentially many defect configurations, enabling us to study statistics for complex higher-group structures where the previous method is computationally prohibitive. These processes detect boundary anomalies of higher-group symmetry-protected topological phases. For example, we distinguish 2-group anomalies in 1, 2, and 3 dimensions. Higher-group statistics provide new invariants for characterizing coupled defects in quantum many-body systems.
- **Summary:** Yang and Chen define statistics of coupled defects through Berry phases of closed lattice hopping sequences and classify sphere processes by higher-group homology. Their constructions uncover a +i Majorana-chain loop-flip phase and distinguish symmetry-enriched toric codes with identical anyon statistics, while a homology-based algorithm avoids enumerating exponentially many configurations. These invariants expose defect couplings and boundary anomalies invisible to conventional particle statistics.
**RELEVANCE:** Bridges topological matter, generalized symmetries, and field-theoretic anomaly structures across Deepak’s interests.  
**TAGS:** `higher-group symmetry` `topological defects` `boundary anomalies`

## 5. Discrete Time Crystals from Anti-Unitary Floquet Driving and Dynamical Freezing
**Authors:** Anirban Das, Asmi Haldar, Arnab Das  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17333v1  
**Categories:** cond-mat.stat-mech, quant-ph
- **Abstract:** We show anti-unitary periodic drive paves a direct path for stabilizing a Discrete Time-Crystal by Dynamical Freezing (DF). The magnetization appears as an emergent conserved operator, and an exact pairing of two-cycle quasi-energies pins the subharmonic response. DF keeps the effective Hamiltonian $\textit{quasi-local}$; infinite-chain simulations show no detectable decay for a generic state with any finite magnetization. We outline an experimental realization of the anti-unitary drive.
- **Summary:** Anti-unitary Floquet driving is combined with dynamical freezing to protect a period-doubled response through emergent magnetization conservation and exact two-cycle quasienergy pairing. Infinite-chain calculations find no detectable decay for generic initially magnetized states while the effective Hamiltonian remains quasi-local, and the authors outline an implementation. This supplies a concrete stabilization mechanism for time-crystalline dynamics, though the reported numerical persistence is not a proof of infinite lifetime.
**RELEVANCE:** A focused nonequilibrium many-body proposal linking emergent conservation laws to robust dynamical order.  
**TAGS:** `discrete time crystals` `Floquet dynamics` `dynamical freezing`

## 6. Detecting non-Hermitian topology from long-time dynamics
**Authors:** Antonio D'Abbruzzo, Rosario Fazio  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16745v1  
**Categories:** cond-mat.stat-mech, cond-mat.mes-hall, quant-ph
- **Abstract:** We propose a protocol to measure the non-Hermitian topological invariant associated with the Lindbladian spectrum of chiral-symmetric open Gaussian systems from the relaxation of the covariance matrix. Importantly, the method works in the long-time regime and does not rely on isolating edge modes, so it remains applicable even when the Liouvillian skin effect may obscure them. We illustrate the technique on a Kitaev chain with reservoir-induced nonreciprocity, for which we first obtain a fully analytical and previously unknown description of its topological phase diagram. We then show that our protocol reliably distinguishes all the different phases.
- **Summary:** D’Abbruzzo and Fazio extract a non-Hermitian Lindbladian topological invariant from long-time covariance-matrix relaxation instead of relying on edge-state isolation. An analytically solved reservoir-driven nonreciprocal Kitaev chain provides a phase diagram on which the protocol distinguishes every phase, including situations where skin effects can obscure edge modes. The result converts bulk dissipative dynamics into an operational diagnostic of open-system topology.
**RELEVANCE:** Combines topological condensed matter with experimentally relevant open-quantum-system observables.  
**TAGS:** `non-Hermitian topology` `Lindblad dynamics` `Kitaev chain`

## 7. Kinessence, phantom crossing and sign-switching dark energy
**Authors:** Philippe Brax, Tom Canaguier, Andrei Lazanu  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16629v1  
**Categories:** astro-ph.CO, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** We introduce Kinessence models where combining K-mouflage and quintessence leads to phantom-crossing of the dark energy equation of state and sign-switching of the dark energy density. This is realised by joining a dark scalar sector comprising K-essence kinetic terms and a quintessence potential together with a Yukawa coupling to matter. We investigate the dynamics of these models in the Jordan frame at the background and perturbative levels. We concentrate on the effective equation of state and the matter fraction for the background. The CMB spectra, the linear power spectrum and the growth of structure via $fσ_8$ are also analysed at the perturbative level. The quintessence potential drives the acceleration of the Universe at low redshift with an equation of state greater than -1. The Yukawa coupling to matter leads to a phantom crossing behaviour depending on its strength. The potential fifth force mediated by the scalar field is screened by the K-mouflage mechanism triggered by the K-essence kinetic terms and passes Solar System tests. This allows for relatively large Yukawa couplings to matter $β\sim 0.1$, large enough to lead to the crossing of the phantom divide at $z\sim 0.5$. Despite a significant scalar interaction on large scales, we find that the growth of structure is reduced compared to LCDM. The matter fraction deviates from the standard model by a few percent and the Hubble rate is larger at low redshift than at recombination whilst the CMB spectra show small deviations from LCDM. The dark energy fraction becomes negative at redshifts greater than 2. All these features should alleviate the various tensions such as the larger value of $H_0$ at small redshift, a possible reduction of growth as measured by $S_8$ and the bound on the sum of the neutrino masses. Deviations from LCDM of the CMB lensing spectrum may provide a detectable window for these models.
- **Summary:** Brax and collaborators combine a quintessence potential, K-essence screening, and a Yukawa matter coupling, then calculate background evolution and linear perturbations in the Jordan frame. Their models allow effective phantom crossing near redshift 0.5 and negative dark-energy density above redshift 2 while screening local fifth forces and reducing structure growth relative to LCDM. The predicted CMB-lensing deviations offer a test of this proposed route to easing cosmological tensions; the abstract does not establish a successful joint observational fit.
**RELEVANCE:** A technically developed modified-dark-energy scenario connecting cosmological expansion, screening, and structure formation.  
**TAGS:** `dark energy` `K-mouflage screening` `cosmological perturbations`

## 8. Monopole catalysis with massive fermions: the fate of the twisted sector
**Authors:** Valentin V. Khoze  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17253v1  
**Categories:** hep-ph, hep-th
- **Abstract:** In the massless limit a fermion scattering on the minimal $SU(5)$ monopole in the lowest partial wave produces an outgoing state with half-integer fermion number in every flavour. This state lives in a twisted sector of the Hilbert space and is now understood as an ordinary fermion attached to a topological line running back to the monopole. In the Standard Model fermion masses and confinement destroy the line at distances greater than the inverse mass scales, causing the twisted state to decay into physical asymptotic states with integer fermion numbers. We study this decay for an incoming lepton or quark of one generation. The cross-section in this partial wave saturates the unitarity bound, $σ=π/p^2$, and the energy of the outgoing state is shared among the four fermion flavours with a broad Dirichlet distribution, which sets the thresholds and the kinematics. Conservation laws allow exactly two decay channels for each incoming fermion, the helicity flip and its $(B+L)$-violating partner. For an incoming electron the $(B+L)$-violating channel is anti-nucleon synthesis, and we determine the branching ratios in two limits: the two channels are equally likely at high energy, and only the helicity flip remains at low energy, below the anti-nucleon threshold. In between we model the energy dependence. For a quark in a slow proton the $(B+L)$-violating channel is catalysed proton decay, which has no threshold and occurs with a probability close to one. Callan's statistical interpretation of the fractional state emerges in a sharper form: the state is not a superposition of two final states with equal weights, but a single state that decays into them with energy-dependent probabilities, equal only at high energy.
- **Summary:** Khoze follows the fractional-fermion twisted state produced in monopole scattering into massive, confined asymptotic channels, using energy sharing and conservation laws to determine allowed decays. The lowest partial wave saturates the unitarity bound, but helicity-flip and baryon-plus-lepton-violating branching fractions depend on energy rather than remaining universally equal; a quark in a slow proton instead has near-unit catalysed-decay probability. This resolves how the topological-line description connects to physical monopole-catalysis signatures.
**RELEVANCE:** A substantive particle-theory result tying topological sectors and unitarity to baryon-number-violating phenomenology.  
**TAGS:** `monopole catalysis` `twisted sectors` `baryon-number violation`

## 9. Numerical tests of chiral fermion weights with $η$-invariant phases in the 3450 model
**Authors:** Tatsuhiro Misumi  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16876v1  
**Categories:** hep-lat, hep-th
- **Abstract:** We test numerically a recently proposed prescription for coupling continuum chiral fermions to interpolated lattice gauge fields, in which the fermion weight has a Pauli-Villars (PV) regularized magnitude and a phase given by an Atiyah-Patodi-Singer (APS) $η$-invariant in one higher dimension. We use the anomaly-free two-dimensional $U(1)$ theory with left-handed charges $3,4$ and right-handed charges $5,0$, whose torus determinant is known exactly. At zero magnetic flux, with gauge fields interpolated from lattice links, the Polyakov loops evaluated on a $16\times16$ grid of flat connections agree with the exact values within $0.04\%$, whereas omitting the APS phase substantially reduces them to about $3\%$ of the exact result. The vacuum-polarization response yields an induced mass $m/e=2.82089$, close to $5/\sqrtπ=2.82095$. At unit flux, where zero modes make the determinant vanish, we calculate a twelve-point correlator on a cylinder with the generalized APS boundary condition. For three smooth backgrounds its magnitude agrees with the exact result within $0.8\%$. Without the $η$-invariant its phase deviates by $1.8$-$3.0$ mrad, and with it by $0.2$-$0.5$ mrad. The phase of each charged fermion changes by $15$-$46$ mrad when the gauge field is extended differently into the third dimension, but the change cancels to below $0.2$ mrad in the anomaly-free combination.
- **Summary:** Misumi benchmarks a Pauli–Villars magnitude plus APS eta-invariant phase prescription against exact results for an anomaly-free two-dimensional chiral gauge theory. Flat-connection Polyakov loops agree within 0.04 percent and a nonzero-flux twelve-point correlator within 0.8 percent in magnitude, while phase tests demonstrate the importance of the eta term and cancellation of extension dependence across anomaly-free fermions. These controlled checks support the prescription as a route toward lattice chiral gauge theories without claiming a full four-dimensional construction.
**RELEVANCE:** Concrete numerical evidence connecting lattice regularization, chiral fermions, and anomaly cancellation.  
**TAGS:** `chiral lattice fermions` `eta invariant` `anomaly cancellation`

## 10. Explicit Absolutely Maximally Entangled states in dimensions twice a prime power
**Authors:** Palash Pandya  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17216v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph
- **Abstract:** Absolutely maximally entangled (AME) states are pure multipartite states with maximally mixed reductions on every set of at most half the parties. We construct explicit families in local dimension $2q$, twice an odd prime power $q$, using an established coupling of qubits and $q$-level systems. The $q$-level states remove interference between branches, so the qubit states need maximally mixed reductions only on average. This gives AME$(9,2q)$ and AME$(11,2q)$ for every odd prime power $q\geq5$, AME$(13,2q)$ for $q\geq17$, AME$(15,2q)$ for $q\geq9$, and AME$(17,2q)$ for $q\geq67$. For every odd $N\geq3$, the same coupling gives AME$(N,2q)$ under the sufficient condition $q\geq\max\{N,2^{N-2}\}$ on the odd prime power $q$. Within this coupling, the smallest such $q$ must grow exponentially with $N$. Combined with known inputs and products, these families complete the nine-party classification: for integer $d\geq2$, an AME$(9,d)$ state exists if and only if $d\geq3$. Choosing the branch pairing also gives AME$(15,2p)$ states with a cyclic shift of the parties compensated by local unitaries, for every prime $p\equiv1\pmod{15}$.
- **Summary:** Pandya constructs multipartite absolutely maximally entangled states by coupling qubit branches to prime-power-dimensional systems that suppress branch interference. The resulting families in dimension twice an odd prime power, combined with known constructions, settle nine-party existence exactly: every integer local dimension at least three works, whereas dimension two does not. The work expands explicit entanglement resources and identifies exponential dimension growth within this particular coupling scheme.
**RELEVANCE:** A sharp multipartite-entanglement construction relevant to quantum-information resources and error-correction-inspired state design.  
**TAGS:** `absolutely maximal entanglement` `multipartite states` `explicit constructions`

## 11. A precision bound for gravitational transseries
**Authors:** Ji Hoon Lee  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17274v1  
**Categories:** hep-th
- **Abstract:** We propose that the large $N$ transseries of a holographic observable above the black hole threshold admits a precision bound, a limit on how precisely the transseries can determine the observable at finite $N$, set by the data of its D-brane instanton sectors. In doing so, we develop methods in large $N$ resurgence, based on the work of Écalle, that capture the $e^{-N^2}$ asymptotics of gravitational saddles beyond the $e^{-N}$ instantons. Under an assumption on the growth of the D-brane instanton sums that we expect in the black hole phase, we find an analytic obstruction to the resummation of the large $N$ transseries. This obstruction sets the precision bound. As our main example and consistency check, we study the $\frac{1}{16}$-BPS index of $\mathcal{N}=4$ super Yang-Mills and its black hole. We derive relations between the D-brane instanton data on thermal AdS and on the black hole, in the form of a duality that exchanges the numbers of D-brane instantons on the two backgrounds. In the index, these relations reproduce the actions of D3-branes wrapped on the black hole. We conclude with our expectations for generic situations and their relation to the double-cone wormhole.
- **Summary:** Lee develops large-N resurgence methods that access gravitational contributions of order exp(−N²) beyond the D-brane instanton scale and proposes a finite-N precision limit on holographic transseries. Assuming a specified growth of instanton sums, an obstruction to resummation supplies the bound; tests using the one-sixteenth-BPS index of N=4 super Yang–Mills recover wrapped-D3-brane actions through an instanton-number duality. The result probes what perturbative-plus-instanton gravitational data can determine, with the central bound remaining conditional on the growth assumption.
**RELEVANCE:** Targets nonperturbative completeness and precision in holographic quantum gravity, beyond conventional semiclassical expansions.  
**TAGS:** `gravitational transseries` `large-N resurgence` `D-brane instantons`

## 12. Ensemble phase transitions in measurement-unitary hybrid quantum circuits
**Authors:** Saptarshi Mandal, Sthitadhi Roy  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17322v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph, cond-mat.dis-nn, cond-mat.stat-mech, cond-mat.str-el
- **Abstract:** The universal properties of state ensembles generated by quantum dynamics provide a refined lens into Hilbert-space ergodicity and deep thermalisation, or lack thereof. We investigate deep-ergodicity breaking transitions of an ancillary qubit entangled with a system undergoing dynamics via hybrid quantum circuits, where unitary evolution is interspersed with projective measurements. Specifically, we analyse the statistical properties of two distinct state ensembles: the trajectory ensemble (TE) conditioned on the measurement outcomes of the hybrid circuit, and the projected ensemble (PE) generated by further projective measurements of the system at the final temporal boundary. We find that both ensembles undergo non-analytic changes -- ensemble phase transitions -- at the critical point of the measurement-induced purification transition, despite their first moments remaining completely maximally mixed and blind to the transition. For Haar-random circuits, the TE transitions from a trivial identity ensemble to the uniform Haar ensemble, while the PE transitions from the Haar ensemble to a trivial singleton pure state. In Clifford circuits, absence of non-stabiliserness limits the PE to a $1$-design, while the TE forms a $k$-design for $k \leq 3$. We demonstrate that these genuine ensemble transitions offer a clear physical mechanism for the measurement-induced entanglement or purification transitions, illustrating how the capacity to ergodically steer a quantum subsystem shifts from the temporal boundary measurements in the mixed phase to the bulk trajectory measurements in the pure phase.
- **Summary:** Mandal and Roy analyze ancillary-qubit trajectory and projected-state ensembles in monitored circuits, finding nonanalytic ensemble changes at purification criticality that their maximally mixed first moments cannot detect. Haar-random and Clifford circuits yield different design structures, revealing how non-stabilizer resources constrain ensemble ergodicity and how measurement-based steering shifts between final-time and bulk measurements. This reframes measurement-induced transitions through the full distribution of accessible subsystem states rather than density matrices alone.
**RELEVANCE:** Connects monitored many-body dynamics, quantum designs, and information-theoretic diagnostics of phase transitions.  
**TAGS:** `measurement-induced transitions` `quantum designs` `ensemble ergodicity`
