## 1. From arithmetic spectra to a quantum-corrected black hole geometry
- **Authors:** Kimet Jusufi, Ankit Anand
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.23528
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23528
- **Categories:** hep-th, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** The Euler product of the Riemann zeta function is the partition function of a free bosonic gas whose mode energies are the logarithms of the primes. We show that this arithmetic gas, combined with the assumption that the entropy--geometry correspondence holds, leads to a quantum-corrected black-hole metric. The prime gas is a Hagedorn system. Its entropy is linear in the energy, which is exactly what an entropy linear in the horizon area requires, and the simple pole of the zeta function at $\beta=1$ fixes the coefficient of the logarithmic correction to the area law. The nontrivial zeros cannot play this role: their level density grows only logarithmically, and far too slowly to be extensive. Demanding that the reconstructed geometry reduce to Schwarzschild at large radius then fixes the map from arithmetic energy to horizon area and yields the closed-form metric $f(r)=1-2GMr/(r^{2}+\lz^{2})$ with $\lz^{2}=\alpha G/\pi$. This describes a two-horizon black hole with Reissner--Nordström horizon structure but no Coulombic hair, a positive-energy anisotropic source obeying the null energy condition, a softened central singularity, a bounded Hawking temperature, and a cold extremal remnant that ends the evaporation. The same length scale follows independently from requiring that the first law hold exactly with the corrected entropy. The nontrivial zeros survive only as exponentially suppressed log-periodic ripples in the area, which suggests a physical interpretation of the Riemann hypothesis as the statement that arithmetic corrections to black-hole thermodynamics are as small as they can be.
- **Summary:** Jusufi and Anand construct a remarkable bridge between number theory and quantum gravity by showing that the statistical mechanics of the "prime gas" — whose partition function is the Euler product of the Riemann zeta function — can be mapped, via an entropy-geometry correspondence, onto a quantum-corrected black hole metric. The Hagedorn nature of the prime gas naturally produces an entropy linear in energy, matching the Bekenstein-Hawking area law, while the zeta function's pole at β=1 fixes the logarithmic correction coefficient. The resulting metric is a regular, two-horizon geometry with a softened singularity, bounded temperature, and an extremal remnant that halts complete evaporation. A striking byproduct is a physical interpretation of the Riemann hypothesis: the nontrivial zeros manifest as exponentially suppressed oscillatory corrections to the horizon area.
- **Relevance:** This paper sits at a fascinating intersection of quantum gravity, black hole thermodynamics, and number theory. The use of arithmetic structures to derive quantum-corrected geometries resonates with ideas in loop quantum gravity where discrete spectra and statistical mechanical methods underpin the derivation of black hole entropy. The explicit metric with a regular interior and extremal remnant is directly relevant to ongoing debates about black hole evaporation and information preservation.
- **Tags:** Quantum Gravity, Black Hole Physics, Mathematical Physics

## 2. The Schwarzschild Black Hole in an External Gravitational Tidal Field: the Quasinormal Spectrum
- **Authors:** Dimitrios Giataganas, Alex Kehagias, Antonio Riotto
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.21797
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21797
- **Categories:** hep-th, astro-ph.CO, hep-ph, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** A black hole need not ring down in isolation, since a nearby companion can subject it to an external gravitational tide. We calculate how a quadrupole tide modifies the Schwarzschild black hole resonances. Linearising the exact tidally distorted Schwarzschild solution in the tidal amplitude $A$, we derive the diagonal odd- and even-parity perturbation equations on the deformed background and compute the formal first order displacements of analytically continued Schwarzschild resonances. Each diagonal first order shift factorises as $\delta\omega_{n\ell m}=A\,c_{\ell m}\kappa_{n\ell}$, giving one reduced complex coefficient for each $(n,\ell)$, expressed as a ratio of contour integrals, multiplied by a universal Zeeman-like pattern that splits the $2\ell+1$ azimuthal multiplet. For the $\ell=2$ and $\ell=3$ sectors analysed explicitly, the odd- and even-parity shifts coincide, so Schwarzschild isospectrality survives at first order in the tidal amplitude. Quadratic tidal forces, by contrast, can break this degeneracy. Moreover, for a physical companion, the astrophysically dominant $(n,\ell,m)=(0,2,2)$ mode oscillates faster and decays more slowly. Finally, we show that the eikonal shifts admit a geometric description in terms of the Penrose limit about the tidally deformed photon ring.
- **Summary:** The authors develop a first-principles calculation of how an external quadrupole tidal field — as would be produced by a nearby companion in a binary system — modifies the quasinormal mode spectrum of a Schwarzschild black hole. They find that the first-order frequency shift factorizes elegantly into a reduced coefficient (dependent on overtone and angular quantum numbers) and a universal Zeeman-like splitting of the azimuthal multiplet. Surprisingly, Schwarzschild's celebrated isospectrality between odd and even parity sectors survives at linear order in the tidal perturbation, though quadratic tides can break it. The dominant (0,2,2) mode is predicted to ring faster and decay more slowly in the presence of a companion, with eikonal shifts mapping geometrically onto the tidally deformed photon ring.
- **Relevance:** With gravitational wave detectors now routinely observing binary black hole mergers, understanding how environmental tides modify ringdown spectra is essential for precision tests of general relativity and for correctly interpreting post-merger signals. The preservation of isospectrality at linear order is a nontrivial structural result that constrains how quantum corrections or modified gravity might alter black hole spectroscopy.
- **Tags:** Gravitational Waves, Black Hole Physics, High-Energy Physics

## 3. `It from Bit': is there a second law of quantum complexity?
- **Authors:** S. Davatolhagh, A. Sheykhi, M.H. Zarei
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.22977
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22977
- **Categories:** hep-th, cond-mat.stat-mech, quant-ph, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** At a deeper level the principle of least action is interpreted as the law of least entropy increase consistent with Prigogine's principle of minimum entropy production, and the implications of quasistatic information quantization rule (Proc. R. Soc. A 480: 20240024), are explored for the conjectured second law of quantum complexity. It is thus shown that the conjectured second law of complexity is derivable from the information quantization rule such that long after heat-death the quantum state complexity evolves as $C(t)=C_{\rm max}\exp(-1/t)$, increasing with time to a saturation value $C_{\rm max}$ that is exponential in the equilibrium entropy $S_{\rm max}$. For the out-of-equilibrium circumstances, however, the quantum complexity can decrease with the time, asymptotically tending to a minimum determined by the distance from equilibrium.

In thermal equilibrium, complexity is shown to grow monotonically toward a saturation value exponential in the equilibrium entropy, following C(t) = C_max exp(−1/t). Away from equilibrium, however, complexity can actually decrease, asymptoting to a minimum set by the system's distance from equilibrium. This establishes a direct link between thermodynamic entropy production and the growth of quantum state complexity, with distinct in-equilibrium and out-of-equilibrium regimes.
- **Relevance:** Quantum complexity has emerged as a central concept in holography, where the quantum extremal surface proposal and complexity=volume conjectures relate the growth of computational complexity to black hole interior geometry. Understanding whether complexity obeys a genuine second law — and how it behaves near and far from equilibrium — is directly relevant to the black hole information paradox and to whether complexity can serve as a proxy for interior volume in quantum gravity.

## 4. Kerr-Newman and genuine Kerr black holes in the Bertotti-Robinson magnetic field

- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21672
- **Categories:** hep-th, astro-ph.HE, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** We present a spacetime that describes a rotating and charged black hole immersed in an external magnetic field. We show that the Kerr-BR solution presented in [Phys. Rev. Lett. {\bf 135}, 181401 (2025)] has a special charge, and we identify a genuine Kerr black hole without any charge, in the magnetic field. In the weak-field limit it gives the Wald solution. We analyze main physical properties of the new family, namely singularities, horizons, regularity of axes, charges, entropy and temperature.

The solution smoothly interpolates between the Kerr-Newman metric and the Bertotti-Robinson magnetic universe, recovering the Wald solution in the weak-field limit. The authors analyze the full physical portrait — singularities, horizons, axis regularity, electromagnetic charges, entropy, and temperature — establishing the solution space for magnetized rotating black holes.
- **Relevance:** Astrophysical black holes are commonly threaded by large-scale magnetic fields, and understanding exact solutions for magnetized Kerr black holes provides essential benchmarks for numerical relativity and for interpreting observations of accreting black holes. The existence of a charge-free Kerr black hole in a magnetic background is particularly interesting for understanding how external fields interact with rotating horizons, with implications for black hole energetics and jet formation.

## 5. Thermodynamics of Genuine Kerr-Bertotti-Robinson Black Holes
- **Authors:** David Kubiznak, Hryhorii Ovcharenko, Jiri Podolsky
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.22292
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22292
- **Categories:** hep-th, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** We propose a thermodynamic description of the genuine Kerr-Bertotti-Robinson (Kerr-BR) black hole spacetimes. Contrary to previous studies, such solutions no longer have electric charge. Consequently, the thermodynamics is governed by simple relations. Namely, upon introducing a suitable normalization factor for the timelike Killing vector, these spacetimes are characterized by the standard Hawking temperature, Bekenstein's entropy, Komar mass and angular momentum, and horizon angular velocity. The corresponding mass is shown to obey the standard Christodoulou-Ruffini formula, along with the standard first law and Smarr relations. In fact, by a proper rescaling of coordinates and solution parameters, and up to a non-trivial parameter space constraint, we formally recover the thermodynamic quantities of the vacuum Kerr solution. This is used to bring the genuine Kerr-BR spacetime into a Kerr-like form.
- **Summary:** The authors develop a complete thermodynamic description for the newly identified genuine Kerr-Bertotti-Robinson black holes — neutral rotating black holes immersed in a uniform magnetic field. By choosing an appropriate normalization for the timelike Killing vector, they show that all standard thermodynamic quantities — Hawking temperature, Bekenstein-Hawking entropy, Komar mass, angular momentum, and horizon velocity — take their familiar forms. Remarkably, the mass obeys the Christodoulou-Ruffini formula and the first and Smarr laws hold unchanged. A coordinate rescaling maps the thermodynamics onto that of the ordinary vacuum Kerr solution, subject to a parameter-space constraint, effectively reducing the magnetized problem to the unmagnetized one.
- **Relevance:** The thermodynamics of black holes in external fields tests the robustness of black hole mechanics beyond isolated systems. That the first law and Smarr relation survive unchanged for neutral Kerr black holes in a magnetic background suggests deep structural stability in black hole thermodynamics. This provides a useful reference point for comparing with quantum gravity corrections, where similar universal structures are sought.

## 6. Chiral symmetry and black hole isospectrality
- **Authors:** Colin Weller, Dongjun Li, Pratik Wagle, Andrew Laeuger, Yanbei Chen, Nicolás Yunes
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.21532
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21532
- **Categories:** hep-th, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** We prove that black-hole isospectrality between linearly independent solutions follows whenever radiative metric and matter perturbations are reconstructed from complex master variables obeying a closed, complex-linear system with complex-linear boundary conditions. If a phase rotation maps one parity sector to the other, then the even- and odd-parity QNM spectra coincide, and the perturbations are parity isospectral. A broad class of chiral-aligned theories satisfies these criteria. As an application, we demonstrate that subextremal Kerr--de Sitter black holes are parity isospectral.
- **Summary:** The authors establish a general theorem linking black hole isospectrality to a chiral symmetry in the perturbation equations: whenever radiative perturbations can be reconstructed from complex master variables that obey a closed, complex-linear system with complex-linear boundary conditions, and when a phase rotation maps even parity to odd parity, the quasinormal mode spectra of the two parity sectors coincide. This "parity isospectrality" is shown to hold for a broad class of "chiral-aligned" theories. As a concrete application, they prove that subextremal Kerr–de Sitter black holes are parity isospectral — extending known results beyond asymptotically flat spacetimes.
- **Relevance:** Isospectrality is a powerful structural constraint on black hole perturbation theory that can be exploited to test general relativity and its modifications. Understanding the symmetry principles — here, a chiral phase rotation — that enforce isospectrality helps identify which theories share this property and which may violate it, providing clear targets for gravitational wave spectroscopy to distinguish quantum gravity corrections from standard GR.

## 8. Dual entropy gain by radiation at the zero temperature limit
- **Authors:** Koray Düztaş
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.21593
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21593
- **Categories:** hep-th, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** Recently we have demonstrated that black hole radiation can be smoothly extended to the zero-temperature limit. Here we analyse entropy variations at the zero temperature limit and evaluate the validity of the Generalized Second Law (GSL). Typically one verifies that the entropy gain in the exterior region compensates for the entropy loss of the black hole due to evaporation. However an anomalous feature emerges at the zero temperature limit. The fact that the emission is restricted to the modes $\omega < m\Omega$, renders the variation of the horizon area of a Kerr black hole positive definite. Simultaneously the von Neumann entropy of the exterior region increases both for bosonic and fermionic modes. The dual entropy gain at the zero temperature limit marks a robust conservation of the GSL. We demonstrate that the dual increase also applies to Kerr-Taub-NUT black holes whose entropy is neither directly proportional to the horizon area nor analytically integrable.
- **Summary:** Düztaş investigates the fate of the Generalized Second Law (GSL) at the zero-temperature limit of black hole evaporation, where radiation was previously shown to persist smoothly. An unexpected feature emerges: for Kerr black holes, the emission is restricted to superradiant modes with ω < mΩ, which causes the horizon area to increase rather than decrease. At the same time, the exterior region's von Neumann entropy also increases for both bosonic and fermionic emissions. This "dual entropy gain" — where both black hole and exterior entropy grow simultaneously — represents a robust preservation of the GSL at the extreme limit. The result extends to Kerr-Taub-NUT black holes, whose entropy is not simply proportional to area.
- **Relevance:** The Generalized Second Law is a cornerstone of black hole thermodynamics and a key constraint on any viable quantum theory of gravity. Understanding how the GSL behaves at the extremal limit — where standard evaporation arguments break down — is essential for assessing whether black hole remnants are consistent with thermodynamics. The dual entropy gain provides a novel mechanism by which information may be preserved without violating entropy bounds.

## 9. Dynamical Love numbers of analogue rotating black and white holes
- **Authors:** Satadal Datta, Wei-Can Syu, Da-Shin Lee
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.23496
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23496
- **Categories:** hep-th, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** We calculate the dynamical tidal response coefficients (TRCs) of 2+1D analogue black and white holes generated by draining and fountaining bathtub flows, respectively. The parameter space is characterized by the frequency and azimuthal number of the Fourier modes and the rotation of the analogue black or white hole. In general, the TRC is a complex-valued function of these parameters. Its real and imaginary parts are defined as the tidal Love number (TLN) and the tidal dissipation coefficient (TDC), respectively. The TRC of the analogue black hole (ABH) exhibits several interesting features. At certain points in the parameter space, including the static case of vanishing perturbation frequency, the TLN exhibits logarithmic running, while the TDC vanishes. Unlike in Einstein gravity, fluid dynamics allows for the physical existence of an analogue white hole (AWH) event horizon. Time-independent, torque-free, barotropic, inviscid, axisymmetric fluid dynamical equations can yield a pair of transonic background flow solutions. The solutions in the pair, corresponding to an ABH-AWH pair, share the same angular momentum and Bernoulli constant but have opposite mass flow rates, all of which are conserved quantities. For such an ABH-AWH pair, the TRC of the AWH is the complex conjugate of the TRC of the ABH.
- **Summary:** The authors compute dynamical tidal response coefficients for 2+1D analogue black and white holes realized in draining and fountaining bathtub fluid flows. The tidal Love number exhibits logarithmic running at specific parameter values — including the static limit — while the dissipation coefficient vanishes there, signaling a non-dissipative resonant response. A novel feature of the fluid analogue is the existence of a genuine white hole horizon, which is forbidden in Einstein gravity; the authors construct ABH-AWH pairs with identical angular momentum and Bernoulli constant but opposite mass flow, and prove that the white hole's tidal response is the complex conjugate of the black hole's.
- **Relevance:** Analogue gravity systems provide experimentally accessible tabletop models for testing black hole phenomena, including Hawking radiation and tidal effects. The logarithmic running of Love numbers and the conjugate relation between black and white hole responses offer concrete predictions that could be probed in fluid experiments. These results also inform the broader theoretical understanding of how tidal deformability depends on horizon structure in quantum gravity models.

## 10. Curvature and Isocurvature Perturbations in multi-field Gauss-Bonnet inflation
- **Authors:** Seoktae Koh
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.22970
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22970
- **Categories:** hep-th, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** We study cosmological perturbations in multi-field inflation in which the scalar fields couple to the Gauss-Bonnet terms through a general coupling function $f(\phi^a)$. We derive the complete quadratic action for the field perturbations in spatially flat gauge and find that, besides corrections to the kinetic, gradient and mass matrices, the Gauss-Bonnet coupling induces an antisymmetric velocity coupling that vanishes in Einstein gravity. Decomposing the field perturbations into curvature and isocurvature modes, we show that the effective mass of the curvature perturbation and the non-derivative mixing mass vanish identically, $\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{R}}^2 = \mathcal{M}_{\rm mix}^2 =0$ which are justified by the Weinberg's adiabatic mode. We then compute the curvature power spectrum in two limits. First, when the isocurvature mode is heavy, it can be integrated out over all scales. This yields an effective single-field theory of the curvature perturbation with a modified sound speed. Second, when the gradient of the coupling function is aligned with the background trajectory ($f_N =0$), the derivative mixings disappear and the superhorizon transfer of isocurvature into curvature perturbations is governed by the turn rate, which is modified by the Gauss-Bonnet corrections. We also present the tensor power spectrum and the tensor-to-scalar ratio at linear order in the coupling.
- **Summary:** Koh analyzes cosmological perturbations in multi-field inflation where scalar fields couple to the Gauss-Bonnet invariant through an arbitrary function f(φᵃ). Beyond the expected modifications to kinetic, gradient, and mass terms, the Gauss-Bonnet coupling generates a new antisymmetric velocity mixing absent in Einstein gravity. The curvature and isocurvature decomposition reveals that the curvature perturbation retains zero effective mass and zero mixing mass, consistent with Weinberg's adiabatic mode theorem. In the heavy-isocurvature limit, the theory reduces to an effective single-field description with a modified sound speed; when the coupling gradient aligns with the background trajectory, superhorizon isocurvature-to-curvature transfer is governed by a Gauss-Bonnet-corrected turn rate. The tensor sector is also computed to linear order in the coupling.
- **Relevance:** Inflationary perturbations are our most direct observational window into quantum gravity effects in the early universe. Gauss-Bonnet corrections arise naturally in string theory and other UV completions of gravity, and their imprint on the curvature power spectrum, sound speed, and tensor-to-scalar ratio provides model-independent constraints on quantum gravity corrections to cosmic initial conditions.
