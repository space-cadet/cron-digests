- **Abstract:** Scaling fault-tolerant quantum error correction (QEC) requires suppressing errors in both space and time, yet standard logical memory experiments primarily probe spatial faults. ## 2. Static Bounds on Thermal Fast Scrambling
**Authors:** Yixuan Ma, Weikang Li, Dong-Ling Deng  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20958v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph, hep-th, math-ph
- **Abstract:** Scrambling, the process by which initially localized quantum information spreads across an interacting many-body system, is a hallmark of quantum chaos and plays a central role in understanding black hole dynamics. In thermal systems, the chaos bound of Maldacena, Shenker and Stanford limits the exponential growth rate of out-of-time-order correlations to $2π/β$, assuming that time-ordered correlation functions factorize after a dissipation time. Establishing the corresponding rigorous logarithmic lower bounds on scrambling times at arbitrary fixed temperature has remained an open problem, most notably in disordered systems, where the Gibbs state depends on the same random couplings that drive the dynamics. Here we prove such bounds at every fixed temperature for the Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev (SYK) model and for random $k$-local spin systems, without assuming real-time factorization. Our approach uses the operator-size distribution of the square root of the Gibbs state to quantify how local probes modify its structure. This static information bounds the thermal correlator uniformly in time along the edges of its analytic strip in complex time, and analyticity then limits how rapidly scrambling can develop. For both families of models, we prove that with probability tending to one the scrambling time is at least $(1-ε)[β/(2π)]\log N$ for any $0<ε<1$, with the coefficient $β/(2π)$ suggested by the chaos bound. The bound holds for each fixed pair of distinct Majorana modes in the SYK model and for the target average of any fixed single-site source in the spin model. These results place the thermal fast-scrambling timescale on a rigorous footing and show how static properties of the Gibbs state can constrain real-time information spreading.
- **Summary:** Static operator-size information from the square root of the Gibbs state, combined with complex-time analyticity, yields rigorous thermal scrambling-time bounds without assuming real-time factorization. For SYK and random k-local spins, the high-probability lower bound has the chaos-bound coefficient β/(2π) multiplying log N at every fixed temperature, strengthening the mathematical basis of fast scrambling in these model families.
**RELEVANCE:** Bridges quantum-information dynamics, many-body chaos, and black-hole-motivated models without claiming a general theorem for all gravitational systems.  
**TAGS:** `thermal scrambling` `SYK model` `Gibbs-state bounds`

## 4. Lifting asymptotic symmetries from metric to tetrad formulation
**Authors:** Antoine Vincenti  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21296v1  
**Categories:** hep-th
- **Abstract:** Metric solution spaces defined by gauge and boundary conditions are associated with a residual symmetry algebra, consisting of the diffeomorphism generators whose action preserves these conditions. Describing a metric solution space in terms of tetrad variables adds a local Lorentz freedom. When part of this freedom is fixed by additional tetrad gauge and boundary conditions, metric residual symmetries must be supplemented by compensating Lorentz transformations. If a residual Lorentz freedom remains, there is an ambiguity in these compensating transformations. The most direct choices generally fail to realize the metric residual symmetry algebra as a subalgebra of the tetrad one, because of a field-dependent cocycle. We show that a reference tetrad, that is a section of the tetrad solution space seen as a principal bundle over the metric one, always provides a lift free of this cocycle, so that the tetrad residual symmetry algebra is the semi-direct sum of the metric one and the ideal of field-dependent residual Lorentz transformations. The construction is independent of the gauge conditions, of the residual group, of the first-order Lagrangian, and reduces to the Kosmann lift on metric isometry generators. We illustrate it on a Weyl BMS-type solution space with time-dependent boundary metric, described in tetrad variables with a residual U(1) Lorentz freedom, for which the cocycle of a naive lift is computed and then explicitly removed.
- **Summary:** A reference tetrad supplies a systematic lift of metric residual symmetries that removes the field-dependent cocycle introduced by naive compensating Lorentz transformations. The resulting semidirect-sum algebra is demonstrated for a Weyl-BMS-type boundary problem and reduces to the Kosmann lift for isometries, clarifying how first-order variables preserve metric symmetry structure.
**RELEVANCE:** Useful for boundary symmetries and tetrad formulations underlying quantum gravity, including comparisons with LQG variables.  
**TAGS:** `tetrad symmetries` `asymptotic algebras` `Lorentz gauge freedom`

## 5. Localization transition in a spin chain with an exact Fock-space tree
**Authors:** Bernhard Jobst, Simone Warzel, Alexander Altland, Frank Pollmann  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21445v1  
**Categories:** cond-mat.dis-nn, cond-mat.quant-gas, cond-mat.stat-mech, cond-mat.str-el, quant-ph
- **Abstract:** Many-body localization is often pictured as Anderson localization on the graph of many-body configurations. Strongly correlated disorder and a dense net of loops, however, make this graph hard to analyze. We introduce a local disordered spin-1 chain in which a single domain wall explores an exact binary tree of configurations. Random fields create strongly correlated disorder on this tree, as in generic interacting systems, while the absence of loops keeps the problem tractable. We rigorously bound the spreading of the domain wall at strong disorder, and efficient numerics on exponentially large trees indicate a localization transition at finite disorder strength.
- **Summary:** A local disordered spin-1 chain makes a single domain wall explore an exact binary configuration tree, retaining correlated many-body disorder while eliminating graph loops. Rigorous strong-disorder spreading bounds and numerical access to exponentially large trees support a finite-disorder localization transition, providing a tractable test of the Fock-space localization picture.
**RELEVANCE:** A controlled many-body model for separating the roles of configuration-space geometry and disorder correlations in localization.  
**TAGS:** `many-body localization` `Fock-space trees` `disordered spin chains`

## 6. Torsional Hall Viscosity of Massive Chern Insulators: Finite Temperature and Chemical Potential
**Authors:** Weizhen Jia, Ioannis Matthaiakakis, David Rodríguez Fernández, Zhuo-Yu Xian, Johanna Erdmenger, Ewelina M. Hankiewicz, René Meyer  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21217v1  
**Categories:** cond-mat.mes-hall, hep-th
- **Abstract:** We study the torsional Hall viscosity $ζ_{\rm H}$ of Chern insulators at finite temperature and chemical potential. Our analysis considers the massive Dirac fermion and two deformations: a constant out-of-plane magnetic field and a momentum-dependent mass described by the single-block Bernevig-Hughes-Zhang (BHZ) Hamiltonian. For the massive Dirac theory, we show that temperature and chemical potential lead to UV-finite corrections and that a chemical potential outside the bulk gap modifies $ζ_{\rm H}$ even at zero temperature. In a constant magnetic field, the response of massive Dirac fermions is dictated by the relativistic Landau-level spectrum, leading to a plateau structure at zero temperature that is smoothed at finite temperature. The jumps are proportional to the Dirac mass and inversely proportional to the squared magnetic length and the newly occupied Landau-level energy. Finally, we present a detailed study of the correction to $ζ_{\rm H}$ in the single-block BHZ continuum model due to temperature and chemical potential. For parameters relevant to (Hg,Mn)Te quantum wells, the magnitude of the jump across a mass domain wall retains its vacuum enhancement due to the BHZ deformation as long as the chemical potential lies sufficiently deep inside the gap compared with the thermal energy. Our results extend the theory of torsional Hall viscosity to experimentally relevant conditions and may facilitate its future detection.
- **Summary:** Calculations for massive Dirac fermions and a BHZ deformation extend torsional Hall viscosity to finite temperature, finite chemical potential, and magnetic fields. They find UV-finite thermodynamic corrections and thermally rounded Landau-level plateaus, while the enhanced domain-wall response survives when the chemical potential is well inside the gap, defining conditions relevant to future measurements.
**RELEVANCE:** Connects topological condensed matter with geometric response and torsion, an especially useful conceptual overlap with gravitational field theory.  
**TAGS:** `torsional Hall viscosity` `Chern insulators` `geometric response`

## 7. Relaxation Critical Dynamics of Strong-to-Weak Spontaneous Symmetry Breaking
**Authors:** Jiaqiang Li, Xianquan Yan, Han-Ze Li, Shuai Yin, Shuo Liu  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20581v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph, cond-mat.stat-mech, cond-mat.str-el
- **Abstract:** Strong-to-weak spontaneous symmetry breaking (SW-SSB) is unique to mixed states: strong symmetry is broken while weak symmetry is preserved, requiring two order parameters. We study the critical relaxation of both order parameters in a monitored $\mathbb{Z}_2$ circuit, which maps exactly onto two nested bond-percolation problems. The initial state acts as a temporal boundary condition for each problem, wired if the state is ordered in the corresponding order parameter and free otherwise. An SW-SSB initial state wires only the strong-symmetry order parameter, a configuration that no pure state realizes. Each autocorrelation exponent is the sum of an initial scaling dimension, set by the initial state, and an observation dimension, set by the observation time, whereas the equal-time correlator gives only the observation dimension. For the SW-SSB state, the initial dimension is $1/3$ for the weak-symmetry order parameter and $0$ for the strong-symmetry one, each at its own critical point. These initial dimensions also set how fast a stored bit is lost. The probability of decoding the initial value of a single qubit from the system alone decays as a power law, whereas system and environment together retain it. Stabilizer simulations confirm the theoretical prediction, and we propose an experimental protocol that measures these exponents without postselection. Our results reveal a form of boundary criticality inaccessible to pure states.
- **Summary:** An exact mapping of a monitored Z₂ circuit onto nested percolation problems separates initial-state boundary scaling from observation-time scaling in mixed-state symmetry breaking. The analysis predicts distinct initial dimensions of 1/3 and zero, relates them to stored-bit loss, and is checked with stabilizer simulations, exposing boundary criticality unavailable to pure states and suggesting a postselection-free measurement protocol.
**RELEVANCE:** Links many-body critical phenomena to quantum memory and monitored dynamics rather than treating entanglement and information retention separately.  
**TAGS:** `mixed-state criticality` `monitored circuits` `boundary exponents`

## 8. Cosmological Collider Signals from Fermion Bubble Loops with a Chemical Potential
**Authors:** Chengcheng Han, Zhanhong Lei, Zhehan Qin, Wuzhou Yin  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20919v1  
**Categories:** hep-ph, astro-ph.CO, hep-th
- **Abstract:** We present the first systematic analytical calculation of the local and nonlocal cosmological collider (CC) signals generated by massive fermion bubble loops in the presence of an effective chemical potential during inflation. Working in the de Sitter approximation, we investigate the four-point correlator of inflaton perturbations in the single-squeezed limit. By combining CC cutting rules, late-time expansions, and partial Mellin-Barnes (MB) techniques, we derive analytical expressions for both local and nonlocal signals. In particular, we identify two distinct contributions to the local signal, arising from spectrum and integer poles and from ultraviolet poles of the loop-momentum integral. We further investigate the three-point limit, where the separately extracted local and nonlocal contributions develop apparent folded singularities. By reorganizing the MB representation, we demonstrate that these singularities cancel in the complete signal and obtain a convergent double-MB integral suitable for numerical evaluation. Independent calculations using complementary MB representations provide nontrivial consistency checks of our results. We find that the chemical potential can significantly enhance the oscillatory signal while increasing its logarithmic oscillation frequency. Our analytical results provide a foundation for further studies of chemical-potential-enhanced fermionic CC signals, including fermion triangle loops.
- **Summary:** Cutting rules, late-time expansions, and Mellin–Barnes representations give analytic local and nonlocal fermion-loop signals in squeezed inflationary correlators with a chemical potential. Recombining the contributions cancels apparent folded singularities, and the resulting signal can have both enhanced amplitude and higher logarithmic oscillation frequency, sharpening theoretical templates for cosmological collider physics.
**RELEVANCE:** Connects inflationary observables to heavy-particle field theory with explicit control of loop-level analytic artifacts.  
**TAGS:** `cosmological collider` `fermion loops` `inflationary correlators`

## 9. Galaxy number counts in area distance space
**Authors:** Chris Clarkson, Roy Maartens, Stefano Zazzera  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20910v1  
**Categories:** astro-ph.CO, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** We investigate how galaxy clustering changes when area distance is used as the radial coordinate. We derive the linear relativistic number counts for a flux-selected population and compare them with redshift and luminosity distance space. Source motion does not change the beam area at a fixed emission event, so area-distance clustering has no Kaiser distortion. Lensing changes both the angular and radial positions of sources, and can enhance area-distance clustering while weakening its correlation with redshift-space clustering. Observer motion shifts area-distance shells in the opposite direction to redshift and luminosity-distance shells, allowing their kinematic dipoles to point in opposite directions. A combination of the three count fields cancels their common intrinsic density and explicit evolution and magnification biases, leaving a signal dominated by velocities nearby and optical effects at larger distances. We relate this signal to galaxy-size magnification, so that its correlations can be inferred statistically without precise distances to individual galaxies.
- **Summary:** Deriving relativistic galaxy counts with area distance as the radial coordinate removes the usual Kaiser distortion while introducing distinctive lensing and observer-motion effects. Combining area-, redshift-, and luminosity-distance count fields cancels common density and explicit bias terms, isolating velocity and optical contributions that can be accessed statistically through galaxy-size magnification.
**RELEVANCE:** Offers an observational-cosmology strategy for separating relativistic projection effects from intrinsic clustering.  
**TAGS:** `relativistic number counts` `area distance` `cosmic magnification`

## 10. A consistent (3D) EFT expansion of the bubble nucleation rate. Part I: scalar theories
**Authors:** Luis Gil, Javier López Miras  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20938v1  
**Categories:** hep-ph, hep-th
- **Abstract:** Precise predictions of bubble nucleation are essential for connecting cosmological phase transitions to their potential observational signatures. In this first paper of a two-part series, we develop a general, fully perturbative framework for incorporating higher-dimensional operators (HDOs) into nucleation calculations in scalar high-temperature effective field theories. Our approach consistently includes their effects on the bounce and the fluctuations around it, including interactions with higher derivatives. We prove that the resulting statistical rate is invariant under field redefinitions, and establish sufficient conditions for the existence of the perturbative bounce solution in the presence of HDOs. We illustrate the framework by applying it to a model containing a scalar and a fermion, where we find modest HDO corrections in its regime of moderately strong transitions. We thus extend the systematic study of HDO effects beyond critical thermodynamics and provide a controlled method for determining their impact on bubble nucleation.
- **Summary:** A perturbative high-temperature scalar EFT framework incorporates higher-dimensional operators in both bubble profiles and their fluctuations, including higher-derivative interactions. The authors prove field-redefinition invariance of the statistical rate and sufficient bounce-existence conditions; a scalar–fermion example gives modest corrections for moderately strong transitions, providing a controlled route beyond critical thermodynamics.
**RELEVANCE:** Relevant to early-universe phase transitions and the reliable field-theory inputs needed to connect them to observational signatures.  
**TAGS:** `bubble nucleation` `thermal effective theory` `higher-dimensional operators`

## 11. The Shape of the Strongly Coupled MQM Ground State
**Authors:** Alexander Frenkel  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21606v1  
**Categories:** hep-th
- **Abstract:** We study a surprisingly efficient family of variational ansatze for the ground state of bosonic matrix quantum mechanics at large $N$. These are ansatze whose norm-squared probability distributions are themselves matrix integrals with single-trace actions, and so have their own independent `t Hooft expansions. They are efficient in the sense that optimizing over a small number of parameters produces many digits of accuracy for the energy, single-trace moments, and connected correlators. For infinite-$N$ planar data our results rapidly converge to within published bootstrap intervals and stabilize to a few digits of precision beyond them. We also demonstrate that we can efficiently capture $1/N$ corrections by computing connected two-point functions. These are matched against resummed quantum perturbation theory and Monte Carlo simulations.
- **Summary:** Variational wavefunctions whose probability measures are single-trace matrix integrals give an economical approximation to strongly coupled bosonic matrix quantum mechanics. Optimizing few parameters reproduces planar observables inside published bootstrap intervals and captures subleading connected correlators checked against resummed perturbation theory and Monte Carlo, offering a compact nonperturbative computational tool.
**RELEVANCE:** Matrix quantum mechanics is a useful quantum-gravity-adjacent many-body setting; these results concern the bosonic model rather than establishing a holographic duality.  
**TAGS:** `matrix quantum mechanics` `variational methods` `large-N expansion`

## 12. The speed limit of gravitational entanglement and the cost of noise
**Authors:** Ekta Panwar, Anubhav Chaturvedi  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21546v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph
- **Abstract:** Is gravity quantum? A proposed test lets two small masses, each in a superposition of two places, become entangled through their mutual gravity. Gravity is weak, so the masses must stay coherent for seconds while random forces disturb them. Here we ask how fast gravity can entangle two masses, and what noise costs. Without noise we find the maximum rate exactly: it falls as the inverse cube of the separation, and two positions per mass achieve it. Above a noise level set by the masses and their separation, gravity's leading pull can be imitated by classical signals, and every strategy is limited to a rate falling at least as the inverse fourth power. Exact, continuous error correction shields the masses from noise but hides them from each other: the best rate falls as the inverse seventh power, and six positions on a hexagon come within 10% of it at large separation.
- **Summary:** Optimizing gravity-mediated entanglement gives an exact noiseless rate scaling as inverse separation cubed, attainable with two positions per mass. Above a specified noise threshold the leading interaction admits classical simulation and the rate is at most inverse fourth power, while exact continuous error correction imposes inverse-seventh-power scaling, quantifying a sharp tradeoff between protection and gravitational coupling.
**RELEVANCE:** Directly joins quantum-gravity phenomenology and quantum information by bounding the performance of gravity-mediated entanglement tests.  
**TAGS:** `gravitational entanglement` `noise-limited sensing` `error-correction tradeoffs`
