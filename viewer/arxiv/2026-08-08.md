## 1. Information--Theoretic Black Hole Entropy II: Infrared Gravity and Charged/Rotating Extensions
- **Authors:** Alex Kehagias
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.05801
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05801
- **Categories:** hep-th, gr-qc, hep-ph
- **Abstract:** We investigate gravitational and Kerr-Newman extensions of an information-theoretic black-hole entropy that satisfies the Nernst formulation of the third law of thermodynamics. The entropy is identified with the Kullback-Leibler divergence between a mass-dependent Bernoulli ensemble and an unbiased reference ensemble, and therefore measures relative information rather than the logarithm of the number of black-hole microstates. We show perturbatively that its temperature and entropy can be reproduced by an infrared deformation of General Relativity. To linear order in the deformation couplings, the surface-gravity temperature and the Wald entropy agree with the information-theoretic results. In an explicit realization, the matching generates a positive effective cosmological term whose smallness is related to the large microscopic parameter $N$. We also propose an extension to Kerr-Newman black holes based on the irreducible mass. This construction preserves the connection with the horizon area and the semiclassical limit, while distinguishing geometrical extremality from the universal statistical-freezing endpoint, which is independent of angular momentum and charge.
- **Summary:** Building on a recently proposed information-theoretic entropy formula that resolves the tension between the Bekenstein-Hawking area law and the third law of thermodynamics, this work embeds the construction into a gravitational effective field theory. By deforming General Relativity in the infrared, the author shows that the information-theoretic entropy and temperature emerge naturally from a Wald entropy calculation at linear order. The deformation introduces a small positive cosmological constant tied to the large microscopic parameter $N$, offering a potential link between black hole microphysics and dark energy. The extension to charged and rotating black holes via the irreducible mass reveals a striking distinction: geometric extremality (where the horizon area formally vanishes) decouples from the statistical freezing point where entropy hits a universal floor—suggesting that quantum information constraints may be more fundamental than classical geometry near extremality.
- **Relevance:** This is a direct follow-up to yesterday's digest entry on information-theoretic black hole entropy, extending it to realistic Kerr-Newman black holes and providing an explicit gravitational action that reproduces the entropy formula. For LQG and quantum gravity researchers, the idea that entropy arises from relative information (Kullback-Leibler divergence) rather than state counting resonates with recent holographic and informational approaches to spacetime. The decoupling of geometric and statistical extremality is particularly provocative.
- **Tags:** Black Hole Thermodynamics, Quantum Gravity, Holography

## 2. The Quantum Mechanics of Rare Events: From Quantum Walks to Stochastic Inflation
- **Authors:** Daniel Green, Kshitij Gupta, Akhil Premkumar
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.06319
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06319
- **Categories:** quant-ph, astro-ph.CO, hep-th, gr-qc, hep-ph
- **Abstract:** Rare fluctuations in physical systems depend on the detailed microphysics responsible for the fluctuations. In classical statistical systems, the large deviation principle has elucidated the role of semi-classics in describing this regime, and has simultaneously provided a the mathematical foundation of statistical mechanics. Large deviation theory for quantum system is considerably less developed. As all physical systems are fundamentally quantum mechanical, this leaves a major gap in our understanding of rare fluctuations relevant to statistical physics, cosmology, and more. In this paper, we develop the practical aspects of the theory of large deviations relevant for calculating rare events in physical systems from quantum walks to cosmology. We first analyze the case of the anharmonic oscillator coupled to a bath, showing explicitly how the system evolves from dominantly statistical (e.g. thermal) to quantum fluctuations. We then generalize these results, showing that the dominant rare fluctuations minimize the measurement-induced relative entropy. This perspective provides a thermodynamic description of a wide range of open quantum systems. We apply these results to random walks that arise in cosmology through stochastic inflation. We show that the evolution of the density matrix of long wavelength fields on a fixed de Sitter background breaks the KMS symmetry, giving rise to a stationary density matrix that does not respect detailed balance.
- **Summary:** The authors develop a practical framework for quantum large deviation theory, bridging statistical mechanics and open quantum systems. Using the anharmonic oscillator as a testbed, they track how fluctuations cross over from thermal/statistical dominance to genuinely quantum behavior. The key insight is that rare quantum fluctuations are governed by minimization of measurement-induced relative entropy, yielding a thermodynamic description of non-equilibrium quantum processes. Applied to stochastic inflation in de Sitter space, the formalism reveals that long-wavelength modes evolve into a non-KMS stationary state that violates detailed balance—a signature that quantum cosmological fluctuations cannot be fully captured by classical stochastic methods.
- **Relevance:** The cosmological application directly addresses how quantum effects in the early universe (during inflation) deviate from classical stochastic approximations. For quantum gravity researchers, the breakdown of KMS symmetry and detailed balance in de Sitter space has implications for understanding the quantum nature of spacetime and the emergence of classical cosmology from quantum foundations.
- **Tags:** Quantum Cosmology, Stochastic Inflation, Open Quantum Systems

## 3. Asymmetric Quantum Oppenheimer-Snyder Collapse
- **Authors:** Michał Bobula, Tomasz Pawłowski
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.05818
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05818
- **Categories:** hep-th, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** We study black hole formation resulting from the collapse of a homogeneous and isotropic dust ball within a framework built upon loop quantum cosmology. Using loop dynamics formulated for Lemaitre-Tolman-Bondi spacetimes---which reduce to the asymmetric loop quantum cosmological bounce scenario---we analyze the dust ball undergoing power-law contraction followed by a de Sitter-like expansion. We discover an emergence of a physical shock at the dust ball surface, characterized by a continuous induced metric and a discontinuous extrinsic curvature arising from a dynamical mismatch between the expanding interior and the contracting exterior vacuum shells. Furthermore, we explicitly derive two variants of Schwarzschild-like line elements for the vacuum sector, and we demonstrate that the resulting spacetime is geodesically complete featuring a rich causal structure. This establishes, for the first time, fully dynamical regular black hole formation within a framework consistent with loop quantum cosmology.
- **Summary:** This paper presents the first fully dynamical model of regular black hole formation within loop quantum cosmology (LQC). By studying the collapse of a dust ball using LQC-modified Lemaître-Tolman-Bondi dynamics, the authors find that the interior undergoes a quantum bounce (de Sitter-like expansion) while the exterior remains in gravitational collapse. This mismatch creates a physical shock at the boundary—a discontinuity in extrinsic curvature despite a continuous metric. Remarkably, the resulting spacetime is geodesically complete, avoiding the classical singularity and establishing a concrete realization of regular black hole formation from first principles in LQC.
- **Relevance:** This is a landmark result for loop quantum gravity and quantum cosmology. It provides the first complete, dynamical regular black hole formation scenario within LQC, showing how quantum geometry effects resolve singularities while maintaining a well-defined causal structure. The shock-wave formation at the matching surface is a novel prediction that could have observational consequences. For Deepak's interests in LQG, black holes, and quantum gravity phenomenology, this is a must-read.
- **Tags:** Loop Quantum Gravity, Black Holes, Regular Black Holes

## 4. Integrable Models of Inflation Beyond Slow-Roll
- **Authors:** M. Bianchi, G. Dibitetto, J.F. Morales, V. Zevola
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.06071
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06071
- **Categories:** hep-th, gr-qc, astro-ph.CO
- **Abstract:** We propose a novel analytic approach to the study of multi-component FLRW cosmologies and their perturbations. The dynamics is triggered by a single scalar field with a scalar potential codifying the energy density and pressure of the multi-component fluid. This description unifies standard Big Bang cosmologies, models of inflation and dark energy under a unique framework. The key to integrability is to express the scalar potential in terms of the Hubble function $H(\phi)$ that plays the role of a fake superpotential, turning the dynamics into a first order problem, that may be analytically solved in a suitable time coordinate. In this framework, we propose integrable inflationary models with similar properties to the ones analysed in the literature and compatible with observations. Finally, we study scalar and tensor cosmological perturbations in each model by integrating Mukhanov-Sasaki equations via numerical and (semi-)analytic techniques. This allow us to compute the power spectrum, the spectral indices and the tensor-to-scalar ratio beyond the slow-roll approximation and compare our general results {against} the currently available observations and the theoretical predictions based on the slow-roll approximation.
- **Summary:** The authors introduce a powerful integrability technique for cosmological dynamics by encoding multi-component fluids into a single scalar field potential expressed through a fake superpotential $H(\phi)$. This first-order formulation unifies Big Bang cosmology, inflation, and dark energy models under one framework. Going beyond the slow-roll approximation, they solve the Mukhanov-Sasaki equations analytically and numerically to compute primordial power spectra, spectral indices, and the tensor-to-scalar ratio. The method yields exact or semi-exact results that can be directly compared with Planck and BICEP/Keck observational constraints without relying on slow-roll approximations.
- **Relevance:** The connection between quantum gravity and early universe cosmology is a major research direction. This integrability approach provides exact analytical control over inflationary dynamics, which could be valuable for connecting quantum gravity corrections (such as those from LQG or string theory) to observable CMB signatures. The beyond-slow-roll calculations are particularly important for testing quantum gravitational effects on primordial perturbations.
- **Tags:** Inflation, Cosmological Perturbations, Early Universe

## 5. Charged Penrose Extraction in RN--AdS Black Holes with Dark Energy: Ergosphere Geometry and Rigorous Efficiency Bounds
- **Authors:** Anirudh Pradhan, K. Ghaderi, M. Zeyauddin
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.05641
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05641
- **Categories:** gr-qc
- **Abstract:** We develop a quantitative framework for charge assisted Penrose extraction in Reissner--Nordström Anti-de Sitter black holes surrounded by a Kiselev type anisotropic dark energy sector. The generalized electrostatic ergosphere is defined by the balance between redshift and electrostatic work. Under explicitly stated monotonicity assumptions on the lapse and electrostatic potential, we prove uniqueness of the exterior ergosphere boundary and of the turning point of negative energy trajectories. We derive a local upper bound on the escaping energy, formulate a finite radius reception criterion appropriate to AdS asymptotics, and map the allowed extraction domains in terms of the mass, charge, cosmological constant, Kiselev normalization, and state parameter. We also introduce first order adiabatic discharge and accretion laws for slowly varying mass and charge, clarifying the regime in which the evolving horizon and ergosphere can be tracked consistently. The results provide controlled analytic and numerical diagnostics for charged Penrose extraction in RN--AdS backgrounds with a dark energy environment, while clearly separating local kinematic bounds from backreaction, radiative losses, and near extremal stability effects.
- **Summary:** The authors provide a rigorous mathematical framework for charge-assisted Penrose energy extraction from Reissner-Nordström-AdS black holes embedded in a Kiselev dark energy environment. They prove uniqueness theorems for the ergosphere boundary and negative-energy turning points, derive concrete efficiency bounds, and establish adiabatic evolution laws for slowly varying mass and charge. The work systematically separates kinematic extraction limits from dynamical backreaction and radiative losses, offering a clean theoretical laboratory for studying energy extraction in astrophysically relevant black hole configurations.
- **Relevance:** Black hole thermodynamics and energy extraction processes are central to quantum gravity. The Penrose process provides a classical analog for understanding Hawking radiation and black hole evaporation. The inclusion of dark energy (via the Kiselev model) makes this relevant for understanding black hole physics in our observed accelerating universe. The rigorous bounds derived here could inform studies of black hole engines and quantum information extraction.

## 6. Scalar Hair at the String-Black-Hole Correspondence
- **Authors:** Eliseo Pavone
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.06016
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06016
- **Categories:** hep-th, gr-qc, math-ph
- **Abstract:** We study static, spherically symmetric axion--dilaton solutions of the tree-level four-dimensional string effective action. Using the sigma-model structure of the scalar sector, we show that the full family of static, spherically symmetric and asymptotically flat solutions is obtained as the $SL(2,\mathbb R)$ orbit of the pure-dilaton FJNW solution, and we characterize the associated axion--dilaton charge space. We then analyze the perturbative regime of these solutions by comparing the onset of $\alpha'$ curvature corrections with that of string-loop corrections. Finally, we apply the string--black-hole correspondence criterion to the FJNW family by evaluating the local $\alpha'$ curvature diagnostic at the physical size $R_{\rm typ}$ of a highly excited string state. With the normalization fixed on the Schwarzschild branch, Schwarzschild saturates the nominal $\alpha'$ threshold at the correspondence surface, whereas every scalar-haired FJNW branch lies above it. More generally, independently of the common overall normalization within the matching prescription, every nonzero-hair branch has a larger curvature diagnostic at the correspondence surface than Schwarzschild. Away from the correspondence point, scalar-haired exteriors may remain under $\alpha'$ control at sufficiently weak local self-gravity and may provide perturbatively controlled long-distance fields of highly excited string states whenever the local string coupling also remains weak.
- **Summary:** This work explores the relationship between string theory and black hole physics through the lens of scalar hair. Using the $SL(2,\mathbb{R})$ symmetry of the axion-dilaton system, the author maps out the complete family of static hairy black holes in tree-level string effective action. A key finding is that when applying the string-black-hole correspondence criterion—matching black hole curvature to the typical size of highly excited string states—all scalar-haired branches lie above the $\alpha'$ control threshold, unlike Schwarzschild which sits exactly at the boundary. This suggests that purely gravitational black holes (Schwarzschild) are uniquely ``critical'' in the string correspondence, while hairy solutions generically require stronger curvature corrections.
- **Relevance:** The string-black-hole correspondence is a foundational conjecture linking classical gravity to string theory. This work refines our understanding of where hairy black holes fit in this correspondence, with direct implications for quantum gravity. The $SL(2,\mathbb{R})$ orbit structure and charge space characterization provide concrete tools for further investigations in string-inspired quantum gravity.
- **Tags:** String Theory, Black Holes, Quantum Gravity

## 7. Approximate Quantum Error Correction at Chiral Topological Edges
- **Authors:** Yuntai Song, Zejun Liu, Zhencheng Wang, Jong Yeon Lee, Bowen Shi
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.06258
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06258
- **Categories:** quant-ph, hep-th, cond-mat.str-el
- **Abstract:** Topologically ordered phases naturally realize quantum error correction through nonlocal encoding of quantum information. More recently, conformal field theories have been shown to realize approximate quantum error-correcting codes, but such constructions generally require fine tuning to criticality. Here we introduce a family of approximate quantum error-correcting codes realized by the chiral edges of two-dimensional topologically ordered phases. The proposed encoding combines the robustness of a gapped topological bulk with the flexibility of gapless edge conformal field theories. To characterize its robustness, we study coherent-information loss under local erasure. We derive an exact expression relating coherent-information loss to relative entropy, reducing the recoverability problem to universal properties of the edge theory. This leads to power-law scaling of coherent-information loss with the size of the erased region. We further show that, for geometrically local erasures near one edge, the two-dimensional chiral edge code is at least as robust as the dimensionally reduced CFT code, and is strictly more robust in several representative examples. For Abelian code subspaces, we further construct a power-law-range recovery map supported on the erased region together with a power-law-range buffer; this recovery map depends only on the code subspace, not on the unknown encoded state. We provide numerical calculations for lattice realizations of compact free boson and Ising CFT examples that support the theoretical predictions of the power-law exponents.
- **Summary:** This paper proposes a novel quantum error-correcting architecture that leverages the chiral edges of 2D topological phases. Unlike previous CFT-based codes that require fine-tuning to criticality, this construction benefits from the intrinsic robustness of a gapped topological bulk while using the gapless edge CFT for encoding. The authors prove that coherent-information loss under local erasure scales as a power law with the erasure size, and derive an exact relation between information loss and relative entropy of the edge theory. For geometrically local erasures, the 2D edge code outperforms its dimensionally reduced CFT counterpart. Numerical lattice simulations for free boson and Ising CFT edges confirm the predicted power-law exponents.
- **Relevance:** Quantum error correction is essential for quantum computing and has deep connections to quantum gravity via the AdS/CFT correspondence and holographic entanglement. The use of topological edge states as natural error-correcting codes bridges condensed matter physics and quantum information theory. For researchers interested in the quantum information foundations of spacetime, this provides a concrete physical realization of approximate quantum error correction with rigorously characterized robustness.
- **Tags:** Quantum Error Correction, Topological Order, Conformal Field Theory

## 8. Self-dual $S_3$ gauge theory in 2+1d: lattice model and topological phase transitions
- **Authors:** Da-Chuan Lu, Chong Wang, Ashvin Vishwanath
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.05294
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05294
- **Categories:** quant-ph, hep-th, cond-mat.str-el
- **Abstract:** Electric-magnetic self-duality of the $\mathbb{Z}_2$ gauge theory, realized microscopically as a half-lattice-translation exchanging electric charge and magnetic flux, has been an influential example of a duality symmetry with an exact lattice realization. We construct the first non-Abelian generalization of this construction: a lattice model of the $S_3$ quantum double $\mathcal{D}(S_3)$ on a tensor product Hilbert space in which the $\mathbb{Z}^{\mathrm{em}}_2$ anyon-permutation symmetry, exchanging the non-Abelian chargeon $C$ and fluxon $F$, is realized via lattice translation. Consequently we find that the zigzag boundary termination of the model realizes, without fine-tuning, a gapless critical edge state described by the tetracritical Ising CFT. The bulk admits three independent $\mathbb{Z}_2^{\mathrm{em}}$-preserving bosonic perturbations, driving $\mathcal{D}(S_3)$ into distinct gapped phases. We analyze these transitions by three independent methods: category-theoretic anyon condensation, microscopic lattice Hamiltonians, and Chern-Simons-Higgs theory, which all agree, yielding a unified picture. These examples motivate a minimal-condensation principle: proliferating a bosonic anyon generically drives condensation of a minimal condensable algebra containing it, with symmetry-related condensates appearing as degenerate vacua that spontaneously break the anyon-permutation symmetry. Our model construction extends to an infinite family of self-dual dihedral quantum doubles $\mathcal{D}(D_{2n})$. Notably, each model is sign-problem-free, opening the door to large-scale numerical exploration of the phases of non-Abelian Chern-Simons-Higgs theories.
- **Summary:** The authors achieve a breakthrough in non-Abelian topological phases by constructing the first lattice model with exact electric-magnetic self-duality for a non-Abelian gauge group ($S_3$). The key construction maps lattice translations to anyon-permutation symmetry, exchanging chargeons and fluxons. The model's zigzag boundary hosts a gapless tetracritical Ising CFT without fine-tuning. Three independent analytical tools—anyon condensation, lattice Hamiltonians, and Chern-Simons-Higgs theory—converge on the same phase diagram, revealing a minimal-condensation principle where bosonic anyon proliferation drives condensation of the smallest algebra containing it. The construction generalizes to all dihedral groups and is sign-problem-free, enabling quantum Monte Carlo studies.
- **Relevance:** Non-Abelian topological phases and anyon braiding are central to topological quantum computing and have connections to 3D quantum gravity through the Turaev-Viro and Crane-Yetter models. The exact self-duality on the lattice provides a rare analytically controlled setting for studying non-Abelian Chern-Simons-Higgs theories, which appear in fractional quantum Hall physics and potentially in quantum spacetime models.
- **Tags:** Topological Order, Non-Abelian Anyons, Lattice Gauge Theory

## 9. Corrections to the Unruh Effect from Robin Boundary Conditions in Punctured Minkowski Spacetime
- **Authors:** Nickolas P. Botta, João Paulo M. Pitelli, Ricardo A. Mosna
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.04705
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04705
- **Categories:** hep-th, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** We consider a real massless scalar field in punctured Minkowski spacetime, endowed at the removed origin with the stable one-parameter family of Robin boundary conditions $G(0)-\beta G'(0)=0$, $\beta\geq0$. We obtain the boundary-induced part of the static ground-state Wightman function in closed form and study the response of a uniformly accelerated Unruh-DeWitt detector. The puncture breaks the boost symmetry underlying the stationary Unruh response, so the boundary-induced pullback is nonstationary in proper time. The corresponding subtracted detector contribution can either enhance or suppress the ordinary Minkowski response and depends on the Robin parameter, the detector gap, and the portion of the trajectory sampled. For the unshifted, radially aligned trajectory studied in detail, we prove that the boundary-induced pullback is absolutely integrable in the two proper-time variables. Consequently, as the smooth interaction is extended over the full detector history, this contribution approaches a finite limit and remains $O(1)$, producing no additional term linear in the interaction duration. The formal Neumann limit is singular: the boundary-induced two-point function grows logarithmically because of an infrared singularity in the $s$-wave sector, and the numerical response exhibits growth consistent with this asymptotic behavior.
- **Summary:** This paper studies how a topological defect (punctured spacetime with Robin boundary conditions) modifies the Unruh effect experienced by an accelerated detector. The puncture breaks Lorentz boost symmetry, rendering the detector response non-stationary in proper time. Remarkably, the authors prove that for generic Robin parameters the boundary-induced contribution remains finite and bounded as the interaction duration grows, neither enhancing nor suppressing the thermal response in a sustained way. However, the Neumann limit ($\beta \to \infty$) is singular due to an infrared divergence in the $s$-wave sector, causing logarithmic growth in the two-point function. This establishes a sharp distinction between generic Robin conditions and the Neumann case for Unruh physics in topologically non-trivial backgrounds.
- **Relevance:** The Unruh effect is a cornerstone of quantum field theory in curved spacetime and has deep connections to Hawking radiation and the black hole information paradox. Understanding how topological defects and boundary conditions modify Unruh radiation is essential for modeling realistic quantum field theory in non-trivial spacetimes, including those with conical singularities or cosmic strings.
- **Tags:** Quantum Field Theory in Curved Spacetime, Unruh Effect, Topological Defects

## 10. Measurement-induced entanglement Hamiltonian
- **Authors:** Viktor Eisler, Erik Tonni
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.06006
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06006
- **Categories:** quant-ph, hep-th, cond-mat.stat-mech
- **Abstract:** We study the entanglement Hamiltonian of an infinite hopping chain in its ground state, after partial projective measurements in the occupation basis. For a segment separated by two measurement regions from the rest of the chain, we show that the reduced density matrix can be related to a grand-canonical state via a conformal mapping and a gauge transformation in the underlying field-theory description. The entanglement Hamiltonian is then described by a local inverse temperature that vanishes as a square root around the endpoints and is independent of the particular measurement outcome. In sharp contrast, the local chemical potential is shown to be related to the induced charge density in the segment. Hence the entanglement Hamiltonian of a post-selected state contains much more information on the measurement outcome than the respective entropy.
- **Summary:** The authors investigate how projective measurements modify the entanglement Hamiltonian of a free fermion chain. Using conformal mapping and gauge transformations, they show that post-measurement entanglement is described by a spatially varying temperature profile that vanishes as a square root near the measurement boundaries, independent of the measurement outcome. However, the local chemical potential directly encodes the measurement-induced charge density, meaning the full entanglement Hamiltonian captures more information about the measurement than the entropy alone. This reveals a rich structure in how local measurements reshape quantum correlations in critical systems.
- **Relevance:** Entanglement Hamiltonians are increasingly recognized as fundamental objects connecting quantum information, conformal field theory, and holography. The Ryu-Takayanagi formula and its quantum extensions relate spacetime geometry to entanglement structure. Understanding how measurements alter entanglement Hamiltonians provides insights into measurement-induced phase transitions and the interplay between quantum information and spacetime geometry.

## 11. Relative entropy of entanglement of Haar random states
- **Authors:** Takato Mori, Beni Yoshida
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.05274
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05274
- **Categories:** quant-ph, hep-th
- **Abstract:** We determine the relative entropy of entanglement of a bipartite mixed state $\rho_{AB}$ obtained by tracing out one subsystem of a tripartite Haar-random pure state $|\psi\rangle_{ABC}$, finding $E_R(\rho_{AB})=\log\frac{d_Ad_B}{\max(d_A,d_B,d_C)}+O(1)$. Equivalently, the relative entropy of entanglement nearly saturates the smaller of the entanglement of formation $E_F(\rho_{AB})$ and the mutual information $I(A:B)$. The upper bound is achieved by an explicit separable state obtained through one-sided Schmidt dephasing, which is therefore approximately closest.
- **Summary:** The authors solve exactly for the relative entropy of entanglement of a bipartite mixed state obtained by tracing out a subsystem from a Haar-random tripartite pure state. The result takes a remarkably simple universal form: $E_R \approx \log(d_A d_B / \max(d_A, d_B, d_C))$, valid up to an additive constant. This shows that the relative entropy of entanglement essentially saturates the minimum of the entanglement of formation and the mutual information. The achievability of the bound is demonstrated through an explicit separable state constructed via one-sided Schmidt dephasing, providing both an exact formula and an operational understanding of entanglement in typical high-dimensional quantum states.
- **Relevance:** Haar-random states are the canonical ensemble in quantum information theory and appear naturally in holographic contexts through the Page curve and black hole evaporation. The exact formula for entanglement measures of random states provides a benchmark for understanding typical entanglement in quantum gravity, where random tensor networks and typical states play a central role in the quantum information approach to spacetime.

## 12. Holographic entanglement entropy with conformal boundary conditions
- **Authors:** Elena Cáceres, Hare Krishna, Harita Palani Balaji, Vaishnavi Patil
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.06277
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06277
- **Categories:** hep-th
- **Abstract:** We study holographic entanglement entropy in 3-dimensional AdS gravity with conformal boundary conditions which fix the conformal class of the boundary metric and its extrinsic curvature, $K$, while leaving the Weyl mode dynamical. Extending Lewkowycz-Maldacena-Dong's replica construction, we derive the corresponding holographic entanglement entropy formula. We show that the fluctuating Weyl mode does not contribute additional entropy. The entropy of the full boundary is therefore the Bekenstein-Hawking entropy, and the entropy of a boundary subregion continues to obey the Ryu-Takayanagi prescription, namely, the area of a minimal surface divided by $4G_N$.
We carry out explicit calculations for global AdS, rotating and non-rotating BTZ geometries. For an interval in AdS$_3$ we find that the $K$-dependent holographic entanglement entropy is governed by $c_m=\frac{3 \ell}{2 G_N}.$ For a thermal state at high conformal temperatures we find that, the entropy is governed by $c_{\rm eff}=\frac{3 \ell}{2 G_N} \frac{K \ell- \sqrt{K^2 \ell^2-4}}{2}$, the same effective central charge that governs the Cardy-like density of states, in agreement with previous results in the literature.
Finally, we also compute the entanglement entropy directly from the conjectured dual boundary theory - a holographic CFT coupled with time-like Liouville theory and deformed by a marginal $T \bar{T}$ like operator - and find $S_{EE} =\frac{c_{\rm eff}}{3} \ln\!\left(\frac{2 R \sin\phi_0}{\epsilon}\right) .$ This result for the state with no operator insertions (vacuum state ), provides an independent boundary realization of $c_{\mathrm{eff}}$ while clarifying that this state is not the state dual to global AdS.
- **Summary:** The authors generalize the Ryu-Takayanagi holographic entanglement entropy formula to AdS$_3$ gravity with conformal boundary conditions that fix the extrinsic curvature $K$ but leave the Weyl mode dynamical. Despite the boundary metric fluctuations, the minimal surface prescription survives unscathed—the Weyl mode contributes no additional entropy. For thermal states, the effective central charge $c_{\rm eff}$ that governs entanglement matches the one appearing in the Cardy-like density of states. The boundary theory—a holographic CFT coupled to timelike Liouville theory with a $T\bar{T}$-like deformation—independently reproduces the same entanglement entropy formula, providing a nontrivial consistency check of the duality and clarifying that the vacuum state of this deformed boundary theory is not the standard global AdS dual.
- **Relevance:** Holographic entanglement entropy is one of the most profound connections between quantum information and gravity. This work extends the Ryu-Takayanagi formula to a new class of boundary conditions and connects the bulk geometric prescription to a specific deformed boundary CFT, advancing our understanding of how boundary conditions in quantum gravity are encoded in the dual field theory.
- **Tags:** Holography, Entanglement Entropy, AdS/CFT
