## 1. Gauge aspects of teleparallel gravity and Noether's second theorem
**Authors:** Sebastian Brezina, Martin Krššák  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17147v1  
**Categories:** gr-qc
- **Abstract:** We examine the gauge structure of the Teleparallel Equivalent of General Relativity (TEGR) from the perspective of Noether's second theorem. We show that diffeomorphisms and local Lorentz transformations are the gauge symmetries of TEGR, as these are the only variational symmetries that give rise to Noether identities. We then discuss the distinct roles of these two symmetries and their implications for the gauge-theoretic interpretation of gravity. Finally, we discuss consequences of these observations for the role of translations in gauge theories of gravity and the coupling of matter in TEGR. In particular, we demonstrate that the matter coupling prescription is uniquely determined and coincides with that of general relativity, including in the case of spinor fields.
- **Summary:** Applying Noether’s second theorem to TEGR identifies diffeomorphisms and local Lorentz transformations as its gauge symmetries, rather than an independent translation symmetry. The resulting analysis fixes matter coupling to the general-relativistic prescription even for spinors, clarifying which gauge-theory interpretations preserve equivalence to Einstein gravity.
**RELEVANCE:** Relevant to connection and tetrad formulations underlying Deepak’s LQG interests; this is a classical structural result, not an LQG quantization.  
**TAGS:** `teleparallel gravity` `Noether identities` `spinor coupling`

## 2. Higher Categorical Bistellar Moves
**Authors:** Aleksandar Ivanov  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17244v1  
**Categories:** math.CT, hep-th, math-ph, math.QA
- **Abstract:** We construct $n$-categories $\mathscr{P}_n$ with generators given by simplices and relations given by bistellar moves between them, in any dimension $n$. We show that functors out of $\mathscr{P}_n$ into an $n$-category of theories, give the correct input for the orbifold construction for TQFTs, and subsume all previously known examples, which are all in dimensions $n \leq 3$. In particular, in dimension 3 the construction gives a highly nontrivial categorification of a kind of nonunital, noncounital, Frobenius algebra.
- **Summary:** Ivanov builds higher categories from simplices and bistellar-move relations in arbitrary dimension, then uses functors into categories of theories to specify TQFT orbifold data. The construction recovers known low-dimensional cases and produces a nontrivial three-dimensional categorified Frobenius-type structure, extending the algebraic machinery available for triangulated topological theories.
**RELEVANCE:** Bistellar moves and triangulation-based TQFT constructions are mathematically adjacent to spin-foam and discrete quantum-gravity methods, without constituting a direct LQG result.  
**TAGS:** `bistellar moves` `higher categories` `topological field theory`

## 3. A constructive computer-assisted existence proof for a Calabi--Yau metric
**Authors:** Michael R. Douglas, Javier Gómez-Serrano, Fabian Lehmann, Daniel Platt, Yidi Qi  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16908v1  
**Categories:** math.DG, hep-th
- **Abstract:** We give a rigorous, constructive proof that there exists a Ricci-flat Kähler metric on the Fermat quartic hypersurface, independently of Yau's famous theorem, along with an explicit and accurate analytic approximation to the metric. The proof combines numerical techniques to obtain the approximately Ricci flat metric, a fixed point theorem to prove existence, and verified numerics to get the bounds used by the fixed point theorem. The method is general and we discuss the computational issues in applying it to other manifolds.
- **Summary:** A numerical approximation to the Fermat quartic’s Ricci-flat metric is promoted to a rigorous existence proof through a fixed-point theorem with computer-verified bounds. Besides establishing existence independently of Yau’s theorem, the work supplies an explicit accurate analytic approximation and examines obstacles to extending the method to other manifolds.
**RELEVANCE:** Connects rigorous computational geometry with the Calabi–Yau metrics used in string-theoretic gravity and compactification.  
**TAGS:** `Calabi–Yau metrics` `verified numerics` `Ricci-flat geometry`

## 4. Sharp Uniform Fisher Information Bounds from Projective Designs
**Authors:** Zhiyao Yang, Bowen Xiao, Huangjun Zhu  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17288v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph
- **Abstract:** Fixed measurements for multiparameter pure-state metrology must remain informative without knowing the input state. We quantify this requirement by the Fisher floor, the smallest fraction of the quantum Fisher information that a measurement retains over all pure states and tangent directions. We determine the Fisher guarantees, namely lower bounds on Fisher floors, implied by projective design order alone in every dimension $d\ge 2$. These guarantees are sharp at order $t=2$ and every odd order $t\ge 3$, and finite weighted designs attain them; at even orders, our bounds are nearly tight. As the order increases, the guarantees approach one half, the single-copy quantum limit. Sampling and spectral completion of any 3-design yield, with probability $1-O(1/d)$, a measurement with linearly many outcomes and a dimension-independent Fisher floor. Such a measurement separates all pure states in Hellinger distance, so pure-state maximum-likelihood estimation from $N$ copies attains mean infidelity $O(d/N)$ uniformly over pure states, optimal up to a universal constant factor.
- **Summary:** Yang and collaborators derive worst-case Fisher-information guarantees for fixed measurements from projective-design order, with sharp bounds at second and odd orders and nearly tight even-order bounds. Sampling and completing a 3-design yields only linearly many outcomes while retaining dimension-independent sensitivity and uniform O(d/N) pure-state estimation error, giving a concrete route to near-optimal nonadaptive tomography.
**RELEVANCE:** A substantive quantum-information result connecting measurement geometry, multiparameter metrology, and efficient state reconstruction.  
**TAGS:** `projective designs` `Fisher information` `quantum tomography`

## 5. Reduced filtering using classical control hierarchy with linear optimality for continuous quantum error correction
**Authors:** Manuel Guatto, F. A. Cárdenas-López, Felix Motzoi  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16751v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph
- **Abstract:** Continuous quantum error correction promises reduced thresholds and hardware overhead compared to the digital paradigm, but its complex model estimation has greatly reduced its practicality. We recast this problem as a classical control-theoretic state hierarchy made of an exact bilinear reduced model, an exact conditional filter, and standard estimation and feedback design. For any $[[n,k,d]]$ stabilizer code whose generators are all continuously monitored and which is driven by a single Pauli recovery channel, we prove that the exact filter closes at dimension $3\cdot2^{\,n-k-1}$. This dimension is set by the syndrome space instead of the Hilbert space and is minimal within invariant-subspace filtering. The reduced model exposes and corrects two failure mechanisms of the feedback loop. Strong monitoring damps the rotational partner through which the controller steers the stabilizer and injects back-action diffusion along the recovery path; lowering the measurement strength during correction raises the entanglement fidelity of partner-steering LQR control by $0.096$ in the three-qubit code with three monitored parities. Linearizing at the codespace leaves the syndrome uncontrollable, so the controller can stall; a gain designed at a shifted operating point removes this degeneracy. In the limit of zero measurement during correction, we prove that a syndrome-only filter is exact at every recovery boundary. The resulting committed-recovery controller needs only $2^{\,n-k}$ class probabilities and gives the highest entanglement fidelity compared among the methods tested in the literature, $0.947$ against $0.920$ for a measurement-based estimator protocol in the three-qubit bit-flip code, and $0.726$ vs at most $0.407$ for continuous controllers in the five-qubit perfect code.
- **Summary:** An exact syndrome-space filtering hierarchy reduces continuous stabilizer-code feedback to dimension 3·2^(n−k−1) under the stated single-Pauli-recovery assumptions, exposing monitoring-induced damping and a control-linearization degeneracy. Adjusting measurement strength and controller design improves tested entanglement fidelities, reaching 0.947 for the three-qubit code and 0.726 for the five-qubit code with committed recovery. The advance is a structured reduction of estimation and feedback overhead, not a general fault-tolerance threshold result.
**RELEVANCE:** Directly relevant to practical quantum error correction, especially the classical estimation bottleneck in continuously monitored hardware.  
**TAGS:** `continuous error correction` `syndrome filtering` `quantum feedback`

## 6. Statistics of Fock-space correlation lengths in the many-body localised phase
**Authors:** Bibek Saha, David E. Logan, Sthitadhi Roy  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17306v1  
**Categories:** cond-mat.dis-nn, cond-mat.stat-mech, quant-ph
- **Abstract:** The presence of long-ranged, many-body resonances in the many-body localised phase of disordered quantum systems is manifest in strong inhomogeneities of eigenstate amplitudes on the Fock space. We develop a quantitative, microscopic theory for this, centred on two-point Fock-space correlation functions. Exact diagonalisation of the disordered tilted-field Ising chain uncovers a stark contrast: typical correlations decay perfectly exponentially, whereas average correlations exhibit subexponential decay with systematic system-size dependence driven by rare resonant nodes. We show that this behaviour stems from an underlying distribution of Fock-space correlation lengths with a variance that scales inversely with system size. Crucially, the interplay of this distribution with the high-dimensional geometry of the hypercubic Fock-space graph, leads to the qualitative difference between the typical and average behaviours. The theory also allows us to derive the singularity spectrum of the many-body localised eigenstates, and to establish theoretically the anomalous symmetry violation of quantum multifractality as an inherent consequence of the Fock-space geometry.
- **Summary:** Exact diagonalization of a disordered Ising chain motivates a microscopic distribution-of-lengths theory that explains why typical Fock-space correlations decay exponentially while averages decay more slowly. Combining rare resonances with hypercubic graph geometry also yields the eigenstate singularity spectrum and anomalous multifractal symmetry violation, linking finite-size correlation statistics to the structure of many-body localization.
**RELEVANCE:** Offers quantitative tools for disordered many-body eigenstates and rare-event physics, connecting condensed matter to quantum-information diagnostics.  
**TAGS:** `many-body localization` `Fock-space geometry` `multifractality`

## 7. Symmetry-Controlled Criticality and Zeno Localization in a Monitored Quasiperiodic Superconductor
**Authors:** Pinaki Singha, Marcin Szyniszewski, Nilanjan Roy, Auditya Sharma  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16912v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph, cond-mat.dis-nn, cond-mat.stat-mech, cond-mat.supr-con
- **Abstract:** Measurement-induced transitions of free fermions depend strongly on symmetry, but their interplay with quasiperiodic localization and superconducting pairing remains largely unexplored. We study a continuously monitored model of free fermions with pairing, where quasiperiodicity both controls localization and changes the symmetry class of the monitored field theory. Using topological entanglement entropy as a probe of nonlocal correlations in quantum state diffusion simulations, we identify a transition between a critical phase whose von Neumann entanglement entropy scales as $(\ln L)^2$ and an area-law phase, whereas no transition occurs in the clean limit, consistent with the corresponding DIII and D nonlinear sigma models. The transition line exhibits strong multifractality and depends nonmonotonically on quasiperiodicity, which we associate with the symmetry crossover. Deep in the Zeno regime, we derive an effective non-Hermitian Bogoliubov-de Gennes description of individual trajectories and obtain a closed-form localization length that quantitatively agrees with numerics. Our results reveal how symmetry, quasiperiodic localization, and measurement backaction combine to determine both critical entanglement and microscopic trajectory localization in monitored superconductors.
- **Summary:** Quantum-state-diffusion simulations of a monitored quasiperiodic superconductor reveal a transition from squared-logarithmic entanglement growth to an area law that is absent in the clean model. Symmetry-class crossover explains the nonmonotonic transition line, while a Zeno-limit non-Hermitian Bogoliubov–de Gennes theory predicts localization lengths matching numerics. The work ties measurement-induced collective criticality to microscopic trajectory localization.
**RELEVANCE:** Bridges superconducting many-body physics, measurement backaction, and entanglement transitions across Deepak’s condensed-matter and quantum-information interests.  
**TAGS:** `measurement-induced transitions` `quasiperiodic superconductors` `Zeno localization`

## 8. Geometric Theory of Quasiperiodically Driven Systems
**Authors:** Chengzhi Ye, Marin Bukov  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16683v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph, cond-mat.quant-gas, cond-mat.stat-mech
- **Abstract:** Quasiperiodically driven systems give rise to a rich set of physical phenomena in contemporary quantum physics, with applications ranging from nonequilibrium dynamics to Hamiltonian engineering and control. However, unlike periodically driven (Floquet) systems, their study is hindered by the absence of a generic theory to describe their evolution using an effective time-independent Hamiltonian and its eigenstates. Based on counterdiabatic driving, we construct a general framework to identify and determine the dynamically invariant spaces of arbitrary quasiperiodic drives (i.e., the generalization of Floquet states). We decompose their unitary evolution using the exact effective average-energy operator Æ and the corresponding gauge potential, which we compute via a variational principle in frequency-lattice operator space. We then formulate a four-fold taxonomy of quasiperiodic drives based on the geometry and topology of their dynamically invariant spaces into (non)abelian and (un)twisted classes. The abelian untwisted class admits a direct extension of Floquet's theorem to incommensurate quasiperiodic drives; going beyond, we derive a nonabelian generalization of Floquet theory. Finally, we propose a concept of ergodicity whose onset is marked by coalescing dynamically invariant eigenspaces, in direct analogy to the emergence of ergodicity in a mixed classical phase space. The effective mathematical framework, variational principle, and nonabelian ergodicity we put forward apply beyond quasiperiodic drives.
- **Summary:** Ye and Bukov use counterdiabatic gauge potentials and a frequency-lattice variational principle to construct invariant spaces and an exact average-energy description for quasiperiodic driving. Their four-class geometric taxonomy identifies when ordinary Floquet reasoning extends and when nonabelian structure is required, while suggesting an ergodicity criterion based on merging invariant spaces. This provides organizing machinery for incommensurately driven dynamics beyond a single effective-Hamiltonian ansatz.
**RELEVANCE:** A broad theoretical framework for nonequilibrium many-body dynamics and quantum control, with explicit geometric and topological structure.  
**TAGS:** `quasiperiodic driving` `nonabelian Floquet theory` `counterdiabatic geometry`

## 9. Localization of gravitational-wave source behind galaxy clusters by gravitational-wave lensing
**Authors:** Brian H. Y. Cheng, Laura Uronen, Souvik Jana, Samuel Lange, Thomas E. Collett, Otto A. Hannuksela  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16916v1  
**Categories:** astro-ph.CO, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** Gravitational-wave observations have opened new opportunities to study massive object events like merging binary black holes. When combined with electromagnetic counterparts, accurate localization of gravitational-wave sources could allow us to identify their host galaxies and carry out follow-up studies, such as study the environment of merging black holes and determine the Hubble constant. One of the possible methods for precise localization is strong gravitational-wave lensing. Previous studies have considered strong gravitational-wave lensing with galaxy lenses. However, galaxy clusters have yet to be investigated due to the complexity of their mass structures. Here we take the list of known cluster lenses from the Hubble Frontier Fields, simulate lensing properties of gravitational waves from their reconstructions as they would be observed by the ground-based gravitational-wave detectors. When same model family is used in simulation and localization, we find that the correct lens is identified for around 90\% of the simulated events. We also show that the source positions within the cluster can be pinpointed at sub-arcsecond precision with an estimate on source redshift. When we marginalize over all possible lens models for the clusters, the lens identification and localizations remain valid as long as the correct lens model is contained in the available cluster lens models. However, most localization attempts fail at initialization if the correct cluster model is excluded. These tests establish a proof of concept of localization under idealized conditions, while more work is needed to understand the cluster lens modeling discrepancies and reconstruction bias to validate the approach further.
- **Summary:** Simulations using reconstructed Hubble Frontier Fields cluster lenses identify the correct lens in roughly 90% of events and localize sources to sub-arcsecond precision when the generating model is available. Marginalizing over models preserves this performance only if the correct family is included; excluding it causes most attempts to fail at initialization. The result establishes a conditional route to host identification and cosmological inference while quantifying a decisive lens-model limitation.
**RELEVANCE:** Relevant to gravitational-wave cosmology and potential Hubble-constant inference, with unusually explicit tests of reconstruction dependence.  
**TAGS:** `gravitational-wave lensing` `cluster lens models` `source localization`

## 10. Traces of Vacuum Squeezing: A Superconducting Transmission-Line Analogue of Cosmological Particle Creation
**Authors:** Paula I. Villar, Federico Guglielmucci Nazar, Elián Urtubey, Fernando C. Lombardo  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17295v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph
- **Abstract:** Particle creation in time-dependent quantum fields is a central phenomenon in early-universe cosmology, yet it remains experimentally inaccessible in its original context. We show theoretically that superconducting circuits based on a waveguide-like transmission line loaded with dc-SQUIDs can provide a direct analogue of a scalar field in a radiation-dominated Friedmann--Robertson--Walker universe in conformal time. The mode equation of the circuit reduces to the Weber equation, the same equation governing cosmological particle creation in a radiation-dominated background, admitting an exact solution in terms of parabolic cylinder functions and an exponentially suppressed particle spectrum. We analyse the conditions under which the analogy holds, which require a compromise between the duration of the pulse, the uniformity of the modulation and the symmetry of the SQUIDs, and show that dissipation does not erase the cosmological signal. We use the anomalous moment of each mode as a witness of the Bogoliubov mixing: it vanishes for any thermal state and survives dissipation, and, together with the photon number, it certifies squeezing, and with the purity of the state, it identifies whether the pairs created from the vacuum dominate.
- **Summary:** A dc-SQUID-loaded transmission line is mapped theoretically to scalar-field evolution in a radiation-dominated universe, allowing exact Weber-equation solutions and a predicted particle spectrum. The authors identify pulse and device constraints and propose anomalous mode moments, photon counts, and purity as complementary diagnostics that separate vacuum-pair squeezing from thermal populations despite dissipation. This is an experimentally motivated analogue proposal rather than an observation of cosmological particle creation.
**RELEVANCE:** Connects curved-spacetime quantum field theory and early-universe physics to superconducting quantum hardware.  
**TAGS:** `analogue cosmology` `vacuum squeezing` `superconducting circuits`

## 11. Is Spin Slow in Hot QCD?
**Authors:** Zi-Hao Liu, Mei Huang  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16684v1  
**Categories:** hep-th, hep-ph
- **Abstract:** Promoting spin to a hydrodynamic variable requires slow relaxation, as mass-suppressed helicity flips in hot QCD suggest. Using the full leading-order collision operator, we show instead that light-quark spin damps at the transport rate $g^4T\ln(1/g)$, nearly as fast as vector currents. Since spatial spin locks to momentum, helicity-conserving soft, small-angle scatterings readily rotate it. Damping involves multiple kinetic modes beyond a single relaxation time, global rotation adds only reversible precession, and stronger coupling further disfavors a slow spin mode in quark-gluon plasma.
- **Summary:** Evaluating the full leading-order QCD collision operator, Liu and Huang find light-quark spatial spin relaxes at the transport rate g^4T ln(1/g), because even helicity-preserving scattering rotates momentum-locked spin. Multiple kinetic modes govern the decay, and global rotation contributes reversible precession rather than a protected slow mode. The calculation challenges the assumed timescale separation needed to treat spin as an independent hydrodynamic variable in quark-gluon plasma.
**RELEVANCE:** A focused high-energy result with direct implications for spin hydrodynamics and nonequilibrium effective descriptions of hot QCD.  
**TAGS:** `spin relaxation` `hot QCD` `kinetic theory`

## 12. Discrete anomaly inflow from SymTFT of $U(1)$ global symmetry: the modified Villain resolution
**Authors:** Napat Poovuttikul, K. Sapmee  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16696v1  
**Categories:** hep-th, cond-mat.str-el
- **Abstract:** We construct a lattice SymTFT for a $U(1)$ global symmetry with anomaly coefficient $k$ using the modified Villain formulation, in which the spin structure required for odd $k$ is realised explicitly by lattice Majorana fermions. We demonstrate that the same Majorana fermions sit at the junctions of the symmetry lines, exactly where the fusion of two $U(1)$ elements wraps around the circle. From the lattice algebra of lines and junctions we derive the $R$- and $F$-symbols in the bulk and on the gapped boundary, and show that for odd $k$ the boundary $F$-symbol obeys the Gu-Wen relation for the $U(1)$ symmetry defect, without passing through a bosonic shadow. Restricting to $\mathbb{Z}_2\subset U(1)$, we reproduce the mod-4 anomalous phase from a mapping torus and the fermionic stacking rule. As a simple application of this SymTFT, we also determine which subgroups $\mathbb{Z}_d\subset U(1)$ can be gauged and the symmetry of the resulting theories. This is a first step towards deriving, from the anomaly inflow of a continuous symmetry, the junction data of global anomalies beyond group cohomology.
- **Summary:** A modified-Villain lattice construction realizes anomalous U(1) symmetry and its odd-level spin structure explicitly through Majorana fermions at defect junctions. Deriving fusion and braiding data directly reproduces the fermionic Gu–Wen relation and discrete anomalous stacking behavior, and determines which finite subgroups can be gauged. The method links continuous anomaly inflow to concrete fermionic defect data without an intermediate bosonic-shadow construction.
**RELEVANCE:** Bridges high-energy anomaly methods and strongly correlated topological matter through an explicit lattice realization.  
**TAGS:** `anomaly inflow` `fermionic symmetry defects` `lattice SymTFT`
