## 1. Stationary Dirac condensates around Kerr black holes
- **Authors:** Sen Guo, Peng-Yu Chen, Yi-Han Huang et al.
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.25436
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25436
- **Categories:** gr-qc, hep-th
- **Abstract:** Ultralight bosonic fields can form macroscopic clouds around rotating black holes, whereas the existence of analogous stationary fermionic condensates is strictly constrained by their intrinsic spin. Here we establish a complete geometric and kinematic framework to resolve the stationary bound states of massive Dirac fields on Kerr and Kerr-Newman backgrounds. By mapping the Kerr-Dirac system to a globally integrated sourced radial problem, we strictly isolate the boundary constraints dictated by horizon causality. The angular sector reveals a fundamental topological distinction: because the azimuthal quantum number is strictly half-integer, the regular boundary branches prevent the local field density from vanishing on the rotation axis. Consequently, rotating fermionic clouds inherently form globally filled, oblate geometries, in stark contrast to the hollow toroidal structures characteristic of scalar condensates. Crucially, our radial indicial analysis unveils the exact mathematical origin of the absence of synchronized Dirac hair. Precisely at the kinematic synchronization locus, the Frobenius matrix of the Dirac operator is non-defective and entirely devoid of logarithmic divergences. Without these singular branches to be selectively excised by boundary regularity, the physical burden of existence falls entirely onto the causal flux barrier, which strictly trivializes the zero-source amplitude. This synchronization veto demonstrates that a black hole&#39;s capacity to support macroscopic stationary fields is governed not merely by superradiant kinematics, but by the profound interplay between local horizon causality and quantum spin statistics.
- **Summary:** The authors develop a complete framework for stationary bound states of massive Dirac fields around Kerr and Kerr-Newman black holes, resolving a long-standing question about whether fermionic analogs of bosonic superradiant clouds can exist. Using a global radial formulation and careful boundary analysis, they discover that fermionic clouds form fundamentally different geometries—globally filled oblate shapes rather than hollow toroidal ones—due to the half-integer nature of the azimuthal quantum number. Their key result is a "synchronization veto": at the critical superradiant frequency, the Dirac equation loses the logarithmic singularities that would allow regular solutions, proving that stationary fermionic hair is impossible regardless of kinematic tuning. This establishes that black hole hair formation depends crucially on quantum spin statistics combined with horizon causality, not merely on superradiant kinematics.

## 3. Hubble tension: the shape wall
- **Authors:** Miguel A. Sabogal, Luis A. Escamilla, Davide Pedrotti et al.
- **arXiv ID:** 2607.21244
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21244
- **Categories:** hep-ph, gr-qc, hep-th, astro-ph.CO
- **Abstract:** The standard &#34;no-go theorem&#34; against late-time solutions to the Hubble tension is essentially a normalization wall, since Baryon Acoustic Oscillation (BAO) measurements constrain the product $H_0r_d$, with $r_d$ the sound horizon at baryon drag. However, late-time solutions (which keep $r_d$ fixed) are tightly constrained not only by the BAO normalization $H_0r_d$, but also by the shape of the expansion history, i.e. the dimensionless expansion rate $E(z) \equiv H(z)/H_0$. We show that, if $r_d$ and the acoustic angular scale $\theta_s$ are fixed, an increase in $H_0$ needs to be matched by an equal fractional increase in the dimensionless distance integral $I \equiv \int dz/E(z)$: $\delta H_0/H_0 \simeq \delta I/I$. We use this to quantify the &#34;shape wall&#34; set by relative distance constraints on $E(z)$, which we reconstruct nonparametrically, using Gaussian Processes and the latest unanchored Type Ia Supernovae (SNeIa) and BAO data. In our most conservative analysis using PantheonPlus SNeIa and DESI DR2 BAO data, we find a maximum fractional increase in $H_0$ of $\lesssim 2\%$, falling well short of the $\gtrsim 8\%$ required to solve the tension. This shape wall holds even in the presence of early-time new physics, and limits the maximum increase in $H_0$ which can be contributed by late-time modifications to $E(z)$ at fixed $\theta_s$. Therefore, late-time modifications, whether invoked alone or alongside early-time new physics, face not only the well-known normalization wall, but also a stringent percent-level shape wall.
- **Summary:** The authors identify a previously unrecognized obstacle to late-time solutions of the Hubble tension that operates independently of the well-known BAO normalization constraint. Using Gaussian Processes and the latest PantheonPlus supernova and DESI DR2 BAO data, they reconstruct the expansion history nonparametrically and show that the shape of $E(z)$ imposes a "shape wall" limiting any late-time increase in $H_0$ to $\lesssim 2\%$. This falls far short of the $\gtrsim 8\%$ needed to resolve the tension, and the constraint persists even when early-time new physics is included. The result significantly narrows the viable parameter space for cosmological models addressing the Hubble tension.
- **Relevance:** Important for understanding cosmological tensions that may hint at new physics beyond $\Lambda$CDM, relevant to quantum gravity phenomenology and early universe physics.

## 5. Low-Frequency Gravitational Bremsstrahlung and Memory in a Medium

- **arXiv ID:** 2608.24994
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24994
- **Categories:** hep-ph, gr-qc, hep-th
- **Abstract:** Gravitational radiation from the early Universe offers a powerful window into physics beyond the Standard Model (SM). In this paper, we investigate the low-frequency tail of gravitational radiation produced by the decay of massive particles. The energy spectrum in Minkowski space-time, $dE/d\omega$, exhibits two distinct regimes: a flat bremsstrahlung/memory spectrum above a characteristic cutoff frequency and a suppressed quadratic frequency scaling below it. The typical rate of momentum change of the decay products determines the cutoff frequency. We calculate $dE/d\omega$ in two independent but equivalent ways: using Weinberg&#39;s soft graviton theorem and by solving the equations of motion. This further establishes the connection between Weinberg&#39;s soft graviton theorem and the gravitational memory formula, which we generalize to incorporate the effects of final-state scattering. We demonstrate that $h^2\Omega_{\rm h}$ exhibits linear scaling with frequency above the cutoff frequency, transitioning to cubic scaling below it. Our findings reveal a suppression of the low-frequency signal compared to a naive linear extrapolation, with implications for detection prospects. Our results also reveal the connection between bremsstrahlung and memory in the context of cosmological stochastic gravitational energy spectra from the early Universe.
- **Summary:** The paper studies low-frequency gravitational radiation produced by massive particle decays in the early Universe, focusing on the transition between bremsstrahlung and memory regimes. The authors compute the energy spectrum using both Weinberg's soft graviton theorem and direct equation-of-motion methods, finding a characteristic cutoff frequency set by the momentum transfer rate of decay products. They show that the cosmological stochastic background exhibits linear frequency scaling above the cutoff but cubic suppression below it—substantially weaker than naive extrapolations would predict. This suppression has important implications for the detectability of primordial gravitational waves from particle decays.
- **Relevance:** Relevant to gravitational wave cosmology and early universe physics, connecting particle physics beyond the Standard Model to observable gravitational wave signatures.

## 6. Anti-Ultralocality and Plateau Models of Inflation
- **Authors:** Joshua Shterenberg, David Garfinkle, Anna I. Rosenzweig et al.
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.24997
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24997
- **Categories:** gr-qc, hep-th
- **Abstract:** Anti-ultralocality refers to the growth of spatial gradient terms relative to velocity terms in the coupled Einstein--scalar field equations. It is a characteristic feature of decelerated expansion before the onset of inflation. Previous numerical relativity studies have shown that anti-ultralocality prevents the onset of inflation in models with power-law inflaton potentials. In this paper, we show that models with plateau-shaped inflaton potentials, which are considered to be the simplest way to generate a tensor-to-scalar ratio below current observational upper limits, are especially vulnerable to anti-ultralocality effects. The reasons are the flatness of the plateau and the energy density gap of $\sim 10$ orders of magnitude between the Planck density and the plateau potential energy. To study the problem, we develop a protocol for assessing the viability of inflationary models in general, and we apply it to a plateau potential using a previously validated numerical relativity code. We find that, starting from generic initial conditions, the growth of gradient terms in the Einstein equations relative to non-gradient terms either prevents inflation from lasting for enough $e$-folds or triggers a phase of quantum runaway. We show that the fine-tuning of initial conditions necessary to avoid these issues becomes more severe as the energy scale of inflation is made smaller, disfavoring common approaches for reducing the tensor-to-scalar ratio.
- **Summary:** The authors investigate whether plateau-shaped inflaton potentials—favored for producing low tensor-to-scalar ratios—can successfully initiate inflation when generic initial conditions include significant spatial inhomogeneities. Using numerical relativity, they find that anti-ultralocality (the dominance of spatial gradients over kinetic terms) poses a severe threat to plateau models: the extreme flatness and the $10^{10}$ energy gap between Planck scale and plateau energy make these models especially sensitive to gradient growth. Generic initial conditions either fail to produce enough e-folds or trigger quantum runaway, with the fine-tuning required becoming more severe as the inflationary energy scale decreases. This challenges common strategies for suppressing the tensor-to-scalar ratio.
- **Relevance:** Important for understanding the early universe and quantum gravity effects during inflation. The interplay between quantum field theory and classical gravity in the pre-inflationary era is directly relevant to quantum gravity phenomenology.

## 13. An exact dyonic black hole at the integrable locus of quadratic nonlinear electrodynamics

- **arXiv ID:** 2608.23620
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23620
- **Categories:** gr-qc
- **Abstract:** We obtain an exact dyonic black-hole solution in quadratic nonlinear electrodynamics defined by $-F+aF^{2}+bG^{2}$ on the special locus $b=a/2$. - **Summary:** The authors perform a comprehensive thermodynamic analysis of a dyonic Reissner-Nordström-AdS black hole in nonlinear electrodynamics surrounded by quintessence dark energy. Within extended phase space, they derive analytical expressions for temperature, heat capacity, Gibbs free energy, Joule-Thomson coefficient, and Ruppeiner curvature, finding Van der Waals-like phase transitions strongly modified by quintessence and nonlinearity. The Joule-Thomson analysis reveals distinct heating and cooling regimes, while Ruppeiner geometry indicates strong microscopic correlations near critical points. The combined effects of dyonic charge, nonlinear electrodynamics, quintessence, and AdS pressure substantially reshape both the macroscopic phase structure and effective microscopic interactions of the black hole.
- **Relevance:** Black hole thermodynamics is a cornerstone of quantum gravity, providing insights into the microscopic degrees of freedom of black holes. The extended phase space approach connects to the cosmological constant as a thermodynamic variable.
