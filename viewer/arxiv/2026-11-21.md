## 1. Holographic Tessellations Tensor Networks $\{p,q\}$ from Entangled Quantum Tetrahedron in Loop Quantum Gravity
**Authors:** Deden M. Akbar, Freddy P. Zen, Jusak S. Kosasih  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25993v1  
**Categories:** hep-th
- **Abstract:** The relationship between spin network states in loop quantum gravity and bulk--boundary duality is further explored using the tessellated holographic tensor network. We develop a holographic tensor network construction based on quantum entanglement between tetrahedra in loop quantum gravity (LQG). This construction relates four-valent intertwiner spaces and Bell states dressed with $SU(2)$ holonomies to effective tensors on hyperbolic $\{p,q\}$ tessellations. For the $\{5,4\}$ tessellation, six tetrahedra are assembled into a single effective cell with five network legs and one logical index. Provided that the microscopic transfer map has full row rank, a right inverse and a completion on the kernel space are employed to construct an isometric embedding, yielding an effective tensor proportional to a perfect tensor. The construction is generalized to the $p=2n-1$ family, with local dimensions supporting absolutely maximally entangled states. On finite networks satisfying a radial orientation condition, an appropriate normalization produces an isometric bulk--boundary map. For cuts satisfying the isometry condition on both sides, the boundary entropy decomposes into a link contribution $\sum_{\ell}\log(2j_{\ell}+1)$ and the bulk entropy, thereby providing a discrete realization of the quantum Ryu--Takayanagi structure. Applications to the black hole interior are formulated via postselection-based non-isometric encoding. Assuming additional Haar statistics, this framework is used to analyze the preservation of overlaps on average and the competition between entropy branches underlying the Page Curve. These results establish a constructive link between the geometric degrees of freedom of LQG and holographic codes, while clarifying the conditions required to connect them to geometric area in LQG, the location of the quantum extremal surface, and evaporation of black hole.
- **Summary:** Entangled LQG tetrahedra are assembled into hyperbolic-network cells, with rank assumptions and kernel completion producing effective perfect tensors and conditional isometric bulk–boundary maps. On admissible cuts, entropy splits into link-dimension and bulk terms; the proposed Page-curve application additionally assumes Haar statistics and postselected encoding, so the construction supplies a conditional bridge to holographic codes rather than a derivation of black-hole evaporation.
**RELEVANCE:** Directly connects LQG intertwiners, quantum geometry, and holographic quantum information, with explicit assumptions worth scrutinizing.  
**TAGS:** `LQG intertwiners` `holographic codes` `quantum RT structure`

## 2. High-temperature Gibbs states are uniformly Markovian and trivial
**Authors:** Andrew D. Kim, Tai Hsuan Yang, Jong Yeon Lee  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26626v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph, cond-mat.stat-mech
- **Abstract:** We prove that, above a constant temperature threshold, Gibbs states of local noncommuting qudit Hamiltonians exhibit exponential decay of mutual information and conditional mutual information (CMI). Specifically, we prove exponential decay of the CMI for arbitrary spatial tripartitions $A \sqcup B \sqcup C$ which need not comprise the entire system, thereby establishing a \textit{uniform Markov property} for high-temperature Gibbs states. A central obstruction to proving such bounds is that conventional cluster expansions for the logarithm of reduced Gibbs states can fail to converge due to noncommutativity. Our main insight is to construct a classical representation of the Gibbs state using an overcomplete basis. Although such representations generally involve negative quasiprobability weights, we show that a decomposition based on projective 2-designs admits a convergent cluster expansion with strictly positive weights at sufficiently high temperatures, allowing us to circumvent the obstruction. The separability of high-temperature Gibbs states is a natural byproduct of this decomposition. Finally, in the context of mixed-state phases of matter, our results imply that high-temperature Gibbs states belong to the same mixed-state phase as the maximally mixed state.
- **Summary:** A projective-2-design representation converts sufficiently hot noncommuting Gibbs states into a positive-weight expansion, bypassing the convergence problem of reduced-state logarithms. This establishes exponentially decaying conditional mutual information for arbitrary spatial tripartitions, together with separability and membership in the maximally mixed phase, sharpening the information-theoretic meaning of thermal triviality.
**RELEVANCE:** Connects quantum-information measures to rigorous many-body thermal phases and approximate locality.  
**TAGS:** `uniform Markov property` `cluster expansion` `mixed-state phases`

## 3. Scalable Simulation of Strongly Correlated Fermions with Neural Gaussian Tensor Networks
**Authors:** Runze Chi, Ao Chen, Garnet Kin-Lic Chan  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26604v1  
**Categories:** cond-mat.str-el, quant-ph
- **Abstract:** Accurate simulations of strongly correlated electrons remain a central challenge in computational physics. We introduce neural-network Gaussian projected entangled-pair states (NN-GPEPS), which exploit the locally-entangled subspace of Gaussian states to give an architecture more compatible with modern neural networks and an exact contraction cost of $O(N^{3/2})$ on two-dimensional lattices. For the Hubbard model at $U/t=8$ and $1/8$ hole doping, we obtain state-of-the-art variational energies in large lattices of up to 1024 sites. These results demonstrate an accurate approach to strongly correlated fermions by connecting neural networks with Gaussian tensor networks.
- **Summary:** Neural-network Gaussian PEPS exploit locally entangled Gaussian subspaces to achieve exact contraction scaling as O(N^{3/2}) on two-dimensional lattices. Applied to the doped Hubbard model at U/t=8 and one-eighth hole doping, the ansatz delivers reported state-of-the-art variational energies through 1,024 sites, making tensor-network structure a practical route to larger correlated-fermion calculations.
**RELEVANCE:** Relevant to tensor-network methods and scalable simulation of strongly correlated quantum matter.  
**TAGS:** `Gaussian PEPS` `doped Hubbard model` `neural quantum states`

## 4. Inhomogeneous Cutoff Regularization of Loops in Cosmological Correlators
**Authors:** Jacopo Fumagalli, Keisuke Inomata  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26495v1  
**Categories:** hep-th, astro-ph.CO, gr-qc, hep-ph
- **Abstract:** We revisit the use of cutoff regularization in computing loop corrections to cosmological correlators. We argue that a cutoff corresponding to a fixed physical scale must be defined with respect to the local background geometry. In the presence of superhorizon perturbations, this is naturally implemented within the separate universe picture, where different local patches are regulated at the same physical scale. Expanding this cutoff in the long-wavelength background generates a series of regulator-induced contributions to loop amplitudes, which admit a diagrammatic interpretation as long-mode insertions on the corresponding loop contractions. Our cutoff prescription preserves residual spatial dilatations and volume-preserving anisotropic rescalings. It is therefore particularly relevant for loop correlators with soft external modes. We illustrate the mechanism by computing the contributions to the one-loop scalar and tensor power spectra on superhorizon scales. In both cases, the additional corrections induced by the inhomogeneous cutoff cancel the ultraviolet boundary terms arising in the ordinary diagrams. These calculations establish, within cutoff regularization, the late-time constancy of both power spectra and clarify its physical origin. We then investigate the squeezed one-loop scalar bispectrum and show that the additional cutoff contributions are essential for satisfying the consistency relation at finite cutoff. Finally, we discuss the role of the inhomogeneous cutoff in the late-time constancy of the one-loop bispectrum.
- **Summary:** Defining a physical ultraviolet cutoff separately in locally perturbed cosmological patches generates extra long-mode insertions in loop diagrams. These terms cancel ultraviolet boundary contributions to superhorizon scalar and tensor power spectra and restore the squeezed-bispectrum consistency relation at finite cutoff, showing why regulator geometry matters for reliable inflationary predictions.
**RELEVANCE:** Targets quantum corrections in cosmology and the symmetry constraints needed to interpret inflationary observables.  
**TAGS:** `cosmological loops` `inhomogeneous cutoff` `soft consistency relations`

## 5. Scaling classical hardware for accurate real-time quantum error correction
**Authors:** Chenyang Jin, Susanne F. Yelin, Andi Gu  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26447v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph, cs.AR, cs.LG
- **Abstract:** Fault-tolerant quantum computation requires a classical decoder fast enough to process syndrome measurements at the rate at which they are generated, and to return its decisions with low latency. This is most demanding for superconducting processors, whose fast syndrome cycles force a trade-off between decoding speed and accuracy. Decoders fast enough to keep up sacrifice accuracy, which must be compensated for with larger codes and thus additional physical qubits. Here we introduce Rapids, a streaming neural decoder that is both fast and accurate. We achieve this by co-designing the decoder with a field-programmable gate array (FPGA): the network is built from operations that map cheaply onto the device and trained in its integer arithmetic. For the distance-19 surface code, Rapids decodes each round of syndromes in under a microsecond on a single FPGA, fast enough for superconducting processors, and returns its decisions with the low latency that fault-tolerant algorithms require. Its logical error rate is 240 times lower than that of existing real-time hardware decoders. The same design extends to the color code and the [[144,12,12]] bivariate-bicycle code, on which our decoder is 16 times more accurate than the real-time Relay decoder. We further show that the decoder scales: spread over several FPGAs or hardened into custom silicon, it becomes faster, more accurate, or able to decode larger codes. Classical hardware thus becomes a further resource, alongside qubits, that can be scaled to improve fault-tolerant quantum computers.
- **Summary:** Rapids co-designs a streaming neural decoder with FPGA-friendly operations and integer-arithmetic training, processing distance-19 surface-code syndrome rounds in under a microsecond on one FPGA. The reported logical error rate improves by a factor of 240 over existing real-time hardware decoders, with extensions to color and bivariate-bicycle codes, identifying scalable classical hardware as an additional fault-tolerance resource.
**RELEVANCE:** Directly relevant to real-time quantum error correction and the practical resource balance between classical decoding and physical qubits.  
**TAGS:** `FPGA decoding` `surface codes` `real-time error correction`

## 6. Field theory and the biquadratic spin-1 chain
**Authors:** Luisa Eck, Paul Fendley  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26610v1  
**Categories:** cond-mat.stat-mech, cond-mat.str-el, hep-th
- **Abstract:** The antiferromagnetic biquadratic spin-1 chain is gapped, $SU(3)$ symmetric, and integrable. Standard arguments suggest that its low-energy physics is described by the $\mathbb{CP}^2$ nonlinear sigma model at $θ=π$. Using the Bethe ansatz, we show that its low-energy excitations can be interpreted as deconfined domain walls, just as in the sigma model. However, the lattice model is integrable, whereas the field theory is generally believed not to be. We propose a solution to this mystery by finding an integrable field theory with the same low-energy limit but which is not Lorentz invariant. The integrability-preserving scaling limit is obtained by adding a higher conserved charge and tuning the chain to criticality. The gap closes quadratically, so that the limit must be described by some nonrelativistic field theory rather than the relativistic sigma model. We describe the two-particle scattering matrix of this field theory.
- **Summary:** Bethe-ansatz analysis identifies deconfined domain-wall excitations in the biquadratic spin-1 chain, but an integrability-preserving critical limit obtained with a higher conserved charge closes its gap quadratically. The resulting nonrelativistic field theory and its two-particle scattering matrix reconcile lattice integrability with a low-energy description that need not be the relativistic CP² sigma model.
**RELEVANCE:** Offers a concrete bridge between integrable quantum matter, emergent field theory, and deconfinement.  
**TAGS:** `spin-1 integrability` `deconfined domain walls` `nonrelativistic scaling`

## 7. Half-strings across the deconfinement transition of a $U(1)$ quantum link model
**Authors:** Indrajit Sau, Arnab Sen, Debasish Banerjee  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26415v1  
**Categories:** hep-lat, cond-mat.str-el, hep-th
- **Abstract:** The phenomenon of confinement is arguably the key feature of gauge theories regularly studied in high-energy and condensed matter physics. This causes hadrons to exist as stable particles in quantum chromodynamics (QCD) as bound states of quarks (and gluons), and strings in pure gauge theories. The promise of realizing such strings in quantum simulator setups have fueled extensive studies of a plethora of simpler lattice gauge theories (LGTs). Quantum link models (QLMs) generalize Wilsonian LGTs and yield richer scenarios of confinement. For example, the string joining two static charges in the spin-$\frac{1}{2}$ $U(1)$ QLM fractionalize, giving rise to confining phases that break the point group and internal symmetries spontaneously. It was further shown in PRL 130, 071901 (2023) that adding annealed $Q = \pm 2$ charges to this model qualitatively changes the confinement-deconfinement transition. In this paper, we examine the finite temperature phase transition both in the presence and the absence of annealed charges through detailed extraction of critical temperature, $T_c$ , and exponents. The (effective) string tension $σ$ at zero and finite- temperatures are compared between the theory with and without annealed charges. The quantity $T_c/\sqrtσ$ for the QLM with annealed charges is similar to the usual $\mathbb{Z}_2$ gauge theory, while the theory without the charges provides a new universal value.
- **Summary:** Detailed finite-temperature critical exponents and string-tension measurements compare a spin-one-half U(1) quantum link model with and without annealed charge-two matter. The charged model has a deconfinement temperature-to-square-root-tension ratio resembling Z₂ gauge theory, whereas the charge-free model yields a distinct universal value, exposing how dynamical charge content changes confinement diagnostics.
**RELEVANCE:** Connects lattice gauge theory, fractionalized strings, and quantum-simulation models of confinement.  
**TAGS:** `quantum link models` `deconfinement` `fractionalized strings`

## 8. UV Bounds on Inflationary Parity Violation
**Authors:** Alexander Cassem, Mark P. Hertzberg  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26594v1  
**Categories:** hep-th, astro-ph.CO, gr-qc, hep-ph
- **Abstract:** A question of fundamental interest is whether parity is violated on large scales in the Universe. This possibility has been motivated by recent analyses of the four-point matter correlation function. It has also been suggested that detectable parity violation could be generated during inflation by the dynamical Chern-Simons operator, a leading effective field theory correction to general relativity. In this work, we examine this possibility in realistic UV completions, including fermionic and string-theoretic examples. We show that existing bounds on the tensor-scalar-scalar (TSS) bispectrum strongly constrain the possibility of generating a detectable parity-odd trispectrum. For tensor-to-scalar ratios near current observational limits, detectability is incompatible with, or pushes against, the regime of validity of these UV completions. A detectable signal can remain possible for a sufficiently small tensor-to-scalar ratio, but only at the cost of an extremely large enhancement of the TSS vertex. Thus, while a future search for large scale parity violation is worth pursuing, generating a detectable signal within a consistent effective theory is highly nontrivial.
- **Summary:** Fermionic and string-theoretic ultraviolet completions are used to test whether an inflationary dynamical Chern–Simons interaction can generate observable parity-odd matter trispectra. Existing tensor–scalar–scalar bispectrum bounds put detectability near current tensor limits outside, or at the edge of, theoretical validity; small tensor amplitudes leave a loophole only with extreme vertex enhancement, sharply qualifying the mechanism’s observational prospects.
**RELEVANCE:** Relates quantum-gravity-motivated effective operators to consistency bounds on observable primordial parity violation.  
**TAGS:** `inflationary parity violation` `UV consistency` `Chern–Simons gravity`

## 9. Thermal Resummation Effects on Electroweak Phase Transitions and Gravitational Waves in Conformal and Non-Conformal 2HDMs
**Authors:** Yu-Bo Zhao, Ji-Wei Li, Zhi-Wei Wang  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26417v1  
**Categories:** hep-ph, gr-qc, hep-th
- **Abstract:** We quantify how thermal resummation affects electroweak phase transitions and gravitational-wave predictions in classically conformal and non-conformal two-Higgs-doublet models. We compare multi-field partial dressing (PD), which retains the full thermal functions and determines scalar masses and mixing from gap equations, with the Parwani and Arnold-Espinosa (AE) prescriptions at identical zero-temperature inputs. Completed first-order transitions dominate the conformal scan under all three prescriptions. PD typically gives the strongest and most supercooled transitions, reaching $T_p/T_c \simeq 0.5$ versus $T_p/T_c \gtrsim 0.8$ for Parwani and AE. At every conformal point where all three predict completion, PD gives the largest gravitational-wave peak amplitude, followed by Parwani and AE. The non-conformal model has no universal ordering, and the prescription changes the type of thermal history across extended regions of parameter space. For representative benchmarks, PD and AE differ by up to about nine orders of magnitude in peak amplitude and a factor of about fifty in peak frequency. In multi-field PD, the dressed tadpole is not a gradient, so the reconstructed potential depends on the integration path. In benchmark tests, changing the path shifts the transition temperatures by at most about 7% and the peak amplitude by up to a factor of about three. This intrinsic ambiguity is smaller than the largest differences between prescriptions. Thermal resummation is a major source of uncertainty in these phase-transition and gravitational-wave predictions.
- **Summary:** Matching zero-temperature inputs across three thermal-resummation prescriptions reveals electroweak gravitational-wave predictions differing by up to nine orders of magnitude in peak amplitude and roughly fifty in frequency for representative two-Higgs-doublet benchmarks. Gap-equation-based partial dressing often strengthens conformal transitions, while its path-dependent potential introduces a smaller tested ambiguity, demonstrating that resummation uncertainty can dominate proposed gravitational-wave forecasts.
**RELEVANCE:** Links high-energy phase-transition calculations to early-universe gravitational-wave phenomenology and theoretical error control.  
**TAGS:** `thermal resummation` `electroweak transitions` `gravitational-wave uncertainty`

## 10. Fast extraction of universal data from temporal spectrum: Applications to Haagerup CFT and monopole scattering
**Authors:** Tokiro Numasawa, Boris De Vos, Masataka Watanabe, Atsushi Ueda  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26257v1  
**Categories:** cond-mat.str-el, hep-lat, hep-th, quant-ph
- **Abstract:** Extracting precise universal data on conformal field theory (CFT) on the lattice traditionally requires large-scale simulations of the ground states and excitations of critical systems. Here, we propose an alternative accurate and fast algorithm, where we read off the CFT spectrum from the transfer matrix spectrum of an infinite matrix product state after a short imaginary-time evolution. We apply this method to extract the charge-resolved spectrum of a Haagerup CFT to higher levels and obtain a central charge $c = 2.137(27)$. Moreover, we show that the Maldacena--Ludwig monopole boundary can be realized as the ground state of the Fidkowski--Kitaev Hamiltonian after space-time rotation.
- **Summary:** A short imaginary-time evolution followed by analysis of an infinite-MPS transfer spectrum extracts lattice CFT data without the usual extensive excitation calculations. The method resolves higher charge sectors of a Haagerup CFT and estimates c=2.137(27), while a space-time rotation realizes the Maldacena–Ludwig monopole boundary through a Fidkowski–Kitaev ground state, linking efficient numerics to unusual conformal boundary physics.
**RELEVANCE:** Bridges tensor-network computation, universal many-body data, and field-theoretic monopole boundaries.  
**TAGS:** `temporal transfer spectrum` `Haagerup CFT` `monopole boundary`

## 11. Query-Optimal Quantum Minimum Finding from Numerical Estimators
**Authors:** Egil Bae  
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26606v1  
**Categories:** quant-ph
- **Abstract:** We propose a quantum minimum-finding algorithm for objectives accessed via numerical estimators that avoids the logarithmic query overhead of amplifying the confidence of estimates before the search. A coherent estimator that is $\varepsilon$-accurate with probability at least $2/3$ on each of $N$ labels suffices to find a $2\varepsilon$-optimal label using $O(\sqrt N)$ base-estimator queries at constant confidence. The query bound and the additive-error constant are separately worst-case optimal. More generally, suppose a preparation assigns probability mass at least a known $p$ to objective values at most $a$. Without knowing $a$ or the prepared distribution, our algorithm returns a candidate and an estimate $v$ such that, jointly with constant probability, $v$ is $\varepsilon$-accurate for the candidate and $v\le a+\varepsilon$. The algorithm uses $O(p^{-1/2})$ queries to each of the preparation and estimator oracles in the worst case, and the selected candidate can be retained as a quantum state. For preparation width $s_P$ and estimator workspace width $w$, it requires $O(s_P+w+\log\log(4/p))$ qubits, independently of the requested confidence. Applications give worst-case optimal $Θ(\sqrt N/η)$ queries for selecting an $η$-optimal bounded expectation, and guided ground-energy estimation using $O(ω^{-1})$ preparations and $O((ωη)^{-1})$ elementary controlled-evolution queries at constant confidence, where $ω$ is a lower bound on the guiding state's ground-space amplitude overlap.
- **Summary:** A coherent estimator with only constant success probability is sufficient for a quantum search returning a 2ε-optimal label in O(√N) estimator queries, removing the usual logarithmic confidence-amplification overhead. Separate worst-case optimality results for query cost and additive error, plus a probability-mass-guided extension, give sharper resource bounds for expectation optimization and guided ground-energy estimation.
**RELEVANCE:** Provides algorithmic improvements relevant to quantum optimization and many-body ground-energy estimation.  
**TAGS:** `quantum minimum finding` `query optimality` `ground-energy estimation`
