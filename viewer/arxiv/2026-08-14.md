## 1. Coherent quantum geometry: de Sitter spacetime in different foliations
- **Authors:** Tommaso Bambagiotti, Roberto Casadio, Andrea Giusti et al.
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.12161
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12161
- **Categories:** hep-th, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** In any theory of quantum gravity, an interesting question to address is to what extent known solutions of the Einstein field equations can be obtained as expectation values of metric operators on suitable quantum states. In this work, we consider coherent states (to ensure minimum uncertainty) for different foliations of the de~Sitter spacetime and study temporal evolution and coordinate invariance. A general framework will first be introduced for metrics that can be diagonalised globally. We will then find that the normalisability of coherent states in this framework requires using reference frames without coordinate singularities.
- **Summary:** The authors develop a framework for constructing coherent states on globally diagonalisable metrics and apply it to de Sitter spacetime in various foliations. A key finding is that the normalisability of these minimum-uncertainty quantum states depends critically on the choice of foliation — coordinate singularities can render the states non-normalisable. This establishes a direct link between the geometric structure of the classical spacetime and the viability of its quantum description, with implications for how semiclassical spacetime emerges from quantum gravity.
- **Relevance:** Directly relevant to quantum gravity and semiclassical spacetime emergence. The approach of using coherent states to connect quantum operators to classical geometries is closely related to techniques used in loop quantum gravity.
- **Tags:** Quantum Gravity, Semiclassical Spacetime, de Sitter

## 2. Wormhole Geometry from a Magnetic Vortex
- **Authors:** Dušan Đorđević, Fabián Molina, Vladimir Juričić
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.12285
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12285
- **Categories:** hep-th, gr-qc, cond-mat.mes-hall
- **Abstract:** Strong coupling to a magnetic texture makes an electron propagate through an emergent curved space. We show that an elementary vortex realizes the exterior spatial geometry of an Ellis wormhole: an ultrastatic throat with radius fixed by the topological charge and Hund exchange, cut off at short distances by the microscopic core. Two separable signatures follow directly: the electron deflection collapses onto a single Ellis curve governed by the vortex winding and exchange coupling, while the spin Berry phase produces a half-flux Aharonov--Bohm response switched on and off by winding parity. The same metric can be emulated in a designer honeycomb lattice, where the valley-symmetrized wave-packet response follows the predicted exterior geodesic. These signatures are accessible through real-space electron deflection and scattering, providing experimentally distinct probes of the emergent geometry and Berry flux. The magnetic vortex thus turns a topological defect into a tunable curved-space lens for electrons in quantum materials and designer lattices.
- **Summary:** The authors demonstrate that a magnetic vortex in a 2D material can act as an experimental analogue of an Ellis wormhole, with the vortex's topological charge and exchange coupling setting the wormhole throat radius. They identify two clear experimental signatures: electron trajectories that follow Ellis geodesics and a spin-dependent half-flux Aharonov-Bohm effect. The proposal is concrete enough that the same geometry can be engineered in a designer honeycomb lattice, making this a rare tabletop system where curved-spacetime effects could be directly observed in condensed matter.
- **Relevance:** Bridges gravitational physics with condensed matter in an experimentally accessible way. Analogue gravity systems like this provide valuable insights into quantum field theory in curved spacetime.
- **Tags:** Analogue Gravity, Topological Defects, Emergent Geometry

- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12303

- **Abstract:** We construct phase transitions that proliferate condensable anyons in general 2+1d topological orders, including non-abelian ones. ## 4. On-Shell Amplitudes and Black-Hole Perturbations: Exact Reissner-Nordström Mixing
- **Authors:** Kento Takahara, Teppei Kitahara
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.11703
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11703
- **Categories:** hep-th, hep-ph, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** Can flat-space on-shell amplitudes determine the channel basis of a coupled black-hole perturbation problem? We address this question for electromagnetic and gravitational perturbations of a Reissner-Nordström (RN) black-hole. We organize the minimally coupled photon-graviton tree amplitudes off a heavy charged source into a $2\times 2$ channel-space matrix and perform a parity-resolved Jacob-Wick partial-wave projection. For every radiative multipole $\ell \geq 2$ and in both parity sectors, we show that the trace-free fixed-source partial-wave matrix is exactly proportional to the trace-free Moncrief coupling matrix, and therefore selects the same constant spectral projectors. Through a first-Born matching, the amplitudes determine the same eigenspaces in the leading $r^{-3}$ weak-field potential, but not the complete radial potentials. Using the exact classical RN potentials as independent curved-background input, we show that these projectors persist throughout the full radial domain. We also explicitly retain finite-mass effects through $\mathcal{O}(\omega/m)$, finding a nonvanishing commutator with the Moncrief coupling matrix, which shows that the RN-projector alignment is spoiled by genuine two-body recoil effects. As a first step toward rotation, we further extract the representation-independent linear-spin term from a minimally coupled Dirac amplitude. We find that the complete tree-level channel matrix factorizes with a single linear-spin dressing, while the formal $J=2$ block fails to preserve the unchanged RN projectors. This restricted result does not constitute a test of Kerr-Newman separability, but it indicates that a rotating generalization must account for spin-induced angular-mode mixing. We expect that this on-shell method can be extended to more general long-range scattering systems with two asymptotic channels.
- **Summary:** The authors use flat-space on-shell scattering amplitudes to reconstruct the channel-mixing structure of coupled electromagnetic-gravitational perturbations around a Reissner-Nordström black hole. Remarkably, the amplitude-derived spectral projectors match the exact Moncrief coupling matrix across the entire radial domain, not just in the weak-field limit — though this alignment breaks down when finite-mass recoil effects are included. The work also takes a first step toward the rotating Kerr-Newman case by extracting spin-dependent corrections from a Dirac amplitude, finding that rotation introduces additional angular-mode mixing not captured by the RN projectors.
- **Relevance:** Represents a promising on-shell approach to black hole perturbation theory that connects scattering amplitudes with curved-spacetime physics. Relevant for gravitational wave physics and understanding black hole spectroscopy.
- **Tags:** Black Hole Perturbations, Scattering Amplitudes, Gravitational Waves

## 5. On divergences in a four-derivative scalar field theory
- **Authors:** Maegan Anderson, Sam Bateman, Franz Herzog et al.
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.12210
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12210
- **Categories:** hep-th, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** We perform a detailed diagrammatic analysis of the renormalisation of a family of asymptotically free, shift-symmetric four-derivative scalar field theories introduced by Holdom in arXiv:2303.06723 and arXiv:2402.09223. We extend the renormalisation of the theory from one to three loops using both an $R^*$ method and an asymptotic expansion in momenta. We prove that the Euclidean correlators (or off-shell amplitudes) are IR finite, to all orders in perturbation theory, and derive a non-renormalisation theorem describing the all-order structure of the renormalisation constants. In particular, a purely cubic interaction is RG invariant and a perfect square Lagrangian density is preserved under renormalisation. The latter result is due to a Ward identity in a related $\textit{ gravitational}$ theory $-$ the conformally flat limit of quadratic gravity (CFQG). We show that the beta function for the perfect square theory maps exactly to that of an $O(2)$-symmetric, two-derivative, massless $\phi^4$ theory at negative coupling. We verify this relationship explicitly up to three loops and thus determine the beta function and anomalous dimension for both the perfect square theory and CFQG to six loops.
- **Summary:** The authors perform a multi-loop renormalisation analysis of a family of asymptotically free, shift-symmetric scalar theories with four-derivative kinetic terms. A key result is a non-renormalisation theorem proving that certain interaction structures — including a purely cubic term and a perfect-square Lagrangian — are preserved to all orders. Surprisingly, the beta function of the perfect-square theory maps exactly onto that of an $O(2)$ $\phi^4$ theory at negative coupling, enabling a six-loop determination of the beta function and anomalous dimension for both the scalar theory and the conformally flat limit of quadratic gravity.
- **Relevance:** Provides new structural insights into higher-derivative field theories with connections to quadratic gravity. The asymptotic freedom and exact mapping to lower-derivative theories are mathematically elegant results.
- **Tags:** Higher-Derivative Gravity, Renormalisation, Asymptotic Freedom

## 6. Quantum-Corrected Entropy Bounds on Black Hole Merger Efficiency

- **arXiv ID:** 2608.10477
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10477
- **Categories:** gr-qc
- **Abstract:** We examine the entropy balance of black hole mergers in the presence of quantum gravity corrections described by the Generalized Uncertainty Principle (GUP). Focusing on the coalescence of two non-spinning Schwarzschild black holes with unequal masses, we analyze the thermodynamic evolution from the initial binary system to the final merger remnant, explicitly accounting for energy loss through gravitational-wave emission. Using a logarithmically corrected entropy motivated by a quadratic GUP, we derive a generalized entropy change as a function of the gravitational-wave efficiency and the mass ratio of the merging components. - **Relevance:** Directly connects quantum gravity phenomenology to observable gravitational wave physics. The GUP-based approach offers a testable prediction about where quantum corrections might first show up in LIGO/Virgo data.
- **Tags:** Black Hole Thermodynamics, Generalized Uncertainty Principle, Gravitational Waves

## 7. Constraining ModMax Black Holes with EHT and GRAVITY Observations: Optical Signatures and Accretion Disk Properties

- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10859

- **Abstract:** In this work, we study photon propagation and the radiative properties of a thin accretion disk around a ModMax black hole and examine the effects of the charge $Q$ (i.e. $Q^2 = Q^2_e +Q^2_m$ is the total dyonic charge, with $Q_e$ and $Q_m$ denoting the electric and magnetic charges, respectively) and the nonlinearity parameter $v$. ## 8. Periodic orbits and gravitational wave signatures from magnetic dipoles around magnetized Kerr black holes
- **Authors:** Shokhzod Jumaniyozov, Javlon Rayimbaev, Chengxun Yuan et al.
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.11048
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11048
- **Categories:** gr-qc
- **Abstract:** We study periodic orbits and the associated gravitational radiation of a magnetized (uncharged) test particle carrying a magnetic dipole moment with coupling constant beta, moving in the equatorial plane of a rotating, magnetized Kerr black hole immersed in an external asymptotically uniform magnetic field, starting from the effective potential derived for such particles. We compute the marginally bound orbit (MBO) and the innermost stable circular orbit (ISCO) as functions of the black hole spin a and the magnetic coupling beta, and map out the allowed region of the orbital energy-angular momentum (L, E) plane for bound motion. We then classify periodic orbits using the topological zoom-whirl scheme of Levin and Perez-Giz, characterized by three integers (z,w,v) through the rational rotation number q=w+v/z, and construct a family of closed rosette orbits at fixed angular momentum. Using the numerical-kludge, restricted-quadrupole approximation for an extreme-mass-ratio inspiral consisting of a stellar-mass magnetized secondary orbiting a supermassive magnetized Kerr black hole, we compute the time-domain gravitational waveforms h_+(t), h_\times(t) produced by these periodic orbits and their frequency-domain characteristic strain, and compare the latter with the anticipated instrumental sensitivity curves of LISA, Taiji and TianQin. We find that the magnetic coupling \beta systematically shifts the MBO and ISCO outward and lowers their orbital energy and angular momentum, that the zoom-whirl structure of the periodic orbits is imprinted directly on the burst-like morphology of the emitted waveform, and that the resulting gravitational-wave signals fall within the sensitivity band of upcoming space-based detectors for suitably close and massive sources.
- **Summary:** The authors extend the study of extreme-mass-ratio inspirals to magnetized test particles orbiting magnetized Kerr black holes, computing how magnetic dipole coupling modifies the innermost stable circular orbit and marginally bound orbit. They classify periodic orbits using the zoom-whirl scheme and show that the resulting gravitational waveforms retain clear burst-like signatures of the orbital topology. For realistic astrophysical parameters, the predicted signals fall within the sensitivity curves of future space-based detectors like LISA, Taiji, and TianQin — making this a potentially observable effect.
- **Relevance:** Relevant for future gravitational wave astronomy with space-based detectors. The magnetized-particle scenario provides a more realistic model for astrophysical extreme-mass-ratio inspirals than pure geodesic motion.
- **Tags:** Extreme-Mass-Ratio Inspiral, Gravitational Waves, LISA

## 9. A Diverse Distribution of Black Hole Spins from Stable Mass Transfer
- **Authors:** Linhao Ma, Jakub Klencki, Eliot Quataert et al.
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.11311
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11311
- **Categories:** astro-ph.HE, astro-ph.SR, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** Gravitational wave observations have found over 300 merging binary black holes, yet their origins remain uncertain. Recent work showed that many may come from isolated stellar binaries whose orbits shrink through stable mass transfer. If true, their spins may help to distinguish this channel from other formation pathways. We investigate the tidal spin up of black hole progenitor stars with detailed modeling of binaries undergoing stable mass transfer. We calculate the tidal torques by solving tidally excited oscillation modes and predict the resulting black hole spins. We find a diverse spin distribution strongly affected by the mass transfer histories of the progenitors. Binaries can form black holes with moderate spins ($0.1\lesssim\chi_\mathrm{eff}\lesssim0.3$) if they only go through case A or case B mass transfer. In the former case, they can become super-synchronized upon detachment, while in the latter case, the donor is usually only partially stripped, leaving a puffy envelope where strong tides are excited. If both case A and case AB mass transfer occur, the resulting black hole spins are almost always negligible. As the mass transfer history is jointly determined by mass ratio and initial binary period, our results predict an anti-correlation between black hole spins and mass ratio, consistent with limited evidence from data. Our results can also potentially explain the case of GW190412, a moderately-spinning binary with a high mass ratio. We discuss the limitations of our methods and additional physics (e.g., nonlinear tides, case C, and L2 mass transfer) that need to be incorporated in future work.
- **Summary:** Using detailed tidal spin-up models for black hole progenitors undergoing stable mass transfer, the authors predict a diverse spin distribution that depends sensitively on the mass transfer history (case A vs. case B vs. case AB). A key prediction is an anti-correlation between black hole spin and mass ratio, which could serve as a discriminator between isolated binary formation and dynamical channels. The model successfully explains the anomalous GW190412 event, which has moderate spin and high mass ratio.
- **Relevance:** Important for interpreting the growing population of gravitational wave events and understanding black hole formation channels. The spin-mass ratio correlation is a key observable for distinguishing binary evolution scenarios.
- **Tags:** Black Hole Formation, Gravitational Wave Sources, Stellar Evolution

## 10. Imprints of core/cusp dark matter distributions on black hole signatures in galaxies
- **Authors:** Hassan Hassanabadi, Che-Yu Chen, Soroush Zare et al.
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.11430
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11430
- **Categories:** gr-qc
- **Abstract:** In galactic environments, a host dark matter (DM) halo can imprint weak but coherent corrections on black hole (BH) strong-field observables. We construct an exact family of static and spherically symmetric BH spacetimes sourced by a generic core/cusp DM halo, described by an anisotropic stress-energy tensor with nonvanishing radial pressure. The resulting geometry is determined self-consistently from the Einstein equations for a broad $\{\alpha,\beta,\gamma\}$ density profile, including the NFW, Moore, Hernquist, Jaffe, and core/cusp Dehnen models as special cases. We discuss the asymptotic structure, horizon location, curvature scale, and energy conditions of the corresponding geometries, emphasizing that the inner logarithmic slope $\gamma$ controls the amount of DM probed by the relativistic region. We then obtain perturbative analytic estimates for the characteristic circular geodesics, demonstrating that the leading strong-field corrections are controlled by the dimensionless compactness $q_\gamma$, rather than by the total halo mass alone. To leading order in $q_\gamma$, we derive analytic expressions for the light ring radius, angular frequency, critical impact parameter, Lyapunov exponent, innermost stable circular orbit (ISCO) radius, and ISCO frequency. For cuspy profiles, the light ring and ISCO are displaced outward, while the corresponding orbital frequencies are redshifted; for cored profiles, the light ring radius is unchanged at this order, although its frequency and capture impact parameter still carry finite environmental corrections. We also investigate the weak- and strong-deflection angles and their dependence on the halo's inner structure. We further discuss how these environmental corrections may affect ringdown physics, in particular through the perturbative imprint of the halo on quasinormal-mode redshifts and late-time wave propagation.
- **Summary:** The authors construct exact black hole spacetimes embedded in generic dark matter halos with core/cusp profiles, deriving self-consistent geometries from the Einstein equations. They show that strong-field observables — including the light ring, ISCO, and quasinormal modes — carry corrections controlled by a dimensionless compactness parameter rather than total halo mass. A striking finding is that cuspy profiles push the light ring outward and redshift orbital frequencies, while cored profiles leave the light ring radius unchanged at leading order, offering a potential discriminator between dark matter models through black hole imaging and ringdown.
- **Relevance:** Connects dark matter astrophysics with strong-field gravity observables. The analytic treatment of environmental corrections to black hole spacetimes is valuable for both theory and observation.
- **Tags:** Dark Matter, Black Hole Strong-Field, Environmental Effects

## 11. Constraints on ultralight bosons from merging binary and remnant black holes observed during the second and third parts of the fourth LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA observing run
- **Authors:** LIGO Scientific Collaboration, Virgo Collaboration, KAGRA Collaboration et al.
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.11620
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11620
- **Categories:** astro-ph.HE, hep-ph, gr-qc
- **Abstract:** We present constraints on ultralight bosons using binary black hole mergers observed in the second and third parts of the fourth LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA observing run. Directed searches are conducted for long-transient gravitational waves from ultralight vector boson clouds around merger remnants, using a hidden-Markov-model (HMM) tracking scheme. We target the remnant black holes formed in the binary coalescences that produced GW250114 and GW250207. We find no evidence for such signals from either target. Estimating our search sensitivity at a threshold corresponding to a 1% false alarm probability, we thus disfavor vector boson masses in the range of $[2.80, 3.95]\times 10^{-13}$ eV with greater than 90% confidence. At $10^5$ years, corresponding to typical dynamically formed binaries, we exclude scalar and vector bosons in the ranges $[1.39, 6.94]\times 10^{-13}$ eV and $[0.32, 14.4]\times 10^{-13}$ eV at 90% confidence, respectively.
- **Summary:** The LVK collaboration presents new constraints on ultralight boson masses using O4 data, combining two complementary methods: directed searches for gravitational wave signals from boson clouds around merger remnants (using hidden Markov models), and spin-based constraints from high-spin black holes in three binaries. The combined analysis excludes ultralight vector bosons in the $10^{-13}$ eV range and, assuming typical dynamical formation ages, also constrains ultralight scalars — narrowing the allowed parameter space for dark matter candidates that could form gravitational atom states around black holes.
- **Relevance:** Important observational constraint on ultralight dark matter candidates. The multi-method approach strengthens the robustness of the exclusion limits and demonstrates the power of gravitational wave data for particle physics.

## 12. Finding black hole spins efficiently during a numerical binary evolution
- **Authors:** Himanshu Chaudhary, Rob Owen, Mark A. Scheel et al.
- **arXiv ID:** 2608.12211
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12211
- **Categories:** gr-qc
- **Abstract:** The dynamics of a binary black hole system depend on its masses and spins. For a binary at finite separation, it is not possible to define these quantities in unambiguous way; however, there are several reasonable definitions that reduce to the expected values in the limit of infinite separation. Approximate Killing vector (AKV) spin is one of the spin definitions used in the numerical relativity code SpEC. AKV spin requires finding approximate Killing vectors on an apparent horizon, which reduces to a generalized eigenvalue problem of size $\mathcal{O}(L^2)$, and a direct solve has time complexity $\mathcal{O}(L^6)$, where $L$ is the highest spherical harmonic mode used to represent the apparent horizon. This scaling means that the cost of computing AKV spins increases rapidly as we simulate systems at higher resolutions, especially those with high spin or mass ratios. We describe a new algorithm for computing AKV spins that is much faster than the current algorithm.
- **Summary:** The authors address a computational bottleneck in numerical relativity by developing a faster algorithm for computing approximate Killing vector (AKV) spins on apparent horizons during binary black hole evolutions. The current direct solver scales as $\mathcal{O}(L^6)$ with spherical harmonic resolution, making it prohibitively expensive for high-resolution or high-spin simulations. The new algorithm dramatically reduces this cost, enabling more efficient computation of spin diagnostics without sacrificing accuracy.
- **Relevance:** A practical numerical relativity improvement that matters for large-scale binary black hole simulations. Faster spin computation enables higher-resolution studies that are essential for accurate gravitational wave template generation.
- **Tags:** Numerical Relativity, Binary Black Holes, Computational Methods

## 13. Complexity measures in holographic cascading theories with multiscale dynamics

- **arXiv ID:** 2608.10060
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10060
- **Categories:** hep-th
- **Abstract:** Using the gravitational duals, we perform a systematic study of two notions of complexity in a family of three-dimensional gauge theories with rich infrared structure. For thermofield double states, we employ the complexity=volume prescription, which relates computational complexity to the volume of the dual Einstein-Rosen bridge. For one particle states created by the insertion of a local operator on the vacuum, we study their spreading in Krylov space, encoded holographically by the radial momentum of bulk excitations. We investigate these two notions of complexity across the parameter space of the theories, focusing on the two limiting values of a tunable parameter. They find that near a ``walking'' conformal regime, both measures detect the slow-running dynamics, but their sensitivity to confinement differs: volume complexity is largely blind to it, while Krylov complexity oscillates at a frequency tied to the confinement scale. This suggests that different complexity proposals probe different aspects of the gauge theory's infrared physics.
- **Relevance:** Contributes to the ongoing effort to understand what holographic complexity actually measures. The comparison between volume and Krylov complexity in a nontrivial gauge theory provides valuable data for the broader complexity-in-quantum-gravity program.

## 14. Recursion Coefficients and Krylov Dynamics in Polynomial Random Matrix Models

- **arXiv ID:** 2608.10072
- **URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10072
- **Categories:** quant-ph, hep-th, math-ph
- **Abstract:** We study the recursion coefficients of orthogonal polynomials and their associated Krylov dynamics in random matrix models with high-degree and possibly asymmetric polynomial potentials. We develop a moment recursion method that, when combined with the recursive algorithm, provides an efficient construction of the recursion coefficients. We also obtain their large-$n$ asymptotic behavior for general asymmetric potentials; for $Nw_d=1$, the leading asymptotic form of $R_n$ reproduces Freud's conjecture. - **Summary:** The authors develop an efficient moment-recursion method for computing orthogonal polynomial recursion coefficients in random matrix models with high-degree, asymmetric potentials. Applied to an asymmetric quartic model and the double-scaled SYK model, they find that gradient catastrophes in the recursion functions signal chaotic transition regions in the coefficients. Interestingly, these transitions do not qualitatively alter the spread complexity — instead, a two-branch structure in the recursion function produces early oscillations followed by monotonic growth, providing a diagnostic of the model's spectral structure.
- **Relevance:** Connects random matrix theory and Krylov complexity to quantum gravity via the SYK model. The technical tools for handling asymmetric potentials may be applicable to broader holographic models.
